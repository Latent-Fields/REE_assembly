# Provenance Errors, False Evidence Multiplication, and Psychosis

**Date:** 2026-09-09  
**Status:** unprocessed computational-psychiatry thought; not a scientific claim registration, not a diagnostic model of psychosis, not an experiment-queue mutation  
**Origin:** convergence/divergence programme, especially Assays 001–006 and the finding that corrupted provenance should remain uncertainty-bearing rather than be hardened into categorical ancestry claims  
**Related REE work:** convergence/divergence relation graph; hypothesis-tag / precommit-versus-commit distinctions; hippocampal replay; predictive processing; TCRT; sleep/replay; information hunger and closure.

## 1. Trigger

The convergence programme began from a simple question:

> When several different systems point toward the same thing, should that convergence itself matter?

The synthetic assays quickly forced a qualification. Agreement only carries extra epistemic weight if the agreeing signals are sufficiently independent. Five genuinely independent reasons can justify much more confidence than one reason copied five times.

That immediately raises a computational-psychiatry possibility.

A source-monitoring error may do more than assign the wrong origin to an experience.

It may also cause the architecture to **miscount how many independent pieces of evidence exist**.

This creates a possible bridge from source attribution to confidence formation:

```text
one internally generated event
        ↓
reinstated / transformed through several systems
        ↓
provenance becomes uncertain or wrong
        ↓
related descendants are treated as independent evidence
        ↓
confidence rises without new world evidence
```

The proposed mechanism is therefore not simply:

```text
internal event -> mislabelled external
```

but potentially:

```text
internal event
   -> wrong provenance
   -> false independence
   -> duplicated evidential weight
   -> premature confidence / closure
```

This is a hypothesis for investigation, not an established account of psychosis.

## 2. Literature grounding: source monitoring in psychosis

There is substantial prior literature linking psychosis, and hallucinations in particular, with impaired source monitoring.

### Damiani et al. 2022

**Damiani S, Donadeo A, Bassetti N, Salazar-de-Pablo G, Guiot C, Politi P, Fusar-Poli P. _Understanding source monitoring subtypes and their relation to psychosis: a systematic review and meta-analysis._ Psychiatry and Clinical Neurosciences. 2022;76(5):162–171. DOI 10.1111/pcn.13338. PMID 35124869.**

Across 44 studies, source-monitoring performance was impaired in psychosis relative to healthy controls. Internal source monitoring and imagined stimuli showed particularly marked impairment. Patients with hallucinations versus those without showed more externalising and imagined/auditory source-monitoring errors.

This supports source attribution as a real computational vulnerability in psychosis, but does not establish the REE false-independence mechanism.

### Damiani et al. 2024 (online 2023)

**Damiani S et al. _The noise in our brain: A systematic review and meta-analysis of neuroimaging and signal-detection studies on source monitoring in psychosis._ Journal of Psychiatric Research. 2024;169:142–151. DOI 10.1016/j.jpsychires.2023.11.036. PMID 38039688.**

The meta-analysis found substantial source-monitoring impairment in psychosis and argued that discrimination measures may capture the bidirectional internal/external confusion better than simple error counts.

This is relevant to REE because the problem may not be a unidirectional `internal -> external` switch. The architecture may represent uncertainty over origin poorly or collapse uncertain origin into a categorical assignment.

### Brookwell, Bentall & Varese 2013

**_Externalizing biases and hallucinations in source-monitoring, self-monitoring and signal detection studies: a meta-analytic review._ Psychological Medicine. PMID 23282942.**

The review found moderate-to-large effects linking hallucination proneness / hallucinations with externalising biases in several paradigms.

Again, this supports the source-monitoring component, not the downstream evidence-multiplication hypothesis.

### Important negative evidence

The effect is not universal. First-episode work has sometimes failed to find the predicted generalized externalisation bias; for example, **Varese et al. 2011** found no broad external-misattribution effect and concluded that source-monitoring bias may not be central early in psychosis (PMID 21498051).

Therefore REE should not encode:

```text
psychosis = source monitoring failure
```

or:

```text
hallucinations = internal event labelled external
```

The literature is more heterogeneous than either equation.

## 3. Distributed self-monitoring is a better biological neighbour

A 2026 review by **Skipper, Lametti & Green, _Inner speech and the neurobiology of psychosis_, Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews 106805**, integrates inner speech, efference copy, auditory attenuation and self-monitoring.

The relevant point for REE is that source attribution may emerge from a distributed recurrent process rather than from one explicit source-tag register.

In ordinary speech production, motor predictions / efference-copy-like signals help distinguish self-generated auditory consequences from externally caused input. Failures across prediction, suppression and monitoring could therefore alter the experienced source of internally generated material.

REE should preserve this as a warning against implementing provenance as a decorative immutable label.

A provenance representation may need to be:

- distributed;
- probabilistic;
- inherited through transformations;
- updated by evidence;
- vulnerable to corruption;
- and cross-checked against causal prediction.

## 4. The new REE extension: provenance errors can change evidence cardinality

The literature above asks mainly:

> Where did this experience come from?

The convergence work adds a second question:

> How many genuinely independent reasons does this experience represent?

These questions are related but not identical.

Suppose an internal prediction `H` generates several descendants:

```text
H generated in E1
   ↓
hippocampal replay reinstates H-like content
   ↓
E2 simulates a trajectory consistent with H
   ↓
ambiguous perception is interpreted through H
```

If ancestry is preserved, the architecture can recognise that these are highly dependent observations.

Schematically:

```text
E1(H)
 ├── replay(H)
 ├── simulated consequence(H)
 └── perception interpreted under H

=> one ancestry family, not four independent confirmations
```

If provenance is corrupted, the same state may be represented as:

```text
prediction supports H
memory supports H
simulation supports H
perception supports H

=> apparent independent convergence
```

The world has supplied no corresponding fourfold evidence increase.

The architecture has **manufactured corroboration by losing genealogy**.

This is the core new hypothesis.

## 5. Hallucination-like versus delusion-like consequences

This thought should preserve two different possible consequences rather than collapsing them.

### A. Phenomenological / hallucination-like branch

If a self-generated representation loses evidence that it was self-generated, it may acquire inappropriate external or perceptual status.

Very approximately:

```text
self-generated content
+ degraded self-generation prediction / provenance
→ external-source attribution pressure
```

This is closest to established source-monitoring and inner-speech theories.

### B. Epistemic / delusion-maintenance branch

If the descendants of one representation lose their shared ancestry, they may be counted as several mutually corroborating observations.

```text
one hypothesis
→ several dependent descendants
→ ancestry lost
→ false independent convergence
→ confidence inflation
```

This could, in principle, help stabilize a belief even when external evidence remains weak.

The second branch is much more speculative and should not be described as an established mechanism of delusions.

It is, however, directly testable in an artificial architecture.

## 6. A possible positive-feedback loop

The most concerning form is recursive.

```text
initial uncertain hypothesis H
        ↓
internal prediction / replay / imagery generates H-consistent descendants
        ↓
provenance error makes descendants appear partly independent
        ↓
confidence in H increases
        ↓
H gains precision / attentional control
        ↓
more H-consistent internal descendants are generated or selected
        ↓
more false corroboration
```

This resembles a self-confirming evidence loop.

The key pathology would not be that the system predicts.

Prediction is necessary.

The pathology would be that **predictions are allowed to return as evidence without retaining sufficient information that they descend from the hypothesis being evaluated**.

A compact rule is:

> **A hypothesis must not be allowed to cite its own descendants as independent witnesses.**

This may be a useful general anti-hallucination / anti-delusional design principle for artificial cognition even if the biological psychosis analogy proves incomplete.

---

## 7. Relation to artificial-model “hallucination”

The term `hallucination` in current artificial intelligence is importantly different from a clinical hallucination. It usually means generation of an unsupported or false assertion.

The convergence/provenance framework nevertheless suggests a potentially useful engineering analogy.

A generative model can create an answer, summarize it into memory, retrieve the summary later, reason from it, and then encounter its own derived content again. Without ancestry tracking, repeated transformations can make one unsupported generation appear increasingly well supported.

The design objective is not necessarily to eliminate every initial error.

It may be more achievable to prevent:

```text
one error -> many apparent confirmations
```

Thus error tolerance can arise partly from **containing the epistemic reproductive number of an error**.

An error may occur.

It should not automatically acquire descendants that vote as independent witnesses for their parent.

## 8. Soft provenance may be safer than categorical provenance

Assay 005 produced a relevant synthetic result: when provenance information was missing or wrong, uncertainty-bearing / soft provenance degraded more gracefully than hard categorical ancestry labels.

That suggests a possible general rule:

```text
unknown provenance != independent provenance
```

and equally:

```text
uncertain source != confidently external source
```

A robust architecture should be capable of representing:

- known self-generated;
- probably self-generated;
- mixed ancestry;
- transformed from source X;
- externally observed;
- jointly determined;
- unknown source;
- conflicting source evidence.

Forcing uncertain ancestry into a binary self/external or independent/dependent decision can create avoidable confidence errors.

---

## 9. Provenance is richer than source identity

A useful provenance record may eventually need more than `source = E1` or `source = perception`.

Candidate dimensions include:

```text
originating event / ancestor ID
transformation lineage
hypothetical vs observed status
precommit vs committed-world consequence
replay vs waking observation
self-generated motor consequence prediction
external-agent contribution
confidence in each provenance edge
context / temporal gate
shared latent / representation ancestry
whether this evidence has already contributed to the current belief update
```

This resembles a causal genealogy rather than a label.

The relation-graph work makes that increasingly natural.

## 10. First decisive artificial experiment

The cleanest experiment should hold **content constant** and manipulate only provenance integrity.

### World

Create one latent hypothesis `H` and one genuinely external evidence stream.

Generate matched H-consistent descendants through several internal systems:

- prediction;
- replay;
- counterfactual simulation;
- retrieved memory;
- ambiguous perceptual interpretation.

The actual informational content is held fixed across conditions.

### Conditions

1. **VERIDICAL PROVENANCE**  
   All descendants retain correct shared ancestry.

2. **SOFT PROVENANCE UNCERTAINTY**  
   Ancestry confidence is degraded but not falsely reassigned.

3. **CATEGORICAL PROVENANCE ERROR**  
   Descendants are incorrectly treated as separate / externally grounded sources.

4. **PROVENANCE ABSENT**  
   Source genealogy is removed and the downstream system must decide how unknown ancestry should count.

### Primary dependent variables

Measure:

- posterior confidence in `H`;
- effective independent evidence count;
- number of additional queries requested;
- closure / stop-thinking time;
- sensitivity to contradictory external evidence;
- persistence after replay;
- reversal after true disconfirmatory evidence;
- whether a belief becomes self-maintaining without additional external evidence.

### Key prediction

If provenance errors merely change source labels but do not change epistemic weighting, confidence should remain matched across conditions once information content is controlled.

If the false-evidence-multiplication hypothesis is correct:

```text
categorical provenance error
    > provenance absent (depending on default)
    > soft uncertainty
    > veridical provenance
```

for inappropriate confidence inflation / premature closure.

The exact ordering of the middle two must be preregistered from the implementation semantics rather than assumed.

---

## 11. Strong falsifier

The hypothesis should weaken substantially if:

- content-matched provenance corruption does not inflate confidence;
- provenance corruption alters phenomenological source assignment but not evidence cardinality;
- ordinary covariance/dependency estimation repairs the problem without ancestry information;
- contradictory external evidence is handled normally despite provenance corruption;
- replay does not amplify the effect;
- or the only way to produce the phenomenon is to explicitly program a pathological confidence bonus.

A negative result would still separate source attribution from evidence multiplication, which is scientifically useful.

---

## 12. Potential relation to replay and psychosis-like persistence

Replay may be particularly important because it naturally regenerates internally stored content after the original event.

If replay preserves genealogy, it can strengthen learned structure without pretending to be new observation.

If replay loses genealogy, the architecture risks converting remembered or simulated content into apparent new evidence.

This creates a possible testable interaction:

```text
provenance corruption × replay count
```

A pathological result would be confidence rising with replay exposure despite no additional world evidence.

A healthy result would be improved representation / consolidation without a false increase in independent external evidence.

This distinction may become useful for the broader REE waking-versus-sleep programme.

---

## 13. Relation to prediction, perception and circular inference

This thought also touches predictive-processing accounts of psychosis, but care is needed.

Predictive perception necessarily allows prior beliefs to shape interpretation. That is not pathology.

The specific concern here is narrower:

> **Can evidence shaped by a prediction return upstream and be treated as though it were independent of that prediction?**

If so, feedback can become circular evidence rather than ordinary recurrent inference.

The relation graph may need to distinguish lawful recurrence from epistemic double-counting.

This is conceptually adjacent to circular-inference models of schizophrenia but should not be declared equivalent without a dedicated literature comparison.

---

## 14. Clinical epistemic restraint

This thought is not a claim that psychosis is caused by one provenance mechanism.

Psychosis is heterogeneous, and hallucinations, delusions, thought interference, disorganization, affective changes and negative symptoms need not share one computational lesion.

The strongest biologically grounded statement currently justified is:

> Source-monitoring and self-monitoring abnormalities are repeatedly observed in psychosis, particularly in relation to hallucinations, but they are neither universal nor sufficient explanations of the syndrome.

The REE-specific extension is:

> A provenance error could additionally corrupt the estimated independence of evidence, allowing one internally generated causal lineage to masquerade as several corroborating sources.

That extension remains to be tested.

---

## 15. Architectural design pressure

Even before any psychosis interpretation succeeds, this generates a robust-engineering requirement for REE:

> **Every representation that can later influence belief, confidence, closure or action should preserve enough uncertain causal genealogy to prevent its own descendants from being blindly counted as independent evidence.**

This does not imply storing an unbounded full history.

A compressed provenance structure may be enough:

- shared-ancestor probability;
- externally anchored observation flag / confidence;
- hypothetical/replay lineage;
- already-counted evidence family;
- context-conditioned dependency estimate.

The synthetic convergence work suggests that even approximate soft genealogy may be substantially safer than confident but wrong source assignments.

---

## 16. Compact formulation

> **An error-tolerant cognitive architecture need not prevent every false representation. It must prevent one false representation from reproducing through prediction, memory, replay and interpretation and returning as multiple apparently independent reasons to believe itself. Provenance errors may therefore be computationally dangerous not only because they misidentify the source of an experience, but because they can inflate the apparent cardinality of evidence. This offers a falsifiable bridge between REE's provenance-aware convergence machinery, artificial-model confabulation, and selected source-monitoring phenomena in psychosis, while remaining substantially more speculative than the established clinical source-monitoring literature.**
