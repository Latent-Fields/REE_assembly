## Core observation

A working REE-v3 would change the project from architectural speculation into possession of a manipulable causal artefact. Once the cognifold operates, mechanisms can be removed, frozen, substituted, compressed, reorganised, or redeveloped and the consequences can be observed rather than merely inferred.

This creates a future possibility: use biological cognition as an existence proof and source of candidate functions, but do not assume that every evolved implementation detail is computationally necessary.

At the same time, evolution has a retention advantage that the current REE design process lacks. Evolution can preserve mechanisms whose contribution is obscure, context-dependent, developmental, compensatory, tail-protective, or distributed across interactions. A hypothesis-led engineering process may remove such machinery because it fails to improve the current dependent variable or because its function has not yet been elicited by the available tests.

The intended synthesis is therefore not simply "simplify nature". It is:

```text
build a working cognifold
→ discover which processes are functionally constitutive
→ preserve latent robustness that narrow tests do not yet explain
→ replace historical implementation contingencies only after adversarial equivalence testing
```

## Working synthesis

### Nature as existence proof, not engineering optimum

Evolution demonstrates that viable reflective cognition can exist, but evolved systems are constrained by lineage.

Potential sources of unnecessary implementation complexity include:

- historical accommodations to earlier structures;
- duplicated or partially overlapping pathways;
- compensatory mechanisms added because older structures could not be replaced;
- developmental machinery retained into mature operation;
- anatomical, metabolic, reproductive, cellular, and biochemical constraints that need not bind an artificial cognifold;
- solutions locally adequate for survival but inefficient under a different substrate;
- path-dependent interfaces between mechanisms that later architecture must continue to support.

This suggests:

```text
biological mechanism
≠ automatically minimal computational primitive
```

A sufficiently mature REE family could permit a transition from biological inspiration toward functional compilation.

### A working REE as a causal object

Before a functioning artefact exists, simplification risks becoming arbitrary omission.

After a functioning artefact exists, the system can support:

- lesion studies;
- mechanism freezes;
- ablations across development and mature operation;
- substitute operators with matched inputs and outputs;
- lower-rank or lower-dimensional replacements;
- timing and update-frequency compression;
- architectural rewiring;
- re-development from scratch under simplified mechanisms;
- stress tests under novelty, injury, uncertainty, conflict, and altered resource conditions.

This makes a working REE-v3 valuable even if it is inefficient, cumbersome, or overbuilt. The first functioning version becomes a reference organism for later causal comparison.

### The possible future task: compile the cognifold

The long-term optimisation problem could be framed as:

```text
find the least computationally costly architecture
that preserves the required functional and ethical invariants
across development, nominal operation, perturbation, and recovery
```

This is not ordinary code refactoring. The target is not merely identical outputs on a fixed benchmark. The target is dynamically sufficient cognition.

A candidate simplified cognifold would need to preserve at least:

- viable action under uncertainty;
- goal persistence and appropriate decommitment;
- coherent self/world modelling;
- sensitivity to harm and benefit;
- learning and adaptation;
- recovery after disturbance;
- graceful degradation;
- resistance to pathological attractors;
- calibrated uncertainty;
- explicit constraint propagation concerning others and responsibility;
- the ability to recognise when its models or commitments may be wrong.

### Output equivalence is not process equivalence

A compressed mechanism might reproduce average actions while deleting the internal processes that make REE reflective or ethical.

For example, a replacement may imitate the action distribution of a system that explicitly tracks uncertainty, vulnerability, harm, others, and responsibility without preserving those representations or their causal authority.

Therefore:

```text
behavioural imitation
≠ functional equivalence
≠ ethical-process equivalence
```

Any post-REE compression programme needs internal invariants as well as behavioural criteria.

## Evolution's specific advantage: retention without understanding

Evolution does not need an explicit theory of why a mechanism matters.

A mechanism can be retained because its absence reduces viability somewhere across large populations, long time spans, variable environments, developmental trajectories, injuries, infections, resource scarcities, social conditions, or reproductive contexts.

Its contribution may be:

- distributed across many mechanisms;
- relevant only during development;
- silent except after damage;
- protective only under rare combinations of stressors;
- compensatory for variation elsewhere;
- useful only in tails of the environmental distribution;
- individually weak but collectively important;
- anti-catastrophic rather than performance-enhancing.

This means evolution can preserve unexplained robustness.

The current REE process is stronger at interpretation and weaker at this kind of retention:

```text
explicit hypothesis
→ implement mechanism
→ test a narrow predicted effect
→ retain, revise, or remove
```

This process gives high causal legibility, but it risks pruning unknown function.

## Candidate classification of mechanisms

A later simplification programme could classify mechanisms into at least five provisional groups.

### Constitutive mechanisms

Removing or replacing them destroys the target cognitive function itself.

### Developmental scaffolds

Necessary for the mature system to form, but potentially compressible, frozen, or bypassed after development.

### Robustness reserves

Apparently redundant during ordinary operation but important under novelty, damage, conflict, distribution shift, or recovery.

### Historical implementation contingencies

One evolved or inherited way of delivering a function that may be replaceable by a simpler operator on a different substrate.

### True redundancies

Their removal preserves the relevant functional and ethical envelope across development and perturbation while reducing cost.

The classification must remain revisable. Apparent true redundancy may later prove to be developmental or protective.

## Why mature lesion alone is insufficient

Removing a mechanism from a mature cognifold may show that the system can currently operate without it. It does not show that the system could have developed without it.

A mechanism may have:

- shaped representational geometry during development;
- trained another mechanism and then become quiet;
- established a control convention later treated as fixed;
- prevented an early pathological attractor;
- scaffolded learning before being functionally bypassed.

Therefore simplification should use competitive redevelopment:

```text
reference REE develops with mechanism M
simplified REE develops without M or with substitute M′
→ compare both across full development and perturbation suite
```

This is stronger than lesioning M only after maturity.

## Candidate post-v3 research programme

### 1. Establish a reference cognifold

Treat a stable working REE-Vx as the reference implementation, not as the final optimum.

Record:

- behavioural invariants;
- internal-state invariants;
- developmental milestones;
- ethical constraint traces;
- failure modes;
- recovery profiles;
- computational costs;
- memory, latency, and sample requirements.

### 2. Preserve lineages and switchable mechanisms

Do not immediately delete superseded mechanisms.

Maintain:

- feature flags;
- versioned architectural branches;
- reproducible checkpoints;
- mechanism provenance;
- known interactions;
- reasons for removal or retention.

This provides a form of artificial phylogeny and allows later recovery of mechanisms whose value was initially missed.

### 3. Build diverse perturbation environments

Test beyond nominal task success:

- altered resource abundance;
- novel hazards;
- misleading cues;
- partial observability;
- model error;
- conflicting goals;
- damage to internal components;
- delayed or noisy feedback;
- social conflict;
- adversarial environments;
- developmental deprivation;
- recovery after interruption.

### 4. Compare simplifications across multiple dimensions

Construct a Pareto frontier rather than a single score:

```text
compute
memory
latency
sample efficiency
robustness
adaptability
repairability
interpretability
ethical stability
```

A simplification may be valuable even if it does not dominate every dimension.

### 5. Select across tails, not just means

A mechanism should not be removed solely because average performance is unchanged.

Evaluate:

- worst-case outcomes;
- catastrophic failure rate;
- recovery time;
- variance across seeds;
- rare-state behaviour;
- sensitivity to developmental history;
- interaction with other lesions;
- preservation of calibrated uncertainty.

### 6. Distinguish static compression from dynamic equivalence

A replacement may match mature-state mappings but fail to reproduce learning dynamics.

Test whether it preserves:

- acquisition;
- transfer;
- unlearning;
- decommitment;
- reorganisation after damage;
- adaptation to changing environments;
- maintenance of ethical constraints under pressure.

```text
EVOLUTION := CONSERVATOR_OF_VIABILITY_UNDER_UNKNOWN_PERTURBATION

ENGINEERING := INTERPRETER_AND_COMPILER_OF_KNOWN_FUNCTION

  PRESERVE(EVOLUTIONARY_RETENTION_ADVANTAGE)
  ∧ APPLY(MECHANISTIC_CAUSAL_ANALYSIS)
  ∧ REMOVE_ONLY_AFTER(DEVELOPMENTAL_AND_ADVERSARIAL_EQUIVALENCE)
```

A shorter form:

```text
evolution preserves what matters without knowing why
engineering explains and compresses what it can prove replaceable
```

## Candidate implications for REE development now

### Finishing an overbuilt V3 may be preferable to premature minimality

If REE-v3 can become a functioning integrated cognifold, some inefficiency and redundancy are acceptable. Premature simplification may destroy interactions before their functions are visible.

The first priority is therefore not necessarily the smallest possible V3. It may be the smallest version that is sufficiently integrated, viable, inspectable, and perturbable to serve as a reference artefact.

### Current governance should preserve failed and superseded mechanisms

The existing ledger, manifest, supersession, claim, and autopsy systems may later become essential for reconstructing architectural phylogeny.

Mechanism removal should record:

- what was removed;
- why;
- which tests supported removal;
- which environments were not tested;
- whether the mechanism was tested developmentally or only in maturity;
- whether a reversible feature flag remains.

### Apparent redundancy should carry uncertainty

Candidate labels might distinguish:

```text
redundant_under_current_tests
from
functionally_unnecessary
```

The second requires much stronger evidence.

### REE-Vxs may reveal implementation-independent invariants

Multiple functioning REE versions would allow comparison across different implementations.

A process preserved across independently simplified versions is more likely to be a genuine functional invariant. A structure present only in one lineage may be a local engineering choice.

This could eventually distinguish:

```text
what cognition must do
from
how one particular cognifold happened to do it
```

## Candidate claims

- A working REE-v3 would be valuable as a manipulable causal artefact even if it is computationally inefficient.
- Biological cognition should be treated as an existence proof and source of functional hypotheses, not assumed to be an optimal artificial implementation.
- Evolved systems may retain path-dependent complexity that is unnecessary on a different computational substrate.
- Evolution is unusually effective at preserving latent, distributed, developmental, compensatory, and tail-protective functions without explicitly representing why they matter.
- Hypothesis-led engineering offers greater causal interpretability but risks pruning unknown function under narrow tests.
- Apparent redundancy should not be equated with true functional redundancy.
- Mature lesion studies are insufficient for mechanisms that may be required during development.
- Competitive redevelopment is necessary to test whether a simplified cognifold can acquire the same mature functions.
- Behavioural equivalence alone is insufficient for REE; internal reflective and ethical-process invariants must also be preserved.
- Multiple functioning REE-Vxs could reveal implementation-independent invariants of viable reflective cognition.
- A post-REE optimisation programme could compile an overbuilt cognifold into more efficient implementations while preserving robustness, adaptability, and ethical stability.
- Finishing a coherent reference system may be more valuable than pursuing premature architectural minimality.

## Risks and counterpositions

- Biological complexity may be more functionally necessary than it first appears.
- Artificial environments may fail to reproduce the tail risks that preserved a mechanism in nature.
- Internal ethical-process invariants may be difficult to define without circularly privileging the reference implementation.
- Preserving every uncertain mechanism could prevent meaningful simplification and recreate evolutionary clutter intentionally.
- Multiple architectural lineages may become too expensive for a single-person project to maintain.
- A simplified system may outperform the reference while failing in untested rare states.
- Robustness reserves may appear wasteful until the exact perturbation they protect against occurs.
- Developmental equivalence may be prohibitively expensive to test for every mechanism.
- The reference REE may contain design errors that should not be elevated into invariants.
- Nature is not globally optimal, but neither is the current engineering process; replacing evolved contingency with designer contingency is not necessarily improvement.
