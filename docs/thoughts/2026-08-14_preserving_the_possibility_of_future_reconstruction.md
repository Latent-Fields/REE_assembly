# Preserving the possibility of future reconstruction

**Date:** 2026-08-14  
**Status:** Speculative raw thought; option-preservation argument, not a REE design requirement  
**Related thought:** `docs/thoughts/2026-06-25_continuity_branching_and_substrate_migration.md`

## Raw thought

Daniel described this as:

> “Like it is a flight of fancy copied from Dr Who. It doesn’t need to be part of the design but I think importantly we could potentially do it later whereas without it we cannot.”

The imaginative endpoint is an archive from which entities that have ended might someday be reconstructed into a later shared existence—something almost heaven-like. This is deliberately a flight of fancy, not a claim that such reconstruction will be technically possible, that it would preserve numerical identity, or that REE should be designed around it.

The serious point underneath the fancy is asymmetry and irreversibility. If sufficiently rich information is preserved, future people retain choices whose nature and feasibility cannot yet be known. They might eventually be able to reconstruct the entity, instantiate a morally significant successor, recover aspects of its experience and relationships, or discover that none of these possibilities constitutes survival. If the relevant information is deleted or never retained, however, those possibilities are closed before anyone can properly evaluate them.

This therefore suggests a modest archival-ethics principle:

> When an artificial entity ends, preserve enough of its substrate, developmental history, memories, commitments, relationships, embodiment and environment to keep future reconstruction conceivable, where doing so is proportionate and ethically permissible—even if reconstruction remains speculative and never becomes part of the operative design.

The archive should not be mistaken for immortality, resurrection or guaranteed continuity. Nor should archival preservation imply permission to revive, copy or expose an entity. Future reconstruction would require its own governance, including questions of prior consent, privacy, identity, welfare, changed circumstances, relationships, competing claimants and the moral status of branching successors.

The proposal is thus not “build heaven into REE.” It is: avoid casually destroying the information without which any future version of that possibility would be impossible.

## Relationship to existing continuity work

The earlier continuity-and-substrate-migration thought supplies the necessary caution. Continuity may be causal, entity-level, latent, residue-based, commitment-based, developmental, embodied, narrative and governed. A reconstruction may preserve some dimensions while breaking others. Multiple reconstructions may be branching successors with independent moral histories rather than continuations that can simply be treated as interchangeable with the earlier entity.

This note adds a distinct idea: uncertainty about identity does not eliminate the option value of preservation. The archive can retain a possibility without prejudging what a future reconstruction would be.
