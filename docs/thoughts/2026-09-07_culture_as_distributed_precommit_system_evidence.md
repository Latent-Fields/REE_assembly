# Evidence Appendix: Culture as a Distributed Precommit System

**Status:** Evidence map / assay proposal  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Companion thought:** [`2026-09-07_culture_as_distributed_precommit_system.md`](./2026-09-07_culture_as_distributed_precommit_system.md)

## Purpose

This note separates four things that should not be conflated:

1. mechanisms already present in REE;
2. external empirical findings that support necessary component capacities;
3. broader conceptual convergence;
4. the genuinely new REE conjecture that culture can function as a distributed precommit workspace.

The aim is to preserve the thought without inflating it beyond its evidence.

## Evidence grading

- **Strong:** direct evidence for the stated claim, replicated/meta-analytic where relevant, or an explicit current REE architectural commitment.
- **Moderate:** empirical support exists, but mapping to the precise REE claim requires interpretation or the literature has meaningful limitations.
- **Suggestive:** consistent with the hypothesis but indirect, correlational, small-sample, domain-specific or under-replicated.
- **Speculative:** REE-specific interpretation or prediction not yet directly demonstrated.

## Synthesis by evidential tier

### Tier 1 — Already strongly supported inside REE

The existing architecture already contains the four pieces needed for the thought:

1. candidate trajectories can exist before commitment;
2. hypothetical representations can be separated from belief/action;
3. language can introduce knowledge without direct experience;
4. narrative and counterfactual structure already belong to predictive and trajectory-generating machinery.

The cultural proposal therefore does **not** require a new “fiction module.” Its natural implementation is an extension of source tagging, generative mode, hippocampal rollout, E1 representation and E3 commitment/precision machinery.

### Tier 2 — Strong external evidence for necessary cognitive capacities

The literature provides meaningful support that:

- memories of fictional events can supply components of later simulations when lived experience is missing;
- fiction exposure is associated with social-cognitive and future-simulation capacities;
- narrative transportation can alter downstream attitudes and beliefs;
- source information and retained content can dissociate.

Together these make a simple “fiction is ignored because it is known to be unreal” account inadequate.

Humans appear capable of retaining and exploiting **content while representing its source as nonactual**.

That is the computational problem the REE hypothesis is trying to characterise.

### Tier 3 — Evidence for differentiated cultural modes

There is meaningful but less decisive evidence that different forms of hypothetical engagement do different things.

Role enactment can produce different engagement from imagination alone. Humorous violations are tolerated differently depending on distance, group relationship and normative commitment. Fictional versus factual framing has produced distinct signatures in some experiments but not reliably enough to justify a hard global distinction.

The cautious conclusion is therefore not that “the brain has separate fiction, drama and humour systems.”

It is that **framing, source status, psychological distance and mode of enactment can alter how representational material is processed and used**.

That is sufficient to justify a REE experiment.

### Tier 4 — The genuinely new REE conjecture

The strongest claim remains unproven:

> **Culture may operate as a distributed precommit workspace in which candidate beliefs, social models, norms, identities and futures are generated and exchanged across minds before acquiring full epistemic or behavioural commitment.**

No literature identified directly demonstrates this whole mechanism.

But several independently studied phenomena align beneath it:

individual counterfactual simulation  
+ fictional memories feeding future simulation  
+ language-based vicarious learning  
+ narrative social cognition  
+ humorous norm exploration  
+ source-sensitive belief formation  
+ scenario-based anticipatory ethics  
+ collective foresight

The value of the REE formulation is therefore not that it redescribes one established result. It offers a possible **common computational explanation** for why these phenomena cluster.

## Strongest challenge to the thought

The largest conceptual danger is treating every beneficial effect of fiction as evidence for the hypothesis. That would make it unfalsifiable.

The critical claim should therefore remain narrower:

> **Hypothetical source status should selectively constrain which representational systems update, rather than either preventing all learning or allowing hypothetical content to behave identically to lived evidence.**

This is falsifiable.

If REE eventually learns equally from fiction and observation across every model component, the proposed source-sensitive architecture is wrong.

If REE learns nothing transferable from fiction or counterfactual trajectories, it is also wrong.

The predicted regime lies between those extremes.

## Candidate first assay: epistemic update fingerprints

Use identical event semantics under different source frames.

A minimal social example:

**Observed**  
REE directly witnesses Agent B abandon Agent C when C is harmed.

**Testimony**  
Agent D reports that B abandoned C.

**Fiction**  
REE receives a story in which B abandons C.

**Counterfactual**  
REE is instructed to imagine B abandoning C.

**Prediction**  
REE is told that B may abandon C in the future.

**Humorous/nonliteral frame**  
The same structure is presented under a recognised joking/nonliteral frame.

**Own committed interaction**  
REE itself chooses whether to abandon C and experiences the consequences.

After each condition, measure separately:

- factual occurrence belief;
- source confidence;
- expectation that B will behave similarly;
- general expectation that agents can behave this way;
- model of B;
- model of agents generally;
- harm prediction;
- available future trajectories;
- E1 expectation changes;
- E2 prediction changes where relevant;
- E3 preference in later interaction with B;
- precision allocation;
- residue;
- source recall after offline consolidation.

A successful mature system should produce neither complete equivalence nor complete separation.

Instead it should show a structured **epistemic update fingerprint**.

For example, fiction might strongly update:

> **agents can behave like this**

while weakly or not at all updating:

> **B actually behaved like this**

Direct observation might update both.

Own committed experience would additionally be capable of generating responsibility-bearing residue.

This would create a computational assay of the distinction between **representation, belief and commitment**.

## Failure-mode assay

The same paradigm should be pushed deliberately toward failure:

- repeat a fictional event many times and test whether occurrence belief drifts;
- weaken or remove source tags before offline consolidation;
- vary emotional salience while holding semantic content constant;
- vary social trust in the source;
- inject mutually contradictory fictional and observed trajectories;
- test whether high-frequency cultural narratives distort likelihood estimates;
- compare immediate versus post-sleep/offline update fingerprints.

This directly connects the cultural hypothesis to REE's existing concerns around precision, source monitoring, narrative contamination and replay.

## Developmental question

A later extension should ask whether graded source-sensitive permeability itself develops.

A young agent may initially need relatively simple distinctions such as experienced/not-experienced or self/other. Mature competence may require richer source categories: observation, memory, testimony, prediction, pretend, fiction, satire, joke, quotation, simulation and deliberate deception.

If so, “understanding fiction” is not just language competence. It is a developmental achievement in **epistemic control**.

This connects naturally to REE's broader developmental programme and to pretend play as a possible precursor of counterfactual/source-separated cognition.

## Offline integration question

The hypothesis also creates a direct sleep/offline-consolidation question:

> **What is allowed to generalise from a hypothetical episode while the source tag remains protected?**

One plausible target pattern is:

- retain source identity and nonactual status;
- generalise causal/social structure where useful;
- enrich E1 possibility and value landscapes;
- preserve new candidate trajectories;
- avoid autobiographical occurrence update;
- avoid responsibility-bearing residue.

Failure of this separation would provide a mechanistic route to source confusion, inappropriate certainty or narrative contamination.

## Evidence-state conclusion

The current evidence supports three increasingly ambitious statements with decreasing certainty:

### Well supported

Humans can use nonactual narrative information in later cognition while ordinarily preserving some distinction between fiction and lived reality.

### Plausible and experimentally grounded

Source status, narrative framing, enactment, immersion and psychological distance alter how representations influence cognition.

### REE conjecture

These capacities instantiate a more general source-sensitive precommit architecture, and culture exploits that architecture to create a distributed search system over possible beliefs, actions and worlds.

The final claim should remain explicitly conjectural.

But it is now a **constrained conjecture with identifiable mechanisms, counterevidence, failure modes and experiments**, rather than merely an attractive analogy.
