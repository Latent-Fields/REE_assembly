## 1. Adjudication

The strongest surviving account is a **distributed relational-memory system with selective reinstatement and state-dependent routing**. The hippocampal formation can bind event/context relations, express different codes at successive processing stages, and contribute causally to cortical reactivation. Retrosplenial, parietal and entorhinal circuits supply substantial reference-frame organisation. None of these results requires a general hippocampal translator.

There is a serious, testable stronger hypothesis: offline activity may maintain access between representations as their codes change. This has a close computational precedent in Káli and Dayan (2004), rather than being a new theoretical invention. The missing biological discriminator is **replay-dependent rescue of cross-system use after controlled representational change, with memory strength and local information availability held constant**. See sources E15–E23 and E30 below.

| Ledger category | Adjudication |
|---|---|
| **Supported** | Relational/context-sensitive coding; some genuine representational reformatting; causal hippocampal contributions to cortical reinstatement; pathway- and state-dependent routing; causal contributions of appropriately timed offline activity to some memory outcomes; developmental sparsification in specified hippocampal circuits. These are bounded biological findings, not validation of the REE architecture. |
| **Plausible but unproven** | R1 as a shared relational address across specialised consumers; R3 as a receiver-state-conditioned transform beyond cue selection; R4 as interface repair beyond ordinary consolidation; R5 as a necessary developmental route to efficient cross-system access. |
| **Weakened** | Hippocampus-exclusive coordinate transformation; a single homogeneous CA1 decoder; replay as uniformly stabilising; sparsity as one quantity that monotonically increases everywhere; representational alignment as proof of information transfer. |
| **Refuted / unnecessary** | Universal hippocampal dependence of replay is contradicted within the procedural-memory paradigm of E23. A universal translator is unnecessary for the findings assembled here, but has not been experimentally refuted as an unconstrained proposition. Treating repeated descendants as independent observations is epistemically invalid; a hippocampal implementation of ancestry accounting is unestablished. |
| **Highest-information next experiments** | A: frozen-endpoint index/retrieval/frame/receiver comparison. B: controlled remapping × replay pairing × timing, against local co-adaptation. C: matched-final-capacity developmental histories, on a later developmental substrate. |
| **Literature debts** | Direct causal maintenance of interareal access; receiver-state effects beyond query/attention; longitudinal pruning of interareal mappings; content-matched provenance and confidence experiments; independent replication and methodological audits of the newest geometry studies. |

The operative distinction remains:

`encoded != decodable != natively accessible != bridgeable != pairing-specific != causally used != behaviourally useful`

Add recurrent stability as a separate requirement. The interface product `content × communication subspace × reference frame × receiver context × temporal gate` is a useful experimental decomposition, **not an established biological multiplicative law**. The factors can interact, correlate, or be implemented by the same circuit.

## 3. What the systems appear to map or index

### 3.1 Reference frames: distributed conversion, not a hippocampal monopoly

RSC and PPC contain mixtures of body-, route- and world-related variables. Yang et al. report aRSC/pRSC differences and an output population to MEC enriched for integrated world-referenced signals. This is evidence for organised cross-frame processing, but a gradient of tuning is not by itself proof that one recorded population causally computes the transform consumed by another. Høydal et al. provide a complementary relational code: position relative to objects. These findings constrain where to look for R2 and weaken hippocampus-exclusive localisation. [E01–E03, E41]

Within hippocampus, Qian et al. implicate changing input balance and plasticity in expressed space/goal reference frames. Maimon et al. demonstrate strikingly different CA3/CA1 coding regimes in bats in large environments. **Code transformation, frame selection, and inverse reconstruction are different operations.** A sparse-to-dense population transformation does not show that CA1 reconstructs the original cortical pattern, nor that the transform depends on the consumer's state. [E04–E05]

**Established result:** different spatial relations and coding formats are measurable in the distributed circuit. **Computational inference:** combining reference variables can make a relation usable in another frame. **REE hypothesis:** a constrained frame-conditioned bridge improves held-out consumer use at frozen endpoints. Only the third proposition is the proposed REE assay.

### 3.2 Relational indexing: stronger than a location list, weaker than a universal language

Nonspatial task dimensions and graph relationships are represented in hippocampal–entorhinal activity. However, an abstract graph can reflect learned transitions without becoming a universal geometric coordinate system. Garvert et al.'s result specifically favours predictive relational structure over a blanket Euclidean interpretation. Aronov et al.'s sound task extends the coding problem beyond locomotion without independently establishing transfer between consumers. [E12–E13]

Relational invariants can survive changes in local activity. Keinath et al. found preservation of contextual structure despite drift; Fyhn et al. relate hippocampal remapping to entorhinal grid realignment. Such results resist the claim that all remapping destroys shared structure, but they do not identify a mapping-maintenance mechanism. They also leave open a simpler possibility: drift occurs largely in directions irrelevant to the stable readout. [E14, E40]

The strongest cross-system evidence here is not abstract coding alone. Selective silencing of encoding-tagged CA1 ensembles impaired both memory and reactivation of associated cortical ensembles in Tanaka et al. This supports an indexing/reinstatement contribution. It still permits cortical pattern completion after a hippocampal trigger, rather than requiring detailed hippocampal reconstruction of cortical geometry. [E07]

**Established result:** contextual and relational memories can depend on hippocampal ensembles and their interactions with cortex. **Computational inference:** indices can coordinate reactivation of distributed traces. **REE hypothesis:** the same index preserves reference across heterogeneous consumers under remapping. The last step needs pairing-specific cross-system use, not just correlated recall.

### 3.3 Query selection, routing and receiver conditioning must be split

Top-down cortical intervention can elicit hippocampal memory retrieval; prefrontal/hippocampal interactions vary over context-guided retrieval; human EC neurons can code the location of the cued memory. These are substantive R3 neighbours. Yet query selection can be implemented by selecting an index and returning its ordinary associate. Nothing in that operation necessarily requires a map that reads the recipient's latent state. [E09–E11]

Gonzalez et al. separate partner-associated subspaces through CA1, including state-dependent changes. Kitanishi et al. add target-related subicular routing and timing. This brings the evidence closer to an actual interface than anatomical overlap or regional coactivation. But projection identity is not receiver-state conditioning; a fixed projection can select different signals without constructing `T(A,B)`. [E06, E36]

Anatomical direction, temporal precedence, decoding direction and causal influence should each receive a separate label. Partial CCA can control recorded common signals; it cannot guarantee removal of unrecorded common causes. Neither lag nor the word “communication” upgrades a representational result to perturbational evidence.

### 3.4 Replay: maintenance is a serious hypothesis, not the default explanation

The decisive theoretical predecessor is Káli and Dayan (2004): cortical learning can change the interpretation of old hippocampal traces, and replay can maintain access. This directly addresses a correspondence problem. It is computational evidence that the mechanism can work under the model's assumptions, not an observation that biological replay performs this job. [E15]

Biology provides complementary pieces. Maingret et al. manipulated timing of hippocampo-cortical coordination and improved later memory. Rothschild et al. found cortical activity predicting hippocampal replay content and hippocampal patterns predicting subsequent cortical activity. Nitzan et al. identify a subiculum–RSC propagation pathway. These support timed, content-related interaction; they do not isolate maintenance of a changing code-to-code relation. [E16–E18]

The strongest counterweight is heterogeneity. Roux and van de Ven found causal stabilisation effects in particular learning/assembly conditions; Kovács found CA1 spatial stability surviving sleep/rest SWR blockade in their paradigm. Maboudi and Bollmann show offline reorganisation rather than simple freezing of yesterday's activity. A universal prediction that replay always stabilises every representation is therefore untenable. [E19–E22, E42]

The 2026 human retrieval geometry study associates ripple timing with cortical expansion and successful reinstatement. Its causal-sounding title must not replace its observational design. Increased dimensionality is neither reduced minimum bridge complexity nor proof of a compressed hippocampal code being transmitted. A separate human study links prestimulus ripples to uncertainty-dependent cortical responses, reminding us that ripples can accompany perceptual preparation as well as episodic replay. [E28–E29]

**Established result:** offline events can alter memory and ensemble dynamics; their effects depend on timing, region, task and learning history. **Computational inference:** timed interaction could maintain correspondence. **REE hypothesis:** correctly paired replay selectively repairs cross-system use after known representational drift, beyond local rehearsal or co-adaptation. That is the priority experiment.

### 3.5 Development: three kinds of sparsity and a missing causal bridge

Do not conflate (i) fewer recurrent synapses, (ii) fewer active neurons per memory, and (iii) a lower-dimensional interareal communication surface. Vargas-Barroso et al. concern local CA3 connectivity and synaptic efficacy; Ramsaran et al. concern CA1 engram allocation, inhibition and memory precision. Neither measures the complexity of a cross-system translator. Early spatial-code development is also heterogeneous rather than a single dense-to-sparse schedule. [E24–E27]

Thus R5 must split into an established circuit-development phenomenon and an unproven functional-history claim. The latter asks whether a mature interface needs to have passed through a broader developmental regime. Cross-sectional age comparisons cannot answer the matched-final-capacity counterfactual. The observed CA3-to-CA1 sparse-to-dense spatial code (E05) is not a contradiction of CA3 synaptic sparsification (E24): they measure different quantities and axes.

## 4. Strongest alternative model and what would defeat it

The strongest rival is **indexed associative reinstatement plus distributed frame processing and adaptive routing**:

1. RSC/PPC/EC provide appropriate spatial and task coordinates.
2. Hippocampal ensembles bind episode/context associations and support completion from partial cues.
3. Task/cortical input selects which memory is retrieved.
4. Projection structure and oscillatory timing determine its influence on a target.
5. Target-local plasticity and repeated activation preserve access or consolidate content; an explicit generic translation stage is unnecessary.

This is an analytical combination of E01–E18, E30, E36 and E39, not a new experimentally established unified model. It predicts much of the currently positive literature. It must be a **working executable comparator**, not a weak “no bridge” straw man.

| Rival explanation | Current adjudication | Observation needed to distinguish it |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary association/index lookup | Sufficient for much reinstatement evidence. | Novel cross-frame or cross-query transfer beyond stored pairs, with the same episode content. |
| Upstream coordinate conversion | Strong regional evidence; excludes a hippocampal monopoly, not hippocampal participation. | Selective perturbation of frame conversion with local content and attention intact. |
| Routing without translation | Strong comparator for subspace/projection findings. | Fixed routing and index selection fail while a matched-capacity conditional transform succeeds specifically for correctly paired receivers. |
| Local/task-specific representations | Real limitation on generality, not proof of no relational structure. | Transfer across held-out tasks/contexts with preserved relations and changed sensory content. |
| Stable invariant subspace | Could make maintenance unnecessary for some drift. | Verified drift in consumer-relevant directions that impairs transfer while local competence remains stable. |
| Local co-adaptation | Computationally sufficient under some assumptions. | Replay of episode-specific cross-system pairs outperforms matched local adaptation at equal data and plasticity budgets. |
| Replay merely rehearses/strengthens | Remains viable for many causal memory findings. | Access restoration at matched local recall, selectively dependent on correct interareal pairing. |
| Shared cortical coordinates without hippocampal mediation | Cortical shared-frame findings permit it; causal independence is not established by fMRI. | Hippocampal perturbation spares the cortical correspondence and its actual use. |
| Development improves precision, not legibility | Best direct reading of the engram data. | Pruning changes transfer independently of local precision, at matched mature architecture. |

“Not demonstrated” is not a negative experimental result. Conversely, a positive behavioural effect after a large lesion is not specific to an indexing or translation mechanism.

### Survive, narrow, split, abandon

- **Survive:** selective mutual legibility (ARC-139), routing versus full representation (MECH-537), frozen-endpoint bridge ladder (MECH-538), dynamic/recurrent checks (MECH-539/548) as discriminating research commitments.
- **Narrow R1:** event/context/relational addressability, not one universal common ontology.
- **Split R2:** upstream frame transformation, CA1 frame selection, coding reformatting, and downstream reconstruction. None substitutes for the others.
- **Split R3 / MECH-547:** cue-conditioned retrieval, fixed partner-specific exposure, and receiver-state-conditioned transformation. The first two have stronger biological neighbours than the third.
- **Split R4 / MECH-540:** memory strengthening, local retuning, index maintenance, stable output scaffolds, and active interface repair. Preserve its existing two-interface prediction; do not reduce it to global alignment.
- **Split R5:** local wiring sparsification, activity/engram sparsification, and historical necessity of interface overcapacity. Only the last directly answers the September thought.
- **Abandon as defaults:** hippocampus-exclusive translation; “more alignment means more integration”; “more replay means more independent evidence”; a generic hippocampal translator as the explanation before rivals are tested.

No new scientific claim is registered. The matrix is non-scoring: it is not a substitute for governed literature intake. The most promising future evidence chain is E15 (computational correspondence maintenance) + E06/E16 (interareal organisation/timing) → assay B (causal pair-specific access repair). Its strongest falsifier is successful matched local co-adaptation with no additional benefit of correct interareal replay. Until that discriminator is run, the chain motivates an experiment rather than promotion.

## 6. Structured evidence matrix

**Coding:** R1 relational indexing; R2 reference-frame mediation; R3 query/receiver-conditioned exposure; R4 replay/interface maintenance; R5 developmental scaffold/pruning. “Supports” always names the narrow role; “permits” means compatibility without discrimination. “Weakens” targets a specified stronger interpretation. No numerical confidence is assigned by paper count.

**Source access:** citations and findings were checked against primary publisher, PubMed, PMC or author-hosted records on 2026-09-09. Some full-text opens were blocked; indexed primary-source passages and abstracts were then used. `A` marks abstract/record-level assessment; `T` marks relevant full-text/results/discussion passages inspected (not a claim of exhaustive supplement audit); `M` marks modelling/theory; `V` review. The evidence type is independent of this access code. Preprints are labelled. Sources cited elsewhere in this dossier are rows here, so the matrix also serves as the reference inventory.

## 7. Three high-information assay families

These are **designs**, not written scripts, queued experiments, or authority grants. IDs A–C are local design labels, not EXQ identifiers. Implementation must first check current substrate, active proposals and 1010's adjudicated outcome. No new runner work is justified merely by this dossier.

### A. Frozen-endpoint index versus frame versus receiver access

**Question:** does a conditional transformation add anything beyond good retrieval and routing?

**Manipulated variables:** source-only constrained bridge; episode-index lookup plus native reinstatement; fixed partner-specific route; explicit frame-conditioned bridge; receiver-state-conditioned bridge. Manipulate reference frame and receiver query independently using the same episodes. Repeat with correct and within-context permuted sender–receiver pairings.

**Held constant:** frozen source/consumer weights; retained source content; training examples; total trainable parameters and optimisation budget; receiver query information; output norms; gating opportunities. Match model selection on validation data and reserve unseen episodes, frame combinations and contexts for final testing. Split by episode/environment, not neighbouring timesteps. A receiver-only arm must quantify information contributed by B.

**Dependent variables:** minimum predeclared bridge family achieving held-out functional criterion; consumer-specific relation accuracy; native consumer response to message ablation; held-out behaviour where the behavioural instrument has dynamic range; both endpoints' local competence. Report full-source decoding, communication-surface decoding and conditional joint decoding separately.

**Positive controls:** information-preserving known rotation/frame transform with its known inverse; raw-input/PCA adequate representation; a task requiring query selection but no format conversion. **Negative controls:** zero message, random matched-norm message, wrong episode, within-query receiver permutation, shuffled frame and receiver-only predictor. Permutations must preserve marginal difficulty and stay on-distribution; otherwise a drop only shows distribution sensitivity.

**Strongest falsifier:** a well-trained index-plus-native-retrieval/fixed-route model matches conditional transformation on compositional holdouts, or receiver permutation leaves its advantage intact. The stronger R3 is then unnecessary at this interface.

**Meaning of outcomes:** frame-only rescue supports R2; query-selection rescue supports retrieval/routing; an additional matched-capacity receiver-pairing-specific gain supports conditional access, but only conditional information controls distinguish translation from new information supplied by the receiver. Good decoding without intervention-sensitive consumer use stops at decodable/bridgeable. Behavioural success is a further rung.

**V3 gate:** if 1010 confirms an inadequate sender under valid probe guards, test this family on a known information-preserving synthetic source or adequate PCA/raw control. Do not interpret an upstream content deficit as failure of every possible interface mechanism.

### B. Controlled drift × replay pairing × temporal engagement

**Question:** does replay repair a relation between systems, rather than only rehearse each system's content?

**Manipulated variables:** (1) no drift, known invertible drift of task-relevant source coordinates, or drift confined to unused coordinates; (2) correctly paired intersystem episode replay, independently shuffled pairings with identical marginals, local-only rehearsal, local homeostatic/Hebbian adaptation, or no maintenance; (3) correctly engaged versus shifted/shuffled receiver update windows. Start with separate pairing and timing contrasts, then test the interaction only where each instrument is valid.

**Held constant:** snapshot endpoints before each evaluation; local task information and competence; replay items, frequencies, activity magnitude, number of updates and plasticity capacity. Drift one endpoint under an explicit controlled transformation while freezing its task content. Allow only the designated maintenance machinery to update. Include an unconstrained co-adaptation upper bound at matched data exposure. Evaluate at least two interfaces, one expected to change and one expected to remain stable, as MECH-540 already requires.

**Dependent variables:** pre/post cross-system functional transfer; correct-episode specificity; local recall and source decodability; estimated bridge complexity and output dimensionality as distinct measures; transition compatibility and repeated-use stability. The primary contrast is the additional gain of correct pairing over shuffled pairing under access-disrupting drift, relative to the same contrast with no drift. A separate timing contrast holds pairing fixed.

**Positive controls:** known inverse adapter restores access; deliberately disrupted relevant coordinates reduce transfer while local competence remains stable. **Negative controls:** non-task-direction drift; shuffled interareal pairs with each region's replay histogram unchanged; matched plasticity/no-content activity; matched event count at the wrong update phase. If no-drift performance is at ceiling, tune task difficulty or report the ceiling rather than manufacturing a maintenance interaction.

**Strongest falsifier:** local adaptation/rehearsal restores access equally well; correct-pair advantage disappears after matching local memory quality; or drift leaves native transfer intact. The last result supports a stable scaffold and makes active repair unnecessary in that condition.

**Meaning of outcomes:** correct-pair-specific rescue at stable local competence supports R4's interface component; improved local recall only supports consolidation; changed output geometry without restored use is representational reorganisation; rising global similarity plus lost specialisation is homogenisation. Repeated-use degradation blocks architectural adoption even after one-step rescue. Matching recall should be designed/titrated prospectively, not produced solely by conditioning on a post-treatment variable.

### C. Developmental path at matched mature interface

**Question:** did overcapacity discover a useful mature architecture, or is its learning history itself required?

**Manipulated variable:** four histories already motivated by the September thought: small structured interface from start; large interface retained; large interface learned then pruned/distilled; final sparse topology reinitialised and trained from start. Add random pruning at matched density as a negative control. Separate structural pruning from inhibitory/activity sparsification.

**Held constant:** mature capacity in the decisive pruned versus reinitialised-final comparison; data curriculum; task exposures; evaluation tasks; local endpoint competence. Report results under both matched training examples and matched compute, because overcapacity changes optimisation cost. Track dense-stage cost rather than presenting it as free.

**Dependent variables:** held-out cross-system transfer, local precision, retained relational invariants, bridge complexity, learning sample efficiency and recurrence stability. **Positive controls:** a benchmark where a known correct sparse transform works; unpruned large model demonstrating trainability. **Negative controls:** random pruning; uninformative pairing; identical final architecture without inherited weights.

**Strongest falsifier:** the reinitialised final sparse topology matches large→pruned transfer after fair training. That supports architecture discovery without necessary developmental weight history. If a small structured interface matches both, transient overcapacity is unnecessary in the tested regime. If only local precision improves, the result resembles engram allocation rather than cross-system legibility.

**Routing:** later developmental substrate, consistent with MECH-362/Q-057. This is not a new V3 task. A and B have higher immediate information value.

## 8. Provenance: a real connection, bounded at the correct level

E33 demonstrates that reactivated context can acquire an association with an outcome experienced elsewhere. E43 shows that offline co-reactivation can link temporally separated memories. E34 supplies a behavioural source-memory result without identifying hippocampal causality. These make **binding, contextual source and offline updating** legitimate neighbours of the provenance programme.

They do not show that descendants are treated as independent witnesses, that source uncertainty inflates confidence, or that such a mechanism causes psychosis. Fear generalisation is not confidence inflation; memory-source colour/location is not a causal derivation graph; a memory link does not imply a mistaken independence judgment. No clinical conclusion is drawn here.

The useful extension fits the existing [provenance ladder](../../evidence/planning/provenance_false_evidence_multiplication_experiment_ladder.md), especially P1/P3, and should not become a duplicate experiment family:

- Hold one external event fixed, produce replay/retrieval descendants, vary known/uncertain/false-split ancestry, and measure **source attribution, association strength, effective independent-source count and calibration separately**.
- Add a positive control with genuinely independent new observations and a control where replay improves a noisy estimate of the original event. Confidence can legitimately improve when computation extracts previously unused information; the forbidden gain is extra weight attributed solely to copied ancestry.
- The strongest falsifier of false-independence is that ancestry corruption affects source labels or associative generalisation but not independent-source count/calibrated belief. The strongest falsifier of recursive amplification is no ancestry×replay-count interaction at fixed external evidence and matched retrieval quality.

The rule “a hypothesis must not cite its own descendants as independent witnesses” survives as an epistemic constraint. This campaign supplies no evidence for a literal hippocampal ancestry register.

## 9. Search tranches, limits and remaining literature debts

This was a targeted multi-tranche campaign, not a preregistered systematic review or meta-analysis. Tranches covered (i) validation of the existing CA1/RSC anchors, (ii) frames and abstract relations, (iii) causal retrieval and projection routing, (iv) sleep/replay/drift with positive and negative interventions, (v) developmental mechanisms, (vi) computational maintenance rivals, and (vii) provenance/source-memory boundaries. Search families included “replay representational drift alignment,” “CA3 developmental sparse structured connectivity,” “query context prefrontal hippocampal retrieval,” “SWR blockade map stability,” and “hippocampus independent procedural replay.” Publisher dates and article versions took precedence over search-engine relative dates. For example E43 is online 2024/issue 2025; E34 online 2024/issue 2025; E35 is a reviewed preprint.

Coverage is sufficient to narrow the hypothesis and specify its missing discriminators. It is not sufficient to assert that no relevant paper exists. Abstract-level rows carry lower methodological resolution; no effect-size pooling or independent raw-data replication was performed.

Outstanding debts, ordered by potential to change this adjudication:

1. **Cross-system repair causality:** simultaneous sender/receiver recordings with controlled representational drift, replay perturbation, local-memory controls and actual receiver use. Current pieces do not complete this chain.
2. **Receiver state versus cue identity:** content-matched interventions on the receiving population, not only task/query switches or projection labels. Separate missing sender content from receiver-provided information.
3. **CA1→EC reconstruction versus trigger/comparison:** perturbational work with matched retrieved content and downstream ensemble geometry. **Publication status resolved (2026-09-10):** the Butola et al. direct-feedback study, recorded here as a 2023 Research Square preprint, is peer-reviewed — Butola T, Hernández-Frausto M, Blankvoort S, et al., *Hippocampus shapes entorhinal cortical output through a direct feedback circuit*, Nat Neurosci 2025 Apr;28(4):811–822, [DOI 10.1038/s41593-025-01883-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-01883-9), PMID 39966537. The version question is closed; **the detailed-controls review is not**, so the debt stands at that rung rather than being discharged outright. Handover from `hippo-lit-tranche3-20260910-xref`, verified independently against PubMed before recording.
4. **Developmental historical necessity:** longitudinal interareal synapse/function measures and interventions separating local wiring, engram sparsity, experience selection and cross-system transfer. Mouse slice development cannot settle this.
5. **Newest geometry findings:** independent replication and audits of subsampling, dimensionality estimators, neural-state matching, common inputs and selection effects for E02/E05/E06/E28. Their distinct populations/paradigms are not independent replications of one translation mechanism.
6. **Temporal relations:** dedicated time-cell/episode-boundary and nonspatial causal-transfer tranche. Nonspatial coding is covered, but time-frame mediation has less depth than spatial-frame mediation here.
7. **Provenance:** no located experiment jointly manipulates replay genealogy and measures effective evidence count/calibrated confidence. The existing psychosis review remains separate; no diagnostic claim intake follows.

The stopping rule is discriminative coverage: the principal positive streams, serious alternatives and counterexamples are represented, and unresolved mechanisms have explicit tests. The next useful step is assay selection under live substrate constraints, not adding a generic hippocampal module or promoting a claim by accumulation of adjacent citations.
