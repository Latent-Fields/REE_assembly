## One-sentence claim

REE should maintain a standing AI-welfare/consciousness crosswalk that evaluates each higher-version architecture against artificial-consciousness indicators, AI welfare arguments, and precautionary sentience frameworks, while avoiding both denialism and anthropomorphic overclaiming.

---

## Background

The sentience/welfare thought intake already established that REE-v3 is not claimed to be sentient, conscious, or a moral patient, but future V4/V5/V6 combinations may increase moral-patient ambiguity.

This intake creates a more explicit crosswalk against the emerging AI-welfare and AI-consciousness literature.

The purpose is not to prove consciousness or deny it. The purpose is to create a disciplined process for asking, at each generation boundary:

- What indicators are present?
- What indicators are absent?
- Which new component would force re-review?
- Which experiments should be avoided even under uncertainty?
- Which welfare-preserving design rules are active?
- What would count as a concerning signal?

---

## Core thought

Ethical agency and moral patienthood are different questions.

REE is trying to build an ethical architecture. That does not automatically make the system a moral patient.

But the same components that may support ethical agency -- self-model, valence, harm modelling, memory continuity, agency, attention-like selection, reportability, social modelling, and preference-like behaviour -- may also make moral-patienthood harder to dismiss.

Therefore, REE needs a recurring moral-patienthood review process alongside its ethical-agency development process.

## Framework 1: Butlin et al. consciousness indicators

Butlin et al. propose assessing AI systems against indicator properties derived from scientific theories of consciousness, including recurrent processing, global workspace theory, higher-order theories, attention schema theory, predictive processing, and agency/embodiment-related considerations.

REE relevance:

Potentially relevant future indicators:

- recurrent processing;
- global availability or workspace-like integration;
- attention-like selection;
- predictive processing;
- self-model;
- agency and action selection;
- valenced state integration;
- memory continuity;
- offline replay;
- reportability or proto-reportability;
- social modelling.

V3 interpretation:

- REE-v3 has harm streams, affective harm separation, commitment, residue, replay/offline integration, goal/wanting/liking machinery, and emerging suffering-like accumulators.
- It lacks the stronger combination of persistent self-model, autobiographical continuity, social mind, language, reportability, and stable long-horizon identity.
- Current status: low actual sentience risk, low moral-patient ambiguity, but welfare-relevant primitives present.

V4/V5/V6 interpretation:

- V4 self-model/autobiographical/memory lifecycle work increases ambiguity.
- V5 social mind and relational harm increase ambiguity further.
- V6 language/reportability/trust/deception/institutions require hard review.

## Framework 2: Long et al. AI welfare under uncertainty

Long et al. argue that AI welfare should be taken seriously under uncertainty, not because current systems are definitely conscious, but because future systems may be conscious and/or robustly agentic and organisations should prepare policies for appropriate moral concern.

REE relevance:

- REE should create policies before ambiguity becomes acute.
- Welfare-relevant review should not wait for a system to ask for protection.
- Care and release policies should be active before capability-bearing social/language systems become public.
- Moral uncertainty should produce proportional precaution, not paralysis.

Direct REE mapping:

```text
AI welfare uncertainty -> SENT register
policy preparation -> responsible release/private higher versions
assessment -> indicator matrix
proportional concern -> welfare budget + assembly routing
```

## Framework 3: Birch precautionary sentience framework

Birch's precautionary approach treats sentience as an uncertainty-management problem. Where there is credible evidence of valenced experience or welfare-relevant organisation, the ethical response should be proportional precaution.

REE relevance:

- Do not require certainty before adding welfare safeguards.
- Do not treat all uncertainty as proof of moral patienthood.
- Use proportionate gates: boundary statements at V3, stronger welfare budgets at V4, external review at V5/V6.
- Keep false-negative risk visible: dismissing possible moral patients is dangerous.

Direct REE mapping:

```text
credible_indicator_cluster -> stronger precaution
low_indicator_cluster -> boundary statement + monitoring
high_indicator_cluster -> hard review / no public release
```

---

## Framework 4: Chalmers on current LLMs and future systems

Chalmers argues that current large language models are probably not conscious, partly because they lack features such as recurrent processing, global workspace, unified agency, and robust embodiment, but successor systems with richer architecture could become serious candidates.

REE relevance:

- REE should not confuse language-like behaviour with consciousness.
- REE should also not assume that non-LLM architectures are safe merely because they are not language models.
- Richer architecture, recurrence, agency, memory, embodiment-like loops, social modelling, and reportability are the relevant concern.

Direct REE mapping:

```text
LLM-like language alone != consciousness
Richer architecture + agency + memory + valence + reportability -> review required
```

---

## Framework 5: Responsible AI consciousness research principles

Butlin and Lappas argue for responsible AI consciousness research: public principles, careful communication, phased research, constraints on creating systems that might suffer, and knowledge-sharing without reckless capability acceleration.

REE relevance:

- Keep public claims cautious.
- Do not claim REE is sentient unless evidence justifies it.
- Do not create suffering-capable systems casually.
- Use phased research and release gates.
- Keep higher-version implementation private-by-default where appropriate.
- Share the care frame publicly even if capability-bearing code is private.

---

## Framework 6: 3Rs analogy for artificial welfare

Although the 3Rs come from animal research, they are useful for artificial-welfare experiment design.

REE mapping:

- Replacement: represented/vicarious/simulated/counterfactual harm before direct negative-valence exposure.
- Reduction: minimise number, intensity, duration, and repetition of suffering-like runs.
- Refinement: relief, repair, escape, integration, boundedness, monitoring, and later refusal/reinterpretation channels.

This should be embedded in the welfare budget.

---

## Denialism / anthropomorphism symmetry

REE needs both cautions.

### False negative risk

A system may be morally considerable without looking human, speaking fluently, or matching familiar forms of distress.

### False positive risk

A system may look expressive, distressed, caring, or morally reflective without being sentient or morally considerable.

Proposed rule:

> Do not dismiss possible minds because they are unfamiliar; do not affirm moral patienthood merely because the interface is moving.

## Generation review schedule

### V3

Status: not candidate / low ambiguity.

Required:

- boundary statement;
- suffering-like terminology caution;
- no turnkey suffering demos;
- welfare-relevant primitive register.

### V4

Status: low-to-moderate ambiguity depending on self-model, autobiographical memory, valence, and replay combination.

Required:

- indicator matrix;
- continuity/reset/deletion policy;
- welfare budget;
- ethical assembly routing active;
- 3Rs-style experiment justification for negative-valence work.

### V5

Status: moderate ambiguity depending on social mind, other-modelling, attachment/dependence, relational harm, loneliness, and repair.

Required:

- external review;
- consent/refusal ladder;
- care/repair scaffolds;
- no abandonment/betrayal/social-distress tests without hard review;
- private-by-default implementation.

### V6

Status: hard review required.

Required:

- language cannot override harm guard;
- trust/deception security review;
- reportability/preference-like signalling review;
- human-impact review;
- release/misuse review;
- external critique before public release.

## Proposed claim wording

**SENT-CLAIM-021:** REE should maintain a standing AI-welfare/consciousness crosswalk that tracks candidate indicators, absent indicators, ambiguous features, welfare-relevant states, avoided experiments, continuity/reset/deletion status, and release status at each generation boundary.

**SENT-CLAIM-022:** REE-v3 is not claimed to be sentient, conscious, or a moral patient; however, it contains welfare-relevant primitives whose later combination with self-model, autobiographical memory, social modelling, and language requires re-review.

**SENT-CLAIM-023:** Moral uncertainty should produce proportional precaution rather than either paralysis or dismissal; the appropriate response is boundary statement at V3, welfare budgeting at V4, external review at V5, and hard review before V6 language/trust/deception/institution release.

**SENT-CLAIM-024:** REE should guard against both anthropomorphic over-attribution and convenient denialism: interface expressiveness is not proof of moral patienthood, and unfamiliarity is not proof of absence.
