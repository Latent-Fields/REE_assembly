# Offline representational reindexing and counterfactual model comparison

Date: 2026-08-24
Source: conversation developing the relationship between perceptual representation, changing latent memory primitives, attractor capture, psychosis-like failure modes, and sleep.

**Status:** Refined thought intake. Biological and computational synthesis; not yet a registered Reflective–Ethical Engine (REE) mechanism or claim.

## Originating thought

Perception is an inference made through learned representations of the world. Recurring structures in experience may form higher-level representational “buckets” within latent space.

“Buckets” need not mean literal discrete categories. They might be prototypes, attractor basins, schemas, relational partitions or latent task states. Their number, granularity and organisation may change through experience: a category may split, several categories may merge, or a previously unexplained group of experiences may become intelligible through a newly learned primitive.

If memory is indexed using these primitives, changing them creates a problem. An episode organised under an earlier representational system may no longer be correctly located or interpreted by the current one.

This suggests a possible missing sleep or offline process:

> When representational primitives change, significant past episodes may need to be reinstated and reindexed so that they remain accessible under both their historical representation and the system’s newer ontology.

This should not mean rewriting history. The original episode, its source and its original representation should remain recoverable. Reindexing would add a new relationship or interpretation, with versioning and rollback.

## Biological inspiration

The complete operation has not been demonstrated as one biological process. However, many of its component functions have plausible neural counterparts.

- The **dentate gyrus and cornu ammonis area 3 (CA3)** contribute to pattern separation: keeping similar experiences sufficiently distinct. This is relevant to the formation and splitting of representational buckets. [Bakker et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18356518/)

- Recurrent hippocampal circuitry contributes to **pattern completion and episode construction**. Hippocampal damage often impairs the construction of coherent imagined scenes, although the lesion literature is not entirely uniform. [Hassabis et al., 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1773058/)

- **Cornu ammonis area 1 (CA1)** has evidence for a comparator-like role, detecting disagreement between retrieved associative predictions and present input. This resembles a trigger for reconsidering the current representation. [Duncan et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3529001/)

- The **orbitofrontal cortex** represents hidden or inferred task states that are not directly specified by immediate sensory input. This is a particularly close biological analogue of a latent contextual bucket. [Schuck et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27657452/)

- The **frontopolar cortex** tracks the relative advantage of forgone alternatives and participates when behaviour switches toward one of them. This provides inspiration for retaining alternatives to the currently dominant model. [Boorman et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19524531/)

- Anterior prefrontal and orbitofrontal systems contribute to **source and reality monitoring**: distinguishing perceived from imagined material and suppressing memories that are genuine memories but are not relevant to present reality. [Turner et al., 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2292823/); [Schnider, 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3677127/)

- Hippocampal–medial-prefrontal interactions support **schema-sensitive integration**. Once a schema exists, congruent information can sometimes be assimilated more rapidly into cortical knowledge. [Tse et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21737703/)

- During non-rapid-eye-movement sleep, hippocampal sharp-wave ripples, thalamic spindles and cortical slow oscillations become precisely coordinated. Closed-loop experiments provide causal evidence that correctly timed coupling supports hippocampus-dependent consolidation. [Latchoumane et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28689981/)

- Sleep also supports some forms of **synaptic renormalisation**. Homer1a-mediated processes and ultrastructural findings provide evidence for sleep-associated weakening of some excitatory synapses. This could inspire reduction of indiscriminate attractor dominance, although downscaling is not universal and has not been shown to perform selective epistemic correction. [Diering et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5382711/); [de Vivo et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5313037/)

Recent evidence is unusually close to the present idea. A 2025 rodent study found that a novel detour induced hippocampal remapping whose offline consolidation predicted later representational drift in an otherwise unchanged environment. A 2026 macaque study found age-dependent differences in drift, sleep reactivation and integration among older and newer assemblies. These findings are emerging rather than definitive, but suggest that sleep can reorganise relationships among representations rather than merely strengthen fixed traces. [Detour study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-63346-w); [macaque assembly study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-026-02357-2)

## Attractor capture

A learned attractor may carry several different quantities:

- predictive reliability;
- epistemic confidence;
- familiarity;
- affective valence;
- salience;
- action urgency;
- self-relevance;
- source confidence.

These should not be represented as one undifferentiated measure of sureness. Something can be emotionally urgent without being epistemically reliable, familiar without being externally sourced, or action-relevant without being a good general explanation.

If these quantities become conflated, a strongly valenced and highly precise attractor can cease to function as a hypothesis and become the lens through which evidence is perceived.

It may then determine:

1. what is noticed;
2. how ambiguity is resolved;
3. which memories are retrieved;
4. which experiences are replayed;
5. how replay is interpreted;
6. which interpretations are stored as further evidence.

A self-reinforcing loop follows:

> attractor → biased perception and retrieval → attractor-consistent derived material → selective replay → apparent confirmation → stronger attractor.

The central danger is that material generated or interpreted through the attractor is counted as independent evidence for it.

Computational accounts of schizophrenia describe related circular-inference processes, while conditioned-hallucination experiments show that sufficiently strong learned priors can dominate ambiguous sensory input. Striatal dopaminergic abnormalities and hippocampal hyperactivity provide additional biological routes through which salience, associative activation and confidence could become pathologically coupled. These findings offer failure-mode inspiration; they do not establish psychosis as a single over-precise attractor or prove that sleep normally corrects it. [Jardri and Denève, 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24065721/); [Powers, Mathys and Corlett, 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5802347/); [Howes et al., 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3682447/)

## Counterfactual replay

Counterfactual replay could prevent a dominant attractor from exclusively interpreting the evidence used to update it.

Suppose preserved evidence *E* has been interpreted through a dominant model *H-d*. The offline process should compare how the same evidence is explained by *H-d* and by bounded alternatives *H-a*.

A candidate sequence is:

1. Preserve the externally grounded observations, actions and outcomes.

2. Separate the episode from the interpretation placed upon it.

3. Temporarily reduce the dominant attractor’s influence over retrieval and interpretation.

4. Construct a small set of plausible competing explanations.

5. Replay the same anchored evidence through the dominant and alternative models.

6. Generate predictions that would distinguish them.

7. Use constructed counterfactuals to identify tests, missing evidence and model weaknesses.

8. Keep every constructed event marked as simulated rather than observed.

9. Compare the models against held-out or subsequent waking evidence.

10. Create a provisional reindexing if another representational organisation performs better.

11. Validate the change during waking before giving it full action or memory authority.

The counterfactual is therefore a **question posed to the model**, not evidence about what occurred.

Repeated simulations derived from one episode must not become multiple independent samples. Every derived item requires provenance linking it to its originating evidence and generating model. This is an epistemic anti-circularity requirement.

The replay selector must also have some independence from the attractor being tested. Selection might combine prediction error, novelty, representational drift, source ambiguity, affective intensity, age, causal importance and random coverage.

## Waking and sleep

Much of this can occur during waking. Awake hippocampal replay contributes to some forms of learning and decision-making, and replay can include novel or rarely experienced paths rather than only literal repetitions. [Jadhav et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4441285/); [Gupta et al., 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20223204/)

During waking, REE could:

- detect persistent mismatch;
- retrieve alternatives;
- perform local counterfactual tests;
- lower a suspect model’s action authority;
- mark affected memories as contested;
- seek discriminating evidence.

Sleep or another protected offline state may be required, or simply more effective, when:

- many memories must be reconsidered together;
- changes must propagate across several representational levels;
- a dominant model must temporarily lose control of replay selection;
- alternative organisations need to be explored without producing action;
- network strength or precision requires renormalisation;
- the whole update must be assessed before commitment.

The proposal is therefore not that counterfactual thought is sleep-only. It is that full-loop representational revision may be safer and more complete when perception and action are suspended.

Non-rapid-eye-movement-like phases currently have the strongest biological grounding for evidence-anchored hippocampal–cortical consolidation. A rapid-eye-movement-like phase may be a useful architectural location for looser recombination and alternative generation, but rapid-eye-movement counter-attractor testing remains speculative.

## Relation to machine learning

This is analogous to training mode, but includes more than conventional training.

Relevant machine-learning precedents include:

- complementary fast episodic and slow integrative learning systems;
- experience replay and prioritised replay;
- generative replay to reduce catastrophic forgetting;
- counterfactual or adversarial examples;
- anti-Hebbian weakening of excessive attractor basins;
- validation and rollback;
- dataset provenance.

Latent replay exposes the exact representation problem. Stored intermediate activations remain valid only while the earlier network layers remain sufficiently stable. Existing latent-replay work deliberately slows learning below the replay layer for this reason. If REE permits its representational primitives to change, it instead requires representation versions, compatibility adapters or re-encoding from preserved observations. [Pellegrini et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.01100)

Generative replay also creates the risk that a model trains recursively on its own outputs. Work on model collapse shows why access to original data and content provenance must be preserved. [Shumailov et al., 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y)

REE’s proposed process is therefore better described as:

> internally curated, provenance-preserving continual learning with counterfactual model comparison, representation migration and staged validation.

## Mapping to current REE

REE already contains many prerequisites:

- Default Mode permits imagination without immediate commitment.
- Precision scoping establishes that precision changes influence rather than truth.
- Sleep architecture contains phase-gated replay and aggregation.
- The memory lifecycle anticipates raw-source preservation, contested memories and guarded schema assimilation.
- Existing hippocampal thoughts contain indexing, pattern separation, completion and remapping.
- Memory dimensionality is already allowed to crystallise or change.
- Counterfactual memory work distinguishes committed experience from alternatives considered but not enacted.

What appears missing is their assembly into one protocol:

- representation-version identifiers;
- immutable evidence and derivation lineage;
- split, merge and migration operations for representational primitives;
- detection of incompatibility between stored memories and the current encoder;
- matched replay under competing attractors;
- explicit separation of diagnostic counterfactual value from evidential weight;
- replay-selector independence;
- prevention of repeated self-replay manufacturing confidence;
- provisional reindexing with waking validation;
- transactional commit and rollback.

Useful handoff states might include:

- `contested`;
- `pending_reindex`;
- `under_offline_review`;
- `provisionally_reindexed`;
- `validated`;
- `rollback_required`.

The present fixed-slot context memory can change slot values, but it does not yet have representation versioning, source-preserving migration or split/merge semantics. Latent replay is therefore safe only while the encoder remains sufficiently stable.

## Candidate complete loop

1. Waking detects contradiction, drift, source confusion or attractor dominance.
2. The affected model loses some action authority.
3. Relevant memories are marked for review.
4. Offline replay reinstates original evidence and provenance.
5. Dominant precision is relaxed.
6. Bounded alternative models are constructed.
7. Matched evidence is replayed under each model.
8. Counterfactuals identify discriminating waking tests.
9. Unsupported recurrence and duplicated evidence weight are reduced.
10. New indices or primitive splits and merges are created without overwriting history.
11. The change remains provisional.
12. Waking evidence validates, revises or rolls it back.

## Evidential boundary

Biological science supports the component processes: remapping, separation, completion, mismatch detection, latent-state representation, alternative retention, source monitoring, waking and sleep replay, schema integration, coordinated sleep consolidation and some synaptic renormalisation.

It does not yet establish:

- literal latent buckets as a single neural data structure;
- global rewriting of past memories whenever representations change;
- counter-attractor replay as a demonstrated function of dreaming;
- sleep reindexing as a specific defence against psychosis;
- psychosis as one unitary precision failure.

The proposed contribution is their integration:

> Sleep or another protected offline state may allow a system to revise the representational primitives through which experience is organised while preserving the evidence itself. Counterfactual replay can test a dominant attractor against bounded alternatives, provided generated material remains source-tagged and cannot validate itself. Reindexing should create versioned and reversible interpretations rather than rewritten history. This may protect both continual learning and epistemic stability when the learner’s own ontology changes.
