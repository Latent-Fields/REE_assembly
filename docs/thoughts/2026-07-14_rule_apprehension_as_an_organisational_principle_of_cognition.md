---

# Central Hypothesis

Reasoning within REE is better understood as the apprehension, evaluation, refinement and application of regularities than as the execution of language or formal logic.

Language is one interface to these processes.

It is unlikely to be their fundamental substrate.

---

# Motivation

Classical cognitive science often divides reasoning into categories such as:

- induction
- deduction
- abduction

The MIT work further separates formal logical reasoning from language.

These distinctions are useful but may not describe cognition at the appropriate organisational level.

REE instead asks a more fundamental question:

> How does a cognitive system discover, evaluate, maintain and exploit regularities in order to remain a viable agent?

---

# Candidate Organisational Pipeline

Rather than treating reasoning as a single computation, REE may comprise a family of interacting organisational processes.

```text
Experience
        ↓
Pattern sensitivity
        ↓
Candidate regularity apprehension
        ↓
Representation
        ↓
Prediction
        ↓
Counterfactual simulation
        ↓
Behavioural interaction
        ↓
Outcome comparison
        ↓
Regularity refinement
        ↓
Generalisation
        ↓
Long-term integration
```

Each stage may involve distinct computational mechanisms.

None require language.

---

# Rule Apprehension

"Induction" already implies a formal reasoning framework.

Rule apprehension is intentionally broader.

It simply denotes:

> Recognition that the world appears to contain a reusable regularity.

The regularity may concern:

- physical dynamics
- spatial relationships
- motor control
- social interaction
- emotional behaviour
- ethics
- language
- mathematics

The architecture should remain agnostic regarding domain.

---

# Rule Representation

Apprehended regularities require internal representation.

Possible forms include:

- predictive models
- latent dynamics
- procedural policies
- constraints
- causal models
- relational structures
- affordance landscapes

The choice of mathematical representation is secondary to the organisational requirement.

---

# Rule Application

Once a regularity exists it can be used.

This corresponds broadly to what classical reasoning describes as deduction.

However the more general computation is:

> If this regularity is correct...

> ...what follows?

This applies equally to:

- planning
- prediction
- motor behaviour
- ethics
- social cognition
- causal reasoning

Formal logical deduction becomes one specialised instance.

---

# Behavioural Validation

Rules are not accepted because they appear elegant.

They are continuously evaluated against behavioural consequences.

Candidate regularities generate expectations.

Behaviour provides evidence.

Prediction errors drive refinement.

The process is therefore cyclical rather than linear.

---

# Generalisation

Successful regularities become increasingly abstract.

Initially they may apply only within one situation.

Over time they may transfer across:

- environments
- contexts
- goals
- agents
- modalities

Generalisation therefore becomes an explicit organisational process rather than an incidental property.

---

# Language

Language should remain separate from the core reasoning architecture.

Its likely functions include:

- compression
- communication
- coordination
- long-horizon scaffolding
- working-memory support
- explanation
- cooperation

Language may also compensate for weaknesses in other reasoning systems.

Highly trained language systems such as LLMs demonstrate that considerable reasoning-like behaviour can emerge from language alone.

However this does not imply that language constitutes the native substrate of reasoning.

Instead it may function as a remarkably powerful approximation or coordination interface.

---

# Candidate Organisational Principle

Reasoning is not one process.

Reasoning is a coordinated ecology of processes involved in discovering, evaluating, refining and exploiting regularities.

Classical categories such as induction, deduction and abduction are useful descriptions of particular operations occurring within this broader organisational cycle.

---

# Implications for REE

The architecture should continue distinguishing:

- rule apprehension
- rule representation
- rule application
- behavioural testing
- rule refinement
- generalisation

rather than collapsing these into a single "reasoning module".

Similarly, language should remain an interface capable of interacting with each stage without becoming the substrate of the stages themselves.

---

# Separation from REE Assembly

This thought concerns REE as a cognitive architecture.

It should not be conflated with REE Assembly, which is the scientific and engineering machinery used to develop and govern REE.

The two have strong parallels, but they are distinct systems:

```text
REE
Concern:
How an agent apprehends regularities, predicts, tests, reasons and acts.

Objects:
Latent states, predictive models, trajectories, behavioural outcomes,
internal regularities, commitments and residue.

REE Assembly
Concern:
How the REE research programme develops and governs its own theory.

Objects:
Claims, evidence, experiments, implementation decisions,
promotions, demotions and governance records.
```

Any analogy between the two should be identified explicitly as an analogy, not used as evidence that REE itself operates in the same way.

# Current Working Claim

The primary organisational function of reasoning is not language manipulation nor formal logical deduction.

It is the continual apprehension, evaluation, refinement, application and integration of regularities that improve prediction, behaviour and long-term viability.

Language functions as a powerful interface to these processes rather than their fundamental substrate.
