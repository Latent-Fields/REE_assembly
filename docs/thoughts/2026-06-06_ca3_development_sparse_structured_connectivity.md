# THOUGHT INTAKE: CA3 development from dense random connectivity to sparse structured memory circuitry

## 0. Summary claim

A saved REE email pointed to a Nature Communications article titled "Developmental emergence of sparse and structured synaptic connectivity in the hippocampal CA3 memory circuit". The direct `share.google` link was not opened during intake, and the primary Nature Communications page was not located by search. Secondary coverage identifies a study by Peter Jonas and Victor Vargas-Barroso on developmental changes in mouse hippocampal CA3 connectivity.

The reported pattern is:

- immature CA3 begins with dense, strong, relatively random connectivity
- development reduces/prunes connections
- mature CA3 becomes sparser and more structured
- individual immature synapses may be strong enough to trigger spikes
- mature neurons require coordinated activation from multiple weaker inputs

The useful REE idea is:

> a memory circuit may not develop by adding connectivity to an empty substrate, but by pruning an overconnected substrate into sparse, structured, cooperative computation.

For REE, this supports a developmental principle: early cognifold construction may require overconnected exploratory coupling followed by pruning, sparsification, and structured gating rather than starting from clean modular sparsity.

---

## 1. Why this belongs in REE_assembly

REE already depends on hippocampal-like trajectory generation, relational memory, replay, sparse cues, structured retrieval, and self/world/action binding.

This thought is relevant because it suggests that mature sparse memory structure may be the product of developmental pruning rather than a starting condition. That matters for REE design because a system may need:

- early broad connectivity / high exploratory coupling
- developmental pruning
- transition from single strong cues to coordinated weak evidence
- sparse but structured retrieval
- context-dependent pattern completion
- protection against overfitted early attractors
- maturation of memory circuits before full action authority

This is a developmental architecture compass, not a direct implementation instruction.

## 4. REE-specific hypothesis

REE may require an explicit developmental distinction between:

1. **Overconnected exploratory phase** — many weakly constrained associations, high plasticity, broad coupling, low action authority.
2. **Pruning / selection phase** — repeated offline and waking experience down-weights unstable or non-useful pathways.
3. **Sparse structured retrieval phase** — mature relational/trajectory memory requires coordinated multi-cue activation rather than single-cue domination.

This may matter especially for the REE-v3 and later goal-stream / cue-authority work: a cue should not become behaviourally powerful simply because it is individually strong. Mature recall should be structured by convergent context, self/world status, goal state, harm/residue, and action-readiness.

Possible computational primitive:

```text
mature_recall_activation = f(convergent_weak_inputs, context_match, residue_state, goal_state, self_world_tag, precision_gate)
```

rather than:

```text
mature_recall_activation = max(single_strong_cue)
```

---

## 5. Relevance to REE failure modes

Failure to mature from dense/random to sparse/structured connectivity could map to several failure patterns:

- **confabulatory completion:** overconnected pattern completion with insufficient structure
- **belief fixation:** early strong attractor survives without pruning
- **precision misallocation:** a single cue receives excessive authority
- **feedback entrapment:** dense positive coupling without pruning
- **goal proxy lock-in:** early association becomes over-authoritative
- **provenance collapse:** memory traces bind without structured source tagging

This may also be relevant to the `ai-cognitive-failure-taxonomy` later, but the immediate home is REE_assembly because the primary issue is developmental memory architecture.

---

## 6. Important cautions

Do not overgeneralise from mouse CA3 development to human cognition or REE design.

Do not treat dense early connectivity as automatically good or bad.

Do not assume pruning means deletion only; down-weighting, gating, or contextualisation may be better computational analogues.

Do not make this a REE-v3 implementation target without a specific existing substrate gap.

The useful extraction is:

> mature sparse structured memory may emerge through developmental pruning of an initially overconnected substrate.
