## Originating framing

The immediate relevance of the paper is not that REE should begin to incorporate heterogeneous processes operating at different timescales.

REE already does.

The more useful question is:

> What organisational principles allow heterogeneous and asynchronous cognitive processes to remain parts of one integrated system without collapsing into either lockstep synchrony or unrelated modular activity?

The paper appears relevant because it reports shared spatial and temporal organisation across connectome dynamics expressed at different timescales.

The central REE-facing interpretation is therefore not:

```text
REE should add multiple dynamic streams.
```

It is:

```text
REE already contains heterogeneous asynchronous streams.

The paper may help clarify which shared organisational
principles should connect those streams and what observable
signatures those principles should generate.
```

## Correction arising during discussion

An initial interpretation treated the paper as a source of new architectural design principles for REE.

That interpretation underestimated the existing architecture.

Review of REE showed that the programme already explicitly describes itself as a multi-timescale predictive architecture, with E1 as a slow persistent predictive substrate, E2 as a faster forward model, and E3 as a trajectory-selection and commitment process. REE also already includes a multi-rate clock, heterogeneous control plane, hippocampal rollout, residue field, and offline integration.

REE-v3 goes further by making asynchronous multi-rate execution a founding substrate decision:

```text
SD-006:
E1, E2, and E3 run at characteristic rates rather than lockstep.
```

The transition to V3 was itself partly motivated by failure to obtain sufficient E1/E2 timescale separation in a shared latent representation.

The programme also contains multiple functionally non-identical streams, including self, world, sensory-discriminative harm, affective-motivational harm, goals, resources, control, salience, residue, hippocampal representations, and offline processes.

The paper should therefore be treated primarily as:

```text
external empirical convergence
+
conceptual clarification
+
a prompt for stronger cross-stream experiments
```

rather than as the origin of REE's multi-stream design.

DLIF might instantiate some organisational principles, but it is not required for this thought intake.

```text
the empirical consequences of that implementation
```

## Core thought

Cognitive integration may depend less on every process sharing the same representation, clock, or computational mechanism than on heterogeneous processes obeying a partially shared organisational grammar.

A system can therefore be:

```text
heterogeneous
asynchronous
multi-rate
partially independent
```

without becoming:

```text
fragmented
incoherent
or centrally lockstepped
```

provided that its processes share sufficient organising structures.

For REE, these shared structures may include:

```text
self/world distinction
hypothetical/committed distinction
causal attribution
event boundaries
mode structure
precision and salience regulation
goal and harm relevance
trajectory organisation
commitment gating
completion and interruption
consequence ownership
residue persistence
offline integration
```

These structures need not be represented identically in every stream.

They may instead provide homologous transition rules, landmarks, constraints, or coupling surfaces across otherwise distinct processes.

## Federation rather than monolith or module collection

REE appears best understood neither as one homogeneous latent process nor as a loose collection of independent modules.

A more accurate framing is:

```text
REE is a federation of asynchronous,
partially independent cognitive streams
coordinated by shared organisational constraints.
```

This federation is important to REE's anti-collapse architecture.

REE explicitly treats viable cognition as involving partially independent control systems that prevent one gradient from dominating behaviour. Harm, relief, safety, wanting, liking, curiosity, blocked agency, salience, and commitment release have distinct gating and learning functions rather than being reduced to one optimiser.

Integration must therefore not be confused with homogenisation.

The research question is:

> What must heterogeneous streams share, and what differences must remain protected?

## Candidate organisational principles

The following are provisional candidate principles rather than settled claims.

### 1. Characteristic-timescale differentiation

Different processes should be able to evolve at rates appropriate to their function.

Examples may include:

```text
rapid threat or cue response
short-horizon motor-sensory prediction
medium-horizon trajectory evaluation
slower goal and context maintenance
persistent consequence and residue integration
offline consolidation
```

The rates should not necessarily be simple integer subdivisions of one master clock.

### 2. Asynchronous coordination without lockstep

Streams should be able to update independently while still exchanging information at relevant moments.

Coordination may occur through events rather than through continuous timestep-by-timestep synchronisation.

Candidate coordination landmarks include:

```text
prediction-error peaks
event boundaries
mode transitions
commitment entry
urgency interruption
trajectory completion
closure events
offline-phase transitions
```

### 3. Shared event organisation

Different streams may segment ongoing activity into corresponding events even when they represent different information.

REE-v3 already contains a two-scale event segmenter using faster boundary detection over self/world prediction signals and slower change detection over goal dynamics, with nested segment identifiers.

A stronger hypothesis is that event segmentation may provide common temporal landmarks around which otherwise asynchronous streams become comparable.

### 4. Homologous state or transition motifs

Different streams may exhibit analogous organisational states, such as:

```text
stable maintenance
exploration
rising conflict
boundary detection
reorientation
commitment
interruption
completion
release
offline reorganisation
```

The content of the states may differ while their transition grammar remains related.

### 5. Shared constraints without shared content

Integration may require streams to obey common boundaries or invariants without using the same latent representation.

Examples:

```text
simulation cannot create owned action residue
committed actions become causally attributable
harm information cannot be silently erased
mode changes alter precision and gating
closure requires specific conditions
offline integration cannot bypass waking authority boundaries
```

### 6. Partial independence as a functional requirement

Some streams must remain non-redundant.

REE-v3 explicitly separates sensory-discriminative and affective-motivational harm streams and constrains them against representational collapse.

This suggests that shared organisation should not mean identical dynamics.

An adequate theory must explain both:

```text
integration
and
protected non-equivalence
```

### 7. Cross-timescale influence without simple averaging

Fast streams may interrupt or redirect slower processes.

Slow streams may shape the precision, thresholds, priors, or terrain through which fast processes operate.

This interaction should not collapse into averaging all streams into one common value.

### 8. Persistent historical deformation

Committed outcomes may alter the future organisation of multiple processes through residue.

Residue provides continuity across episodes and timescales because past consequences alter the terrain of later trajectory generation and selection.

### 9. Offline reorganisation under preserved authority boundaries

Slow or offline processes may consolidate, reorganise, and contextualise information without retroactively converting imagined events into committed actions or erasing owned consequences.

### 10. Organisational closure around action

Multiple streams may remain exploratory and partially divergent until an action reaches the commitment boundary.

Commitment then creates a shared system-level event:

```text
a hypothetical trajectory
becomes an owned intervention
whose consequences can propagate
through attribution, residue, learning, and consolidation
```

## What the PNAS paper may newly motivate

The paper may motivate looking beyond the presence of multiple clocks or streams.

REE already has those.

The stronger question is whether REE exhibits **shared organisation across its existing streams**.

Possible empirical questions include:

### Shared recurrent-state question

Do E1, E2, E3, harm, goal, salience, hippocampal, and control signals repeatedly occupy recognisable system-level configurations?

### Shared sequencing question

Do these configurations recur in related orders across streams, even when the streams update at different rates?

### Event-relative alignment question

Do streams appear more strongly coordinated when aligned around event boundaries, commitment, interruption, completion, or offline transitions than when aligned by wall-clock timestep?

### Scale-invariance question

Are some transition motifs preserved across short, medium, and long horizons?

### Shared-principle versus imposed-wiring question

Does common organisation emerge from interaction, or is apparent similarity merely the direct consequence of manually shared gates and clocks?

### Protected-difference question

Which stream differences are necessary for anti-collapse, causal attribution, and ethical function?

## Important distinction: architecture, mechanism and observed dynamics

Future discussion should distinguish four levels:

```text
Architectural principle:
multiple asynchronous processes should share organising constraints

Implemented mechanism:
multi-rate clocks, event broadcasts, commitment gates,
mode coordination, residue, offline passes

Observed dynamics:
recurrent states, transition motifs, cross-scale alignment,
metastability, event-relative coordination

Functional consequence:
better prediction, flexible switching, commitment integrity,
non-erasure, and viable ethical action
```

The existence of an implemented mechanism does not prove the expected dynamics.

Likewise, finding similar dynamics does not by itself prove that they perform the intended ethical or cognitive function.

## Targeted literature-search programme

The next literature work should be deliberately divided into questions.

### Search 1: Shared recurrent states across measurement timescales

**Question:** What exactly is conserved across the timescales in the PNAS study?

Clarify:

```text
spatial state topographies
state-transition probabilities
sequence order
dwell times
recurrence structure
network participation
or some combination
```

This determines whether the REE analogue should be sought in spatial patterns, transition grammar, or both.

### Search 2: Metastability and coordination without synchrony

**Question:** How does cognitive neuroscience formalise integration among partially independent systems that neither synchronise permanently nor become independent?

Search terms:

```text
brain metastability
coordination dynamics
metastable neural dynamics
integration segregation balance
asynchronous coordination cognition
winnerless competition
heteroclinic neural dynamics
```

This literature may clarify how stable-enough coordination can coexist with flexible switching.

### Search 3: Event segmentation across nested timescales

**Question:** Do event-boundary models provide the best bridge between REE's asynchronous streams?

Target questions:

```text
Are event boundaries shared across cortical timescales?
How are nested event models coordinated?
Can different systems disagree about boundaries?
What triggers global versus local updating?
```

### Search 4: Temporal receptive windows and processing hierarchies

**Question:** How do neural systems integrate information over differing temporal horizons while remaining coordinated?

Search terms:

```text
temporal receptive windows
cortical temporal hierarchy
intrinsic neural timescales
hierarchical timescale processing
long-timescale cortical integration
```

Important distinction:

```text
hierarchical accumulation
versus
parallel streams with shared organisation
```

### Search 5: Cross-frequency and cross-timescale coupling

**Question:** Is coupling between timescales best understood as synchronisation, phase-amplitude interaction, event alignment, gain modulation, shared state transitions, or something else?

Search terms:

```text
cross-frequency coupling cognition
phase amplitude coupling
nested neural oscillations
cross-timescale neural coordination
aperiodic dynamics timescale integration
```

The aim should not be to import oscillatory mechanisms directly into REE, but to identify abstract coupling principles.

### Search 6: Shared dynamics across heterogeneous state spaces

**Question:** Can systems with different representations exhibit homologous dynamics?

Potential mathematical threads:

```text
dynamical systems conjugacy
state-space alignment
manifold alignment
shared latent dynamics
multi-view dynamical systems
Koopman representations
categorical or structural equivalence of transitions
```

This may help define "shared organisational principle" without requiring identical variables.

### Search 7: Control architectures with multiple partially independent loops

**Question:** What engineering and biological architectures coordinate multiple loops without a single scalar controller?

Search domains:

```text
hierarchical control
heterarchical control
blackboard architectures
global workspace coordination
basal ganglia cortico-thalamic loops
multi-rate control systems
distributed model predictive control
```

REE may be closer to a heterarchy or federation than to a conventional hierarchy.

### Search 8: Anti-collapse through differentiated affective and control systems

**Question:** What literature supports the claim that heterogeneous motivational and affective systems prevent pathological single-objective optimisation?

Search terms:

```text
multiple value systems neuroscience
wanting liking learning dissociation
opponent process control
neuromodulatory systems behavioural flexibility
multi-objective control biological agents
degeneracy and robustness cognition
```

### Search 9: Offline integration across timescales

**Question:** How do sleep, replay, consolidation, and synaptic homeostasis reorganise multiple streams while preserving distinctions among them?

Search terms:

```text
sleep multiscale integration
systems consolidation timescales
replay cross-network coordination
REM slow wave complementary functions
synaptic homeostasis temporal hierarchy
```

### Search 10: Substrate-independent organisational invariants

**Question:** Which principles can be specified independently of neurons, oscillations, fields, graphs, or a particular implementation?

Candidate formal lenses:

```text
dynamical invariants
organisational closure
viability theory
process architecture
category-theoretic system descriptions
constraint closure
causal emergence
multi-scale causal organisation
```

This is the broadest search and should follow, not precede, the more concrete searches.

## Possible outcomes

### Outcome A: Shared organisation is observed

This would provide evidence that REE's heterogeneous streams form an integrated dynamical organisation rather than merely coexisting.

### Outcome B: Streams are coordinated only by explicitly wired gates

This would show that REE has shared control but not broader emergent organisation.

That would still be informative.

### Outcome C: Streams remain largely unrelated

This would identify an integration gap despite the presence of asynchronous multi-rate components.

### Outcome D: Too much shared organisation causes collapse

This may validate the importance of protected heterogeneity and anti-collapse design.

### Outcome E: Event-relative organisation is stronger than clock-relative organisation

This would support event boundaries and commitment landmarks as the architecture's effective shared temporal grammar.
