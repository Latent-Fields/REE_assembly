## Source thought

> I don’t think there will be something inspectable until v3 closes. I aim to leave it inscrutable until I have that done. Then perhaps implementing your ideas would be reasonable.

## Context

This follows an external review of the Latent-Fields repositories which found that the project is internally serious and increasingly governed, but externally difficult to enter. The main public-facing weaknesses identified were not absence of structure, but poor compression and routing: too many repositories, accumulated operational history, no single inspectable artefact, and no simple route from the central idea to a reproducible result.

The immediate response is not to make the moving V3 system publicly legible before closure. External legibility would create a second design target while the architecture is still changing. It would require stabilising terminology, curating history, explaining provisional mechanisms, maintaining public entry paths, and avoiding promises based on intermediate states. This could consume attention needed for experimental closure and could distort the research toward what is easiest to explain rather than what most needs to be tested.

## Working interpretation

The present inscrutability can be treated as **construction-phase opacity**, rather than as evidence that the project lacks structure. Internally, the claims, experiments, evidence, governance, closure map, and active bottlenecks are increasingly organised. The external surface exposes the workshop rather than a finished object.

V3 closure should therefore act as a deliberate phase boundary:

### Before V3 closure

Optimise for:

- epistemic integrity;
- experimental throughput;
- architectural convergence;
- resolution of strict green-board blockers;
- preservation of failures and provenance.

Do not optimise for casual external entry or for a polished public account of mechanisms that may still change.

### At V3 closure

Pause before allowing the project to escape immediately into V4. Freeze V3 as an object that can be inspected independently of the live construction process.

The post-closure externalisation task may then include:

- one canonical entry point for the organisation;
- one frozen V3 specification;
- one reproducible flagship demonstration;
- one concise statement of what V3 does and does not establish;
- one findings document separating positive results, failures, unresolved claims, and scope limits;
- clear separation between current work, supporting research, and archaeology;
- explicit human/agent provenance where needed.

The aim should be to make V3 legible primarily by **subtraction and compression**, not by rewriting the entire research history.

## Governance implication

External inscrutability is acceptable as deliberate technical debt only while it has a defined repayment boundary. The risk is not that V3 remains difficult to inspect during construction. The risk is that V3 closes and the project moves directly into V4 without producing a stable, inspectable V3 artefact.

A possible transition gate is therefore:

> V4 expansion should not erase the opportunity to freeze, reproduce, describe, and externally inspect the completed V3 system.

This is not yet a proposal to block all V4 work. It is a prompt to decide what minimum V3 closure package must exist before V3 becomes submerged beneath the next development wave.

## Open questions

- What exact event counts as V3 closure for this purpose: strict green board, governance acceptance, or an additional reproducibility check?
- What is the minimum frozen artefact set required before substantial V4 expansion?
- Which V3 result should serve as the flagship external demonstration?
- How should claims that remain weakened, unresolved, or substrate-limited be represented in the public V3 account?
- Which operational records should remain available as audit history but be removed from the primary reader path?
