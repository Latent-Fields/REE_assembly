## Source thoughts (verbatim)

> The residue leads me to want to surface it and discuss it and consider what else could have been done. It can lead to rumination and sharp motivation. It can lead to calls for help and calls for restraining, it can lead to doubting the very consideration and the capacity of the self to act. This is intrinsic to loving. The harm we cause may be unavoidable and it drives the ongoing search for a way to navigate around or away from it. To accept the wounds and keep trying anyway it important.

> As an aside I can see how mood as we think of it in psychiatry is not just about affect it is a variation from a middle ground where there is motivation but not callousness from the residue. Instantaneous affect is one thing but mood must be regulated or else ethics breaks down

## Two insights (see intake for full development)

1. **Residue is the behavioural marker distinguishing care from mere simulation.** The `z_beta` leak (MECH-164) gives simulation of another's affect, which is equally the torturer's tool; the discriminator is that the torturer feels no residue. Residue that *drives the search for another way* is the observable signature of care — and hence a falsifier for INV-001's "ethics from prediction alone." (Answers the WS-13 audit's §2.5 crux.)

2. **Mood (psychiatric) = the regulated baseline of residue-processing.** Not instantaneous affect but the tonic setpoint — "a middle ground where there is motivation but not callousness." It is the regulator that routes residue to the adaptive branch (surface/discuss/repair/recruit-help/ask-restraint/accept-and-continue) vs the maladaptive poles (rumination + agency-eroding doubt; or numbing → callousness/dehumanisation, EXT-009). Healthy conscience is a narrow homeostatic band; mood-regulation is therefore an ethical precondition, not merely an affective state.
