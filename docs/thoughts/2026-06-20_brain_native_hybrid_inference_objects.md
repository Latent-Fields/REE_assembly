## Core thought

The brain may use a kind of structured uncertainty representation that is neither a strict Bayesian network nor a strict Markov network, while capturing strengths of both.

This may be more than an REE implementation detail. It may be a distinct mathematical / statistical object worth researching in its own right.

Working hypothesis:

```text
Biological cognition may rely on dynamic structured-uncertainty fields that combine directed evidence update, cyclic coherence constraint, embodied action, precision / gain modulation, affective salience, and offline consolidation.
```

---

## Why this is not simply Bayesian networks

Bayesian networks are useful because they represent directed conditional dependency.

But a brain-native system is unlikely to be a hand-authored directed acyclic graph. It must handle:

- recurrent loops;
- changing context;
- embodiment;
- local approximate update;
- uncertain and changing graph structure;
- action selection;
- metabolic cost;
- affective salience;
- developmental learning;
- offline consolidation;
- partial observability;
- multiple timescales.

Bayesian networks capture one face of this: evidence and directed dependency.

They likely do not capture the whole object.

---

## Why this is not simply Markov networks

Markov networks are useful because they represent mutual constraint and cyclic compatibility.

But a brain-native system also needs directionality:

- evidence arrives from perception;
- action causes outcome;
- prediction error updates memory;
- prior state shapes interpretation;
- goals change policy;
- harm / benefit prediction changes commitment.

Markov-style constraint captures coherence, but not the full causal / temporal / evidential flow.

## Relation to factor graphs

Factor graphs may be an important bridge because they can express variables and factors separately, and can be used to represent both Bayesian-network-like and Markov-network-like structures.

However, a brain-native object would likely need to go beyond static factor graphs by adding:

- changing topology;
- active sensing;
- action selection;
- affective salience;
- multiple timescales;
- developmental reconfiguration;
- sleep / offline updating;
- approximation under resource constraints.

Therefore factor graphs may be a starting lens, not the final form.
