## Core mechanistic hypothesis

The associated thought proposes that the rich subjective present may be an affordance-indexed, temporally displaced predictive construction: a short-horizon estimate of organism and world lying slightly ahead of delayed sensory evidence, structured by currently available actions and by commitment.

This thought proposes a possible biological mechanism for rapidly constructing the state from which new possibilities are generated.

> **Ephaptic coupling may provide a very fast physical mechanism for aggregating information carried in distributed oscillatory activity. The resulting field-level state may help provide hippocampal generative machinery with a rapidly updated estimate of the organism's actionable “now,” from which prospective proposals can be generated.**

The basal-ganglia-like system need not receive this aggregate directly. Its role may instead be constitutional: to govern which of the proposals generated from the current actionable state acquires behavioural authority and when commitment may land.

In shorthand:

**distributed oscillatory state**  
→ **rapid ephaptic aggregation / coordination**  
→ **actionable predicted-now**  
→ **hippocampal prospective proposal generation**  
→ **branching candidate futures**  
→ **basal-ganglia-like selection / commitment**  
→ **constrained predictive trajectory**  
→ **updated now**.

Prediction violation can interrupt this cycle:

**major mismatch**  
→ suspend commitment  
→ reconstruct actionable now  
→ reopen hippocampal branching  
→ renewed constitutional selection.

## Why proposal generation needs “now” quickly

A system that generates possible futures needs a starting state.

If proposal generation begins from a state reconstructed only after slow, sequential communication among many specialised systems, that starting point may already be stale by the time candidate futures are produced. This matters especially if the subjective/actionable present is itself displaced slightly ahead of incoming sensory evidence.

The relevant computational requirement is therefore:

> **Proposals need to come from “now” quickly.**

The hypothesis is that oscillatory activity distributed across specialised systems carries information about the organism's current state, while the extracellular electric field generated by that activity provides a physical, rapidly available collective consequence of those states. Ephaptic coupling may allow that collective field to influence neuronal timing and synchrony without requiring a dedicated executive circuit to poll every subsystem and explicitly compute a central summary.

The field need not “understand” anything. Its attraction as a mechanism is precisely that the physical substrate may perform part of the aggregation automatically.

## Oscillatory information rather than a simple scalar

The hypothesis should not be reduced to “ephaptic coupling averages activity.”

Oscillatory systems can carry information in amplitude, phase, relative phase, frequency, synchrony, coherence, and cross-frequency relationships. If ephaptic effects participate in coordination, then what is available to downstream hippocampal machinery may be a temporally structured population state rather than one scalar measure.

This matters because an affordance-indexed present is itself fundamentally temporal. Different systems may be estimating different aspects of body, world, prediction, threat, goal, salience, and action readiness on different timescales. Relative timing may therefore be as important as average activity.

A stronger version of the hypothesis is consequently:

> **Ephaptic coupling may rapidly aggregate and feed back information embodied in the temporal relationships among distributed oscillatory systems, helping stabilise a coherent organism-level state from which hippocampal prospective sequences can be generated.**

## Hippocampus as generator of possible continuations

The hippocampal system is proposed here not merely as a store of episodic memory or a spatial map, but as machinery capable of generating possible continuations from the current state.

The relevant biological literature already supports several ingredients of this interpretation:

- hippocampal theta sequences can represent trajectories extending ahead of the animal;
- the extent and content of prospective theta sweeps can depend on current goals;
- hippocampal activity can represent prospective choices even when immediate movement direction does not specify them;
- hippocampal–prefrontal synchrony is associated with memory-guided decision performance;
- theta phase can organise current and prospective information within a tightly structured cycle.

These findings do not establish the ephaptic aggregation hypothesis. They do support the idea that the hippocampus is a plausible recipient of rapidly updated state information when the computational task is to generate candidate futures from the organism's current situation.

## Established ingredients versus proposed integration

The epistemic status of the components should remain explicit.

### Established or comparatively well supported ingredients

- Neural populations generate endogenous extracellular electric fields.
- Physiological extracellular fields can influence neuronal excitability, spike timing, synchrony, and network activity under some conditions.
- Oscillatory synchrony and phase relationships can carry behaviourally relevant information.
- Hippocampal theta sequences can encode current and prospective trajectories.
- Hippocampal–cortical coordination is associated with planning, memory-guided decisions, and prospective behaviour.

### Suggestive bridge

- Mesoscale ephaptic interactions may participate reciprocally in ongoing oscillatory dynamics rather than functioning only as passive by-products of neural activity.
- A collective extracellular field is, by construction, generated from the activity of many nearby neurons and therefore contains information about population state.

### Specific mechanistic hypothesis proposed here

> **Ephaptic coupling functions as a rapid physical aggregation mechanism for distributed oscillatory information, helping deliver or stabilise a temporally coherent actionable-now for hippocampal prospective generation.**

### Further proposed function

> **The coherence of this rapidly aggregated state may help locate the moving frontier where a previously branching future becomes sufficiently well predicted for commitment to land.**

This final role is especially speculative and should be treated as a hypothesis to test, not as an established biological function of ephaptic coupling.

## Division of labour with basal-ganglia-like machinery

The hypothesis explicitly separates two questions that should not be collapsed:

### What possibilities exist from now?

This is proposed to depend heavily on hippocampal and related generative machinery receiving a sufficiently current, coherent state.

### Which possibility acquires behavioural authority?

This is proposed to depend on basal-ganglia-like constitutional machinery integrating goals, predicted harm, wanting, context, uncertainty, salience, residue, current commitments, and other relevant influences.

Thus the ephaptic aggregate is not proposed as another “vote” in the action selector.

Instead:

> **ephaptic/oscillatory integration helps establish the state from which proposals are generated; basal-ganglia-like machinery governs the proposals.**

This distinction may be especially important for REE, where failures can arise either because candidate futures are poorly generated from an impoverished or stale current state, or because valid candidates fail to acquire behavioural authority.

## Relationship to the commitment boundary

The associated temporal-present hypothesis proposes a boundary between:

- a relatively smooth, well-predicted, committed region; and
- a branching probabilistic region where future choices remain unresolved.

The present mechanistic hypothesis adds:

> **A fast cross-system coherence process may be needed to keep that boundary correctly located.**

Commitment should not land arbitrarily far down a branch that may not happen. A candidate future should first become sufficiently coherent and predictable to support a stable committed trajectory.

The ephaptic/oscillatory aggregate may therefore contribute not primarily to *which* candidate is best, but to *when* distributed predictive machinery has cohered sufficiently for commitment to become permissible.

This introduces an important constitutional distinction:

- **content authority:** which future should win;
- **temporal/readiness authority:** whether that future is sufficiently coherent for commitment to land.

These may require different mechanisms.

## Potential REE implications

This thought does not imply that REE needs a literal simulation of biological ephaptic fields.

Instead it suggests computational dependencies worth examining through the existing claims/closure machinery:

1. Does REE possess a fast organism-level representation of the current actionable state from which candidate futures are generated?
2. Is E2/HPC proposal generation anchored to sufficiently current information, or can it operate on temporally stale or internally inconsistent inputs?
3. Does REE distinguish candidate desirability from candidate readiness/coherence?
4. Is commitment permitted only after a candidate trajectory becomes sufficiently well formed?
5. Could a fast distributed-coherence variable improve commitment timing without becoming another scalar value term in E3?
6. Does interruption rapidly invalidate the current proposal-generation anchor and force reconstruction of the actionable state?
7. Are phase/coherence relationships potentially useful computational analogues even if the implementation is not biologically ephaptic?

These are theory-derived implications, not established architectural requirements.

## Falsifiable consequences of the biological hypothesis

If ephaptic aggregation contributes materially to rapid construction of actionable-now and proposal generation, then one would expect some combination of the following:

- perturbing endogenous field structure while minimally altering ordinary synaptic drive should alter timing/coherence of prospective hippocampal representations;
- effects should be strongest where temporally coordinated population activity is required rapidly;
- disrupting relevant oscillatory coherence should impair prospective proposal organisation more than simple sensory representation;
- commitment timing should correlate with measures of cross-system coherence independently of candidate value;
- unexpected interruption should rapidly reorganise the relevant coherence structure before or alongside renewed prospective branching;
- the relationship should be timescale-sensitive rather than reducible to a static average field magnitude.

The literature currently supports ingredients of these predictions but does not establish the full mechanism.

Basal-ganglia-like machinery then constitutionally governs which proposals acquire commitment and behavioural authority. The same fast coherence process may help locate the moving frontier at which a branching future has become sufficiently well predicted for commitment to land.**

> **Proposals need to come from “now” quickly.**
