# Prospective navigation through goal topology

**Date:** 2026-08-08
**Origin:** User-directed, dialogue-assisted refinement of the 2026-08-07 goal-topology thought
**Evidence status:** This is a new conceptual refinement explicitly requested by the user after comparison with the previously ingested goal-topology thought. The prose below is dialogue-assisted synthesis rather than verbatim user wording. It should therefore be treated as secondary formulation of the new idea, while the linked 2026-08-07 thought remains the primary evidence for the earlier goal-topology proposal.

## Relationship to the earlier thought

The earlier thought proposed that goals may be represented within a changing relational topology rather than as a fixed hierarchy. Pursuit of a goal can reveal necessary subgoals, reveal a larger goal of which the current goal is itself a component, or expose orthogonal possible goals that may be retained without becoming current priorities. Potential goals may therefore remain represented within the topology even when they receive little current attention.

The present thought does not restate that proposal. It adds a narrower computational hypothesis prompted by new evidence concerning hippocampal prospective activity: the relevant hippocampal contribution may involve not only representing such a topology, but also **prospectively traversing it**.

## Prospective traversal

During spatial navigation, hippocampal activity can rapidly sweep through possible future trajectories, with trajectories toward remembered goals preferentially represented. This suggests a computational distinction between:

1. representing a relational space;
2. privileging some state within that space as a destination;
3. prospectively generating paths through the relational space capable of reaching it; and
4. selecting among the resulting candidate trajectories.

If this computational primitive generalises beyond physical navigation, abstract goal-directed cognition might similarly operate partly by navigating a learned relational topology. A currently privileged goal would function as a destination within that topology. Prospective traversal could generate candidate routes from the present state toward it, while other mechanisms determine which destination matters and which generated trajectory should actually be pursued.

## Provisional REE division of labour

A useful provisional division is:

- **Goal/commitment mechanisms** privilege some represented state or region as a destination.
- **Hippocampal-like relational machinery** maintains the topology and prospectively traverses possible routes through it.
- **E3 trajectory selection** evaluates or selects among the resulting candidate trajectories.
- **Precision and attentional mechanisms** regulate which parts of the topology receive sufficient processing to influence current behaviour.

This separation matters because hippocampal traversal need not itself determine what ought to be wanted. A navigational or generative substrate can propose routes toward a currently privileged state without also being the mechanism that grants that state motivational authority.

## Goal and subgoal as relational roles

This also sharpens what it means for something to be a goal or subgoal. These may not need to be intrinsic properties stored on individual nodes. A represented state can be an overarching goal in one context, an intermediate subgoal in another, or merely an available possibility in another.

**Goal-ness and subgoal-ness may therefore be relational properties arising from the conjunction of the agent's present state, its active commitments, its currently privileged destination, and the trajectory under consideration.**

A fixed node label such as `goal` or `subgoal` may consequently be less fundamental than the role a represented state occupies within a particular prospective traversal.

## Registered but weak possibilities

The earlier thought already proposed that possible goals can remain within the topology despite receiving little current attention. The present refinement preserves that idea rather than claiming it as new. A useful formulation is that such possibilities may be **topologically registered but motivationally weak**: their existence and relations to other states can be retained while they remain below the threshold required to substantially influence prospective traversal or trajectory selection.

## Stronger hypothesis

The resulting hypothesis is stronger than the claim that the hippocampus is merely well suited to representing goal topology:

> **Abstract prospective planning may reuse a navigation-like computational primitive. Goal-directed cognition may involve prospective traversal through a dynamically learned relational topology toward states privileged by commitment and valuation mechanisms, with trajectories subsequently subject to selection.**

This reframes planning, at least in part, as navigation through relational structure rather than solely as symbolic decomposition of a fixed goal hierarchy.

## Epistemic boundary

The prompting evidence does not demonstrate this generalisation. The experimentally demonstrated phenomenon concerns hippocampal prospective activity during navigation through physical space toward remembered locations. Extending the mechanism to abstract goal spaces remains an architectural hypothesis.

The evidential contribution is therefore narrower: it provides a biological precedent for a system that represents a learned topology and rapidly generates prospective trajectories through it toward remembered goals. The proposed reuse or generalisation of that primitive for abstract goal-directed planning remains to be tested rather than assumed.

## Novel delta relative to the 2026-08-07 thought

The new content to preserve is specifically:

- topology **representation** and prospective **traversal** are separable operations;
- the mechanism that privileges a destination can be separated from the mechanism that navigates toward it;
- goal/subgoal status may emerge relationally from current state, privileged destination, commitment, and trajectory rather than being a permanent node type; and
- abstract planning may reuse or generalise a navigation-like prospective-sweep primitive.

The earlier ideas of dynamic goal topology, lateral possibility retention, and low-attention represented possibilities should remain attributed to the 2026-08-07 thought rather than back-projected as discoveries of this refinement.
