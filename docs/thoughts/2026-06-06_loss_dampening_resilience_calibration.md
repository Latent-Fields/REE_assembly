The useful REE idea is:

> resilience may require active loss-dampening / setback-calibration mechanisms, not merely strong reward drive or high goal persistence.

In REE terms, a failed or costly action should not automatically terminate the goal stream, but neither should the system ignore loss. The architecture needs a calibrated middle layer: losses should update expectations, risk, strategy, and decommit thresholds without necessarily collapsing motivation.

REE already treats goals, commitment, residue, action selection, uncertainty, and offline integration as intertwined. Loss processing sits at the boundary between learning and persistence.

A mature REE should not be a reward-maximiser that ignores loss, nor a fragile system that abandons a viable goal after one negative outcome.

## 4. REE-specific hypothesis

REE may require distinct channels for:

1. **Outcome loss** — an action failed, cost more than expected, or did not obtain reward.
2. **Harm residue** — the system caused harm or violation that should persist ethically.
3. **Goal viability update** — the target may still be valid, but route/strategy/effort estimates need revision.
4. **Motivational dampening/amplification** — how much the loss should alter future activation.
5. **Commitment recalibration** — whether to retry, revise, pause, or decommit.

A simple architecture where loss directly subtracts from goal value is probably too brittle.

Possible computational primitive:

```text
post_loss_goal_state = f(loss_magnitude, harm_residue, controllability, goal_importance, uncertainty, fatigue, social_support, alternative_routes)
```

This allows loss to change strategy and confidence without automatically destroying the underlying goal stream.

## 5. Important cautions

Do not treat loss dampening as denial.

Do not confuse resilience with ignoring evidence.

Do not allow loss dampening to suppress harm residue.

Do not encode a simple toughness parameter that overrides caution.

Do not make this a REE-v3 implementation target without source verification and linkage to existing motivational-persistence work.

The useful extraction is:

> loss should modulate goal persistence through calibrated strategy and commitment updates rather than raw motivational subtraction.
