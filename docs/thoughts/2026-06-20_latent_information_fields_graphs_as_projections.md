## Why field-first may be better than graph-first

Graphs are useful when the system already knows:

```text
these are the nodes
these are the edges
these are the relation types
```

But cognition may often begin before that. The system may first hold a distributed pattern of:

- uncertainty;
- salience;
- prediction error;
- affective pressure;
- ethical pressure;
- affordance;
- residue;
- partial structure;
- scale ambiguity;
- unresolved coherence tension.

Only later does this crystallise into graph-like structure.

Possible mappings:

```text
field pattern -> latent cause -> node
field gradient -> likely transition -> edge
field basin -> stable concept
field tension -> unresolved conflict
field compression -> abstraction
field deformation -> residue
field flow -> action affordance
```

## Residue as field deformation

Residue may be better understood as a persistent deformation in the latent information field, not merely a memory, penalty, or confidence adjustment.

Provisional formulation:

```text
Residue is a persistent deformation in the latent field caused by unresolved morally relevant structure.
```

This would explain why residue should not simply be erased after an action succeeds.

It remains part of the field geometry and influences future perception, action, and consolidation until properly integrated.

## Claims index implication

The claims index may be a discrete externalisation of field tensions.

A claim is not necessarily the field-native object. It may be a projection extracted so that the system can:

- inspect it;
- debate it;
- test it;
- connect it to evidence;
- promote or demote it;
- dispatch it to implementation;
- preserve unresolved conflict.

This means claims-index graphs are still useful, but they may be measurement / governance artefacts rather than the deeper substrate.
