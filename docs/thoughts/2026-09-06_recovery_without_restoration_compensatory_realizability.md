# Recovery without restoration: compensatory realizability as a property of adaptive artificial organisms

**Date:** 2026-09-06  
**Status:** thought + experiment seed  
**Primary source:** Grover S, Wen W, Reinhart RMG. *Neuromodulation for restoring and amplifying brain function.* Nature Neuroscience. Published 2026-09-04. DOI: 10.1038/s41593-026-02434-6  
**Source URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-026-02434-6

## Source-grounded starting point

Grover, Wen and Reinhart distinguish two strategies for recovering or improving function:

- **restorative normalization (RN):** push neural dynamics back toward a previous or healthy-like state;
- **compensatory amplification (CA):** identify a repurposed process that is already supporting function and selectively strengthen it.

The Perspective names four factors that enable compensatory amplification: **multiple realizability, multiscale neuroplasticity, precision readiness, and activity selectivity**.

The publicly accessible Nature preview is sufficient to establish this framework, but not the details of the authors' full argument. The REE mapping below is therefore a derived hypothesis, not a claim about what the paper itself demonstrates.

## Core REE thought

An adaptive artificial organism should not be required to recover by reconstructing its former internal state.

If a functionally important route is degraded, an organism with genuine internal adaptability may recruit another representation, memory route, predictor, latent dimension, or regulatory relationship that partially restores useful behaviour. If that substitute route can then be selectively strengthened while the original deficit remains, the organism has demonstrated **recovery without restoration**.

This is stronger evidence of organism-like adaptation than simply showing that a damaged engineered pathway can be repaired.

The relevant property is **compensatory realizability**:

> the capacity to preserve or recover a useful function by reorganizing causal responsibility across internal processes rather than by returning the system to one canonical implementation.

## Why the four CA prerequisites are unusually relevant to REE

### 1. Multiple realizability

A useful behavioural objective need not have one privileged internal solution. In REE, the same competence should in principle be supportable by different internal representations or causal routes.

This suggests an empirical criterion: after perturbation, recovered behaviour should be accompanied by a demonstrably altered distribution of causal importance rather than merely a hidden reconstruction of the original pathway.

### 2. Multiscale neuroplasticity

REE already separates processes operating over different characteristic timescales. Compensation may therefore unfold in stages:

- rapid reweighting or routing during waking behaviour;
- slower reorganization of persistent representations;
- hippocampal or trajectory-memory support for newly successful substitutes;
- sleep-mediated consolidation of the altered solution.

This makes compensation potentially observable as a temporal process rather than a single endpoint.

### 3. Precision readiness

The Nature Perspective uses **precision readiness** as one of the conditions for compensatory amplification. This should not automatically be equated with REE's own precision/gain machinery; the terminological overlap may conceal different technical meanings.

Nevertheless, the conceptual correspondence is important enough to test: a latent process may already exist, be weakly expressed, and become functionally useful when its weighting or gain changes after failure elsewhere.

A useful REE question is therefore not only *what new representation was learned?* but also *what previously available representation became newly consequential?*

### 4. Activity selectivity

Compensation should depend on what the organism is actually doing. A global parameter increase is weak evidence. Selective amplification of a process specifically when it contributes to the recovered behaviour is much stronger evidence of adaptive control.

This aligns naturally with state-dependent regulation in REE: modulation should be conditional on current state, uncertainty, action context, or task demand rather than implemented as indiscriminate gain.

## Proposed compensation assay

Once a V3 organism has a stable competence worth lesioning, run the following sequence.

### Phase A — establish baseline competence

Characterize:

- behavioural success;
- representation geometry;
- which internal variables and routes have causal importance;
- recovery from ordinary perturbation;
- waking versus sleep-related changes.

### Phase B — perturb a functionally important route

Candidate perturbations could include a representational bottleneck, a memory route, a predictor coupling, or a sensory basis. The exact lesion should be chosen from whichever V3 component is sufficiently mature to support a clean causal intervention.

The lesion should degrade competence without globally disabling the organism.

### Phase C — observe spontaneous compensation

Do **not** immediately repair the damaged route.

Measure what the organism recruits instead:

- latent dimensions whose behavioural relevance increases;
- alternate sensory features;
- changed E1/E2 dependence;
- changed hippocampal or trajectory-memory use;
- altered precision/gain allocation;
- new state-dependent control patterns;
- changes emerging specifically after sleep.

The key question is whether an alternative internal process acquires causal importance before external intervention.

### Phase D — compare interventions

At minimum compare:

1. **No intervention:** lesion remains; spontaneous compensation only.
2. **Restorative normalization analogue:** restore the damaged route toward its baseline operation.
3. **Compensatory amplification analogue:** leave the original lesion in place and selectively strengthen the substitute route the organism actually recruited.
4. **Control amplification:** strengthen a comparably sized but non-compensatory process, to exclude the possibility that generic added capacity explains recovery.

### Phase E — measure more than reward

A successful result should include behavioural and mechanistic evidence.

Measure:

- degree and speed of competence recovery;
- generalization outside the lesion-training context;
- energy or representational cost;
- robustness to a second perturbation;
- representation geometry before lesion, during spontaneous compensation, and after amplification;
- causal attribution by targeted re-ablation or intervention;
- whether compensation persists, disappears, or competes when the original route is later restored;
- whether sleep consolidates the compensatory solution.

The strongest result would be competent behaviour restored through a causally verified route that is meaningfully different from the pre-lesion solution.

## Distinguishing adaptation from reconstruction

A central experimental danger is mistaking covert restoration for compensation.

Evidence for genuine compensation would be stronger if:

- the original pathway remains observably impaired;
- a different internal process becomes necessary for recovered behaviour;
- ablating that substitute selectively removes the recovered competence;
- restoring the original pathway later produces competition, redundancy, or reorganization rather than revealing that it had already silently returned.

This makes causal intervention essential. Similar surface behaviour alone cannot establish multiple realizability.

## Maladaptive compensation

Compensation need not remain beneficial.

A workaround that is initially adaptive may become over-strengthened, over-generalized, or too persistent. The organism could then become trapped in a distorted solution even after the original problem has changed or resolved.

This produces a distinct mechanistic account of pathology:

> pathology may arise not only because the original mechanism is damaged, but because an adaptation to that damage becomes over-stabilized.

This is particularly relevant to REE's existing interest in attractor confidence, precision/gain regulation, perception, and psychiatric-like failure modes.

A compensatory route could:

- acquire excessive precision or gain;
- dominate ambiguous perception;
- become resistant to counterfactual correction;
- persist after the initiating lesion has resolved;
- recruit memory and sleep processes that increasingly stabilize the workaround;
- reduce flexibility by making one emergency solution the default interpretation or policy.

The pathological experiment is therefore the mirror image of the recovery experiment: progressively amplify an initially useful substitute and identify the point at which compensation stops being adaptive.

## Experimental predictions

If REE supports genuine compensatory realizability, then after a partial lesion:

1. Behavioural recovery should sometimes precede restoration of the damaged representation.
2. The causal importance of an alternative process should increase during recovery.
3. Selective amplification of that process should improve recovery more than amplification of an unrelated process.
4. Different developmental histories or random seeds may discover different compensatory routes to similar behavioural competence.
5. Sleep may increase the stability or generalization of a newly recruited substitute.
6. Excessive amplification may produce hysteresis: the compensatory solution may persist even after the original route is restored.
7. Maladaptive compensation should be detectable as improved local performance accompanied by poorer generalization, flexibility, calibration, or counterfactual revision.

## Relation to current REE work

This thought is most useful once a V3 competence is stable enough to perturb cleanly. It should not distract from establishing that baseline.

However, it changes what counts as an interesting failure. When a pathway is weakened, the first question should no longer be only *how do we fix the failed component?* It should also be:

> **What did the organism try to become in order to keep functioning?**

That question can turn lesions, representation failures, and partial regressions into direct probes of adaptability.

Candidate future assays may be especially informative around the observation → latent world representation → predictor interface, memory-route substitution, and state-dependent precision/gain control, provided each target is mature enough to support an interpretable lesion.

## Falsification / weak-result conditions

The idea should be weakened if:

- every successful recovery requires reconstructing the original internal state;
- apparently alternative routes prove causally irrelevant;
- global added capacity performs as well as selective amplification;
- compensation fails to generalize beyond the training context;
- different seeds always converge on an effectively identical solution despite available alternatives;
- the organism cannot preserve function when one canonical route is unavailable.

Conversely, robust recovery through different causally verified routes would be unusually strong evidence that REE is developing adaptive organization rather than merely executing a brittle engineered pipeline.

## One-line experimental programme

**Perturb → observe spontaneous compensation → identify the substitute representation or route → selectively amplify it → test causal dependence and generalization → over-amplify to locate the adaptive-to-maladaptive boundary.**
