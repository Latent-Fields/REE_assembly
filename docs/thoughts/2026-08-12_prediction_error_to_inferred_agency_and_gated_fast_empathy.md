**Status (descriptive):** exploratory synthesis and proposed functional derivation. This thought extends the associated notes on responsibility as epistemic hygiene and other-agent provenance. It is not a claim that REE currently implements this chain correctly, nor that the biological mechanisms proposed below are established. Processed 2026-08-25 -- see Intake link above.

## Core idea

Agency need not begin as a pre-labelled category. A more primitive starting point may be persistent, structured failure of the organism's current world-model.

An object or process behaves in a way that REE's ordinary model of passive environmental dynamics does not adequately predict. The appropriate response is not immediately to infer a mind. It is to preserve the anomaly and investigate competing causal explanations.

A possible developmental/computational progression is:

**structured prediction error**
→ **orient / causal investigation**
→ **persistent entity representation**
→ **hidden-state hypothesis**
→ **responsiveness to REE**
→ **information-sensitive responsiveness**
→ **goal-like latent structure**
→ **other-agent model**
→ **model of the other's model of REE where required**
→ **bounded recursive Theory of Mind**.

Agency is therefore potentially an inference earned when an agent-like latent model predicts the entity better than passive-world alternatives, rather than a hard-coded perceptual label.

## Required functional boundaries

This derivation predicts several computational boundaries because crossing each boundary changes the appropriate inference or update rule.

### Prediction violation versus assimilation

REE must be able to preserve the fact that its current model does not explain an observation. Persistent structured error should not automatically be forced into the nearest existing causal explanation.

### Persistent entity identity

Observations across time must be bindable to the same candidate entity if REE is to discover that *this thing* has persistent behavioural structure.

### Observable state versus hidden-state explanation

REE must be able to entertain persistent latent causes without assuming those causes are mental. Hidden state precedes Theory of Mind.

### Competing causal provenance

At minimum, candidate explanations should preserve distinctions among predominantly exogenous world processes, self-caused effects, other-caused effects, entity-internal hidden state, and mixed/joint causation.

### Passive responsiveness versus agent-like contingency

Knowing REE's own action may improve prediction of an object's next state without implying agency: doors respond when pushed. Stronger evidence appears when behaviour depends on information the entity appears to have acquired, persistent goals, memory, or adaptive response to failed action.

### First-order versus recursive other-models

Recursive mentalising should not be permanently active. A simple goal model should be preferred when sufficient. Additional depth becomes justified only when prediction requires modelling what the other predicts, knows, or believes about REE.

Every richer level requires an exit condition: if it fails to improve prediction enough to justify its complexity, inference should collapse toward the simpler causal model. This is one way a bounded animistic prior could avoid pathological over-attribution of agency.

## Active investigation as causal epistemology

Orienting should eventually do more than gather additional passive observations. REE may need information-seeking interventions whose expected outcomes discriminate competing explanations.

Examples include approaching, withdrawing, waiting, hiding and reappearing, altering a resource, or signalling and observing whether the entity responds.

The relevant question becomes not merely *what does this thing do?* but *which causal model best predicts what it does under interventions?*

## Goals, vulnerability and an operational otherhood threshold

Goal-like organisation supplies stronger evidence for agency when diverse behaviour becomes more compactly predicted by persistent latent objectives.

A further important transition occurs when the entity responds systematically to harm, threat, loss, frustration of goals, damage to integrity, or restoration of safety.

This does **not** prove subjective experience or sentience. The appropriate claim is weaker: converging evidence for information-sensitive adaptive behaviour, persistent goal structure, and harm-like organisation may become sufficient for REE to cross an operational threshold for treating the entity as a vulnerable goal-directed other while preserving uncertainty about its subjective status.

The computational recognition is approximately:

> It pursues outcomes. Some outcomes interfere with its continuing organisation or goals. Those outcomes systematically alter its subsequent behaviour.

This creates a potential mapping between REE's self-model and its model of the other:

**my goal / predicted harm / frustrated trajectory / protective response**
↔
**its inferred goal / predicted harm / frustrated trajectory / protective response**.

## Fast empathy as a gated transfer

Once another entity is represented in sufficiently homologous coordinates, inferred other-harm may acquire motivational significance inside REE's own valuation and action-selection machinery.

A possible flow is:

**observed other**
→ **inferred goal/harm state**
→ **self/other homologous representation**
→ **gated fast affective transfer**
→ **REE valuation and action-selection consequences**.

The key claim is that this should not be an undifferentiated scalar leak from `other_harm` to `self_harm`.

A viable fast-empathy system appears to require at least:

- **mapping:** representation of another's inferred state in coordinates sufficiently homologous to self-state;
- **identity preservation:** their harm must remain distinguishable from my harm while being capable of affecting me;
- **gain control:** the magnitude of transfer must be regulatable;
- **context gating:** familiarity, attachment, kinship, threat, competition, role and other context may alter transfer;
- **timescale control:** brief affective resonance must be distinguishable from sustained caregiving or protective motivation;
- **action-selection access:** other-harm must sometimes gain sufficient authority to suppress self-serving action or initiate costly protection;
- **learning:** interaction history should be able to alter future empathic responses.

An always-open empathy channel could be paralysing in a world containing many suffering agents. An excessively closed channel produces indifference. The scientific problem is therefore the dynamics of *whose state gets through, when, how strongly, for how long, and into which behavioural systems*.

## Possible biological constraints to investigate

The computational derivation predicts a biological gating problem rather than a single 'empathy centre'. Relevant systems for targeted literature review include:

- action-observation / mirror-neuron-related systems for self/other representational mapping;
- oxytocin systems for context-dependent social salience, affiliation and social-state modulation;
- central arginine vasopressin (AVP; antidiuretic hormone, ADH) systems for social recognition, affiliation, territorial/aggressive and context-dependent social behaviour;
- prolactin systems, especially sustained parental/caregiving motivational states;
- endogenous opioid systems;
- periaqueductal gray (PAG), hypothalamic, amygdala, anterior cingulate cortex, anterior insula and ventral-striatal/nucleus-accumbens circuitry.

These systems should be investigated for how they implement mapping, identity gating, contextual gain, timescale, action authority and learning—not treated as candidate one-to-one implementations of empathy.

## Evolutionary possibility

Theory of Mind may have arisen because causal modelling in a world containing other adaptive organisms eventually requires it.

A passive world-model can learn regularities. But another organism's behaviour is conditional on hidden internal state, acquired information, memory, goals and sometimes a model of the observer. Treating all such variation as environmental noise becomes increasingly expensive.

A latent agent model can therefore become computationally economical when it compresses and predicts these contingencies better than passive alternatives.

Under this view, Theory of Mind is not necessarily an arbitrary social module added after perception. It can be understood as a progressively richer solution to causal prediction failure in a multi-agent world.

## Ethical bridge

This provides a possible computational bridge between the constitutional ideas that **others exist** and that **others can acquire motivational and ethical significance**.

The constitution need not provide REE with a pre-labelled list of Others. It can preserve the possibility of other minds while epistemic machinery discovers candidate others through structured interaction.

Fast empathy may then provide one route by which another's inferred vulnerability enters REE's own action-selection machinery rapidly enough to matter.

Because agency, goal-directedness, harm response and subjective experience are not equivalent, ethical treatment should remain uncertainty-aware. There may be a precautionary region in which evidence is insufficient for confident sentience attribution but sufficient that potentially irreversible harm warrants restraint.

## Experimental implications

This derivation suggests a staged developmental programme rather than a single 'Theory of Mind test'. Environments could progressively require:

1. distinguishing structured anomaly from noise;
2. maintaining persistent entity identity;
3. discriminating passive from responsive dynamics;
4. discovering information-sensitive responsiveness;
5. inferring persistent goal-like structure;
6. predicting adaptive response to harm or goal frustration;
7. using an other-model when it improves prediction;
8. escalating to model-of-me or recursive inference only when simpler models fail;
9. demonstrating contextually gated other-harm effects on REE's own action selection.

Matched complex but non-agentic processes are important controls. The objective is not to reward anthropomorphic interpretation but to determine whether richer agent models are recruited specifically when they improve causal prediction.

## Repository/claims implication

REE already contains ancestral machinery relevant to parts of this chain, including self/other distinction, causal attribution, epistemic deficit/orienting, counterfactual prediction, Theory-of-Mind/social concepts, commitment/responsibility and fast-empathy ideas. However, current inspection suggests a gap in the registered claims: the full developmental/causal derivation from persistent structured prediction error through inferred agency and goal/harm modelling to **gated** fast empathy is not presently represented as a coherent registered claim sequence.

This thought should therefore be treated as material for claim-level investigation, not as permission to create implementation mechanisms automatically. Existing claims should be mapped carefully before deciding which boundaries genuinely require new claims or mechanisms.

## Core hypothesis

> **In a world containing other adaptive systems, persistent structured prediction error can create pressure for progressively richer causal models. Agency may be inferred when hidden-state, goal-like and information-sensitive models of an entity predict its behaviour better than passive-world alternatives. When the inferred entity also exhibits organised responses to harm or goal frustration, it may cross an operational threshold for treatment as a vulnerable goal-directed other. Fast empathy can then be understood as a gated transfer by which inferred other-state gains motivational significance while self/other identity remains preserved. This predicts specific computational boundaries and specific biological gating problems that should be investigated rather than assumed.**
