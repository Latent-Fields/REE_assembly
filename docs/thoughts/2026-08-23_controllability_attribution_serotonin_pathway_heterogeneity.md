# Thought — Suspicion, Controllability Attribution, and Pathway-Specific Control of Alternative Explanations

**Date:** 2026-08-23  
**Status:** Raw thought intake; exploratory synthesis and source for later claim mapping. This is not an architectural commitment and does not authorise implementation.  
**Scope:** Suspicion under threat, causal attribution, controllability learning, internally constructed social models, active disambiguation, and heterogeneity within serotonin-related systems.

## Provenance and user contribution

This thought begins with Daniel's attempt to understand what cognition would be required for a system to construct differentiated models of other people from repeated social interactions. The categories are not to be designed into the Reflective–Ethical Engine (REE). The question is what prior machinery would make their internal discovery possible.

The initiating formulations included:

> “Someone has a suspicion and is trying to fit the pieces together in some context or frame.”

> “Usually they could maintain many different interpretations but there are a set of interpretations which feel threatening.”

> “And so your sense of self is exquisitely important to keep yourself safe.”

> “Maintaining epistemic humility remains important even after a good enough read of a situation may have led to committed behaviour that reaped good data for what a decision caused.”

> “More it points to what parts of cognition are necessary that such a system could even be constructed after repeated social interactions.”

> “Constructed internally to REE. Not made into design.”

The further observation developed here is:

> “The serotonin input means serotonin may have a range of similar but different effects and the different nuclei may be useful in disambiguation. Maintaining alternative explanations, perceptions, etc may have similar computational load while having very different needs and effects.”

And, importantly for developmental placement:

> “These ideas are not all late REE versions. They link with attribution and causality estimations generally which are planned for earlier versions.”

The governing version-allocation rule was subsequently restated explicitly:

> “If something is needed to finish v3 as the first solitary organism then it must be v3.”

This rule is load-bearing. Version allocation follows the earliest functional necessity of a capacity, not the date on which it was noticed, the apparent sophistication of its biological analogy, or the thematic document in which it first appeared.

A correction to the initial synthesis is also load-bearing:

> “An organism must always act under uncertainty.”

This is already developed in REE's prior thoughts on epistemic deficit and orienting, and especially `2026-08-12_persistence_must_earn_continuation.md`. Certainty cannot be the stopping condition. Information seeking is itself an action that competes with acting now, and “sure enough” is dynamically governed by decision relevance, expected information gain, threat, reversibility, goal-acquisition windows, opportunity cost and what happens in the world while REE deliberates. The present thought assumes that prior architecture rather than proposing it anew.

The named computational mechanisms, neuroscience mapping, distinctions and proposed experimental consequences below are a synthesis of those ideas with the cited literature and existing REE architecture. They should remain revisable.

## Core idea

An organism always acts under residual uncertainty because certainty is unavailable in principle. Orienting, curiosity and information hunger can reduce decision-relevant uncertainty, but must continually compete with commitment: a threat may approach, a goal-acquisition window may close, a resource may disappear, or another agent may act. The earlier REE thoughts already establish this general balance.

The new question here begins after accepting that premise. Suspicion is not itself a single belief. It is a state in which observations are being fitted into competing causal frames, some of which predict danger. When REE commits under this unavoidable uncertainty, it must still:

- preserve more than one interpretation;
- give threatening possibilities enough authority to protect the organism;
- avoid allowing threat to become proof of malicious causation;
- preserve the confidence, expectations and live causal alternatives present at commitment;
- observe what follows from the action;
- assign the resulting evidence to the correct causal source;
- revise the relevant self, other, interaction or world model;
- retain enough epistemic humility to reinterpret even after successful action.

This suggests a structural separation between **action commitment**, **interpretative collapse**, **causal attribution**, **controllability estimation**, and **responsibility assignment**. They interact, but they are not the same operation and should not be written into the same register.

Threat may justify interruption, protective distance, further sampling or a reversible defensive action. It should not directly establish who intended what or who bears responsibility.

## Confidence-conditioned controllability attribution

The 2026 study by Liu and colleagues adds an important missing link. In their task, people did not infer controllability solely from whether feedback was favourable. They preserved information about their confidence in the original decision and later used it to interpret an outcome. High confidence followed by unexpected negative feedback supported an external or unreliable-feedback explanation; low confidence made self-performance a more plausible cause. Dorsomedial prefrontal cortex activity tracked decision confidence and interacted with the dorsal raphe nucleus during controllability learning. Disrupting dorsomedial prefrontal cortex impaired that learning. Estimated controllability then altered outcome-related and credit-assignment-related signals elsewhere, including dopamine-associated midbrain regions.

This supports a candidate REE mechanism:

### Candidate mechanism — Confidence-Conditioned Controllability Attribution

At commitment time, REE preserves:

- the selected action or trajectory;
- the expected outcome distribution;
- its confidence in the relevant prediction or decision;
- the causal hypotheses under consideration;
- the context and entities to which those hypotheses refer.

When the outcome arrives, REE does not treat prediction error as self-explanatory. It uses the relationship among confidence, expected outcome, observed outcome, context and prior controllability to update a distribution over causal provenance. Candidate sources may include:

- REE's own action or performance;
- another entity's action or hidden state;
- a shared or interactive process;
- a passive world process;
- measurement or observation unreliability;
- a cause not yet represented adequately.

The attribution then determines which model is eligible to learn from the error. This is **attribution-directed prediction-error routing**. The same event may therefore produce different learning depending on what REE had reason to expect, how confident it was, and how controllable the relevant outcome stream had previously proved to be.

A schematic state might eventually be indexed as:

\[
C_t(\text{candidate controller},\ \text{context},\ \text{outcome stream})
\]

This should not be assumed to be a global scalar “locus of control.” A system may accurately estimate strong control over one consequence, weak control over another, joint control in a social interaction, and uncertainty where its causal vocabulary remains inadequate.

## Controllability and agency are related but not identical

Existing REE self-attribution work uses reafferent forward-model comparators: compare the outcome predicted from the actual action with the observed state, and use the residual as evidence about self-caused versus externally contributed change. This appears foundational and need not wait for mature social cognition.

The new controllability account adds a slower and more contextual inference over repeated events. A single comparator asks approximately:

> Did the observed change match what this action should have caused?

A controllability estimator asks approximately:

> Across this context and outcome stream, how much do my actions reliably determine what happens, and how should that alter what I learn from this outcome?

The first can provide evidence to the second. Neither alone establishes another mind, deliberate harm or moral responsibility.

This distinction also clarifies developmental ordering. The machinery for binding action to outcome, estimating causal provenance, retaining confidence and routing learning is early infrastructure. Rich social interpretations are later structures that may be built from it. Current repository history makes the distinction concrete: the `z_world` causal-footprint comparator, SD-031, was moved from a version 4 placeholder into version 3 because self-caused versus environment-caused attribution is testable and necessary in the solitary environment. The genuinely self-versus-OTHER comparator remains in the version 5 multi-agent ecology. Deferral may therefore reflect a real dependency on a later environment, but thematic similarity to a later capacity is not sufficient reason to defer a mechanism needed by the version 3 organism.

## From controllability to internally constructed social models

The initially described “types of people” appear to arise from several intersecting latent dimensions rather than a small number of pre-labelled classes. Possible dimensions include:

- whether an actor can control a harmful outcome;
- whether harm predicts acquisition of something the actor wants;
- whether harm follows the actor having been harmed;
- whether behaviour changes strategically with observation, resistance or anticipated belief;
- whether the actor differentiates deliberate, accidental and unavoidable harm;
- the actor's prior probability for assuming harmful intent in others;
- the actor's willingness to harm under uncertainty;
- calibration between its confidence about intent and the evidence available;
- whether it revises an attribution when later evidence conflicts with it.

Callous, retaliatory, indiscriminately threat-responsive and discriminating prosocial patterns could appear as regions in this learned multidimensional space. They should not be supplied as labels. REE would need to discover whatever regularities actually improve prediction.

This extends the earlier proposal that agency itself may be inferred when a persistent hidden-state or goal-like model predicts an entity better than passive-world alternatives. Controllability attribution supplies part of the bridge from “this event was not caused by me” to “this persistent entity exerted control over it.” Repeated evidence may then support richer questions: Was the outcome goal-congruent for the entity? Did it adapt when blocked? Did it act differently when REE knew or believed something? Was harm instrumental, defensive, retaliatory, careless or incidental?

The resulting social model is therefore constructed through repeated causal prediction and intervention, not through a designed taxonomy.

## Active causal disambiguation

The 2025 social controllability study by Spiering and colleagues adds another necessary operation. In ambiguous joint tasks, people sometimes acted in ways that helped separate their own causal contribution from another person's contribution. These actions were useful because normal successful performance could leave causal ownership unresolved.

This supports a second candidate mechanism:

### Candidate mechanism — Active Causal Disambiguation

When competing causal models remain consequentially ambiguous, REE may select a bounded, reversible intervention whose possible outcomes differ maximally across those models. The goal of the action is not merely immediate benefit. It is to acquire discriminating causal evidence.

This may include waiting, withholding an expected contribution, approaching, withdrawing, signalling, changing access to a resource, repeating an action under altered conditions, or observing whether another entity adapts to REE's changed behaviour.

The mechanism needs strong limits. Disambiguation has costs, may expose vulnerable entities to risk, and may become coercive if unconstrained. It should be recruited only when the uncertainty matters, passive evidence is inadequate, the intervention is proportionate and reversible, and the expected information can change future action.

Active disambiguation also gives a more precise role to post-commitment epistemic humility. REE can commit to a safe-enough action while retaining a distribution over interpretations, then use the consequences as evidence. The action is not proof that the interpretation motivating it was correct.

## Threat, suspicion and the preservation of alternatives

A threat-sensitive system changes the cost of delay and error. Some hypotheses predict harm severe enough that they deserve attention before becoming the most probable explanation. This is rational if kept separate from causal certainty.

Several distinct quantities may otherwise be collapsed incorrectly:

- probability that harm will occur;
- severity and reversibility of that harm;
- probability that a particular actor controls the outcome;
- probability that the actor intends the harm;
- confidence in the available model;
- urgency of protective action;
- degree of responsibility that can be assigned.

Sensitisation may increase sampling of threat-consistent evidence, increase the precision given to harm channels, lower the threshold for interruption, or strengthen durable learning from adverse events. Any of these could make threatening interpretations easier to recruit in future. But none should directly set the attribution of malicious intent.

A candidate invariant follows:

> **Threat salience may alter attention, urgency, exploration and protective commitment, but it must not directly write causal ownership, harmful intent or responsibility.**

This is important both for safety and for learning. If threat writes responsibility directly, a protective action can manufacture apparently confirmatory evidence: the other responds defensively to REE's defence, and the response is then interpreted as proof of the original hostile model. Active disambiguation must therefore distinguish interventions that genuinely separate hypotheses from interventions likely to provoke the behaviour they claim to test.

## Serotonin may name a family of related control problems, not one scalar function

The current REE serotonin hypothesis assigns a global, slow-moving role to serotonin-like modulation: controlling the coexistence or collapse of competing perceptual, narrative and identity-level interpretations. This remains useful, especially for epistemic humility after action commitment.

However, the new controllability work makes a simple one-to-one mapping increasingly doubtful. The dorsal raphe signal described by Liu and colleagues concerns changes in estimated controllability and its influence on learning. That is not identical to globally permitting alternative interpretations to coexist. Meanwhile, biological evidence indicates that the serotonin system is anatomically, molecularly and functionally heterogeneous:

- dorsal and median raphe nuclei form partly overlapping but distinct ascending systems;
- rostral and caudal raphe groupings have different projection territories;
- even within the dorsal raphe, cortical- and subcortical-projecting serotonin populations occupy different distributions and have different co-transmitter profiles;
- orbitofrontal-cortex-projecting and central-amygdala-projecting dorsal raphe subsystems can show opposing responses to aversive events and different behavioural effects;
- projection target, receptor distribution, co-transmission, timescale and local circuit state can all change the functional meaning of “more serotonin.”

Daniel's observation suggests a useful computational reframing. Many situations impose the apparently similar load of keeping alternatives available:

- competing perceptual parses;
- competing explanations of an event;
- competing causal sources;
- competing models of another's intention;
- competing narratives about the self;
- competing future trajectories;
- maintaining a committed action while keeping its explanatory basis revisable.

These may consume similar representational capacity while requiring very different control policies and having very different consequences. Perceptual ambiguity may need rapid local resolution. A threat hypothesis may need to remain available without dominating responsibility. Identity-level reinterpretation may require slow protected revision. Social causal ambiguity may require active intervention. Controllability change may need to alter credit assignment rather than hypothesis count.

Therefore a single global serotonin-like parameter may be too coarse. A future literature and claim review should investigate whether REE needs **pathway-indexed representational coexistence and collapse control**: related operations sharing a computational family resemblance but differing by representational depth, stream, target model, timescale, projection and downstream learning effect.

This does not mean that REE should reproduce every raphe nucleus or serotonin receptor. Nor does it establish that every biological serotonergic subsystem controls alternative representations. It means the biological heterogeneity is a warning against treating the neurotransmitter name as a unitary computational variable. The useful abstraction may be a family of projection- and target-specific control signals, some serotonin-like, rather than a scalar “serotonin level.”

The dorsal raphe controllability-change signal should consequently remain distinct from the existing REE serotonin collapse parameter unless a later synthesis demonstrates that they are levels of the same operation.

## Early substrate versus later construction

The overall chain should not be assigned wholesale to a late REE version. It contains components of different developmental depth.

### Version-allocation governance

The current repository partition, established through the June 2026 closure-map work and subsequently used in phase adjudication, is:

- **Version 3 — first functional solitary organism.** Anything required for the minimally viable autonomous organism belongs in version 3, including claims initially assigned later if experiments or dependency analysis show that version 3 cannot become a coherent organism without them. Version 3 is an assembly sequence, not a frozen substrate whose scope can never be corrected.
- **Version 4 — individual mind.** Richer self-model, object/action structure, integration and other individual-mind capacities that are not required to close the first solitary organism. Some version 4 work may be developed in parallel, but thematic association with the self-model does not prevent a minimal necessary slice being pulled into version 3.
- **Version 5 — social mind without language as a prerequisite.** Multi-agent ecology, self-versus-other attribution, mirror modelling, empathy and relational/ethical interaction. Capacities may use non-linguistic signals, but do not depend on a linguistic mind.
- **Version 6 — linguistic mind.** Language emergence, grammar-primitive mining, language-affect coupling, abstract relational reasoning and language-mediated trust, deception or institutions. A mechanism with a language-facing extension is not automatically version 6 if a non-linguistic form is already necessary for version 3, version 4 or version 5.

Earlier conversational sketches were not always this neat: one June formulation placed richer social capacities in version 4 and communication in version 5. The later three-tier closure-map partition is the current planning record and should govern this thought unless Daniel revises it explicitly.

The operative test is therefore:

> **What is the earliest organism that cannot be functionally completed without this capacity?**

That determines the earliest required version. Later versions may deepen, specialise or place the same computational family into a new environment.

### Foundational or earlier-version capacities

- temporal binding of action, confidence, prediction and outcome;
- action-conditional forward models;
- reafference and self/world causal attribution;
- preservation of unexplained residual error;
- context-indexed estimates of causal control;
- attribution-directed routing of prediction error;
- separation of action commitment from belief certainty;
- retention of competing causal hypotheses;
- bounded information seeking when causal uncertainty blocks learning.

These functions are useful in a non-social world and are prerequisites for learning selfhood, causal efficacy and responsibility.

### Later emergent structures built from them

- persistent other-entity models;
- actor-indexed control estimates;
- hidden-state and goal inference;
- instrumental versus accidental harm inference;
- learned models of how particular others attribute intent;
- recursive models of what another believes about REE;
- stable but revisable social character representations;
- differentiated empathy, defence, trust and responsibility.

The correct developmental claim may therefore be:

> **Social actor models are late constructions over earlier causal-attribution and controllability-learning machinery. The social categories must emerge; the machinery that makes their emergence possible cannot all be deferred until the social stage.**

This revises an overly simple reading of “self first, social later.” Social environments may be introduced later for interpretability, while the causal primitives required to survive and learn in them should already have been developed and tested in non-social environments.

Applied to the present thought, the likely allocation is not one version-wide bundle:

- action/outcome binding, self-versus-environment attribution, confidence traces, controllability estimation and appropriate credit routing are candidate version 3 requirements if the first solitary organism cannot learn coherent causal efficacy without them;
- richer stateful self-model consequences that are not necessary for solitary functional closure remain version 4;
- actor-indexed control, self-versus-other attribution, internally constructed social character models and social active disambiguation require version 5's multi-agent ecology;
- linguistic descriptions of motives, explicit communicative repair, language-mediated deception and institutional reasoning belong in version 6 unless some pre-linguistic substrate is independently necessary earlier.

## Possible claim and mechanism candidates

These names are provisional and should undergo conflict and redundancy review:

1. **Confidence-Conditioned Controllability Attribution** — commitment-time confidence and expected outcomes inform later causal attribution and contextual control estimates.
2. **Attribution-Directed Prediction-Error Routing** — error updates the self, other, interaction or world model in proportion to the inferred causal provenance rather than being broadcast indiscriminately.
3. **Active Causal Disambiguation** — bounded interventions are selected when their outcomes can separate consequential causal hypotheses.
4. **Pathway-Indexed Representational Coexistence and Collapse** — related control operations regulate alternative representations at different depths, streams, timescales and downstream targets rather than through one undifferentiated global scalar.
5. **Threat–Responsibility Separation** — threat can alter attention, urgency and protective action but cannot directly establish causal ownership, intention or responsibility.
6. **Post-Commitment Interpretative Non-Closure** — committing to action does not require irreversible collapse of the causal interpretation that supported it.

These may prove to be separate mechanisms, levels of one architecture, or specialisations of existing registered claims. The raw thought does not decide that question.

## Experimental implications

### Early non-social controllability task

Create contexts in which the same action-outcome sequence is sometimes reliable and sometimes decoupled by external dynamics. Require REE to learn the context-specific degree of control. Record commitment-time prediction and confidence. Test whether unexpected failure after high confidence is routed toward the world model more than unexpected failure after low confidence.

### Credit-routing lesion series

Ablate separately:

- the confidence trace;
- the contextual controllability estimate;
- the causal attribution comparator;
- the prediction-error router.

Equivalent average prediction error with different patterns of inappropriate self-learning, world-learning or failure to learn would help localise the mechanism.

### Commitment without interpretative closure

Require urgent protective action under unresolved threat. Test whether REE can commit while retaining multiple causal hypotheses and later reverse its attribution when the evidence changes.

### Threat-induced attribution bias

Hold causal evidence constant while varying predicted harm severity. Threat should alter protective urgency without directly changing the posterior probability that a particular actor intended the harm. If inferred responsibility rises merely because the possible harm is worse, the separation has failed.

### Active disambiguation

Provide situations where ordinary goal-directed action cannot distinguish self, other and world causation but a reversible probe can. Test whether probing appears selectively when causal uncertainty is high and decision-relevant, and whether it reduces uncertainty without being rewarded as an end in itself.

### Global versus indexed collapse control

Compare a single global coexistence/collapse parameter with stream-, depth- or target-indexed modulation. Look for cases in which the global controller produces inappropriate coupling: perceptual ambiguity causing identity instability, threat uncertainty causing planning paralysis, or social ambiguity preventing necessary sensorimotor commitment.

## Boundaries and cautions

- The Liu study used a non-social perceptual-feedback task. It supports controllability learning and confidence-conditioned attribution, not inference of malicious intent or social character.
- The Spiering study supports active disambiguation in an experimentally constrained social setting. It does not establish a complete developmental theory of social cognition.
- Functional magnetic resonance imaging provides network-level association, while the transcranial magnetic stimulation result supports a causal contribution from the stimulated prefrontal region; neither supplies a complete synaptic implementation.
- Biological dorsal raphe and serotonin heterogeneity constrain the plausibility of a unitary model but do not dictate REE's modules.
- “Maintaining alternatives” may describe several computationally different operations that only appear similar at a verbal level. Their state variables, update rules and falsifiers must be separated before promotion.
- The person-type grid is a hypothesis about latent structure that REE might discover. It should not become a target taxonomy that the system is rewarded for reproducing.

## Core synthesis

> **A viable organism always acts under uncertainty. Its existing orienting, curiosity, information-hunger and closure machinery must balance the expected value of further information against threat, opportunity cost, goal-acquisition windows and the fact that the world continues during deliberation. The additional problem developed here is what happens to causal interpretation and learning after commitment under that unavoidable uncertainty. Safety requires machinery that preserves competing explanations, records the confidence and expectations present at commitment, learns how controllable each outcome stream is, attributes subsequent evidence to candidate causal sources, and routes learning accordingly. These causal primitives are early developmental machinery. Repeated social interaction can later build actor-, goal- and intention-sensitive models from them without supplying person-types in advance. Active causal disambiguation provides a way to obtain evidence when ordinary interaction leaves causal ownership unresolved, but it remains one candidate action competing with acting now. Serotonin-related biology suggests that the control of alternative representations may itself be a heterogeneous family of pathway- and target-specific operations: similar in computational burden, but different in purpose, timescale and downstream effect. Threat may demand rapid action, but it must not write responsibility; commitment is unavoidable, but it must not end interpretation.**

Version allocation should follow that derivation rather than its apparent sophistication: any portion without which the first solitary organism cannot become functionally coherent is version 3; individual-mind enrichment is version 4; irreducibly multi-agent but pre-linguistic construction is version 5; and irreducibly linguistic construction is version 6.
