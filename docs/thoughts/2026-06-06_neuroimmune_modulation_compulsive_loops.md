# THOUGHT INTAKE: Neuroimmune modulation as slow control-plane influence on compulsive loops

## 0. Summary claim

A saved REE email pointed to a Neuroscience News item titled "Brain Immune Cells Drive Compulsive Behavior". The direct `share.google` link was not accessible during intake, and exact-title searches did not locate the article. Therefore this note preserves the possible REE-relevant architectural idea as source-check pending, not as verified evidence.

The useful REE idea is:

> compulsive behaviour may be shaped by slow background modulatory systems, including immune/glial state, rather than only by fast cognitive beliefs, rewards, or action-selection loops.

For REE, this suggests that compulsive failure modes may require modelling both fast loop dynamics and slower field-state modulators that alter gain, threshold, salience, irritation, threat persistence, or decommit friction.

---

## 1. Why this belongs in REE_assembly

REE already treats behaviour as emerging from a control plane over latent fields, salience, residue, goal persistence, uncertainty, and commitment gating.

A neuroimmune/glial framing matters because it pushes against a purely symbolic or purely reward-based model of compulsion. Some compulsive patterns may not arise because the goal is valued or the belief is endorsed. They may arise because the field is biased into a state where certain loops become sticky, urgent, or hard to decommit.

This may become relevant to:

- compulsive loop architecture
- decommit friction
- threat/residue persistence
- salience gain
- irritability / internal state pressure
- slow background modulation
- psychiatric failure-axis taxonomy

## 2. Proposed classification

Likely classifications:

## 4. REE-specific hypothesis

REE may need a distinction between:

1. **Fast loop mechanics** — the immediate action, relief, prediction, and decommit cycle.
2. **Belief or threat content** — the represented reason the system gives for the action.
3. **Slow field-state modulation** — background parameters that make loops more sticky or harder to interrupt.

Possible computational primitive:

```text
compulsion_risk = f(loop_reinforcement, threat_salience, residue_persistence, decommit_friction, slow_modulatory_state)
```

The important implication is that compulsive action should not be modelled only as a rational choice, reward-maximising habit, or false belief. It may be a multi-layer state where the substrate makes certain loops difficult to release.

---

## 5. Relevance to AI cognitive failure taxonomy

This may be relevant to `Latent-Fields/ai-cognitive-failure-taxonomy`, especially around commitment dysregulation, feedback entrapment, precision misallocation, and future compulsive-loop entries.

Potential taxonomy-level addition:

> Some AI failure modes may have analogues of slow background modulation: persistent changes in context window, memory weighting, tool-retry pressure, system prompts, unresolved alerts, or resource constraints that make certain loops more likely even when explicit reasoning appears intact.

This should be handled carefully because AI systems do not have immune cells. The relevant analogue is not biology but slow substrate-state modulation.

---

## 6. Important cautions

Do not cite this as evidence until the article and primary source are located.

Do not claim that immune cells directly explain all compulsive behaviour.

Do not reduce obsessive-compulsive disorder or compulsive behaviour to inflammation.

Do not imply that AI systems have immune systems or biological drives.

Do not make this a REE-v3 implementation target without source verification and a specific existing gap.

The useful extraction is:

> slow non-symbolic modulators may alter the likelihood and persistence of compulsive loops.
