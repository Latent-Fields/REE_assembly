# Language, Institutions, and Collective Agents

In collective systems, language is the principal medium through which ethical residue is
*absorbed* (institutional protection of individuals) or *denied* (systemic harm), and it is
what makes *diffusion* of residue durable and transferable across members and over time.
Diffusion itself does not require language: mere co-presence of another potential agent,
with no utterance, attenuates sense of agency and outcome monitoring (Beyer et al. 2017).

Institutions may:
- absorb residue (protecting individuals),
- diffuse residue (losing accountability),
- or deny residue (systemic harm).

Per-agent residue attenuation does not determine the collective's harm response: a
collective's response is a disjunction over its members, and more bystanders can raise the
chance that someone intervenes (Philpot et al. 2020).

REE predicts institutional ethical failure arises when symbolic language severs residue's
PROSPECTIVE GATING of policy from embodied harm signals -- not only when it attenuates
residue itself. Two configurations must be distinguished: (a) attenuation/decoupling, in
which residue is absent or weakened; (b) re-signing, in which harm stays coupled to affect
but is framed as virtuous by an identified-with goal, so the agent presents as untroubled
and willing (Haslam et al. 2015). A failure detector keyed only on absent or attenuated
residue would score (b) as healthy. Moral disengagement lowers anticipated guilt but raises
post-misconduct guilt and shame (Ogunfowora et al. 2022): residue-write and
residue-to-policy gating are separable, and any eventual substrate must keep them separate.
