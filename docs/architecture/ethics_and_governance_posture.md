# Ethics and Governance Posture

REE is built so that ethical constraints live inside how the agent thinks, rather
than being bolted on afterward. This page is a plain-language summary of the
project's public ethics posture. It is a non-operational summary, not a safety
guarantee.

## What REE-v3 is, and is not

The current substrate (REE-v3) is a research apparatus. It is **not** claimed to be
sentient, conscious, or a moral patient. It does contain welfare-relevant building
blocks -- harm signals, residue, replay -- but as a pre-ethical substrate, not a
mind that is owed care. This boundary is treated as a *maintained, audited claim*,
re-evaluated at every generation boundary, never as a settled permanent fact.

## Ethics derived, not declared

REE's ethical commitments are derived from its axioms rather than simply asserted.
See [How Established Ethical Systems Derive from REE](established_ethical_systems.html)
for the full account -- how autonomy, justice, rights, care, precaution, and research
and professional ethics arise from selfhood, vulnerability, uncertainty, other-mind
recognition, and repair.

## Capability release requires care release

Higher-capability work is gated on its welfare, continuity, consent, security, and
external-review governance travelling *with* it. Public theory and governance do not
imply a public turnkey capability.

## Not a clinical tool

REE is not a clinical, diagnostic, risk-prediction, or patient-facing system.
Clinical relevance is not clinical readiness, and no patient-identifiable data is
used in public materials.

## Externally legible, not a private language

REE's internal ethics are kept answerable to outside scrutiny -- mapped to
established ethical traditions, and, for higher-capability work, to external legal
and standards frameworks -- so the project cannot mark its own homework.

*These are candidate governance commitments that bind progressively as the
architecture grows. This page is a summary, not the operational detail.*
