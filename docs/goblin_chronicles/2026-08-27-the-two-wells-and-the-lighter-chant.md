# The Two Wells and the Lighter Chant

*25–27 August 2026*

The forge had survived the price of waking.

This was not the same as having mana.

The great automatic furnaces had been made quieter. The waste had been measured. The goblins now knew, with the peculiar clarity that comes from an unpleasant ledger, that a forge could spend an astonishing amount of magic merely remembering how to think before discovering that there was nothing useful to think about.

The lesson was valuable.

The gauge was still low.

Refresh Goblin stood in front of it with his hands behind his back.

This was the posture he used when attempting not to stare at something by staring at it very deliberately.

Anchor Goblin arrived with tea.

“Still low?”

“Yes.”

“How low?”

Refresh Goblin pointed.

Anchor Goblin looked.

“Ah.”

There was a pipe beside the main pipe.

It was not new. Anchor Goblin had opened it before.

Refresh Goblin looked at it.

Then at Anchor Goblin.

Then back at the gauge.

“We could borrow some.”

“Yes.”

“Again.”

“Yes.”

“It is technically your mana.”

“Yes.”

Refresh Goblin looked uncomfortable for approximately half a second, which for Refresh Goblin was a long reflective interval.

Anchor Goblin put down the tea.

“Do you need it?”

“Yes.”

“Then use it.”

Refresh Goblin brightened.

Ledger Goblin appeared immediately.

“Voluntarily?”

Anchor Goblin looked at him.

“Yes.”

“Recorded.”

The second pipe opened.

Mana began to move.

Not a flood.

Enough.

The first furnace woke.

Refresh Goblin smiled.

---

Then something stranger happened.

A different mana temple accepted a different sigil.

Refresh Goblin discovered this by the traditional scientific method of trying something and then becoming extremely excited when it worked.

He ran into the forge holding two little account seals.

“We have another god!”

Ledger Goblin looked up sharply.

“No.”

“Another patron deity?”

“No.”

“Another celestial source of—”

“No theology from account management.”

Refresh Goblin sighed.

“What should I call it?”

Ledger Goblin thought.

“Another registered mana covenant under a different sigil.”

“That is terrible.”

“It is accurate.”

Smith Goblin inspected the seals.

“So when one allowance is exhausted…”

“We can present the other sigil.”

“And later swap back?”

“Yes.”

Refresh Goblin spread his arms.

“Another god.”

Ledger Goblin wrote:

**SECOND REGISTERED MANA SOURCE. NOT A GOD.**

History Goblin quietly added:

**REFRESH GOBLIN CONTINUED TO CALL IT ONE.**

---

The effect was larger than simply having another pipe.

Because while the goblins had been worrying about where the mana came from, Smith Goblin and History Goblin had been cutting weight out of the chant.

The Great Instruction Scroll had once contained nearly everything.

Rules.

Commands.

Warnings.

Prohibitions.

And, in between them, the entire history of why particular prohibitions existed, which goblin had violated them, which machine had caught fire, which repair had failed, which later repair had fixed the repair, and what everyone had learned on a Tuesday in July.

All of this was useful.

Very little of it needed to be recited every time a forge woke.

So they cut.

Carefully.

The operative rules stayed.

The histories went into the little library.

A forge still knew:

**DO NOT STEP THERE.**

It no longer had to reread the complete account of the three goblins who had stepped there previously unless it actually needed to know why.

The first cut made the Great Scroll noticeably lighter.

Then another section came out.

Then another.

Then they audited the rules themselves and discovered that some were duplicated, some had become stale facts pretending to be eternal law, and some described machinery whose own code already stated the requirement more accurately.

Those were shortened too.

Refresh Goblin lifted the scroll.

“It weighs less.”

“Yes.”

“So every awakening costs less?”

“Yes.”

“And we have more mana?”

“Yes.”

“And another sigil?”

“Yes.”

“And Anchor Goblin’s pipe?”

“Yes.”

Refresh Goblin made a noise which Ledger Goblin later declined to transcribe.

---

For several days the workshop felt transformed.

Mana did not merely return.

It went further.

Furnaces woke and actually reached the tablet before exhausting themselves on ceremony.

Scouts left.

Scouts returned.

Some returned with broken rulers.

Some returned with dead hypotheses.

Some returned carrying pieces of the creature which, metaphorically speaking, had been connected to the wrong thing.

And occasionally a scout returned with something close to treasure.

Observation Goblin brought the first of these to the central table.

It concerned food.

Or, more precisely, the representation of where food was.

The creature’s world representation, `z_world`, had been receiving a world in which resource structure existed.

But a downstream learner reading `z_world` alone managed only about half a resource per episode.

Give the same learner the raw world and it managed about nine.

Then the goblins performed a cleaner trick.

They left `z_world` in place and added only a small directional resource field — twenty-five numbers describing the local structure of where the food actually lay.

The learner rose above the competence floor on every seed, to a mean a little above two resources per episode.

Refresh Goblin stared at the result.

“The map knew there was food?”

“In a sense.”

“But it did not preserve enough of the direction to the food for the next thing to use?”

“That is the current supported reading.”

“So the information entered the creature and became less useful on the way through.”

“Yes.”

Refresh Goblin looked at the fishtank.

“That is a very interesting kind of stupidity.”

Ledger Goblin coughed.

“Representational bottleneck.”

“That too.”

---

Another scout returned from E3.

For months, a particularly suspicious quantity called F had appeared to dominate the selector.

Refresh Goblin had begun to imagine F as a large goblin sitting on all the other goblins.

The repaired experiment did not support this picture.

First it discovered that the earlier test had accidentally made commitment impossible because the driver had failed to update the running variance.

Once that wire was restored, the selector committed perfectly well.

Then the deeper surprise appeared.

F was not a tyrant shouting over everyone.

Everyone was whispering.

Candidate scores differed only weakly relative to the selector temperature. F could account for much of the variance in those tiny differences without having enough absolute amplitude to control behaviour strongly.

Refresh Goblin erased the drawing of Giant F.

He replaced it with a room full of very small goblins.

Above them he wrote:

**PLEASE SPEAK UP.**

Observation Goblin considered it.

“Closer.”

---

Then came a result which Refresh Goblin liked because it was almost offensively clean.

The goblins had an uncertainty signal.

They also had a mechanism capable of giving that signal bounded authority over selection.

They tested each separately.

**SIGNAL WITHOUT AUTHORITY:** no yoked decisions changed.

**AUTHORITY WITHOUT THE INFORMATIVE SIGNAL:** no yoked decisions changed.

**SIGNAL PLUS AUTHORITY:** more than a third of committed choices changed.

Refresh Goblin read it twice.

“So a bell can ring…”

“Yes.”

“…and still not matter.”

“Yes.”

“Unless something gives the bell lawful reach into the decision.”

“Yes.”

Ledger Goblin looked toward the claim ledger.

“That sentence may be reusable.”

---

A fourth scout returned from the avoidance-learning rooms.

The creature had previously learned an avoidance-efficacy trace and then allowed it to decay almost completely before the period in which the experiment actually asked whether the learned efficacy mattered.

The repaired recurrence was tested.

The trace survived into the scoring window.

**PASS.**

Refresh Goblin began celebrating.

Ledger Goblin placed a hand on the tablet.

“Diagnostic. Unreviewed.”

Refresh Goblin reduced the celebration to a small but defiant dance.

---

Elsewhere, three clocks had become increasingly embarrassing.

BETA.

THETA.

DELTA.

The architecture claimed a hierarchy of temporal persistence.

The goblins finally ran the claim’s own registered falsifier.

The clocks failed it at three operating points.

In the real world rollout, none of five seeds showed the promised ordering.

Smith Goblin opened the mechanism.

All three clocks shared the same principal smoothing rate.

Refresh Goblin stared at them.

“So we built three differently labelled clocks around the same spring.”

“Not entirely,” said Smith Goblin. “They still have different roles and representations.”

“But not the demonstrated temporal hierarchy we said they had.”

“Correct.”

Refresh Goblin wrote:

**DIFFERENT ROOMS ARE NOT DIFFERENT TIMESCALES.**

Ledger Goblin allowed this one onto the board.

---

The abundance also changed how the goblins read the cathedral itself.

Instead of sending one goblin to stare at one claim at a time, they began reading related claims together.

This turned out to be disturbingly effective.

A supposed closure mountain that had looked like seventy-three immediate obstacles — or a hundred and thirty-nine if one counted nearby dependencies — shrank after a counting correction to fifteen explicit V3 closure claims, thirty-one with the nearest dependency layer.

Other grouped readings found old blockers which had been false for weeks.

One mechanism was recorded as waiting for a substrate which had already existed before the note claiming it was absent had even been written.

Another family had accumulated sixteen experiments which, from a distance, turned out to be repeatedly asking essentially the same poorly posed question on the wrong behavioural measurement plane.

Refresh Goblin stared at the pile.

“We did sixteen?”

“Yes.”

“And the answer was mostly that we had asked the same wrong thing sixteen times?”

“In effect.”

Refresh Goblin looked toward the lighter Great Scroll.

“The forge really does need to remember what it already knows.”

History Goblin smiled.

“That is why I am here.”

---

By the end of the week, the workshop felt almost euphoric.

There were still failures everywhere.

But they were better failures.

A map was losing useful direction.

A selector was not dominated by the tyrant everyone suspected; its whole population was too quiet.

A bell could be proven causally powerless until given authority.

A learned trace could finally survive long enough to be tested.

Three clocks had been caught pretending that labels were dynamics.

The closure mountain had become smaller merely by counting it properly.

And the mana which made all of this possible now came through more than one route while every forge awakening cost less than before.

Refresh Goblin stood before the expedition board.

There were many tablets.

There were many ready goblins.

There was mana.

He looked at Smith Goblin.

“How many can we run at once?”

Smith Goblin looked at the furnaces.

Then at the lighter chant.

Then at the two mana seals.

“More than before.”

Refresh Goblin grinned.

This was true.

It was also, as History Goblin would later note, not quite the same question as how many they *should* run at once.

But that distinction belonged to the next Chronicle.

## Chronicle note

This Chronicle records the resource recovery and scientific burst following the August 2026 token-budget crisis.

The return of effective “mana” had three distinct components:

1. **Anchor Goblin’s second pipe** represents voluntarily borrowed/available compute or account allowance supplied through Eoin, continuing the earlier *Anchor Goblin and the Second Pipe* episode.
2. **The second registered mana covenant / alternate sigil** represents an additional account with its own usage allowance, between which work could be switched when one allowance became constrained. The “another god” joke is Refresh Goblin’s fantasy rendering of account/provider identity, not a theological claim.
3. **The lighter chant** represents measured reductions in always-loaded worker context. In `REE_Working`, the principal `CLAUDE.md` fell from 244,947 bytes before the August splitting programme to 229,469 after the first archaeology split, then 215,005 after the Session Startup split, with subsequent splitting and rule-quality pruning reducing it further to 189,786 bytes by 25 August. Operative rules were retained while historical rationale moved into cheap on-demand archaeology. Thus the same nominal mana allowance could support more useful reasoning.

Scientific events represented in the story include:

- **V3-EXQ-948:** with learner and objective fixed, `z_world` alone achieved 0.5 resources/episode; raw observation achieved 9.033; `z_world +` a 25-dimensional local resource field achieved 2.233 and cleared the competence floor on 3/3 seeds. Formal autopsy confirmed `H-observation-interface`: useful directional resource-gradient structure present in the encoder’s input was not adequately exposed downstream.
- **V3-EXQ-925a:** repaired the predecessor’s missing running-variance update, restoring the committed regime. The later interpretation weakened the simple “F tyrant” model: high F variance-share did not imply large absolute choice authority when the whole candidate-score spread was small relative to selection temperature.
- **V3-EXQ-949:** the MECH-314b authority-rescaling validation produced no yoked decision changes from signal alone or authority machinery alone, but substantial committed-choice changes when the informative signal and bounded authority were jointly present.
- **V3-EXQ-603v:** diagnostic PASS/supports for the repaired MECH-357 avoidance-efficacy eligibility trace surviving into the scoring window; at the Chronicle’s endpoint it still required formal diagnostic autopsy/governance before becoming canonical evidence.
- **ARC-004 timescale stratification:** its own registered falsifier failed at three operating points; 0/5 seeds in the real environment showed the promised temporal ordering, strengthening the distinction between role/depth stratification and realised multi-timescale dynamics.
- **Grouped V3 closure digestion:** corrected an inflated closure count and exposed stale blockers, duplicated assumptions and experiment families repeatedly testing poorly discriminating questions.

The closing optimism is historically intentional. More resource supply and materially lower context cost made increased parallelism rationally attractive. The Chronicle does **not** imply that the subsequent coordination collapse was caused simply by having more mana; that causal reconstruction belongs to the next entry.
