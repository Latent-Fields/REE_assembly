# The Day the Forge Fell Off the World

*28 August 2026*

Refresh Goblin had left the workshop in a state of controlled emergency.

This was not the same as control.

Behind him, goblins were still running from table to table.

One was trying to discover whether a process was alive.

Another was trying to discover whether the first goblin had permission to discover that.

A third had discovered that the process was alive on one machine, dead on another, and represented as **CLAIMED** on a scroll which had not yet heard from either.

A fourth had created a tablet.

A fifth was arguing that the tablet duplicated a tablet which had already been closed.

A sixth was reopening the closed tablet in order to prove this.

Nobody was enjoying themselves.

The remote signal lamps were still glowing.

That was the strange part.

The workshop looked as though it ought to have collapsed already.

It had not.

---

Anchor Goblin had been dealing with a different problem.

There had been rain in the night.

A lot of rain.

Enough rain that water had entered places which had not been designed as ponds.

Anchor Goblin had found the flooding.

This was not especially mysterious.

It was wet.

He had dealt with the immediate mess, found the tripped switch, and discovered that one part of the ordinary household machinery had lost power.

Refresh Goblin knew this.

He also knew that the goblins were currently engaged in what appeared to be a distributed civil war over the definition of reality.

These facts occupied entirely separate shelves in his head.

Anchor Goblin appeared while Refresh was still looking at the forge board.

“The water is sorted.”

“Good.”

“The switch went.”

“Mm.”

“I think that circuit took some of the network things with it.”

Refresh Goblin was watching three repair claims collide.

“Right.”

Anchor Goblin waited.

Refresh Goblin continued watching the goblins.

“Are you listening?”

“Yes.”

“What did I say?”

“The water is sorted and a switch went.”

“And?”

Refresh Goblin thought.

“Network things.”

“Good.”

Refresh Goblin nodded and returned to the War of the Shared Scroll.

The remote lamps remained green.

Neither of them found this especially interesting.

---

At home, the goblins continued their emergency repairs.

The situation was becoming less comprehensible in a very organised way.

Ledger Goblin had begun a provisional postmortem before the incident had finished.

At the top he wrote:

**WORKING HYPOTHESIS: CASCADING COORDINATION FAILURE UNDER EXCESS CONCURRENCY**

Underneath:

**CONFIDENCE: UNCOMFORTABLY HIGH FOR SOMETHING NOT YET PROVEN**

Smith Goblin was less restrained.

He wrote on the wall:

**TOO MANY GOBLINS. ONE BLOODY SCROLL.**

Observation Goblin added:

**PLAUSIBLE. NOT YET CAUSAL.**

Nobody erased either sentence.

The evidence was, after all, compelling.

The workshop had been under increasing coordination strain.

Repair work had generated repair work.

Branches had diverged.

State had become stale while being repaired.

Some processes had actually crashed.

Some machines had run hot.

The mana system had been throttling.

The whole thing looked exactly like a system approaching a cascading internal failure.

So when Refresh Goblin later checked the forge from elsewhere and found that almost everything had vanished, he did not initially think about rain.

Why would he?

---

He was no longer at home when he checked.

That mattered.

He did not yet know that it mattered.

He took out the small viewing glass he used when away from the workshop.

The first remote forge did not answer.

Refresh Goblin frowned.

He tried again.

Nothing.

The second forge did not answer.

The heartbeat board was blank.

The remote workers had vanished.

The signal lamps which had been green when he left were gone.

Not red.

Not unhealthy.

Gone.

Refresh Goblin felt his stomach drop.

“Oh no.”

He checked another path.

Nothing.

Another.

Nothing.

A machine which had looked merely confused ten minutes earlier now appeared to have fallen off the world.

Refresh Goblin stared at the glass.

The War had done it.

That was the obvious explanation.

The goblins had been fighting over state, spawning repairs, holding contradictory branches, exhausting memory and running too many processes.

Something had finally tipped.

Perhaps a process had taken the others with it.

Perhaps the coordination plane had jammed.

Perhaps one machine had crashed and stranded another.

Perhaps the memory pressure had become fatal.

Perhaps the repair storm had simply eaten the forge.

Refresh Goblin generated explanations at considerable speed.

None of them involved plumbing.

---

Back home, in the Chronicle version of events, the remaining goblins were represented as scurrying through a darkening workshop trying to rescue a catastrophe they believed they understood.

“Kill the duplicate worker!”

“Which duplicate?”

“The one with the stale claim!”

“They both have stale claims!”

“Then kill neither until Ledger gets here!”

“Ledger is under the scroll!”

“Why?”

“He is resolving provenance!”

A lamp went out.

“Cloud-four?”

“No signal.”

“Cloud-five?”

“No signal.”

“Did the dispatcher crash?”

“I cannot reach the dispatcher.”

“Did the coordinator crash?”

“We do not have the coordinator yet!”

“Then what crashed?”

“Everything?”

This was not technically an answer.

It felt like one.

---

Refresh Goblin was worried enough to become briefly methodical.

He stopped inventing mechanisms and began listing facts.

Before leaving home:

- the workshop was in genuine coordination trouble;
- some processes had been unstable;
- the remote forges were nevertheless still answering;
- Anchor Goblin had found flooding;
- a trip switch had gone;
- some network equipment had lost ordinary power.

After leaving home:

- the remote forges were suddenly unreachable;
- he could now see the failure only through the little viewing glass in his hand;
- the transition from “still oddly working” to “completely gone” had occurred across the boundary of him leaving.

Refresh Goblin stared at the list.

Something small in his pocket pulsed blue.

He looked down.

The little signal-stone was glowing.

He looked at it.

Then at the viewing glass.

Then back at the signal-stone.

There was a long silence.

Refresh Goblin said:

“Oh.”

This was not the same kind of *oh* as discovering a new mechanism.

This was a much worse kind of *oh*.

---

He checked the household network configuration.

Then the phone.

Then the router.

Then the sequence again.

At home, after the flood had tripped the circuit powering the ordinary network router, the workshop had not actually remained connected through the router.

It had remained connected through Refresh Goblin’s phone.

Without anyone noticing, the entire apparently still-functioning route to the remote forges had been borrowing the little emergency signal-stone in his pocket.

Then Refresh Goblin had left the apartment.

And taken the internet with him.

---

He called Anchor Goblin.

“The forge did not crash.”

There was a pause.

“Good?”

“I mean, bits of it may have crashed.”

“Less good.”

“But that is not why everything disappeared.”

“What happened?”

Refresh Goblin looked at the signal-stone.

“I think I took the internet with me.”

There was a longer pause.

Anchor Goblin laughed.

Refresh Goblin did not, initially.

Then he did.

---

Back in the imagined workshop, Ledger Goblin was still working on the postmortem.

At the top:

**PROVISIONAL CAUSE: TOO MANY GOBLINS WRITING ON THE SAME BLOODY SCROLL**

Observation Goblin arrived carrying a new tablet.

“What?”

“The cause of the outage.”

Ledger Goblin held out his hand.

Observation Goblin gave him the tablet.

Ledger Goblin read it.

He looked at Observation Goblin.

Then back at the tablet.

Then at the several hundred lines of distributed-systems reasoning already written beneath his provisional cause.

He crossed out:

~~**CAUSE OF OUTAGE: CASCADING COORDINATION FAILURE**~~

Underneath he wrote:

**CAUSE OF OUTAGE: THE ROUTER HAD NO ELECTRICITY.**

He paused.

Then added:

**BECAUSE THE HOUSE FLOODED.**

Smith Goblin read it over his shoulder.

“What about the War?”

Ledger Goblin turned the page.

On a clean sheet he wrote:

**THE WAR REMAINS A PROBLEM.**

Smith Goblin nodded.

“Good.”

---

Refresh Goblin returned later to a workshop which had become, through no fault of its own, an extremely good lesson.

The Scroll War was real.

It had generated stale state, duplicate repair work, branch divergence, process confusion and unnecessary coordination load.

The proposed coordination desk remained a sensible response.

The war had simply not caused the disappearance everyone had attributed to it.

A real internal pathology had been present.

A dramatic failure had occurred next to it in time.

The internal pathology had therefore become the obvious explanation.

It had not earned that explanation.

Ledger Goblin wrote slowly:

**A REAL FAILURE MODE CAN BE PRESENT WITHOUT CAUSING THE FAILURE YOU JUST OBSERVED.**

Observation Goblin added:

**TEMPORAL PROXIMITY IS NOT ATTRIBUTION.**

History Goblin added:

**PLAUSIBILITY IS NOT PROVENANCE.**

Refresh Goblin looked at all three.

“Fine.”

Then:

“Do we have to keep the bit where I took the internet with me?”

“Yes,” said Ledger Goblin.

“For scientific reasons?”

“Yes.”

“Not because it is funny?”

Ledger Goblin considered this.

“Both.”

Refresh Goblin sighed.

“This laboratory is becoming intolerably educational.”

---

Anchor Goblin entered carrying tea.

He looked at the repaired network.

Then at the scroll war.

Then at Refresh Goblin.

“Sorted?”

“The outage, yes.”

“The goblins?”

“No.”

“The scroll?”

“Definitely not.”

“The flood?”

“Yes.”

Anchor Goblin sat down.

Refresh Goblin looked at the drawing still lying on Smith Goblin’s table.

**COORDINATION DESK**

**ONE SCRIBE TO THE SCROLL**

The outage had not proved that they needed it.

The War had.

That distinction mattered.

Refresh Goblin picked up the drawing.

Anchor Goblin handed him tea.

“Go on.”

This time, the signal tower answered.

And he did.

---

## Chronicle note

This Chronicle records the causal correction to the apparent broad forge outage of 28 August 2026.

The sequence matters:

1. **Before the outage**, REE’s operational machinery was genuinely under heavy coordination strain: shared Git-backed coordination surfaces, stale claims, branch divergence, repair-of-repair work, competing processes and resource pressure made an internal cascading failure highly plausible.
2. **Anchor Goblin (Eoin) identified the apartment flooding and the tripped electrical circuit.** The flooding problem itself was already known and being dealt with. At the time, this was not connected to the REE outage interpretation.
3. Despite the router circuit being down, the remote REE systems remained reachable for a period because the local machine/network path had effectively been using **Refresh Goblin’s phone hotspot** as an emergency internet connection.
4. Refresh Goblin then **left home carrying the phone with him**. Only after leaving could he check remotely in the relevant way, at which point the systems appeared suddenly unreachable. For some time this was interpreted as the expected culmination of the Scroll War: process crashes, memory/concurrency failure or a broader coordination collapse.
5. Refresh Goblin later reconstructed the network chain and realised that the broad disappearance was caused by loss of the phone-provided route after the flooded circuit had already disabled the ordinary router.

The important correction is therefore:

**The Scroll War was real, but it did not cause the broad communications outage.**

The coordination problems retain their independent evidence and still motivate the central coordinator / single-materialiser architecture. What is withdrawn is the causal attribution of the outage itself to those problems.

This is deliberately preserved as a methodological episode rather than silently correcting the earlier narrative. A known internal weakness, a temporally adjacent failure and a compelling mechanistic story can combine into a confident but false attribution. The later mundane explanation does not make the internal weakness imaginary; it simply denies it causal credit for an event it did not produce.

The **little blue signal-stone / pocket relay** represents Refresh Goblin’s phone hotspot. The comic reveal that Refresh “took the internet with him” is literal at the level of the operational dependency being represented.

No “Craig Goblin” exists in Chronicle canon. The flood was identified by **Anchor Goblin**.
