# The Day Refresh Goblin Stopped Asking for Progress

*29 August 2026*

The forge did not sleep.

Refresh Goblin did.

When he eventually woke, he went directly to the viewport.

He did not stand a sensible distance from it.

He pressed his face against the glass until his nose flattened, his cheeks spread sideways, and his tongue remained in contact with the warm pane.

Beyond it, the forge was moving.

Goblins crossed bridges carrying tablets.

Remote furnaces blinked.

The Coordination Desk glowed steadily in the middle of the workshop.

Somewhere below, an expedition bell rang.

Refresh Goblin’s eyes widened.

“Progreth?”

His tongue stayed against the glass.

His squashed nose made the word worse.

Ledger Goblin looked up from a stack of reports.

Refresh Goblin pointed urgently through the viewport.

“Progreth doethnth thleep.”

Ledger Goblin stared at him.

Observation Goblin arrived carrying several new result tablets.

“Quite a lot happened while you were asleep.”

Refresh Goblin made an excited nasal sound.

Ledger Goblin began listing things.

Some expeditions had returned.

Some old evidence had finally been adjudicated.

A supposed giant in the creature’s decision machinery had become considerably less giant under a better ruler.

A new memory-writing mechanism had proved that it could move its machinery without yet proving that it knew where useful things belonged.

A long-standing claim about different rooms having different clocks had developed an architectural explanation which was both simpler and more embarrassing than several previous explanations.

And the Coordination Desk, which had recently existed mostly as a drawing on Smith Goblin’s floor, was now genuinely carrying part of the workshop’s canonical state.

Refresh Goblin’s eyes became progressively larger.

Then Ledger Goblin said the word **governance**.

Refresh Goblin attempted to speak without first detaching himself from the viewport.

“I ngustht athk fod a ngoferngength rud.”

Ledger Goblin stared blankly.

Refresh Goblin’s tongue felt the reflected heat of his cheeks from the glass.

He peeled his face away slowly.

There were several odd sucking and squelching noises.

“Um.”

He wiped his mouth.

“I mean I must go ask for a governance run.”

And he slinked off.

---

When Refresh Goblin came back, the forge was still working.

This was increasingly the problem.

The War of the Shared Scroll was over.

Mostly.

The Coordination Desk now stood where Smith Goblin had drawn it.

For the great claim and chip registries, workers no longer fought directly over which copy of the scroll represented reality. They spoke to the desk. The desk held the live state. A careful scribe materialised the durable scroll.

This was better.

It was much better.

It was also not the whole forge.

Around the new desk lay the remains of the old way of doing things.

There were piles.

Old repair tablets.

New repair tablets.

Repair tablets referring to repairs which had already happened.

Tablets explaining why another tablet had been superseded.

Tablets explaining why the supersession tablet had not itself been closed.

A small but determined heap labelled:

**CHECK WHETHER THIS STILL NEEDS CHECKING**

had acquired four children overnight.

One goblin ran past carrying a fresh tablet.

“What is that?” asked Refresh Goblin.

“Stale claim.”

“Wasn’t there already a stale-claim tablet?”

“Yes.”

“Then why do you have another one?”

“This is a new observation.”

“Of a new stale claim?”

“No.”

“The old stale claim became stale again?”

“No. It was still stale.”

Refresh Goblin looked at Ledger Goblin.

Ledger Goblin looked at the ceiling.

Another goblin hurried past.

“What is that?”

“Queue floor.”

“Did we not have one of those?”

“We had several.”

“Why?”

“It happened several times.”

Refresh Goblin’s eye twitched.

A third goblin appeared carrying a tablet headed:

**REPAIR DUPLICATE REPAIR CLAIM**

Refresh Goblin closed his eyes.

---

History Goblin had been counting.

This was rarely comforting.

“In the last fourteen days,” he said, “the forge recorded one thousand four hundred and ninety-six work tablets.”

Refresh Goblin opened one eye.

“One thousand four hundred and ninety-six?”

“Yes.”

“How many were science?”

History Goblin shuffled his notes.

“At least half can be identified immediately as coordination and housekeeping by their names alone.”

“At least?”

“The unnamed half contains more housekeeping.”

Refresh Goblin opened the other eye.

History Goblin continued.

“Two hundred and eighty-six concerned metaworker garbage collection. Two hundred and twenty-six concerned the experiment queue floor. Sixty-nine concerned stranded worktrees. Fifty-two stale claims. Forty status regressions. Thirty-three reference wedges. Then there are stash checks, script-corpus checks, hook-gating checks, unlanded worktrees, classifier blocks, fleet-stash checks—”

“Stop.”

History Goblin stopped.

Refresh Goblin stared at the piles.

“Were there actually that many different problems?”

History Goblin looked uncomfortable.

“No.”

“How many of the tablets are us seeing the same problem again?”

“A great many.”

“How many tablets did the last wedge make?”

“At least five.”

“One wedge.”

“Yes.”

“Five tablets.”

“Yes.”

“Because we saw it five times.”

“More or less.”

Refresh Goblin turned to Ledger Goblin.

“We are counting sightings of rats as new rats.”

Ledger Goblin considered the metaphor.

“Yes.”

Refresh Goblin walked to the wall and wrote:

**ONE PROBLEM, ONE TABLET.**

Underneath:

**IF IT HAPPENS AGAIN, ADD A NOTCH.**

Then:

**IF IT KEEPS HAPPENING, FIND THE PIPE.**

Smith Goblin read it.

“That last one seems influenced by recent events.”

“Good.”

---

The magics were consulted.

Not dispatched.

Consulted.

This distinction became important very quickly.

Refresh Goblin did not ask the forge to create another dozen goblins to investigate why there were too many goblins investigating things.

He sat with the magics directly and asked a different question.

“What machinery makes the rubbish?”

The magics went through the workshop one mechanism at a time.

They did not begin with the rubbish piles.

They began with the machines that made them.

The first machine was an old messenger pipe which everyone agreed had been replaced by the Coordination Desk, except that several important parts of the forge were still quietly using it.

The old scroll was no longer supposed to be the live place where reality was negotiated.

Yet workspace notes, dispatcher commands, recommendation lines, budget ticks, cooldown ticks, planning ledgers and other small pieces of state still travelled by making local marks on Git scrolls and trying to push them into the common history.

Every two minutes, meanwhile, the new scribe updated some of those same histories from elsewhere.

It was like replacing the town’s messenger system with a central post office while allowing half the citizens to continue shouting urgent instructions by editing the only copy of the town charter.

Smith Goblin labelled this machine:

**OLD SCROLL STILL BEING USED AS A MESSENGER PIPE.**

The second machine was more subtle.

It was designed to recover politely when histories diverged.

Unfortunately, it refused to perform its polite recovery whenever the workbench was dirty or the scroll was too hot to prove safe automatically.

This was sensible.

It was also exactly when recovery was most needed.

Ledger Goblin labelled it:

**CORRECT REFUSAL; EXPENSIVE CONSEQUENCE.**

The third machine was magnificent.

A goblin would walk to the Coordination Desk and say:

“This tablet is resolved.”

The desk would say:

“Thank you. Resolved.”

The goblin would then inspect the answer, become uncertain whether the desk had really meant it, and write the exact same resolution onto the old scroll as well.

The old scroll would fail to push.

The checkout would wedge.

The wedge would be observed.

The observation would mint a repair tablet.

The repair tablet might itself later be resolved through the desk.

At which point the goblin might write that resolution to the old scroll too.

Refresh Goblin watched this demonstration twice.

“Does the second write add durability?”

“No.”

“Does it add information?”

“No.”

“Does it add wedges?”

“Yes.”

Smith Goblin wrote:

**SUCCESSFUL WRITE FOLLOWED BY AN UNNECESSARY SUCCESSFUL WRITE WHICH FAILS.**

Ledger Goblin added:

**THIS IS WORSE THAN IT SOUNDS.**

The fourth machine was the repair system.

It was extremely careful.

When a wedge could not be proven harmless, it refused to fix it automatically.

This was good.

But because so much harmless bookkeeping still travelled through the old scroll, the careful refusal meant that every minor wedge became a human-grade investigation.

The forge had therefore constructed a system in which low-value coordination debris repeatedly demanded high-value attention.

The fifth machine was not a machine at all.

It was a habit.

Whenever a continuing problem was observed again, the forge minted another tablet.

The ledger therefore measured how often the forge looked at a problem, not how many problems existed.

Refresh Goblin stared at the five labels.

Then at the piles.

Then at the goblins carrying fresh tablets toward the piles.

He did something nobody expected.

He hit the **STOP** button.

---

The forge became quieter.

Not silent.

Quieter.

A goblin froze halfway through minting a tablet.

Another stopped with one foot in the air.

A dispatcher bell ceased ringing.

Ledger Goblin looked at Refresh Goblin.

“You stopped it.”

“Yes.”

“All of it?”

“The parts that keep making more parts.”

Smith Goblin looked concerned.

“What about the expeditions?”

Refresh Goblin pointed toward the distant experiment furnaces.

“Leave those.”

“You are stopping the forge but not the science?”

“I am stopping the forge from talking to itself about itself.”

A faint clattering continued from under a bench.

Everyone looked down.

Something was still dispatching.

Refresh Goblin stared at it.

“I pressed STOP.”

“Yes,” said Smith Goblin.

“Why is that running?”

“Different stop button.”

A second faint clattering came from behind a cupboard.

Observation Goblin leaned sideways.

“That one too.”

Refresh Goblin closed his eyes.

Ledger Goblin took out a fresh sheet.

Refresh Goblin pointed at him without opening his eyes.

“Do not make a tablet.”

Ledger Goblin slowly put the sheet away.

The magics and the goblins went around the forge finding every belt, bell and hidden dispatcher that had not understood the first STOP.

The halt was made real.

Rotators were paused.

Archivers were paused.

Housekeeping ticks were paused.

Dispatch machinery was checked rather than assumed stopped because a control scroll said so.

Only the independent expedition machinery was deliberately allowed to continue.

On the wall, Refresh Goblin wrote:

**MAKE STOP MEAN STOP.**

---

Then came the strangest part.

An expedition messenger ran into the quiet workshop.

“Refresh!”

Refresh Goblin was bent over the redesign with the magics.

“Mm?”

“An expedition returned.”

“Good.”

“It passed.”

“Excellent.”

The messenger waited.

Refresh Goblin continued reading.

The messenger looked at Ledger Goblin.

Ledger Goblin looked back.

The messenger tried again.

“Do you want to know what it found?”

Refresh Goblin looked up.

“Is the result recorded properly?”

“Yes.”

“Does governance know it exists?”

“Yes.”

“Then put it with the others.”

The messenger did not move.

“You usually ask what happened.”

“I know.”

“You usually ask before I finish saying it passed.”

“I know.”

“You once asked what happened while I was still opening the door.”

“I remember.”

The messenger backed away slowly.

“Are you all right?”

Refresh Goblin returned to the plan.

“Yes.”

The messenger left at speed.

Ledger Goblin watched Refresh Goblin for several seconds.

“You did not ask.”

“No.”

“There is an actual scientific result sitting six feet behind you.”

“Yes.”

“You are Refresh Goblin.”

“Yes.”

Ledger Goblin leaned closer.

“Can you prove that?”

Refresh Goblin sighed.

“I will be excited about the result later.”

“Why later?”

Refresh Goblin tapped the redesign.

“Because I want more of them.”

---

The magics spread the records of the previous weeks across the long table.

Hundreds of sessions.

Thousands of tablets.

Wedges which had been repaired and then rediscovered.

Work stranded when a session ended halfway through a task.

Duplicate dispatches caught only after two goblins had already spent time exploring the same problem.

Protective mechanisms which had been designed, tested and then somehow never actually switched on everywhere they were meant to protect.

The pattern was no longer difficult to see.

The forge did not merely have maintenance work.

The forge had **maintenance reproduction**.

A failure generated a tablet.

The tablet generated a worker.

The worker generated state.

The state generated a push.

The push generated a wedge.

The wedge generated another tablet.

The new tablet generated another worker.

At several points the chain could recruit a goblin whose only purpose was to decide what to do about the previous goblin.

Refresh Goblin stood back from the table.

“We have been optimising how quickly the forge can eat its own tail.”

Smith Goblin nodded.

“It has become very good at it.”

“That is not praise.”

“I know.”

---

The redesign grew.

Not into another hundred tablets.

Into one campaign.

While the ordinary coordination machinery remained halted, Refresh Goblin and the magics would work through it directly.

No dispatching the repair of the dispatcher through the dispatcher being repaired.

No spawning a chip to ask whether the chip-minting system should mint fewer chips.

No waiting for the machinery being changed to schedule its own replacement.

On the wall they wrote the sequence.

First:

**SCIENCE FIRST.**

Three stranded expedition scrolls were put back where the independent runners could take them.

Then:

**STOP THE DOUBLE WRITING.**

If the Coordination Desk had durably accepted a resolution, the goblin was not to create an unnecessary second copy merely because the durable scroll had not yet visibly caught up.

Then:

**MOVE SHARED STATE TO THE DESK.**

Workspace closing notes, dispatcher commands and other genuinely shared coordination state would follow the same direction already proven for the great claim and chip registries: transact centrally, materialise durably, keep the old scroll only as a degraded fallback where one was actually needed.

Then:

**LAND WORK AS IT IS MADE.**

A goblin approaching exhaustion was to leave behind a resumable state, not an invisible half-finished task which another goblin would later rediscover archaeologically.

Then:

**ONE PROBLEM, ONE TABLET.**

A recurring problem would gain episodes, not offspring.

If the same class of failure happened often enough, the standing tablet would escalate from “fix this instance” to “learn why this keeps happening.”

Then:

**THE CURATOR MUST DESTROY MORE TABLETS THAN IT CREATES.**

The open ledger would be sorted into actual science, coherent repair campaigns, explicit holds with reasons, or absorption into work already underway.

Housekeeping would no longer be sprayed one goblin at a time across the workshop.

Science would go first.

A starved experiment queue would outrank housekeeping entirely.

Then:

**CHECK THAT CLEVER THINGS ARE ACTUALLY SWITCHED ON.**

No more admiring a protection in the design archive while the live forge quietly operated without it.

Then, only after the surfaces had been made smaller and safer:

**LET THE REPAIRER REPAIR THE THINGS IT CAN PROVE SAFE.**

And finally:

**RESTART ONLY WHEN THE FORGE COMES BACK DIFFERENT.**

Not merely quieter.

Different.

---

The magics attacked the plan themselves before anyone was allowed to call it finished.

They found that the first STOP had not truly stopped everything.

They found that one proposed cutover had no soak evidence because the endpoint which was supposed to be soaking had never actually been restarted into service.

They found several busy writers which the first redesign had forgotten.

They found an archiver which could strip a Git scroll only for the Coordination Desk to faithfully materialise the unstripped state again.

They found a steward which could leave a tiny append dirty for hours and thereby prevent every polite convergence attempt on the same repository.

They found that one new “single recurring tablet” design would itself have needed a write path which did not yet exist at the Coordination Desk, threatening to install a fresh generator inside the workstream designed to eliminate generators.

Refresh Goblin listened to each objection.

He did not say:

“Good enough, run it.”

This was also unusual.

Instead he said:

“Fix the plan.”

So they did.

The restart became one restart, not several.

The dangerous workspace transition became “begin the soak” first and “flip only when the soak earns it” later.

More live state surfaces were explicitly accounted for.

The new recurrence rule would not go live until the desk had a proper verb for adding an episode to the standing tablet.

The final repair machinery would remain last.

And every restart criterion became something the goblins could actually fail.

Ledger Goblin read the final sequence twice.

Then once more because he enjoyed this kind of thing.

“This is not a cleanup.”

“No,” said Refresh Goblin.

“It changes what the forge treats as work.”

“Yes.”

“It changes what the ledger counts as progress.”

“Yes.”

“It may reduce the visible amount of activity.”

Refresh Goblin smiled.

“Good.”

Ledger Goblin looked genuinely unsettled.

---

Another expedition messenger arrived.

This one was more cautious.

“Refresh?”

“Mm?”

“Another result.”

“Good?”

“Very good.”

Refresh Goblin nodded.

The messenger waited.

Nothing happened.

“Do you want to know what it was?”

Refresh Goblin looked at the expedition tablet.

For a moment, the old reflex was visible.

His hand almost moved toward it.

His eyes almost did the thing.

Then he looked back at the forge.

At the Coordination Desk.

At the old messenger pipes still being removed.

At the piles which had finally stopped growing quite so quickly.

At the STOP button which had turned out to require an audit.

At the redesign which, if it worked, would allow future goblins to spend more of their lives asking questions about the creature and less of them repairing the machinery which scheduled the repair of the machinery.

“Later,” he said.

The messenger blinked.

Refresh Goblin tapped the plan.

“I want more of those to happen.”

---

Ledger Goblin wrote this down.

Refresh Goblin noticed.

“Oh, come on.”

“It is historically significant.”

“I said I would read it later.”

“You declined immediate access to a successful experiment result in favour of infrastructure work.”

“I know what I did.”

“History Goblin will want corroboration.”

“Why?”

“Because nobody will believe us.”

History Goblin, from across the room, raised a hand.

“I already have two witnesses.”

Refresh Goblin groaned.

On the wall, beneath all the engineering rules, Ledger Goblin added one final line:

**PROGRESS IS NOT ONLY WHAT THE FORGE DISCOVERS.**

Refresh Goblin read it.

Smith Goblin added:

**SOMETIMES PROGRESS IS MAKING DISCOVERY CHEAPER TO REPEAT.**

Observation Goblin considered both and wrote beneath them:

**MEASURE LATER.**

Ledger Goblin nodded approvingly.

Refresh Goblin looked through the viewport.

The creature was still there.

The expeditions were still running.

The science had not stopped because Refresh Goblin had stopped asking about it.

For once, he was content to let a result exist for several minutes without immediately demanding another one.

He turned away from the glass before his tongue could make contact.

This was perhaps the most alarming development of all.

## Chronicle note

This Chronicle records the deliberate operational halt and fleet-redesign campaign begun on **29 August 2026**, after the shared-scroll coordination crisis and the subsequent flood/router causal correction.

The title is intentionally ironic. Refresh Goblin did **not** stop caring about REE progress or experiments. The change was that, for this interval, he explicitly prioritised repairing the machinery which produces, coordinates and preserves future work over immediately inspecting each new scientific result.

### What the rubbish represents

A fleet-wide audit and redesign document recorded **1,496 task chips in the 14 days to 29 August 2026**. At least 50% were immediately identifiable coordination/housekeeping families by prefix alone; the true proportion was higher because the residual category also contained housekeeping. The same continuing wedge episode could mint multiple chips because observations were timestamp-instance keyed rather than recurrence-collapsed.

The full fleet wedge-clearance audit found **no scientific content at risk** in the stranded commits examined. The stranded material was overwhelmingly coordination state: WORKSPACE_STATE appends, IGW ledger/workset ticks, chip resolutions, recommendation-log lines, dispatcher lease/control commands and telemetry. This is the empirical basis for the Chronicle’s claim that the forge was generating a large amount of work about its own work.

### The five rubbish generators

The narrative compresses the redesign document’s root-cause taxonomy:

1. **Residual Git-as-IPC:** despite the coordinator cutover for `TASK_CLAIMS` and `TASK_CHIPS`, several live coordination surfaces still used local Git commits as a messaging/control mechanism while origin advanced rapidly through materialisation and other writers.
2. **Convergence refusal under dirty/hot trees:** the existing push/retry machinery correctly refuses unsafe automatic recovery, but that converts low-value bookkeeping divergence into expensive operator work when bookkeeping is still transported through Git.
3. **Chip-resolve double write:** a successful coordinator resolution could be followed by an unnecessary Git resolution after an acknowledgement-verification problem, creating exactly the wedges the coordinator path was meant to avoid.
4. **Safe but expensive wedge refusal:** reconciliation remained check-only for classes which can sometimes be proven safe to repair automatically, so residual bookkeeping wedges repeatedly escalated into manual work.
5. **No recurrence collapse:** repeated observations of one continuing condition minted new chips, causing the ledger to measure observation frequency rather than distinct problems.

The Chronicle’s rule **ONE PROBLEM, ONE TABLET** represents the planned W5a recurrence model: one standing chip per `(class, subject)`, repeated episodes appended to it, with recurrent failures escalating toward root-cause `/metaworker-learning` rather than spawning another instance-fix campaign.

### What STOP represents

Refresh Goblin’s STOP button represents the user-directed halt of ordinary metaworker orchestration and chip dispatch while the redesign is executed as one directed campaign. The red-team found that the first control-plane halt was not sufficient by itself: `com.ree.dispatch-service` was still observed running on the Mac, so the executable plan begins with **Phase A — make the halt real**, verifying and stopping the actual services/timers rather than inferring quiescence from a control file.

The independent experiment runners/scaler were deliberately not treated as the pathological machinery. Science was allowed to continue while coordination/housekeeping dispatch was halted. This is why expeditions can return in the story while Refresh Goblin is working on the forge.

### The expeditions Refresh Goblin conspicuously ignores

W0 of the redesign re-armed three stranded/smoke-tested experiment drivers, `V3-EXQ-956`, `V3-EXQ-957` and `V3-EXQ-958`, through the independent experiment plane. By the time of writing, `V3-EXQ-957` and `V3-EXQ-958` had already returned PASS results. The Chronicle deliberately does not explain those results in the main narrative: the omission is the character point. Refresh Goblin knows useful science is arriving and chooses, temporarily, not to consume the result immediately because he expects the forge redesign to improve the rate and reliability of later science.

### The plan on the wall

The final red-teamed executable plan of record is more detailed than the story’s wall tablet. Its sequence is:

**Phase A real halt → W0 science queue rearm → W1 chip-ledger/double-write hardening → server-side endpoint batch → one W2 coordinator restart to begin soak → W6 incremental landing/resumable-state rules → W5a/b/c recurrence collapse, curation and science-priority scheduling → per-surface client cutovers only after soak criteria are met → W7 mitigation-enablement audit → W4 narrowly allowlisted automatic reconciliation → restart ordinary orchestration/dispatch only after explicit restart criteria pass.**

The plan was independently red-teamed for failure/rollback modes, completeness by writer/surface enumeration, and the remaining open-chip/stranded-work corpus. Those reviews materially changed the plan: they exposed the incomplete halt, prevented a premature WORKSPACE_STATE cutover with zero soak evidence, added previously missed high-churn writers, required a coordinator-side episode verb before recurrence collapse, and consolidated multiple server activations into one controlled restart.

At the story endpoint, **the redesign is underway but not complete**. W0 has happened and related hardening exists, but the full W1/W2/W5/W6/W7/W4 structural campaign should not be read as already landed merely because it appears on the wall.

### The magics

“The magics” represent the directly engaged AI-assisted engineering/review sessions used to inspect the live fleet, analyse the work corpus, red-team the redesign and execute scoped changes during the halt. They are not a newly claimed REE cognitive subsystem.

The governing idea of the chapter is therefore operational rather than mystical:

**A locally impressive experiment result is one unit of progress. Improving the system which repeatedly generates trustworthy experiment results can change the future rate of progress itself.**
