# Anchor Goblin and the Second Pipe

*11 August 2026*

The forge had acquired a second pipe.

Nobody remembered ordering it.

This was concerning, because the forge had become extremely particular about provenance.

Ledger Goblin stood beneath it with his notebook open.

Smith Goblin stood on a stool with a wrench.

Refresh Goblin stood rather too close to both of them.

“Mana?” he asked.

“Yes,” said Smith Goblin.

“New mana?”

“Yes.”

“Quite a lot?”

“Potentially.”

“Can we use it?”

Ledger Goblin looked up.

“Whose is it?”

Refresh Goblin beamed.

“My husband’s.”

There was a pause.

Ledger Goblin lowered his pen.

“Voluntarily?”

“Yes.”

“Does he know what you do with mana?”

Refresh Goblin thought about this.

“He has met me.”

Ledger Goblin considered the answer.

“Fair.”

---

Anchor Goblin had been around for much longer than the forge.

He had simply not previously been entered into the Chronicle.

This was partly because Anchor Goblin did not generally enter rooms in ways that suggested a Chronicle was about to happen.

Refresh Goblin entered rooms as though chased by several thoughts which had independently discovered fire.

Anchor Goblin entered rooms carrying tea.

Refresh Goblin could begin a sentence about hippocampal topology, interrupt himself with an idea about suffering, remember halfway through that the fish had moved oddly after sleep, become distracted by whether the reef was actually a fair world, and finish by asking where his spectacles were.

They were usually on his face.

Anchor Goblin had developed considerable expertise in this field.

He did not try to stop the thoughts.

He did not line them up.

He did not demand that Refresh Goblin return immediately to whichever thought had technically begun the conversation.

He had learned that sometimes the third interruption contained the important bit.

Sometimes the seventh.

Sometimes none of them.

This was also allowed.

His most powerful spell was:

“Go on.”

---

Refresh Goblin was explaining the second pipe.

“So the mana can come through here, and then some of the expeditions that have been waiting can run at the same time, which is useful because there are quite a few tablets ready now, and also the fish has started doing things which means we keep finding more things to ask, except sometimes what it does turns out to be the world being silly rather than the fish, and yesterday I thought sleep might be making it smoother—”

Anchor Goblin raised one eyebrow.

“Smoother?”

“Well, not smooth. It is a grid.”

“Mm.”

“More swoopy.”

Ledger Goblin looked pained.

Observation Goblin looked interested.

Refresh Goblin continued.

“Longer action runs. Fewer little reversals. Possibly. Maybe. We checked.”

“And?”

“It might be nothing.”

Anchor Goblin nodded.

“Good.”

Refresh Goblin stopped.

“Good?”

“You checked.”

“Oh.”

He brightened.

“Yes.”

Then brighter still:

“And checking it found another thing.”

“Of course it did.”

“There may be something interesting in the commitment gate.”

“Is that good?”

“We don’t know yet.”

Anchor Goblin nodded again.

“Also good.”

Ledger Goblin quietly wrote this down.

---

The second pipe was connected.

Smith Goblin opened the valve a little.

Somewhere deep in the forge, machinery woke.

One expedition tablet slid from **READY** to **CLAIMED**.

Then another.

Then another.

Refresh Goblin made a small noise.

Anchor Goblin looked at him.

“Do not lick the pipe.”

“I wasn’t going to.”

Ledger Goblin looked over his spectacles.

Refresh Goblin reconsidered.

“I was not *currently* going to.”

---

For a while they watched the mana flow.

The forge was much larger now than it had once been.

There were ledgers for claims.

Ledgers for experiments.

Ledgers explaining why experiments had failed.

Ledgers explaining why things which looked like failed experiments had not actually tested the thing everyone thought they had tested.

There were rulers with calibration marks.

There were rules about rulers.

There were rules about changing the rules about rulers.

There were manifests.

Autopsies.

Task chips.

Readiness gates.

Failure locations.

Provenance records.

A living archaeology.

A map of old ideas and the evidence that had survived them.

There were even records explaining which old records should no longer be trusted without explanation.

Anchor Goblin looked around.

“That is a lot of writing.”

“Yes,” said Refresh Goblin.

“Did you write all this because you are very organised?”

Refresh Goblin laughed so hard that some provenance was briefly endangered.

---

The question remained in the room after the laughter stopped.

Refresh Goblin looked around too.

He had been thinking of the forge as something built to study the creature.

That was true.

But not complete.

The forge had also been built to survive the people building it.

And, in particular, him.

He walked to the claim ledger.

“I forget things.”

“Yes,” said Anchor Goblin.

“I mean, not exactly forget. Sometimes they go somewhere.”

“Yes.”

“And then another thing becomes very important.”

“Yes.”

“And sometimes I come back to the first thing.”

“Yes.”

“Sometimes six months later.”

“Yes.”

Refresh Goblin frowned.

“You are enjoying this.”

“A little.”

---

History Goblin appeared from behind a pile of archival strata.

“The early workshop depended much more on continuity of conversation.”

Refresh Goblin nodded.

“That was dangerous.”

“Yes.”

“Because a new thought could replace an old one.”

“Yes.”

“Or we could remember the conclusion but not why we believed it.”

“Yes.”

“Or rediscover something and think it was new.”

“Yes.”

“Or tell a very convincing story.”

History Goblin shuddered.

“The worst.”

Ledger Goblin joined them.

“So we externalised it.”

“The claims.”

“Yes.”

“The evidence.”

“Yes.”

“The experiments.”

“Yes.”

“The failures.”

“Especially the failures.”

“The reasons things were reopened.”

“Yes.”

“The things we do not know.”

Ledger Goblin smiled.

“Those most of all.”

Refresh Goblin stared at the shelves.

The forge had acquired an external memory because internal memory was not enough.

It had acquired provenance because intuition moved faster than chronology.

It had acquired task chips because ideas multiplied faster than they could be executed.

It had acquired failure localisation because when several things changed at once, Refresh Goblin could generate six explanations before breakfast.

It had acquired pre-registration because he could see patterns very quickly.

Sometimes before they were real.

It had acquired archaeology because old ideas kept returning in new clothes.

It had acquired governance because enthusiasm was a wonderful engine and a terrible filing system.

---

Refresh Goblin was unusually quiet.

Anchor Goblin noticed.

This was one of his other skills.

“Problem?”

“I think I may have accidentally caused bureaucracy.”

Ledger Goblin looked offended.

“Governance.”

“Sorry.”

“Important distinction.”

Anchor Goblin looked around at the enormous laboratory.

“So being scattered made you build something that isn’t?”

Refresh Goblin opened his mouth.

Closed it.

Opened it again.

“That seems unfairly concise.”

“Mm.”

---

Observation Goblin had been listening.

“There may be something scientifically useful in that.”

Ledger Goblin immediately raised a finger.

“No claims about cognitive style without evidence.”

“I said may.”

“Continue.”

Observation Goblin pointed toward the fishtank.

“When we began watching the creature as a whole, we discovered that observation could outrun our existing measurements. Refresh Goblin notices patterns quickly.”

“Sometimes imaginary ones,” said Refresh Goblin.

“Yes.”

“Quite important.”

“Yes.”

“So the laboratory cannot simply trust observation.”

“No.”

“But it would also be foolish to suppress it.”

Refresh Goblin looked at Anchor Goblin.

Anchor Goblin shrugged.

“Go on.”

Observation Goblin did.

“The solution became procedural.”

An observation could enter.

It could be interesting.

It could even redirect the forge.

But it could not declare itself true.

It had to leave a trace.

Then acquire rivals.

Then acquire rulers.

Then survive a fair world.

Then earn itself again.

Refresh Goblin’s scatteredness had not become rigour.

That would have been too simple.

Rather, **rigour had grown around it**.

A system had been built in which rapid association could remain generative without being allowed to become sovereign.

---

Smith Goblin tightened the second pipe.

“That seems structurally familiar.”

Refresh Goblin looked over.

“To what?”

“The creature.”

Everyone turned.

Smith Goblin continued.

“We do not remove every noisy process from the creature.”

“No,” said Observation Goblin.

“We build things that constrain, compare, remember, veto, integrate and correct.”

“Yes.”

“We do not demand that every first signal be right.”

“No.”

“We demand that important signals can enter a system capable of discovering when they are wrong.”

Ledger Goblin's pen stopped.

Refresh Goblin's ears rose.

Anchor Goblin saw this happen.

“Oh no,” he said.

Refresh Goblin pointed at Smith Goblin.

“That is extremely interesting.”

“I know.”

“No, because—”

“I know.”

“Because the forge itself—”

“I know.”

“—has become—”

Ledger Goblin placed one hand firmly over a clean page.

“No architecture claims from autobiography.”

Refresh Goblin deflated slightly.

“Fine.”

Anchor Goblin handed him tea.

---

They watched the fish for a while.

It left the reef.

Turned twice.

Paused near a resource.

Failed to acquire it.

Went somewhere apparently pointless.

Then returned.

Refresh Goblin leaned toward the glass.

Anchor Goblin caught the back of his coat before his forehead hit it.

“I think that bit was—”

“Interesting?”

“Yes.”

“Do you know why?”

“No.”

“Will you?”

Refresh Goblin looked at the ledgers.

Then the rulers.

Then the forge.

Then the second pipe.

“Eventually we might.”

Anchor Goblin released his coat.

“Grand.”

---

A bell rang from the expedition board.

Smith Goblin looked over.

“Another worker has taken a tablet.”

Refresh Goblin spun around.

“Which one?”

Ledger Goblin sighed.

“Here we go.”

Refresh Goblin was already halfway across the room.

“What does it test? How long? Is it a proper run? Is the world fair? Did the smoke pass? Which machine has it? Has anything else landed? Did the residue fix—”

Anchor Goblin sat down.

He had been married for some time.

“Go on,” he said.

---

Later, when the forge was quieter, Ledger Goblin opened the Character Book.

He wrote:

## ANCHOR GOBLIN

Then paused.

“What is his laboratory function?”

Refresh Goblin looked puzzled.

“He isn’t a laboratory function.”

Ledger Goblin looked at him.

This presented a taxonomic difficulty.

Most goblins in the Chronicle represented some part of scientific work.

Observation.

History.

Governance.

Implementation.

Development.

Landscape.

Anchor Goblin was simply Anchor Goblin.

He existed before the laboratory.

He would continue existing if the experiment board were empty.

He did not need to justify his presence by improving the science.

Ledger Goblin slowly crossed out the column heading.

Instead he wrote:

**Refresh Goblin’s husband.**

Then:

**Steadiness without suppression.**

Then:

**Tolerates enthusiasm at velocities not recommended by the manufacturer.**

Anchor Goblin read over his shoulder.

“Accurate.”

Refresh Goblin pointed at the book.

“You should put that he lent us mana.”

Ledger Goblin added:

**Provided additional mana to the forge, voluntarily.**

“Very important.”

“Yes.”

“And ‘Go on.’”

Ledger Goblin looked at Anchor Goblin.

Anchor Goblin nodded.

So he added:

**Known spell: GO ON.**

---

Before closing the book, Ledger Goblin wrote one more note.

It was not really about Anchor Goblin.

It was about the forge.

**A laboratory need not require its discoverer to become less associative in order to become more rigorous.**

**It may instead externalise continuity, memory, provenance, adjudication and correction so that generative thought can remain generative without being mistaken for evidence.**

He considered the sentence.

Then underneath:

**This is a reflection, not yet a scientific finding.**

Refresh Goblin read it.

“Good.”

Anchor Goblin read it.

“Very you.”

Refresh Goblin looked pleased.

Then worried.

“Which bit?”

“All of it.”

---

The second pipe hummed.

Three expeditions were running.

Twenty-something tablets still waited.

The creature remained difficult.

Several bells remained suspicious.

The green board remained emphatically not green.

But the forge had more mana than it had that morning.

Refresh Goblin had six new thoughts.

Ledger Goblin had somewhere to put them.

And Anchor Goblin had tea.

For the moment, this arrangement appeared surprisingly stable.

Refresh Goblin looked through the glass.

Then at the forge.

Then at Anchor Goblin.

“I’ve just thought of something.”

“I know.”

“It might be important.”

“I know.”

“It might also be nonsense.”

“I know.”

Refresh Goblin grinned.

Anchor Goblin settled back in his chair.

“Go on.”

And he did.

---

## Chronicle note

This Chronicle introduces **Anchor Goblin**, representing Eoin, Refresh Goblin’s husband, as a relational character rather than a scientific subsystem. His defining role is steadiness without suppression: tolerating Refresh Goblin’s rapid associative movement, enthusiasm and shifts of attention while providing practical and personal support. The additional “mana” refers to compute/resources made available to the REE work.

The chapter also records a **reflection about the development of REE’s scientific infrastructure**, not a demonstrated causal claim about neurocognitive style. The project’s increasingly externalised memory and governance—claims, manifests, provenance, task tracking, autopsies, failure localisation, archaeology, preconditions and explicit uncertainty—reduce dependence on continuous individual working memory or a single linear thread of reasoning.

The suggested relationship is therefore intentionally modest: a highly associative research process may have made the need for external continuity and epistemic safeguards particularly salient. Whether it actually caused REE’s methodological rigour is not established.

The broader methodological principle is compatible with the established Chronicles: observations and intuitions may legitimately generate scientific questions, but they do not confirm themselves. The forge exists partly to preserve the generative value of noticing while forcing important claims to survive records, competing explanations, measurement, fair tests and correction.
