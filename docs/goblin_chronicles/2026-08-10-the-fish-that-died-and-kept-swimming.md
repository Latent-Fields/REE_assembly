# The Fish That Died and Kept Swimming

*10 August 2026*

The next morning, Refresh Goblin ran into the fishtank room and stopped so suddenly that one of his boots came off.

The fish was still swimming.

He stared through the glass.

It crossed the reef.

It wandered into open water.

It came back.

It did something incomprehensible near a rock.

It kept swimming.

Refresh Goblin slowly raised both hands.

“We did it.”

Ledger Goblin looked up from the overnight records.

“Did what?”

“It lived.”

Ledger Goblin looked at the record.

“Which time?”

Refresh Goblin lowered one hand.

“What do you mean, which time?”

“It died twice in 906b.”

Refresh Goblin looked back at the tank.

The fish was still swimming.

“No it didn’t.”

“Yes it did.”

“It is there.”

“Yes.”

Refresh Goblin turned very slowly.

“It died.”

“Yes.”

“And then it got better?”

Ledger Goblin considered the wording.

“Mechanically, yes.”

Refresh Goblin sat down on the floor.

---

History Goblin was summoned.

This was becoming a habit whenever the laboratory discovered that a familiar word had been doing more work than anyone had asked of it.

Smith Goblin brought the source.

Observation Goblin brought the logs.

Landscape Goblin brought a map, even though nobody had requested one.

Childhood Goblin arrived last, carrying a ruler with **AGE** written along one side.

Refresh Goblin pointed accusingly at the tank.

“They say it died.”

“It did run out of health,” said Observation Goblin.

“And then?”

“The segment ended.”

“And then?”

“The world reset.”

“And then?”

“The body got its health back.”

“And then?”

“The next segment began.”

Refresh Goblin waited.

“And the creature?”

Smith Goblin opened the code.

“That depends what you mean by the creature.”

Ledger Goblin sighed.

“That sentence has caused us trouble before.”

---

They traced the boundary carefully.

At the beginning of the first observed segment, the creature received a proper reset.

A great many temporary states were cleared.

But after that, the later segment boundaries were different.

The environment renewed itself.

Hazards moved.

Resources moved.

The creature appeared somewhere new.

Its bodily health returned to full.

The world’s little clock went back to zero.

But the laboratory did **not** build a new creature.

The learned weights remained.

The residue field remained.

The hippocampal exploration memory continued.

The sleep machinery kept its cadence.

Much of the ongoing cognitive machinery crossed the boundary.

No one had written:

**IF HEALTH DEPLETED: THIS CREATURE IS OVER.**

The same boundary machinery ran whether the segment ended because the fish had run out of health or merely because the goblins had stopped watching after five hundred steps.

Refresh Goblin stared at the source.

“So the body dies.”

Ledger Goblin raised a finger.

“The body-state reaches zero health.”

Refresh Goblin rolled his eyes.

“The body-state reaches zero health.”

“Better.”

“Then the world gives it a new body?”

“Not exactly.”

“A repaired body?”

“Closer.”

“A miraculous health refill and teleport?”

Smith Goblin nodded reluctantly.

“That is mechanically fairly good.”

Refresh Goblin looked delighted.

Ledger Goblin did not.

“Do not call it resurrection in the notes.”

“I wasn’t going to.”

“You were visibly going to.”

---

They went back through the fishtank lineage.

906 had hardly allowed enough time for the question to arise.

Its little observations had been short and repeatedly interrupted.

906a had tried to give the creature continuity, but the invisible fire still killed each segment almost immediately.

Then 906b repaired the invisible injury field.

Suddenly the fish lasted nearly the whole viewing window.

Six of eight segments reached the five-hundred-step cap.

Two ended in health depletion.

906c, using the same ecology but better instrumentation, reached the cap seven times out of eight.

Only one segment ended because health ran out.

Then came 911.

Eight segments.

Eight full five-hundred-step windows.

No health-depletion ending at all.

Refresh Goblin brightened.

“So 911 really did live.”

Observation Goblin shook his head.

“No.”

“No?”

“It survived until the goblins stopped looking.”

“That sounds like living.”

“It is evidence that the five-hundred-step cap became the limiting observation, not evidence about how long it would otherwise survive.”

Refresh Goblin looked betrayed by statistics.

“So when it kept dying, we could not measure a long life because the world killed it.”

“Yes.”

“And now that it does not die, we still cannot measure a long life because we stop the clock.”

“Yes.”

Refresh Goblin put his head in his hands.

“Science is very badly designed for satisfying sentences.”

Ledger Goblin wrote that down, then crossed it out.

---

Childhood Goblin had been quiet.

He was looking at the eight segment markers.

“What were you hoping to learn once it survived?”

Refresh Goblin answered immediately.

“Whether it changes.”

“How?”

“Whether experience makes it better.”

“At what?”

Refresh Goblin opened his mouth.

Closed it.

“Things.”

Ledger Goblin looked pleased that this answer had embarrassed itself without assistance.

Childhood Goblin put his AGE ruler beside the tank.

The ruler did not line up with the segment marks.

“That is the next problem.”

---

The creature had something resembling a continuous history across the eight observed chunks.

But the **world** did not.

At every boundary, hazards were rerolled.

Resources were rerolled.

Position was rerolled.

A bad morning could be followed by a kind afternoon.

A difficult landscape could be followed by one in which food happened to lie near the creature’s path.

And because the fish carried much of its accumulated state across that boundary, the record now mixed two things together:

what the creature had learned,

and what sort of world it happened to wake into next.

Refresh Goblin frowned.

“But we can compare early segments with later ones.”

“We did,” said Observation Goblin.

“And?”

“One long run looked somewhat like improvement.”

Refresh Goblin sat upright.

“Hah!”

“The next long run did not reproduce it.”

Refresh Goblin lay back down.

“Oh.”

The first run had shown patterns consistent with getting better: less harm here, more benefit there, changes in the spread of behaviour.

The second did not show the same story.

Because the landscape was redrawn every five hundred steps, the goblins could not tell whether the first pattern had been development or luck.

Landscape Goblin raised his hand.

“This one is partly mine.”

Childhood Goblin raised his.

“And partly mine.”

Refresh Goblin looked between them.

“So now the world and childhood are arguing over custody?”

“No,” said Ledger Goblin.

“They are identifying a confound.”

“That is a much less interesting sentence.”

“It is much harder to misuse.”

Refresh Goblin sighed.

“That keeps happening.”

---

There was another complication.

Refresh Goblin pointed to the labels on the tank.

“Fine. We will watch it longer. 906b, then 906c, then 911. That is a nice long life.”

History Goblin coughed.

Refresh Goblin froze.

“What now?”

“906b, 906c and 911 are not successive chapters in one creature’s life.”

“They are literally successive chapters in my notebook.”

“Not in the creature.”

Each lettered run trained a fresh agent from the beginning.

No checkpoint carried the creature from 906b into 906c.

No persistent individual crossed into 911.

The lineage belonged to the **experiment**.

The lifetime belonged only to the creature inside each run.

Refresh Goblin stared at the labels.

“So we have been making the world progressively better...”

“Yes.”

“...and each time putting a new inexperienced fish into it.”

“Yes.”

“And then talking about what the lineage learned.”

“The laboratory learned.”

“The creature did not.”

“Correct.”

Refresh Goblin looked through the glass again.

This time he seemed to see not one fish but several overlapping shadows, each beginning where the previous experiment had ended, each scientifically related but not personally continuous in any technical sense.

He took down a sign that said **LIFETIME 906 → 911**.

Ledger Goblin nodded.

---

Sleep produced one more trap.

The goblins had finally made its machinery visible enough to show that sleep was not merely a decorative room in the architecture.

When they forced it to fire repeatedly, the slow-wave writes happened.

The replay draws happened.

The rapid-eye-movement rollouts happened.

The refinement measures stopped returning empty sentinels.

Refresh Goblin had immediately wanted to write:

**SLEEP MAKES THE FISH BETTER.**

Ledger Goblin had confiscated the chalk.

The experiment had shown that the sleep machinery could produce a non-null internal readout.

It had **not** measured behaviour immediately before and after sleep in a way that could establish improvement.

And when the goblins looked retrospectively at the rare natural sleep boundaries already present in the fishtank logs, the apparent post-sleep change could not be distinguished cleanly from ordinary segment-boundary change.

The body refreshed there too.

The world changed there too.

The boundary itself was too busy.

Childhood Goblin tapped the AGE ruler.

“If sleep is part of development, you need a life in which sleep can be separated from waking up in a different universe.”

Landscape Goblin looked offended.

“I make excellent universes.”

“I am asking you to stop replacing them every nap.”

“Ah.”

---

By midday, the goblins had changed almost nothing in the creature.

But they had changed the question.

The old question had been:

**Can the fish remain alive long enough for us to watch it?**

The repaired tank had made that question less urgent.

The new question was harder:

**What would count as one life in which development could actually be measured?**

Not eight unrelated creatures.

Not eight landscapes so different that age and luck became indistinguishable.

Not a body whose health could hit zero and return to full without the analysis deciding what that event meant.

Not a five-hundred-step window so short that every successful survival was right-censored by the goblins’ patience.

Not sleep welded to the same boundary that regenerated the body and redrew the world.

A life needed a clock that belonged to the life.

A developmental history needed continuity strong enough that “later” meant something more than “after the next reset.”

Refresh Goblin picked up Childhood Goblin’s ruler.

It had no episode numbers on it.

Only a long line.

“How long should it be?”

Childhood Goblin shrugged.

“Long enough to answer the question.”

Ledger Goblin smiled.

Refresh Goblin groaned.

“You have all started doing that.”

---

That evening, the fish crossed the reef again.

It moved into open water.

It came back.

No one called the segment a life.

No one called the boundary a death.

No one called the next letter the same creature growing older.

They simply watched.

And on the wall beside the fishtank, Childhood Goblin hung a new sign:

**AGE IS NOT THE NUMBER OF TIMES THE WORLD HAS BEEN RESET.**

Refresh Goblin read it twice.

Then, underneath, in smaller handwriting, he added:

**AND DEATH SHOULD PROBABLY MEAN SOMETHING.**

Ledger Goblin considered removing it.

He left it there.

## Chronicle note

This chronicle records the organism-level continuity and lifespan findings reconstructed on 10 August 2026 from the V3-EXQ-906 → 906a → 906b → 906c → 911 Fishtank lineage. It is an experimental-process allegory. References to “death,” “body,” “life,” “age” and continuity describe the mechanics of the REE-v3 simulation and its observational protocol; they are **not** claims of consciousness, personhood, biological life or subjective continuity.

The central source-level finding is that the 906b-family observational loop does not branch on segment termination cause. `env.reset()` occurs at each segment boundary whether the preceding segment ended through `health_depleted` or the step limit. The environment refresh restores `agent_health` to 1.0 and rerolls the immediate grid state, including agent position and hazard/resource placement. The same `REEAgent` object nevertheless continues across non-initial segment boundaries, with substantial longer-lived state persisting, including learned weights, residue field state, hippocampal/exploration memory and sleep-cadence machinery. A full `agent.reset()` is used at the beginning of the observational run, not after every later segment. Therefore a `health_depleted` ending inside these runs is a recoverable simulated body-state termination followed by continued evaluation of the same persisting agent substrate, not a terminal organism event in the code.

The survivability progression is real but is principally evidence about repaired experimental ecology rather than improved REE competence. V3-EXQ-906a ended all 8 evaluation segments by health depletion with mean realised length ≈25.4 steps. V3-EXQ-906b, after the proximity-harm/world-adequacy repair, realised ≈488.6 steps per segment with 6/8 reaching the 500-step cap and 2 ending through health depletion. V3-EXQ-906c, using the same ecology with additive instrumentation, realised ≈474.1 steps with 7/8 capped and 1 health-depleted. V3-EXQ-911 reached 500/500 steps on all 8 segments with 0 health-depletion endings. At that point the observation is fully right-censored by the experiment's step cap: the safe conclusion is that the recording window, not observed health depletion, became the limiting factor.

Within-run continuity must also not be confused with continuity across the lettered lineage. The 906a/906b/906c/911 drivers each train a new agent from scratch; no checkpoint carries one experimental individual across letters. The laboratory therefore accumulates knowledge across the lineage, while each run's agent has only its own bounded within-run developmental history.

A direct retrospective early-versus-late segment analysis of the two long continuous runs available at the time (906b and 906c) did not establish within-life development. 906b showed patterns consistent with improvement on some measures, but 906c did not replicate them. Because `env.reset()` rerandomises resource/hazard layout at every segment boundary, segment age is confounded with layout variation. The chronicle's Childhood Goblin/Landscape Goblin exchange represents that methodological problem: developmental change cannot be cleanly distinguished from successive world difficulty under the current design.

V3-EXQ-909 supplies the sleep-related qualification. It produced a diagnostic PASS showing that, when the required sleep/replay paths are structurally enabled, slow-wave writes, REM rollouts and replay draws occur and non-sentinel sleep-diversity measures can be obtained across 45 forced sleep firings. It did **not** contain a load-bearing pre/post-sleep behavioural comparison and therefore does not establish behavioural improvement from sleep. Retrospective comparisons around the sparse natural sleep boundaries in 906b/906c were not distinguishable from generic segment-boundary effects. The story therefore treats sleep as measurable machinery whose developmental consequence remains unproven.

The prospective implication is experimental rather than ontological: if REE-v3 is to be studied as a developing persistent agent, future designs need an explicit operational definition of lifetime/age, cleaner separation between body recovery, environment transition and sleep, and sufficient uncensored observation to distinguish within-life learning from world rerandomisation. No claim is made here that such a redesigned lifetime experiment has already been implemented.
