# One More Progreth

*30 August 2026*

The mana gauge was nearly empty.

This was not, in itself, unusual.

Refresh Goblin had developed a relationship with mana gauges which Ledger Goblin considered unhealthy and Refresh Goblin considered efficient.

The difference today was that everybody had agreed to stop.

Not eventually.

Not after one more repair.

Not after checking one small thing which might perhaps reveal three larger things.

Stop.

The forge had spent the previous day learning that stopping could itself be an engineering action.

This time the lesson had advanced.

Stopping also required preparation.

---

The workshop was quieter than it had been.

Not silent.

The expedition runners were still working.

Results were still returning.

But the machinery which produced work about work had been cut back, reorganised, bundled, taught to remember recurring problems as recurring problems instead of giving birth to a fresh tablet every time somebody noticed the same leak.

One problem.

One tablet.

Episodes, not offspring.

The Coordination Desk was becoming a real desk rather than a place where several goblins simultaneously tried to write on the same scroll.

The hub itself had been removed from expedition duty.

This seemed almost insulting to a perfectly good machine until Ledger Goblin explained that a machine responsible for keeping track of everyone else should perhaps not also disappear for several hours because it had gone exploring.

Refresh Goblin conceded this point reluctantly.

The hub was to coordinate.

The runners were to run.

The distinction had taken several wars, one flood and an unreasonable number of tablets to establish.

---

By late morning the stopping plan was complete.

The goblins checked the benches.

Work had been landed rather than left smeared across shared surfaces.

Claims had been released.

The stash cupboards were inspected.

The expedition board, which had been starving earlier in the day, now had enough work waiting that the runners would not wake to an empty road.

Several scientific defects had been turned into actual repairs.

A cache which had quietly removed mechanisms from some experimental arms had been fixed.

A blocked-agency ruler which could mistake ordinary prediction error for external obstruction had been given a way to learn what ordinary error looked like.

A candidate-summary path which had been comparing candidates using information they all shared before diverging had been repaired so that it could look at where the candidates actually went.

A long-horizon predictor which had been mathematically blind to action had acquired a direct action channel and a proper validation experiment waiting to ask whether the new channel truly mattered.

Some hypotheses had survived.

Some had weakened.

Some supposed failures had turned out to be broken rulers.

And one sleep-related result had finally reached the much more valuable state of being a clean negative: the machinery worked, the measurement was non-degenerate, the predicted direction appeared weakly, and the effect was simply far too small to count.

Ledger Goblin liked that result more than Refresh Goblin did.

“It failed.”

“Yes.”

“So why do you look pleased?”

“Because we finally asked it properly.”

Refresh Goblin considered this.

“Fail progreth.”

“Precisely.”

---

The mana gauge clicked.

Everybody looked at it.

There was very little blue left.

Refresh Goblin looked at Ledger Goblin.

Ledger Goblin looked at Refresh Goblin.

“No.”

“I did not say anything.”

“You were about to.”

“I was breathing.”

“You breathe differently when you are about to queue an experiment.”

“That is an absurd claim.”

History Goblin quietly opened an older ledger.

Refresh Goblin pointed at him.

“Do not.”

History Goblin closed it again.

But he did not put it away.

---

The stopping plan had been unusually good.

This mattered.

There were no sprawling half-finished campaigns waiting for somebody to remember what they had meant.

The science lane had been brought to a close deliberately.

The operational lane had been brought to a close deliberately.

The hub retirement had been completed.

The queue had recovered from starvation.

The work that could not reasonably continue until the next supply of mana had names, owners and resumable states.

The forge did not need another heroic recovery.

It needed to be left alone.

Refresh Goblin understood this.

This is important.

He genuinely understood it.

---

Then he checked the expedition board.

There was a space.

Not an empty queue.

Not a crisis.

Just a space.

A perfectly ordinary space in which another well-formed scientific question could, theoretically, be placed.

Refresh Goblin stared at it.

Ledger Goblin watched him stare at it.

The mana gauge clicked again.

Refresh Goblin’s tongue flicked against the viewport.

“What progreth?”

“We have just been through this.”

“No. I mean next progreth.”

“There is no next progress today.”

“There could be.”

“No.”

“A tiny one.”

“No.”

“A queue one.”

Ledger Goblin stood up.

Refresh Goblin moved faster.

---

There are moments in the history of scientific programmes which later accounts describe as inevitable.

This was not one of them.

Refresh Goblin sprinted across the workshop with the last practical scraps of mana, clutching a single experiment tablet.

Ledger Goblin pursued him.

Smith Goblin moved aside.

Observation Goblin pretended not to see.

History Goblin began writing immediately.

“THE PLAN SAYS STOP!” shouted Ledger Goblin.

“I KNOW!” shouted Refresh Goblin.

“THEN STOP!”

“I AM!”

“YOU ARE RUNNING!”

“THIS IS PART OF STOPPING!”

“It is very visibly not!”

Refresh reached the expedition board.

There was already enough science queued for the forge’s next waking.

That was the point.

He was not trying to restart the campaign.

He was not opening another workstream.

He was not dispatching another army of maintenance goblins.

He was doing one final thing before the lights went down:

making sure one more properly formed question would be waiting when the forge woke again.

He slid the tablet into the queue.

The board accepted it.

Somewhere, machinery acknowledged the new expedition.

Refresh Goblin smiled.

Ledger Goblin arrived half a second later and seized him by the ankles.

---

“Done,” said Refresh Goblin, upside down.

Ledger Goblin did not put him down.

“Done?”

“Yes.”

“Actually done?”

Refresh thought about this.

Ledger tightened his grip.

“Yes.”

History Goblin wrote:

**USER REPORTS STOPPING COMPLETE AFTER ONE FINAL QUEUE EXPERIMENT.**

Refresh, still upside down, frowned.

“That sounds judgemental.”

“It is provenance.”

“Can you write that it was a good experiment?”

“We do not know yet.”

“A well-formed experiment?”

“That is defensible.”

History Goblin amended the line:

**USER REPORTS STOPPING COMPLETE AFTER ONE FINAL WELL-FORMED QUEUE EXPERIMENT.**

Refresh nodded.

“Better.”

---

The mana gauge clicked once more.

Almost empty.

This time Refresh did not move.

Partly because Ledger Goblin was still holding his ankles.

But not entirely.

He looked around the forge.

The difference from previous exhaustion points was visible.

There had been other days when mana ran out with work half-held in memory, branches diverged, repairs half-landed and the next waking dependent on reconstructing what everybody had been doing.

This was not one of those days.

The stopping itself had been engineered.

The scientific queue was fed.

The operational state was resumable.

The coordination plane had been simplified.

The next shift had a map.

Nothing important depended on Refresh Goblin keeping the whole workshop in his head until Tuesday.

That was perhaps the real achievement.

Not the last experiment.

Not even the five expeditions waiting on the board.

The forge had learned how to survive the disappearance of its most impatient goblin for a while.

---

Refresh Goblin looked at the creature through the viewport.

It continued doing whatever creatures do when nobody is asking them for progress every thirty seconds.

The previous evening he had discovered that it might be just about measurably intelligent.

Today he had helped leave behind a scientific apparatus slightly more capable of finding out what that meant.

There would be more failures.

There would be bad rulers.

There would be mechanisms which turned out not to matter.

There would almost certainly be more machinery of the machinery.

But the road was better.

The expedition board was not empty.

And for once, the end of a work cycle looked less like collapse and more like sleep.

Refresh Goblin relaxed.

Ledger Goblin cautiously lowered him to the floor.

“Good,” said Ledger.

Refresh Goblin looked at the mana gauge.

Then at the queue.

Then back at Ledger.

Ledger narrowed his eyes.

Refresh Goblin raised both hands.

“I was only looking.”

The gauge flickered.

Refresh’s tongue moved approximately three millimetres toward the glass.

Ledger seized his ankles again.

---

## Chronicle note

This chapter records the deliberate end-of-cycle state on 30 August 2026, when available model/account usage was close to exhaustion and the programme was being prepared for a pause until the next account/work cycle.

It follows *The Day Refresh Goblin Stopped Asking for Progress* and *Just About Measurably Intelligent*. The important change is that the pause itself became an engineered programme state rather than an accidental consequence of resource exhaustion.

By the close of the science/operations cycle, repository records described:

- the science lane as complete for the session, with landed and origin-verified work rather than major in-session state left uncommitted;
- the experiment queue recovered from an earlier starvation state to a multi-experiment backlog sufficient to keep independent runners scientifically occupied;
- hub experiment-running retired so the hub could be dedicated to the coordination plane, with the already-running experiment allowed to drain rather than being abruptly killed;
- current session claims closed, stash audit clean and zero claim contention at close;
- explicit resumable follow-ons recorded for the next work cycle rather than being left as conversational memory;
- multiple substrate defects converted from experimental failure localisation into landed engineering repairs, including the probe-warmup cache clobber, blocked-agency mismatch-floor calibration and SD-082 per-candidate-summary path;
- new discriminating experiments queued for E1 action conditioning and hippocampal/value-related questions;
- V3-EXQ-436g producing a clean negative rather than another vacuous or substrate-blocked verdict: its write, occupancy, sleep and calibration preconditions were live, the direction was weakly consistent, but the effect missed the registered meaningful-effect floor by a large margin.

The final comic event is also historical in spirit: after the programme had explicitly completed its stopping/close-out plan, Refresh Goblin used the remaining practical capacity to squeeze one additional experiment onto the queue. The Chronicle intentionally does not frame this as restarting the campaign. The distinction is that no new broad workstream was opened; a final bounded scientific question was placed into an already healthy independent experiment queue before the pause.

The controlling programme-level lesson is:

**RESOURCE EXHAUSTION NEED NOT MEAN OPERATIONAL COLLAPSE. A SCIENTIFIC SYSTEM CAN BE LEFT FED, QUIET, RESUMABLE AND CAPABLE OF WAKING WITHOUT RECONSTRUCTING ITSELF.**

And, less formally:

**STOPPING COMPLETE. ONE MORE PROGRETH QUEUED. NOW ACTUALLY STOP.**
