# Strategy Memorandum: Mission-Aligned Funding Strategy for REE

**Status:** Strategy Memorandum

**Purpose**

Develop a long-term funding strategy that maximises the probability of successfully developing, validating, deploying, and governing the Reflective–Ethical Engine (REE) while preserving scientific integrity and architectural independence.

---

# Executive Summary

REE is an unusually long-term research programme situated between neuroscience, cognitive science, artificial intelligence, ethics, and computational psychiatry.

Its funding requirements are therefore likely to evolve through several distinct phases.

Mission-aligned funding sources such as Effective Altruism may represent an excellent strategic fit for some phases, but funding should be sought only when the maturity of the project, supporting evidence, and governance are sufficient to make a compelling and credible case.

The objective is not simply to secure funding.

It is to secure funding that accelerates REE without distorting its scientific direction.

---

# Strategic Objectives

Funding should increase the probability of:

- completing REE-v3,
- validating the architecture experimentally,
- publishing high-quality scientific work,
- recruiting collaborators,
- supporting infrastructure,
- developing safe governance,
- maintaining long-term scientific independence.

Funding should not become an end in itself.

---

# Core Principles

## Mission alignment

Funding should support REE's scientific and societal goals rather than redirect them.

## Scientific independence

Architectural decisions should remain evidence-driven rather than funder-driven.

## Long-term sustainability

Funding should enable continued development rather than create short-lived bursts of activity followed by instability.

## Transparency

Governance, publications, and architectural decisions should remain transparent wherever practical.

## Incremental scaling

Funding should grow alongside demonstrated capability.

---

# Current Position

Current strengths include:

- coherent architectural vision,
- active implementation of REE-v3,
- growing repository of structured architectural knowledge,
- increasing body of thought intakes,
- emerging governance methodology through REE Assembly,
- interdisciplinary grounding,
- publication efforts already underway.

Current limitations include:

- limited experimental validation,
- limited external collaboration,
- small public profile,
- limited benchmark demonstrations,
- early implementation maturity.

These limitations do not weaken the architecture itself.

They mainly influence funding readiness.

---

# Effective Altruism Assessment

Potential strengths of alignment:

- emphasis on long-term societal benefit,
- concern with artificial intelligence safety,
- willingness to support ambitious foundational research,
- openness to interdisciplinary work,
- focus on neglected but potentially high-impact areas.

Potential challenges:

- REE is broader than conventional artificial intelligence safety research,
- the architecture emphasises cognition rather than solely alignment,
- benefits may be long-term and difficult to quantify initially,
- REE's interdisciplinary nature may require careful explanation.

Overall assessment:

Mission alignment appears promising, but success would depend heavily on communicating REE in terms meaningful to the funding programme.

# Infrastructure Priorities

Funding should preferentially support:

- compute,
- research assistance,
- experimental infrastructure,
- literature review,
- benchmarking,
- software engineering,
- open scientific dissemination.

---

# Governance Considerations

Funding agreements should avoid:

- pressure for premature deployment,
- architectural distortion,
- proprietary lock-in,
- publication restrictions,
- dependence on one funding source.

REE Assembly governance should remain the primary mechanism for architectural evolution.

---

# Risks

## Premature funding

Large funding before sufficient maturity may create pressure to accelerate beyond the available evidence.

## Misaligned incentives

Funding priorities may diverge from scientific priorities.

## Reputation risk

Overstating maturity could reduce long-term credibility.

## Opportunity cost

Applications consume substantial time and attention.

Timing therefore matters.

---

# Recommended Sequencing

## Near term

- complete REE-v3,
- continue structured literature ingestion,
- expand validation,
- increase publication output,
- refine governance.

## Intermediate

- demonstrate capability,
- obtain external feedback,
- strengthen scientific visibility,
- identify aligned collaborators.

## Long term

- pursue substantial mission-aligned funding,
- recruit researchers,
- expand experimental programme,
- establish a sustainable research organisation.

---

# Immediate Actions

1. Review Effective Altruism funding programmes.
2. Identify eligibility requirements.
3. Map REE milestones against funding expectations.
4. Build a funding-readiness checklist.
5. Prepare a concise public description of REE suitable for non-specialist reviewers.
6. Identify two or three additional mission-aligned funding bodies for comparison.

---

# Decision Gate

REE should seek major external funding when:

- the architecture is demonstrably operational,
- evidence supports its central organisational principles,
- governance is mature,
- the expected acceleration from funding exceeds the opportunity cost of applying.

---

# Current Recommendation

Treat funding as an architectural accelerator rather than as validation.

The strongest future applications will emerge naturally from a mature architecture, reproducible demonstrations, credible publications, transparent governance, and a clearly articulated long-term scientific vision.
