## 1. Verdict

**The class survives the sweep and is usable. Three of the audit's readings need correcting, and one of them is a design inference that is simply wrong.**

The audit (§5, §10.2) left C7 with two live methodological threats and a recommendation set built on six papers. Sweeping the full class resolves both threats — one favourably, one unfavourably — and overturns the reasoning behind `P3-R5`:

1. **The regression-toward-the-mean threat is settled, and settled in the class's favour.** Pezdek & Eddy's critique was answered in the same issue by Garry, Sharman, Wade, Hunt & Smith (2001), and then decided *empirically* eleven years later by Von Glahn et al. (2012), whose Experiment 1 ran the exact control `P3-R4` asks for — vary the number of *ratings* with no imagination at all — and found **no inflation**. The confidence-readout members of the class are usable. `P3-R4` stays mandatory, but as a standard design control with a known precedent, not as an unresolved threat to the class.
2. **Mammarella 2010's null interaction is badly underpowered — and the finding it supports is nonetheless stronger than the audit thought.** The study could not detect anything short of a large group × repetition interaction. But the sweep found **two further clinical populations** (Korsakoff's syndrome; very mild Alzheimer's disease) tested on the same paradigm by different groups, and both show the same dissociation: elevated or intact baseline source-monitoring error, **comparable inflation to controls**. One underpowered null is the weakest kind of evidence; three independent underpowered nulls pointing the same way, across three disorders and three labs, is a convergent pattern.
3. **`P3-R5`'s inference is wrong as written and must be corrected before it is built.** It states that if REE's replay effect is reproduced by re-exposure, "the result is fluency and not genealogy". Lindner et al. (2010) falsifies that inference: merely *observing another person* perform an action robustly produces false memories of **self**-performance, the effect **survives warnings** and **survives elimination of sensory overlap**, and the authors conclude it is *not* easily reconciled with a source-monitoring account but fits interpersonal motor simulation. There is a third route — simulation — which is neither self-generation of the target nor fluency. An arm that finds an effect under non-self-generated exposure therefore licenses no conclusion at all as currently specified.

**Net for the branch:** C7's standing as the class that justifies P3 is unchanged and slightly improved. What changes is the *control structure* P3 needs, which is larger than the audit specified, and the confidence with which the clinical comparator can be stated, which is lower per-study and higher in aggregate.

## 3. The class, swept

Forty-one PubMed records match `"imagination inflation"`. The audit cited six. Below is what the remaining thirty-five contain that bears on the branch.

### 3.1 The fluency rival is contested, not established — and this narrows the audit's §5.5

The audit records the fluency rival as "triple-sourced and needed at two rungs" (Weaver 2007 / O'Donnell 2023 for P1; Sharman 2004 for P3; `MECH-544`'s review). The class contains a direct test, and it goes the other way.

**Thomas, Bulevich & Loftus 2003** (PMID 12872878) — one of the two papers the audit located but did not read — ran two experiments in the Goff & Roediger paradigm explicitly to decide between *misattribution of specific characteristics* and *misattribution of global familiarity*. They replicated the dose-response, and found false memories **greatest when participants were forced to include sensory detail** in their imaginings. Critically, they separated the phenomenology: conscious recollection accompanied false memories in perceptually detailed conditions, whereas *feelings of familiarity* accompanied them in conditions lacking sensory cues. Their conclusion is explicit — confusion based on elaborate representations containing specific characteristics, "as opposed to confusion based on processing fluency, is more likely to lead to false memories."

So fluency and elaboration are **separable, and which one operates depends on how much sensory detail the generation produces**. Sharman 2004's flat-beyond-one-exposure confidence result is a low-elaboration finding; Thomas 2003's monotonic result is a high-elaboration one. The audit's framing — that fluency will explain a positive result at either rung unless excluded by design — is right that it must be excluded, and wrong that it is the favoured account. It is one of two routes, selected by elaboration.

**Von Glahn, Otani, Migita, Langford & Hillard 2012** (PMID 24837017) is the second direct test and agrees. Experiment 2 manipulated richness of memory representation (generate zero, three or six details) and found confidence rose in the three- and six-detail conditions, concluding the effect "is based on reality-monitoring errors" rather than familiarity. Experiment 1 is discussed in §4.1 — it is the fluency test, and it came out null.

### 3.2 The effect is manipulable at retrieval, which is the attribution-route signature

The audit's Finding 3 (§6) argued that a stored-tag architecture and an attribution architecture have different empirical signatures, the attribution route being "movable at read time with storage untouched", and recommended `P1-R5` on that basis. **The class supplies the empirical precedent, and it was the other unread paper.**

**Libby 2003** (PMID 14704022) manipulated imagery perspective (first-person versus third-person) and memory-test wording ("remember doing?" versus "happened to you?"). Imagination inflation was **greatest when the test wording encouraged participants to visualise real events from the same perspective they had used to imagine the counterfactual ones**. Her conclusion is precisely the attribution account: inflation "may result not simply from having created imaginary representations of events, but also from having created representations that **match the decision criteria used in source monitoring**."

That is a read-time manipulation — the wording of the test — changing the size of the effect at fixed storage. Two further papers converge:

- **Landau & Von Glahn 2004** (PMID 15605960): warning participants about the effect significantly reduced its magnitude.
- **Sharman, Garry & Hunt 2005** (PMID 15967405): participants given a source cue (perspective) *or* a familiarity cue (a plausibility questionnaire) did not resist inflation; **only those given both did**.
- **Clark, Daley, Deason, O'Connor, Budson & DeCaro 2022** (PMID 35343730, N=57): a cue-utilisation instruction given before encoding and before test improved correct identifications in MCI.

`P1-R5` has since landed as a design note (`b2397e0055`). This tranche adds that its predicted direction is not merely plausible but is the direction the human class actually shows, and that the criterion in Libby's design is *representational match*, not a scalar threshold — which is a more specific target than "a shiftable criterion".

### 3.3 Dose-response need not be monotonic

Von Glahn 2012's Experiment 2 found **greater inflation in the three-detail condition than in the six-detail condition** — a boundary condition the authors flag explicitly, and for which they offer a mental-workload alternative. The audit's §5 treats the replay-count axis as monotonic (Goff & Roediger) or flat (Sharman). A third shape exists: rising then falling. This matters for P3 because a non-monotone result across `0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16` would, under the current predeclaration, be scored as a failure to find a dose-response.

### 3.4 C7b — the effect without internal generation at all

**Lindner, Echterhoff, Davidson & Brand 2010** (PMID 20689054), *Observation inflation*, Psychol Sci. Three experiments: participants observed another person performing actions, some not previously performed by the participant, then took a source-memory test. **Action observation robustly produced false memories of self-performance.** The effect "persisted despite warnings or instructions to focus on self-performance cues given immediately before the test, and despite elimination of sensory overlap between performance and observation." The authors state the findings "are not easily reconciled with a source-monitoring account but appear to fit an account invoking interpersonal motor simulation."

**Lindner & Echterhoff 2015** (PMID 25939137) refines the mechanism across three experiments with a two-week delay: imagining *another person* can trigger false memories of self-performance, but visual similarity between performance and imagination predicted false memories **only for other-imagination, not for self-imagination** — consistent with other-imagination running on visual imagery and self-imagination on motor simulation.

This is the tranche's most consequential finding for design, and §6.2 works through it. Note also that it partially breaks the retrieval-criterion story of §3.2: warnings reduced the effect in Landau 2004 (self-imagination) and did **not** reduce it in Lindner 2010 (observation). The two routes differ in their susceptibility to read-time manipulation, which is itself a usable discriminator.

### 3.5 Boundary cases — the direction is paradigm-dependent

- **Maraver, Lapa, Garcia-Marques, Carneiro & Raposo 2021** (PMID 34489789): in a pragmatic-inference paradigm, imaginal encoding **reduced** false memories. The authors name the tension directly — instructions to imagine increase false memories in the imagination-inflation paradigm and decrease them in DRM (the "imagination facilitation effect"). Imagination is not a monotone generator of false memory; the sign depends on what the competing lure is.
- **Dhammapeera, Brunskill, Hellerstedt & Bergström 2024** (PMID 38362597): counterfactual imagination **impairs memory for true actions**, relative both to veridical imagination and to a not-imagined baseline, with EEG evidence of separate retrieval and post-retrieval processes. Replay of a competing trace degrades the original rather than only inflating the competitor — a readout-1 and readout-4 effect that P3's current readout set would not see.
- **Heaps & Nash 1999** (PMID 12199217): inflation was associated with hypnotic suggestibility and dissociativity, but **not** with imagery vividness or interrogative suggestibility. Set against **Dudek & Polczyk 2025**'s registered-report null on memory distrust, the individual-difference route is mixed, with the better-powered and preregistered study negative.

### 3.6 Representativeness, stated honestly

Not cherry-picked, and the base rate is documented: **Muschalla & Schönborn 2021** (PMID 33586291), a systematic review of 59 experimental studies across 30 databases, treats imagination inflation as one of three main false-memory induction methods and reports that free and guided imagery induced false beliefs or memories in, on average, **20–50% of manipulated participants**.

Three concentration caveats. (i) The class is dominated by **two paradigm families** — the Life Events Inventory pre/post confidence rating (Garry 1996, Heaps 1999, Pezdek 2001, Sharman 2004, Landau 2004, Sharman 2005, Von Glahn 2012) and the Goff & Roediger action-statement source-monitoring test (Goff 1998, Thomas 2003, Mammarella 2010, O'Connor 2015, El Haj 2020, Clark 2022). Almost every methodological dispute in the class is really a dispute about which of those two measures is being used. (ii) Two lab lineages dominate: Garry/Loftus/Sharman, and Lindner/Echterhoff. (iii) Sampling is overwhelmingly healthy undergraduate, with the clinical work concentrated in older-adult memory disorders.

**And the most important representativeness fact for this branch: not one of the 41 records measures effective independent-source count.** The class is rich on readouts 1, 2 and 4 and empty on readout 3.

## 4. The two adjudications the audit asked for

### 4.1 Was Pezdek & Eddy's regression-toward-the-mean critique answered? **Yes — twice, and the second answer is empirical.**

The `discussion 719–729` attached to PMID 11531226 is **not separately indexed in PubMed**; it was located through Crossref at the issue level:

> **Garry M, Sharman SJ, Wade KA, Hunt MJ, Smith PJ (2001).** *Imagination inflation is a fact, not an artifact: A reply to Pezdek and Eddy.* Memory & Cognition 29(5):719–729. [DOI 10.3758/BF03200474](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03200474). 48 references; 18 Crossref citations / 24 on Semantic Scholar. **No PubMed record. No abstract in Crossref or Semantic Scholar; the full text is paywalled at Springer.**

What the reply argues, from the open-access repository record (Warwick WRAP eprint 65933) rather than from the full text, which I could not obtain: Pezdek & Eddy made **four predictions** about which data patterns would demonstrate a genuine imagination effect versus an RTM-explainable one; Garry et al. reviewed each and identified significant problems with each; they concluded the effect is genuine. *I did not read the four arguments themselves and do not restate them here.*

**A reply by the original authors is not, on its own, a settlement.** What settles it is that the critique was later tested directly, by a third group, with a design built for the purpose:

> **Von Glahn et al. 2012, Experiment 1.** Event familiarity was varied by asking participants to rate each event **once, three times, or five times** — repeated measurement with no imagination, no paraphrasing and no elaboration. **No inflation was found.**

That is exactly the control `P3-R4` specifies, run in the human paradigm, and it is negative. If the pre/post design's inflation were a regression artifact of repeated rating, repeated rating alone should have produced it. It did not. Combined with Goff & Roediger's source-attribution dose-response — which is structurally immune to the critique, since a per-count source judgement is not a repeated rating of one scale — and with the class's continued productive use across six clinical populations for twenty-five years, the field settled on *the effect is genuine*.

**Where this leaves the branch.** The chip asked whether this decides that the confidence-readout members of the class are usable at all. **It does: they are usable.** But two qualifications carry into P3, and they are not the same as the RTM critique:

- The settlement is about the effect's **existence**, not about the confidence measure's **construct validity**. Sharman 2004 (flat beyond one exposure, fluency account) and Thomas 2003 (elaboration-dependent, recollection account) still disagree about what the confidence readout indexes. That disagreement is live and is §3.1's business.
- Nothing about the human settlement transfers to the synthetic setting. A noisy, repeatedly-measured synthetic confidence readout **will** regress regardless of what happened in 2001. `P3-R4` is therefore not discharged by this finding — it is *validated* by it, and given a canonical form (§6.1).

### 4.2 Mammarella 2010's power for the null group × repetition interaction: **could not be obtained; answered instead by sensitivity analysis, and the answer is "underpowered for anything below a large effect".**

**Stating the failure plainly first.** I could not obtain the sample size or the interaction statistic from any source I actually retrieved. *Psychiatry Research* 179(3):267–273 is closed access at Elsevier; Europe PMC holds abstract only (`isOpenAccess: N`, `inEPMC: N`); Semantic Scholar's abstract field is elided by the publisher. A web search asserted 26 patients and 26 controls, but I could not corroborate that against any primary record, and the same result bundled in a statement about auditory-hallucination subgroups that appears nowhere in the abstract and probably belongs to a different study. **I therefore do not report N=26 as a fact.**

The question is answerable without it. The 2 (group, between) × 2 (repetition: one versus four imaginings, within) interaction is equivalent to a two-sample test on each participant's within-subject difference score, so required N follows directly. At alpha = .05, two-tailed:

| Interaction effect (Cohen's *d* on the difference score) | N per group for 80% power |
|---|---|
| 0.8 (large) | 25 |
| 0.6 | 44 |
| 0.5 (medium) | 63 |
| 0.4 | 99 |
| 0.3 (small) | 175 |

And the achieved power across the entire plausible range for an in-person single-site clinical sample in this literature:

| N per group | power at *d*=0.8 | at *d*=0.5 | at *d*=0.3 | smallest *d* detectable at 80% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 71.6% | 35.3% | 15.8% | 0.89 |
| 26 | 82.2% | 43.8% | 19.1% | 0.78 |
| 30 | 87.3% | 49.1% | 21.3% | 0.72 |
| 40 | 94.7% | 60.9% | 26.9% | 0.63 |

*(Normal-approximation power; accurate to about one unit of N in this range. The exact N is unknown, which is why the table spans the range rather than reporting one figure.)*

**The conclusion is robust to not knowing N.** Anywhere in that range, the study could detect only a **large** group difference in repetition-driven amplification. Its null therefore excludes a large amplification difference and is close to uninformative about a medium or small one — at the most commonly-cited figure it had under 45% power for a medium effect.

**So the audit's falsifier §10.2 fires, and the clinical limb must be weakened — but not withdrawn, because the sweep replaced the single study with a pattern.** See §5.

## 5. The clinical limb, restated

The audit's epistemic boundary states that "the only clinical members are Mammarella 2010 and Moritz 2012". **For the C7 paradigm that is no longer accurate.** The sweep found two further clinical runs of the same action-statement paradigm:

- **El Haj & Robin 2020** (PMID 32281810), *Korsakoff's syndrome*. Patients and controls listened to, enacted, or imagined actions; in session 2 they imagined actions **once or three times** — a repetition manipulation. Imagining increased false recall of enactment **in both patients and controls**; the authors attribute it to source-monitoring difficulty.
- **O'Connor, Deason, Reynolds, Tat, Flannery, Solomon, Vassey & Budson 2015** (PMID 25893972), *very mild Alzheimer's disease*. Patients were susceptible to imagination inflation "to an extent similar to that of healthy older adults".

Together with Mammarella 2010 (psychosis: more source-monitoring errors, comparable inflation), that is **three clinical populations, three research groups, three disorders, and the same dissociation each time: baseline source-attribution deficit present or intact, repetition-driven amplification not elevated.** None is individually well-powered for its null. Convergence across three independent underpowered nulls in the same direction is materially stronger than any one of them, and it is the honest form of the constraint.

**Three boundaries on that statement, all binding:**

1. **Korsakoff's syndrome and Alzheimer's disease are neurological, not psychiatric, and nothing about them transfers to psychosis.** They strengthen the *general* claim that repetition-driven amplification resists clinical elevation across disorders of source monitoring. They do **not** add a second psychosis data point. For psychosis the evidence remains Mammarella 2010 alone, underpowered, plus Moritz 2012 on a different readout.
2. **No psychosis inference is drawn from any healthy-adult or synthetic result** in this tranche, consistent with the branch's standing boundary.
3. These are *absence-of-interaction* findings in small samples. They constrain; they do not refute. A well-powered clinical study could still find elevated amplification, and the sweep located none.

## 6. Consequences for the assay designs — recommendations only

**No edit is made to [`provenance_p3_replay_amplification_design.md`](provenance_p3_replay_amplification_design.md) or [`provenance_harness_generated_ancestry_design.md`](provenance_harness_generated_ancestry_design.md).** Both were checked: `P3-R4` and `P3-R5` are **not yet incorporated** into the P3 design, so these corrections land before the build rather than after it.

### 6.1 `P3-R4` — keep, and give it Von Glahn's form

The recommendation stands and is now backed by a precedent rather than only by a threat. Two refinements:

1. **The control the human literature actually ran is stronger than the one `P3-R4` specifies.** `P3-R4` asks for matched un-replayed items carried through the identical measurement schedule. Von Glahn Experiment 1 went further: it **varied the number of measurements (1, 3, 5) with the generative process held at zero**. In the synthetic setting that is cheap and strictly more informative — it yields the measurement-schedule dose-response directly, rather than a single flat comparison. Recommend P3 carry a `replays=0 × measurements∈{1,…,k}` cell block.
2. **Retain the preference for per-count judgements over a pre/post delta** (`P3-R4`'s first requirement). The historical settlement does not weaken it: Goff & Roediger's design survived the critique *because* it was a per-count source judgement, and that is the structural reason, not a contingent one.

`P3-R4` is **not** discharged by §4.1. A synthetic readout's regression behaviour is a property of the synthetic measurement, not of the 2001 literature.

### 6.2 `P3-R5` — the inference is wrong; the arm must be split three ways

As written, `P3-R5` adds a non-internal-generation level and concludes: "if REE's replay effect is reproduced by re-exposure, the result is fluency and not genealogy". **Lindner 2010 breaks that disjunction.** Observation of another agent's action — no self-generation, and with sensory overlap eliminated and warnings given — produced robust false memories of self-performance by a route the authors argue is *neither* source-monitoring *nor* fluency, but motor simulation. "Not self-generated" therefore does not imply "fluency".

Recommended replacement structure — three arms, not two, crossed with elaboration:

```text
A. self-generation          run the generative process on own trace        (current P3-R1 levels)
B. simulation-without-self  run the generative process on ANOTHER agent's  (Lindner 2010 analogue)
                            trace / a foreign trace, attributing nothing
C. re-exposure only         present the trace; run no generative process   (Sharman 2004 analogue)

crossed with:  LOW vs HIGH elaboration   (Thomas 2003 -- decides recollection vs familiarity route)
```

The discriminations this buys:
- **C alone positive** -> fluency/re-exposure. This is `P3-R5`'s original intent and it remains a valid negative result for genealogy.
- **B positive with C null** -> a simulation route. Not fluency, and not genealogy either — it is the synthetic analogue of observation inflation, and it would be a genuinely new result for the branch rather than a confound.
- **A > B > C, with the gap widening under HIGH elaboration** -> the elaboration/recollection route (Thomas 2003), which is the route most likely to carry cardinality information.
- **A ≈ B ≈ C** -> the effect is insensitive to what generated the repetition, which is strong evidence against the genealogy account and should be predeclared as such.

The elaboration cross is the part most likely to be dropped for cost, and it is the part that decides which of the two human routes REE has reproduced.

### 6.3 New recommendations arising from this sweep

- **`P3-R8` — predeclare non-monotonicity as admissible.** Von Glahn 2012 Experiment 2 found inflation *greater at three details than at six*. P3's dose-response predeclaration should state that a rise-then-fall across `0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16` is a positive dose-response with a boundary condition, not a null. Without this, a real inverted-U is scored as a failure.
- **`P3-R9` — add a true-trace-degradation readout.** Dhammapeera 2024 shows counterfactual imagination *impairs* memory for the true action beyond baseline forgetting. P3 currently measures what the replayed trace does to belief about itself; it does not measure what replay does to the *competing true* trace. That is a readout-1 and readout-4 effect, it is cheap to instrument in the harness, and if it is present it changes the interpretation of any confidence rise.
- **`P3-R7` restatement (clinical comparator).** Replace "Mammarella 2010 is the nearest comparator and its direction is unfavourable" with: *three clinical populations across three disorders show baseline source-attribution deficit without elevated repetition-driven amplification; each is individually underpowered (detectable only at d ≳ 0.7), so the pattern constrains rather than refutes, and the psychosis-specific evidence remains a single small study.*
- **`P1-R5` (already landed) — sharpen the criterion.** Libby 2003 is the empirical precedent for the read-time criterion shift, and it specifies the criterion more precisely than the design note does: what mattered was the **match between the representation's perspective and the decision criterion invoked by the test wording**, not a scalar threshold. A representational-match criterion is the closer analogue.

## 7. What this tranche does NOT establish

- **It does not close Break A, and it does not close the cardinality half of Break B.** No member of the class measures effective independent-source count. P3 remains the first measurement of readouts 3 and 4 across replay.
- **It does not license any psychosis inference.** §5's three boundaries are binding. Two of the three clinical populations are neurological.
- **It does not decide the `MECH-544` demarcation.** Whether `MECH-544` and this branch's cardinality target are one claim or two is a governance disposition; the audit §4 draws the line on readout 3 and §12 debt 1 records it as unowned. This tranche neither ratifies nor disturbs it, and made no edit to `claims.yaml`.
- **It does not read the Garry et al. 2001 reply's four counter-arguments.** The full text is paywalled; §4.1 reports only what the repository record states, and rests the adjudication on Von Glahn 2012's independent empirical test instead.
- **It does not establish Mammarella 2010's actual N.** §4.2 is a sensitivity analysis across a plausible range, not a report of the study's power.
- **It registers no claim, promotes nothing, queues nothing and scores nothing.** No literature `record.json` entries were written, deliberately: entries carry `claim_ids_tested` and would attach evidence to `MECH-544` or to an unregistered branch claim, which is both scoring and a pre-emption of the demarcation decision. This matches the judgment-class tranche's precedent, which is likewise a planning document with a bibliography.

## Compact statement

> The internally-generated-repetition class was swept in full for the first time, and it is admissible. The regression-toward-the-mean threat that hung over the confidence readout is settled — answered in the same issue by Garry et al. 2001, and then decided empirically by Von Glahn et al. 2012, whose Experiment 1 ran `P3-R4`'s own control in humans, varying measurement count at zero generation, and found nothing. Mammarella 2010's null interaction is underpowered for anything below a large effect, as the audit suspected, but the sweep replaced that single study with a three-disorder pattern: schizophrenia, Korsakoff's syndrome and very mild Alzheimer's disease all show baseline source-attribution deficit without elevated repetition-driven amplification. And `P3-R5` must be rebuilt before it is used: its inference that a non-self-generated arm reproducing the effect means fluency is falsified by observation inflation, where merely watching another agent act produces false memories of self-performance through neither fluency nor source monitoring. The class is rich on association, source attribution and calibration, contested between a fluency route and an elaboration route that Thomas 2003 separates, manipulable at retrieval in the way `P1-R5` predicts — and, across all forty-one records, contains not one measurement of effective independent-source count. Break A is exactly where it was.
