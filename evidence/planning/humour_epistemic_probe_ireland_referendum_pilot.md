# STUDY-HUM-1 -- Second Pilot: Ireland, the Referendum Lead-Lag Natural Experiment

Date: 2026-08-15
Study: `evidence/planning/humour_epistemic_probe_study.md` (STUDY-HUM-1)
Depends on (must pass first): `evidence/planning/humour_epistemic_probe_coding_manual_pilot.md` (US reliability pilot)
Upstream: `evidence/planning/humour_epistemic_probe_mvp_spike_2026-08-15.md`
Claims exercised: SOC-HUM-1 (theory), SOC-HUM-2 (H1 lead-lag), **SOC-HUM-5 (candidate -- medium-externalization / cross-cultural transportability; see study Section 11)**
Status: protocol (pre-pilot). Runs AFTER the US coding-manual pilot clears its gates -- you do not run a natural experiment with an uncalibrated instrument.

> **Why a second pilot, and why Ireland.** The US pilot tests whether the *instrument* works
> (can two blind raters code target-of-violation reliably, does the flip appear on a case that
> resolved, stay absent on one that persists). It cannot test the study's actual thesis -- that
> the flip **leads** a resolution and may do epistemic *work* -- because US resolutions are slow
> poll-drift with no sharp date. **Ireland fixes exactly that.** It has the cleanest resolution
> axis in the world (discrete, dated, exact-share national referendums) and, despite a famously
> oral humour culture, a datable recorded-humour trail whose target is unusually legible. This
> pilot is the first sharp lead-lag test, and simultaneously the first test of the
> medium-externalization sub-question (SOC-HUM-5).

---

## 1. What this pilot tests (three things, distinct from the US pilot)

1. **Transportability.** Does the coding instrument, calibrated on US broadcast humour, apply to
   Irish recorded humour -- same four codes, acceptable inter-rater kappa on an Irish sample? A
   drop in kappa would say the instrument is US-shaped, not general.
2. **Lead-lag (the crux the whole study is for).** Does the target-flip -- butt moving onto the
   **old norm** (canonically the moral authority of the Catholic Church / "old Ireland") -- **lead**
   the discrete referendum midpoint, or lag it? The exact referendum date is what makes this
   answerable here and not in the US cases (study Section 5).
3. **Medium-externalization (SOC-HUM-5).** Is the *recorded* humour trail sufficient to detect the
   mechanism, or does Ireland's largely **oral, small-world** humour culture leave the recorded
   fraction too thin -- producing a false null that is a medium artifact, not an absence of the
   mechanism? This pilot cannot see oral humour; what it *can* do is establish whether the
   recorded proxies alone carry the signal.

---

## 2. The paradox that makes Ireland the sharpest case

Ireland looks like the *hardest* place to run this study and is arguably the *best*:

- **Humour axis (the weakness):** the culture's humour is heavily **oral, relational,
  small-world** -- slagging, pub storytelling, the craic -- with a comparatively thin layer of
  canonical, dated, broadcast humour media. Much of the actual epistemic-probe activity, if the
  theory is right, happens in conversation and never gets recorded. This is a genuine blind spot,
  stated up front as a scope condition (Section 6).
- **Resolution axis (the exceptional strength):** Ireland resolved its major social norm-conflicts
  by **national referendum** -- discrete events with an exact date and an exact vote share, not
  slow poll-drift. That is a far better natural experiment than any US opinion series.
- **A shared old-norm (the structural gift):** Ireland's referendum sequence flips, over and over,
  the **same** old norm -- the moral authority of the Catholic Church over private life. So the
  humour target-flip ("the Church / old Ireland as the butt") is a **repeated, dated** signal
  across four decades, not a one-off. That repetition is exactly what a lead-lag test wants.

The net: a modest recorded sample, if it shows the Church-as-butt flip **leading** the discrete
referendum dates, is strong directional evidence -- stronger than a large US sample against a
blurry poll midpoint.

## 4. Humour axis -- the recorded proxies (tiered by era, and the medium migrates)

There is no single Irish humour corpus; there is a **succession of media**, and the recording
medium itself shifts across the window -- which is a confound, handled in Section 6.

- **Century / mid-century (magazine):** *Dublin Opinion* (1922-1968) -- the Irish analog to
  *Punch*, the national humorous journal. Covers the formative decades but ends before the
  referendum era.
- **Broadcast (1970s-2000s):** **Dave Allen** (Irish, explicitly anti-clerical, 1970s -- *Dave
  Allen at Large*, recorded via British TV); *Hall's Pictorial Weekly* (RTE, 1971-1980);
  **Scrap Saturday** (RTE Radio, 1989-1991, Dermot Morgan); **Father Ted** (Channel 4, 1995-1998,
  the Church rendered absurd -- Dermot Morgan again).
- **Digital / social (2009-):** **Waterford Whispers News** (2009-, satirical news, dated and
  searchable); **Callan's Kicks** / Oliver Callan (RTE); **"Irish Twitter"** -- the small-world
  social network **externalised into recorded, timestamped form**, and the closest thing to
  capturing the oral register in a datable medium (esp. dense during the 2015 and 2018 campaigns).

**Two striking deep-lead anchors already in the recorded trail:** Dave Allen (1970s) and *Father
Ted* (1995-98) both make the **Catholic Church -- the old norm -- the butt**, two to four decades
before the 2015/2018 referendums resolved the conflicts that same authority governed. If the
theory is right, that is the *lead* signature sitting in the record. (Note the medium tell, which
is SOC-HUM-5 in miniature: both were recorded through **British/UK** broadcast infrastructure, not
Irish social networks -- the mechanism may have been just as active in unrecorded Irish
conversation, but only the UK-broadcast fraction left a trace.)

## 5. Design -- exploit the discrete date; measure the flip's TIMING, not volume

The US pilot measures target-flip across coarse 5-year bins. The Irish pilot's advantage is the
**exact referendum date**, so it measures the flip's *timing relative to that date*:

- **Sampling around each primary event:** dated recorded-humour instances across a window
  bracketing each referendum -- e.g. 1990-2020 for SSM/Repeal (long enough to catch the Ted/Allen
  deep lead and the social-media campaign spike), binned finely near the date (annual in
  2010-2020; per-medium before that).
- **Code with the SAME instrument** (the US coding manual, Sections 5-6 there): four target codes
  + benign/taboo flag, blind raters, mixed set.
- **The measurement is the flip point + its sign relative to the referendum midpoint**, per medium
  (never pooled across media -- see Section 6). Does `transgressor`/`out-group` (the deviant: the
  gay couple, the woman seeking abortion) give way to `old-norm` (the Church, "old Ireland") --
  and if so, does that crossover fall **before** 2015-05-22 / 2018-05-25 (lead) or **after** (lag)?

**Do NOT measure prevalence trends as the primary readout** -- medium migration (Section 6) makes
raw Irish humour volume-over-time uninterpretable. Timing and target are the readouts.

---

## 6. The two confounds this pilot must state, not hide

1. **The oral blind spot (SOC-HUM-5, the honest ceiling).** This pilot sees only the *recorded*
   fraction of Irish humour. If the recorded proxies show **no** flip, that is **not** evidence the
   mechanism is absent -- it may be fully present in unrecorded conversation. So a null here is
   interpreted narrowly: "the recorded trail does not carry the signal," never "Ireland does not do
   this." A **positive** here (flip present and leading) is, by contrast, strong -- the recorded
   fraction is a lower bound on the activity.
2. **Medium migration (magazine -> broadcast -> digital/social).** The recording medium changes
   across the window, so prevalence-over-time is confounded with medium availability, and even the
   *type* of target legible in each medium differs (a 1970s TV act vs a 2018 tweet). Control by
   (a) reading the flip **within each medium** rather than pooling, and (b) treating cross-medium
   agreement on the flip's direction/timing as the finding, not a single pooled curve. This is the
   within-culture-structure rule (study Section 11) applied *within-medium* inside one culture.

## 7. Gates (all three to GO to a full cross-cultural corpus)

- **T1 Transportability:** inter-rater kappa on the Irish sample stays >= ~0.6 with no dominant
  `transgressor <-> old-norm` confusion. A material drop from the US kappa says the instrument is
  US-shaped -> refine the manual for cross-cultural use before scaling.
- **T2 Lead-lag direction:** the Church/old-norm flip **crosses over before** the 2015 and 2018
  referendum dates in at least the digital-era medium, corroborated by the Ted/Allen deep-lead
  anchors in the broadcast medium. A consistent **lead** is the first real (still suggestive, not
  dispositive -- study Section 5) evidence humour does epistemic *work* rather than narrating.
- **T3 Medium-sufficiency (SOC-HUM-5):** the flip is **detectable at all** in the recorded proxies.
  If it is, the recorded trail is a usable lower-bound instrument for oral-humour cultures, and
  SOC-HUM-5's measurement worry is bounded. If it is not, SOC-HUM-5 is **confirmed as a live
  threat** -- report that as a first-class finding (the instrument is medium-limited), not a
  failed pilot.

A T1 failure blocks cross-cultural scaling. A T3 failure is itself a publishable result about the
instrument's reach.

## 8. Pre-registered predictions (lock before coding)

- **Deep lead (broadcast):** Dave Allen (1970s) and *Father Ted* (1995-98) code `old-norm`
  (Church as butt), decades ahead of 2015/2018.
- **Campaign flip (digital):** in Waterford Whispers / Irish Twitter, the butt on marriage and
  abortion crosses from `transgressor`/`out-group` (the couple, the woman) to `old-norm`
  (the Church, "old Ireland", the No side) **before** each referendum date, with the benign/taboo
  flag on the residual transgressor-targeting form drifting `taboo` late.
- **Abortion is the harder case:** Repeal-era humour tests the benign/taboo boundary hardest --
  abortion is a harder taboo to joke about than marriage, so expect more `contested`/`taboo`
  flags and possibly a weaker or later flip than SSM. A *dissociation* between the two
  referendums (clean flip for SSM, muddier for Repeal) is an expected, informative outcome, not
  noise -- it mirrors the C4 lesson from the spike.

## 9. Sequencing and scope

- **Runs after** the US coding-manual pilot passes G1-G3 (`humour_epistemic_probe_coding_manual_pilot.md`).
  Calibrate the instrument on the dense-data culture first; transport it second.
- **In scope:** the recorded Irish proxies of Section 4, against the referendum axis of Section 3.
- **Out of scope:** the oral/unrecorded corpus (unmeasurable by design -- it is the SOC-HUM-5
  ceiling, not a task); the full cross-cultural matrix (US x EU x Ireland x worldwide); any
  `claims.yaml` promotion (governance's call -- SOC-HUM-5 is a **candidate** to register via the
  thought-intake / governance route, not registered by this design session).
