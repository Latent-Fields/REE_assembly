## 3. Key formulations

1. **The headline compass:** *staged topology and staged authority may matter more than
   continuous capability scaling.* Authority (when a subsystem is allowed to couple, commit,
   write durable residue, or release action) is a developmental variable in its own right,
   not a side-effect of capability maturity.

2. **Development as phase transition, not ramp.** Human structural connectivity reorganises in
   discrete epochs with sharp turning points (9/32/66/83), not by smooth monotonic improvement.
   REE's analogue: cognifold topology may need to be *reorganised* at transitions, not merely
   *accumulated*. REE currently models transitions as crossing a threshold on a continuous
   metric (curriculum gates) — a weaker notion than topology reorganisation.

3. **Stability is the longest epoch and is *entered* via the largest reorganisation.** The ~32
   turning point is the strongest of the lifespan, and adulthood is the longest, most stable
   epoch. Design intuition: reaching a stable adult REE regime may require the *biggest* single
   architectural reorganisation, after which the system holds a long plateau.

4. **The aging side is missing.** Brains keep reorganising after maturity (early-aging ~66,
   late-aging ~83), plausibly via modularisation / compartmentalisation. REE has no
   post-maturity developmental trajectory. Open whether this is degradation to be resisted or a
   *protective* compartmentalisation mode worth modelling deliberately.

5. **Epoch-indexed vulnerability.** Different developmental epochs carry different dominant
   failure risks (mis-coupling, premature over-commitment, residue-write errors). A maturation
   model could index *which* errors are likely *when*, complementing the epoch-agnostic
   psychiatric failure taxonomy.
