## 1. Verbatim prompt / core proposal

The thought is an explicit extension of `2026-08-12_responsibility_as_epistemic_hygiene.md`
(already ingested -- Stage 2 at `evidence/planning/thought_intake_2026-08-12_responsibility_as_epistemic_hygiene.md`,
registered INV-012 Leg 4 + Q-096, both already live in `claims.yaml`). Where the parent thought
asked how an agent should learn from evidence its *own* commitments helped produce, this thought
asks the multi-agent counterpart:

> **Core hypothesis:** In a world containing other agents, epistemic competence requires
> preserving the possibility that observations were strategically generated by another
> predictive system. A bounded prior for agency may therefore constitute epistemic hygiene:
> too little causes strategically generated evidence to be mistaken for passive world
> structure, while too much causes agency to be hallucinated in exogenous processes.
> Other-mind attribution may consequently serve not only social and ethical cognition, but
> causal provenance and reliable belief updating.

Mechanism, condensed: an observation should be classified among causal hypotheses `C_world`,
`C_self`, `C_other`, `C_joint`. Another agent is not just a cause but a *predictive* cause -- it
may select what it does specifically because it predicts what the observer will infer and do next.
**Truth of observation does not guarantee innocence of evidence**: X can be accurately perceived,
accurately remembered, and correctly attributed to the external world, yet still license an
incorrect inference because its *selection* was strategic. The same structure underlies deception,
teaching, warning, and cooperation alike -- the difference is goals and alignment, not the
existence of strategic evidence generation. The thought explicitly separates this epistemic
requirement from ethical standing (agent detection / ToM / sentience attribution / ethical
standing should not collapse into one binary), and sketches a developmental route: before mature
ToM, a young organism needs experience with *responsive* environmental entities (behaviour
covaries with the agent's own actions) to bootstrap other-agent recognition at all -- not merely
static hazards/resources.

## 3. Key formulations (verbatim, load-bearing)

> Truth of observation does not guarantee innocence of evidence.

> An epistemically competent organism may therefore need to represent not merely: *What
> happened?* but: *What kind of process caused this evidence to become available to me?*

> Not: *Everything has a mind.* Rather: **Some structures in my environment may have been
> produced by agents, and agency should remain among the candidate causal explanations when
> the evidence warrants it.**

> Too little agency attribution produces exploitable naivete. Too much produces intentional
> explanations for stochastic, mechanical or otherwise exogenous events. The computational
> problem is therefore not to eliminate animistic inference but to **calibrate it**.

> This does not require infinite recursion... A useful system may only need enough recursion
> to distinguish: *This happened.* from: *Something may have made this happen.* from:
> *Something may have made this happen because of what it expected me to do next.*

> The appropriate inference is therefore not: *Agent-generated evidence is unreliable.* It is:
> **Agent-generated evidence must be interpreted conditional on the agent that generated it
> and the likely reason it was generated.**

> Recognising another entity as an agent changes both: **how its actions should be interpreted
> epistemically** and **how its existence should be treated ethically.** These need not be
> identical thresholds.

> This suggests that REE may need experience with **responsive environmental entities**, not
> merely static hazards and resources, before sophisticated social inference can meaningfully
> develop. The environment therefore becomes part of the developmental problem.
