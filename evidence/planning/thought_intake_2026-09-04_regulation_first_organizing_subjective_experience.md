## Verbatim prompt (core proposal)

> How does a machine organize experience according to its own enduring regulatory constraints? ...
> for an organism, knowledge of the world may be developmentally downstream of regulation.
>
> **machine -> regulation -> interaction -> organized experience -> knowledge**
>
> **Constraints come first; meaning follows.** A machine is already a structured, vulnerable, acting
> system before it knows anything. Its earliest representational organization should therefore
> reflect what its structure makes consequential.

Applied to the live front:

> The `z_world` problem may therefore be mis-specified if treated primarily as a compression or
> feature-learning problem. A good latent representation is not merely one that reconstructs
> observations. It must retain the distinctions that matter for the organism's ability to predict
> and regulate itself across time.

And the design corollary (representation contract):

> `z_world` should not be asked to know what the universe *is* in advance. It should be required not
> to throw away what the organism repeatedly needs in order to discover what its world is.

The cluster is explicitly self-limiting on every axis: "a hypothesis to test, not an axiom to
protect"; "do not redesign `z_world` wholesale"; "no V3-EXQ identifier is assigned here"; "inspect the
live `ree-v3` implementation before adding architecture or assigning experiment identifiers"; and the
strongest red-team it names against itself (challenge 5) is that unsupervised compression plus generic
predictive learning may already preserve every behaviourally relevant distinction. This intake honours
all of that: it registers the organising hypothesis and its preservation invariant as `candidate`,
registers the one methodological rule the cluster adds to Assembly, and routes the experiment as a
gated chip rather than assigning an id.

## Key formulations (verbatim, load-bearing)

> The machine constrains things first. It functions before it knows. Experience is organized through
> regulation, and knowledge is what that organized experience can become.

> Information destroyed before E1/E2 cannot be reconstructed by better downstream reasoning. A
> representation can therefore be excellent at observation reconstruction while still being unsuitable
> as the organism's world substrate.

> A successful scaffold should be removable without destroying the acquired organization. If
> performance depends permanently on privileged labels at inference, the experiment has demonstrated
> supervision dependence rather than emergence of a useful world representation.

> If regulatory anchoring only beats the baseline but not these matched controls, the result should
> not be interpreted as evidence for the organising hypothesis.

> The self may first become computationally visible as the persistent source of some changes but not
> others.

> Is the system failing to preserve the right information, or failing to revise the categories through
> which that information is organised?

> A null result would be useful: it would argue that generic predictive learning already preserves the
> needed distinctions, reducing pressure to complicate `z_world`.
