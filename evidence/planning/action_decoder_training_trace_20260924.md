## Verdict (short)

1. **No loss anywhere in ree_core, and no loss in any of the ~39 experiment scripts that touch it, ever back-propagates through `action_object_decoder`.** Several scripts put its parameters into a training optimizer, but the loss graph they build never routes through it, so it receives `grad=None` and Adam silently skips it. It stays at random init in every driver checked. **This corrects ADDENDUM 3's own characterization** ("terrain_prior + decoder trained by behavioural cloning of E3's selections") -- the decoder is not actually trained even in that driver; only `terrain_prior` is.
2. `terrain_prior` **is** trained, but only by ad hoc, per-experiment-script optimizers doing E3 behavioural cloning (MSE against E3's own selected trajectory) -- never by anything native to `ree_core`. No flag turns on a production trainer for it.
3. The CEM candidate space **is** the same nominal action-object space `O` that `E2.action_object_head` (encoder) and `HippocampalModule.action_object_decoder` (decoder) both operate in, but the two are independently-initialized random maps that are never tied together by any loss, so decode(encode(a)) != a as a structural fact, not an approximation error. A near-zero-magnitude decoder input (the untrained `terrain_prior`'s near-zero output, or the CEM refit contracting samples toward that region) is why one class wins: at input magnitude near 0 a `Linear -> ReLU -> Linear` random MLP's output is dominated by its bias terms rather than by the input, so its argmax is close to constant across the whole input ball regardless of which action produced it.
4. **Classification: `complicated (buildable)`, not `complex (probe-gated)` or a `mystery`.** The defect is fully legible from the code and requires no spike to characterize further. A closely related row, **SD-080**, is already registered and `pending_implementation` -- but SD-080's scope is the **encoder** (`E2.action_object_head`) only; its own substrate/instrument section says explicitly "no loss in ree_core targets o_t" and cites only the encoder's two call sites. **No substrate_queue row or claim names the decoder (`HippocampalModule.action_object_decoder`) as its own repair target** -- the closest coverage is a descriptive note under ARC-018's `evidence_quality_note` (claims.yaml ~4594-4611) documenting the same round-trip defect, not a registered buildable node. Fixing SD-080 alone (training the encoder) does **not** repair the decoder; both sides of the codec are separately untrained.

## Q1: which module decodes CEM action-object latents to actions; is it trained; under what flags?

`ree_core/hippocampal/module.py`, `HippocampalModule.__init__`:
- `self.terrain_prior = nn.Sequential(Linear(terrain_input_dim, hidden_dim), ReLU(), Linear(hidden_dim, action_object_dim*horizon))` -- `:169-172`. `terrain_input_dim = world_dim*2 + 1 [+1 if benefit_terrain]` (`:167`). Deployed dims (`HippocampalConfig`, `config.py:2715-2718`): `world_dim=32, action_object_dim=16, hidden_dim=128, horizon=10` -> `Linear(65,128) -> ReLU -> Linear(128,160)`.
- `self.action_object_decoder = nn.Sequential(Linear(action_object_dim, hidden_dim), ReLU(), Linear(hidden_dim, action_dim))` -- `:178-181`. Deployed: `Linear(16,128) -> ReLU -> Linear(128, action_dim)` (`action_dim` default 4 in `HippocampalConfig`, `config.py:2716`; environment-set otherwise, e.g. 5 in ADDENDUM 3's C_100 cell).

**No loss, default-on or default-off, in `ree_core` ever touches either module's parameters.** `agent.py`'s full `compute_*_loss` inventory (`compute_prediction_loss, compute_benefit_eval_loss, compute_event_contrastive_loss, compute_resource_proximity_loss, compute_resource_field_loss, compute_resource_encoder_loss, compute_resource_identity_loss, compute_harm_nonredundancy_loss, compute_harm_accum_loss, compute_schema_readout_loss, compute_self_maintenance_loss, compute_e2_loss, compute_e2_world_loss` -- `agent.py:11595-12859`) contains nothing that reads `hippocampal.terrain_prior` or `hippocampal.action_object_decoder`; grepping all of `ree_core` for `action_object_decoder.parameters`/`terrain_prior.parameters`/`hippocampal.parameters` returns zero hits outside `experiments/`. There is also no `_lib` helper: `experiments/_lib/` (56 files) has none referencing either module.

**39 of 1548 experiment scripts** construct their own training loop for these two modules (grep `action_object_decoder\|terrain_prior` under `experiments/*.py`). All follow one of two patterns:

- **Pattern A -- "train" it (terrain_prior only, decoder silently excluded despite being in the optimizer).** Canonical instance: `experiments/v3_exq_042_hippocampal_terrain_training.py` (the driver named for exactly this purpose) and mirrored verbatim in `v3_exq_1061_mech131_anticipatory_residue_lesion.py` (the driver ADDENDUM 3 cited for "terrain_prior + decoder trained by behavioural cloning"), plus `v3_exq_043/044/046/053/054/055/190/190a/397/397a-d/445a`.
  - `terrain_optimizer = optim.Adam(list(terrain_prior.parameters()) + list(action_object_decoder.parameters()), lr=...)` -- `v3_exq_042:128-131`, `v3_exq_1061:411-422` (decoder's parameters ARE in the optimizer's param list).
  - But the loss is `ao_mean_pred = agent.hippocampal._get_terrain_action_object_mean(theta_z, e1_prior=...)` (`v3_exq_042:191-193`, `v3_exq_1061:483-485`) `; terrain_loss = F.mse_loss(ao_mean_pred, selected_ao.detach())` (`v3_exq_042:199`, `v3_exq_1061:491`) `; terrain_loss.backward(); terrain_optimizer.step()`.
  - `_get_terrain_action_object_mean` (`module.py:562-608`) calls **only** `self.terrain_prior(combined)` (`:607`) -- it never calls `self.action_object_decoder` anywhere in its body. So `terrain_loss`'s computation graph has no path through the decoder's weights; after `.backward()`, `action_object_decoder`'s `.grad` is `None` for every parameter; `optimizer.step()` (PyTorch `Adam`) skips any parameter whose `.grad is None`, so those weights are byte-identical to their random init after training, regardless of episode count. `v3_exq_042`'s own comment even says so: "Gradient flows through terrain_prior's own weights" (`:189-190`). **This directly corrects ADDENDUM 3's description of `v3_exq_1061` as training "terrain_prior + decoder"** -- the decoder is nominally in that driver's optimizer and never actually updated.
- **Pattern B -- read-only diagnostic use, no training at all.** `v3_exq_120/120a/114/114a/266/266a/266b/800/801`: `raw_logits = agent.hippocampal.action_object_decoder(first_ao)` (e.g. `v3_exq_266a:281`, `v3_exq_800:462`) followed only by `argmax` for an action-class readout, most inside or immediately preceded by `torch.no_grad()` (`v3_exq_266a:246`, `v3_exq_800:390/415/495`). No `.backward()` anywhere near these call sites.
- `v3_exq_124_mech033_kernel_chain_pair.py:365` (`hippo_opt = optim.Adam(agent.hippocampal.parameters(), lr=lr)`) is the one script that includes the decoder in a param list AND uses a whole-module optimizer without an isolating helper -- but this is scoped to that one driver, not checked further here (out of the brief's question; flagged as the one case worth a second look if this record is extended).

The decoder's only two live (non-diagnostic) call sites inside `ree_core` are both inference-only, inside the CEM proposal loop, never inside a loss: `module.py:2280` and `:2893` (`actions = self._decode_action_objects(action_objects_sample)`).

**Classification for Q1: loss exists (Pattern A, per-driver, ~13 of 39 scripts by this pattern), but it is a dead-weight inclusion -- the decoder's parameters are named in the optimizer and never receive a gradient. Net effect is identical to "no loss."** No production/default driver trains it; the flag that would matter (`use_action_class_scaffold_candidates`, see Q4) does not train anything -- it bypasses the decoder instead.

## Q2: terrain_prior -- re-confirm Worker A's "no loss trains it"

**Confirmed, with a correction of scope.** Worker A's claim (no loss trains `terrain_prior`) is correct for `ree_core` proper: no native waking or sleep loss reads `terrain_prior`. But it **is** trained, by the same Pattern-A experiment scripts above (`v3_exq_042` and its ~13 derivatives) -- `terrain_loss = F.mse_loss(ao_mean_pred, selected_ao.detach())`, gradient flowing cleanly through `terrain_prior`'s own weights only (verified above). This is E3 behavioural cloning: `terrain_prior` is trained to predict whatever `E3.select()` already chose, from the theta-averaged `z_world` and `e1_prior`. Two things worth flagging plainly:
- **It is circular/self-referential as an objective.** `terrain_prior`'s proposals shape which candidates enter the CEM refit and are eventually offered to `E3.select`; `E3.select`'s output is then the training target for `terrain_prior`. There is no external grounding signal (no environment reward, no harm/benefit outcome) in this loss -- only agreement with the agent's own prior choice. `E3` does weight candidates by the residue field (a real, environment-grounded signal), so the loop is not entirely closed on nothing, but `terrain_prior` never sees the outcome directly, only E3's already-residue-weighted pick.
- **What would train it "for real":** no flag exists to turn this on in a production driver. `use_action_class_scaffold_candidates` (`config.py:2741`, default `False`) does not train `terrain_prior` either -- it adds synthetic one-hot-class candidates alongside whatever `terrain_prior` proposes (`module.py:2547`, scaffold source tagged `_ACTION_CLASS_SCAFFOLD_SOURCE`, `:106`); it changes *which candidates exist*, not the prior's weights.

## Q3: is the CEM's action-object space the same space the decoder was fit on; what does the decoder's output depend on; why does 0 map to one class?

**Same nominal space, never actually tied together.** The loop, read directly off `module.py:2265-2280` (CEM inner sampling) and `e2_fast.py:141-147, 660-691` (encoder):

1. `action_objects_sample = ao_mean + ao_std * noise` -- a sample in `O` (`R^16`), from `terrain_prior`'s (trained, per Q2) mean or the prior CEM iteration's refit mean/std.
2. `actions = self._decode_action_objects(action_objects_sample)` (`:2280`) -- runs the **decoder** (untrained, per Q1) to turn the `O`-space sample into a candidate action sequence.
3. `traj = self.e2.rollout_with_world(z_self, z_world, actions, compute_action_objects=True, ...)` -- internally calls `E2.action_object(z_world_t, a_t)` (`e2_fast.py:660-691`, forward through `self.action_object_head`, `e2_fast.py:144-147, 691`) to **re-encode** each rolled-out action back into `O`, storing it as `traj.action_objects` (`e2_fast.py:53, 89-93`).
4. The next CEM iteration's elite refit computes a new `ao_mean`/`ao_std` from the **encoder's** `o_t` values of the elite trajectories (not from the decoder's inputs) -- so what gets refitted toward is always the encoder's output space, while what gets sampled from it is always decoded through the decoder.

So `O` is genuinely one shared 16-d space by construction (both modules' I/O dimension matches `action_object_dim=16`, `config.py:2716`), but `action_object_head` (encoder) and `action_object_decoder` (decoder) are two **independently-initialized random linear maps** with **no loss anywhere tying `decode(encode(a))` back to `a`**, or vice versa. `module.py`'s own docstring on `_decode_action_objects` states this as a design warning, not a possibility: "both halves are untrained and the action-object distribution is a small ball far from the decoder's decision boundaries, so the argmax pins to one constant class. The non-invertibility is a property of the COMPOSITION, not of this decoder" (`module.py:626-634`). This is independently corroborated in `claims.yaml` under ARC-018 (~line 4593-4611): "5 distinct CONSTRUCTED first actions all re-decode to 1 class, untrained and after 40 warmup episodes alike" -- i.e. even after the driver *has* run 40 warmup episodes of Pattern-A "training," the decoder is still at init, exactly as Q1 predicts, because that training never reaches it.

**Why 0 (or any near-zero-magnitude ball) maps to one class -- structural, not measured here:** `action_object_decoder` is `Linear(16,128) -> ReLU(...) -> Linear(128, action_dim)`. For an input `x` near `0`, the pre-activation of the first layer is dominated by its bias `b1` rather than by `W1 x` (which shrinks with `|x|`); `ReLU(b1 + W1 x)` therefore has a nearly input-independent active/inactive pattern across the whole small ball, and the second layer's output is then close to the constant vector `b2 + W2 ReLU(b1)` for every sample in that ball -- whichever class has the largest component of that near-constant vector wins the argmax regardless of which point in the ball was sampled. This is exactly what ADDENDUM 3 measured (R5a: replacing `terrain_prior`'s mean with literal zeros is bit-identical to native on 2/2 seeds; s43 -> class 1, s42 -> class 4, both fixed) and is architecture-level, not seed- or driver-specific: any untrained-decoder driver whose CEM mean stays close to 0 (true whenever `terrain_prior` is also untrained, and largely true even when it is Pattern-A-"trained" per Q2, since that loss never grows the decoder's *effective* input scale on its own) will reproduce it.

## Q4: work-graph classification, repair candidates (not chosen/built), existing coverage

**Classification: `complicated (buildable)`.** Every fact above was obtained by reading the code and existing measured records; nothing here needed a spike to characterize further (no `complex (probe-gated)`), and there is no missing fact about what the code does (no `puzzle (known rules)` or `mystery (known data)`) -- what is missing is a *decision* about which repair to build, which is governance work, not more diagnosis.

**Smallest honest repair candidates, named without choosing or building one:**
1. **Supervised decoder objective on executed `(o_t, a_t)` pairs.** Add `CE(action_object_decoder(o_t.detach()), a_t)` alongside the existing terrain-prior loss (Pattern A already computes `o_t` via the encoder inside rollout, so the executed pairs already exist per step). Smallest lever: does not touch `terrain_prior`, `action_object_head`, or CEM structure. Risk: still trains against whatever the (currently frozen, per SD-080) encoder produces, so it inherits the encoder's lack of structure unless SD-080 also lands.
2. **Tie `action_object_head` (encoder) and `action_object_decoder` jointly via a reconstruction/consistency loss** (`decode(encode(a)) ~ a`, or `encode(decode(o)) ~ o`), independent of E3's selection -- this is the direct fix for the "two untied random maps" mechanism in Q3, and would also partially discharge SD-080's "O is a frozen random projection" finding for the decoder's half, though SD-080 itself proposes a consequence-grounding loss on `o_t`, not a decoder-specific one.
3. **State-conditioned proposal start** (what ADDENDUM 3's brief originally expected before R5a ruled out the terrain-prior-mean mechanism): now known not to touch the actual defect -- R5a (zeroing the mean) was bit-identical to native, so the collapse is set by the decoder's fixed random geometry, not the mean's state-dependence. Any repair here should target the decoder or the encoder-decoder pairing (candidates 1-2), not the mean.
4. **Bypass the decoder structurally** (what `use_action_class_scaffold_candidates`/R5b already does tonight): not a training fix, a workaround -- injects one one-hot-per-class candidate so E3 has real class diversity to choose among, leaving `action_object_decoder` untrained and never called for those injected candidates' action determination. Already shipped and default-off; already known (per ADDENDUM 3) to be state-invariant itself.

**Existing coverage, checked read-only (`substrate_queue.json`, `claims.yaml`):**
- **SD-080** (`substrate_queue.json`, `status: pending_implementation`, `node_class: complicated (buildable)`) is the closest existing row. Its title and "SUBSTRATE / INSTRUMENT REQUIREMENT" are explicit and narrow: `E2.action_object_head` (the **encoder**) "receives zero gradient from every REE training path... no loss in ree_core targets o_t... action_object_head has exactly two references in ree_core -- construction and a forward call inside rollout." It does not mention `HippocampalModule.action_object_decoder` anywhere in its `title`, `implementation_hint`, or `evidence_quality_note` (checked in full). Depends on `SD-004, ARC-018, SD-056`; unblocks `MECH-516/517/518/523/532/537`.
- **No substrate_queue row or claim registers the decoder itself as a repair target.** Grepping `substrate_queue.json`'s 257-row `queue` for `action_object_decoder` returns zero hits. `claims.yaml` mentions `action_object_decoder` only descriptively, under `ARC-018`'s `evidence_quality_note` (~lines 4593-4611, the "RELATED, DISTINCT DEFECT" paragraph quoted in Q3 above) and in one unrelated `substrate_queue` entry about a *different*, not-yet-built discrete option-library codebook (line ~49563, explicitly contrasted with "the existing continuous SD-004 `action_object_decoder`" -- not a repair proposal for it).
- **Practical consequence for governance:** landing SD-080 alone (training the encoder) does not repair this edge -- the decoder would still be a separately-untrained random map receiving the now-trained encoder's output, and Q3's non-invertibility argument does not require *both* halves to be untrained, only that they are untied by any joint loss. A repair proposal for this edge should either explicitly extend SD-080's scope to include the decoder, or be registered as its own row that depends on SD-080.
