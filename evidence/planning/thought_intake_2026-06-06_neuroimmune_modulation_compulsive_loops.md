## 0. Source verification (resolves the raw note's "source-check pending")

The raw note recorded the `share.google` link as inaccessible and the exact title as not locatable. **Both
are now resolved.** Web search located the article:

- **Source (as saved):** Neuroscience News, *"Brain Immune Cells Drive Compulsive Behavior"* —
  <https://neurosciencenews.com/microglia-calcium-signaling-anxiety-ocd-30687/>
- **Underlying primary study:** Naveen Nagarajan (Univ. of Louisville School of Medicine, pediatrics /
  child neurology), published **2026-04-13 in *Molecular Psychiatry*** (Nature Publishing Group).
- **Finding:** internal **calcium (Ca²⁺) signaling in Hoxb8 microglia** acts as a biological switch for
  core anxiety- and OCD-spectrum symptoms. Optogenetics: raising intracellular Ca²⁺ in Hoxb8 microglia
  (light-activated) **immediately triggered** anxiety and excessive grooming; blocking Ca²⁺ entry
  (ChRmine) **prevented** the anxiety-related behaviours — i.e. bidirectional, causal control. Proposed as
  a therapeutic entry point for anxiety/OCD outside classical neuronal/cognitive targets.
- **Corroborating coverage:** News-Medical, ZME Science, TUN, University of Utah Health newsroom (the
  Frontiers 2018 review is background on microglia as behavioural regulators, not this study).

**Verification status: LOCATED + PRESS-SUMMARY-VERIFIED.** The peer-reviewed *Molecular Psychiatry*
article itself was not read directly (abstract not fetched during intake); the mechanism above is
corroborated across four independent secondary sources reporting the same study, author, journal and date.

**Precision guardrail (important):** the paper's mechanism is **microglial intracellular Ca²⁺ signaling**,
which the optogenetic results show acting on a **fast** (immediate) behavioural timescale — *not* a
demonstrated slow tonic process. The "slow background modulation" framing in the raw note is the **REE-side
abstraction** the saved item prompted, not a claim of the paper. Keep these separate: the citable finding
is "a non-neuronal (glial/immune) cell-internal signal causally gates compulsive grooming"; the REE-useful
extraction is the broader "non-symbolic substrate-state modulators can bias compulsive-loop persistence."

> / internal state pressure; slow background modulation; psychiatric failure-axis taxonomy.

## 3. Key formulations (extracted, REE-framed)

1. **Three-layer compulsion decomposition.** Fast loop mechanics (action→relief→prediction→decommit) /
   represented belief-or-threat content / slow field-state modulation. REE currently models layers 1–2
   richly and layer 3 only as monoamine/peptide gain (SD-036/SD-037); the thought asks whether layer 3 is
   under-specified for *stickiness* specifically.
2. **Compulsion-risk readout.** `compulsion_risk = f(loop_reinforcement, threat_salience,
   residue_persistence, decommit_friction, slow_modulatory_state)` — a candidate diagnostic that composes
   existing REE quantities rather than introducing new substrate.
3. **Non-cognitive route to stickiness.** A loop can be hard to release because the *field is biased*, not
   because the goal is endorsed — the slow modulator changes the threshold/decommit-friction landscape, not
   the value estimate. This is the discriminative claim worth holding.
4. **Glial = substrate-state, not biology (for AI).** The cross-repo analogue is slow substrate-state
   modulation (context-window pressure, memory weighting, tool-retry pressure, unresolved alerts, resource
   constraints), explicitly *not* a biological immune system.
