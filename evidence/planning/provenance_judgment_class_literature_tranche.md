## 1. Verdict

**The corrected row survives the sweep, and must be narrowed in three ways. The three papers are not cherry-picked; the generalisation drawn from them is over-stated.**

The supplement (§5) corrected the 2026-09-09 evidence map's row `dependent descendants become independent "votes"` from *not directly established* to *directly established in healthy adults*. A proper sweep of the judgment/testimony class confirms the row is real, replicated across labs and paradigms, and if anything better supported than three papers suggested — Xie & Hayes 2022 finds the same insensitivity in an abstract balls-and-urns task with no news or consensus framing at all, which removes the obvious "it's a media-literacy artifact" objection.

But three findings the targeted query could not have surfaced qualify what the row licenses:

1. **The effect is paradigm-bound, and it fails in the paradigm closest to REE's actual construct.** O'Donnell et al. 2023 orthogonally manipulated repetition and source variability in eyewitness suggestibility and found **no source-variability effect** in two experiments and a small meta-analysis (k=8). REE's hypothesis is about *memory* provenance; the memory-misinformation literature does not show cardinality inflation.
2. **A fluency account is a live rival to a cardinality account, and both source papers for the repetition half endorse it.** Weaver 2007 attributes its own effect to opinion *accessibility*, explicitly "rather than a conscious inference about the meaning of opinion repetition"; O'Donnell 2023 concludes participants "respond based on retrieval fluency rather than source-specifying information". If the human effect runs through fluency, it is not evidence that a genealogy representation is being consulted and misread — which is exactly what P1 tests.
3. **Treating dependent descendants as fewer votes is not straightforwardly the normative ideal.** Pilditch, Hahn, Fenton & Lagnado 2020 prove by construction that dependence is not simply redundancy: where a structural dependency exists but observations are partial or contradicting, dependent reports can provide *more* support than independent ones. The REE engineering rule needs restating against this, not merely asserting.

**Net for the branch:** the correction stands, and P1's value goes **up**, not down. The supplement concluded P1 is now only worth running "for the route". The sweep sharpens that: the human literature may not involve an ancestry representation at all, so the route is nearly the whole content, and P1 needs one control the supplement does not specify (§6.1).

## 3. The judgment class, swept

### 3.1 The core effect and its cross-paradigm reach

**Yousif, Aboody & Keil 2019**, Psychol Sci 30(8):1195–1204, [DOI 10.1177/0956797619856844](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797619856844), PMID 31291546. Corrigendum: Psychol Sci 2020;31(8):1053, [DOI 10.1177/0956797620948511](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797620948511), PMID 32721273. Four experiments; participants equally confident in true and false consensus, robust even immediately after explicitly endorsing that true consensus is more believable. 45 forward citations.

**Xie & Hayes 2022**, *Sensitivity to Evidential Dependencies in Judgments Under Uncertainty*, Cogn Sci 46(5):e13144, [DOI 10.1111/cogs.13144](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.13144), PMID 35579865. **The strongest new support for the row, and it was not in the supplement's three.** Three experiments using a social balls-and-urns task: informants gave either independent testimony or dependent-sequential testimony. Belief updating was "generally insensitive to evidential dependency" (Exps 1–2). Exp 3 separated participants by their stated beliefs about the relative value of independent evidence; only those who already held independent evidence to be more valuable then weighted it accordingly. The authors' own summary: **only a minority of individuals conform to Bayesian predictions** in the relative weighting of independent and dependent evidence.

This matters more than its citation count suggests. It strips the effect of every feature that made the consensus paradigm look domain-specific — no news outlets, no political content, no naturalistic source structure — and finds it anyway, in the canonical formal-inference task. The row is not an artifact of media framing.

**Weaver, Garcia, Schwarz & Miller 2007**, J Pers Soc Psychol 92(5):821–833, [DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.92.5.821](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.92.5.821), PMID 17484607. Six experiments; repeated expression by one group member inflates estimated opinion prevalence, holding "even when perceivers are consciously aware that the opinions come from 1 speaker". 100+ forward citations, overwhelmingly downstream applications in misinformation and consumer research rather than replications. **Mechanism explicitly attributed to accessibility, not inference** — see §1.2 and §4.

### 3.2 The moderator structure is deeper than "ambiguity"

**Connor Desai, Xie & Hayes 2022**, Cognition 223:105023, [DOI 10.1016/j.cognition.2022.105023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2022.105023), PMID 35149359. The supplement's reading is accurate: the illusion appears when the independence relation is ambiguous and attenuates when independence between primary sources is highlighted and measured against a no-consensus baseline. Authors' conclusion: "although the illusion of consensus is prevalent, people do have the capacity to distinguish".

**Connor Desai, Fai, Lee & Hayes 2026**, *Explaining away the illusion of consensus*, Mem Cognit 54(5):1667–1687, [DOI 10.3758/s13421-025-01831-9](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-025-01831-9), PMID 41563579, PMC13407448. **New, and it moves the moderator account on.** Four experiments, political poll-reporting task, news outlets citing the same versus different polling companies. Independent consensus was more persuasive than dependent consensus — and the persuasiveness of *dependent* consensus was raised or lowered by positive ("the source is especially reliable") or negative ("repetition was intended to sway opinion") explanations for the repetition, whether supplied by the experimenter or generated by participants themselves.

**Read this carefully: it is not a failed replication of Yousif 2019.** The 2026 design makes ancestry explicit ("citing the same polling company"), which is the condition under which Connor Desai 2022 already predicted discrimination. What it adds is that the dependent-consensus discount is not a fixed quantity: it is an *inference about why the repetition happened*, movable in both directions. Not preregistered (stated by the authors).

**Madsen, Hahn & Pilditch 2020**, *The impact of partial source dependence on belief and reliability revision*, J Exp Psychol Learn Mem Cogn 46(9):1795–1805, [DOI 10.1037/xlm0000846](https://doi.org/10.1037/xlm0000846), PMID 32437188. Participants' reliability revision tracked the Bovens & Hartmann model, including the U-shaped trajectory, **and** appropriately decreased perceived reliability once sources were known to be partially dependent — both when a specific shared cause was observed and when integrating the mere possibility of shared reliability. A clean demonstration that the discounting machinery exists and is deployed when dependence is given.

**Richardson, Davis & Keil 2025**, *Agenda Setting and The Emperor's New Clothes*, Open Mind 9:2005–2030, [DOI 10.1162/OPMI.a.258](https://doi.org/10.1162/OPMI.a.258), PMID 41497523. Three experiments plus a model: people reason about information cascades using informants' speaking order and social status, and their trust in public consensus tracks how well they believe it reflects informants' private beliefs.

**Young, Madsen & de-Wit 2025**, *Belief polarization can be caused by disagreements over source independence*, Cognition 259:106126, [DOI 10.1016/j.cognition.2025.106126](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2025.106126), PMID 40121821. Preregistered experiment (N=351) plus UK (N=507) and preregistered US (N=300) samples. Perceived source independence is a live, individually varying quantity with large partisan effects (d = 0.87 UK, d = 0.82 US), and those perceptions mediate polarization.

**Together these are a coherent moderator structure, and it is the opposite of a lesion.** The discrimination capacity is intact, sensitive to explicit ancestry, to explanations for repetition, to speaking order and status, and to perceived independence as a graded belief. What is absent is its *default deployment* under ambiguity.

### 3.3 Normative counterweights — the row does not license the engineering rule as stated

**Pilditch, Hahn, Fenton & Lagnado 2020**, *Dependencies in evidential reports: The case for informational advantages*, Cognition 204:104343, [DOI 10.1016/j.cognition.2020.104343](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2020.104343), PMID 32599310. Mathematical proof plus worked examples: the traditional treatment of dependence as redundancy conflates the *structure* of a dependency network with the *observations* across it. Where a structural dependency exists but observations are partial or contradicting, those observations support a hypothesis **more** than the independent case would.

**Pfänder, De Courson & Mercier 2025**, *How wise is the crowd*, Cognition 255:106005, [DOI 10.1016/j.cognition.2024.106005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2024.106005), PMID 39603232. Simulations plus 6 experiments (N=1197): inferring accuracy and competence from convergence is *normatively defensible* over a wide parameter range when estimates are independent and unbiased, participants make that inference, and — importantly — they weaken it when informants are described as systematically biased.

**Orticio, Martí & Kidd 2022**, *Social Prevalence Is Rationally Integrated in Belief Updating*, Open Mind 6:77–87, [DOI 10.1162/opmi_a_00056](https://doi.org/10.1162/opmi_a_00056), PMID 36439065. Prevalence effects on belief endorsement scaled with prior uncertainty as a Bayesian cue-integration framework predicts.

**Hahn 2024**, *Individuals, Collectives, and Individuals in Collectives: The Ineliminable Role of Dependence*, Perspect Psychol Sci 19(2):418–431, [DOI 10.1177/17456916231198479](https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916231198479), PMID 38010950. Review-level argument that several collective phenomena taken as evidence of irrationality stop being surprising once the dependencies governing normal social information acquisition are modelled.

**Consequence for REE.** The branch's engineering rule — *a hypothesis must not cite its own descendants as independent witnesses* — is correct in its negative form and wrong if read as "descendants should be collapsed to one vote". Pilditch 2020 is a proof that the collapse is sometimes the larger error. The defensible restatement is about **representation, not discounting**:

> A system must represent the dependency structure among its evidence and condition on it. It must not *infer* independence from the absence of a recorded dependency.

This is a stronger rule than the original and it is the one P2 actually tests.

### 3.4 Representativeness, stated honestly

Not cherry-picked: the sweep found convergent support in an unrelated task (Xie & Hayes 2022), a review-level treatment (Hahn 2024), and a large preregistered multi-sample study using perceived independence as an individual-difference variable (Young 2025). No study located contradicts the core row *within the judgment class*.

Two concentration caveats worth recording. Three of the primary sources (Connor Desai 2022; Xie & Hayes 2022; Connor Desai 2026) come from one group (Hayes, UNSW), and the dependence-revision and normative work clusters in another (Hahn / Madsen / Pilditch / Lagnado). The row rests on two labs plus Yousif/Keil and Weaver. That is normal for a young literature and is not a reason to discount it, but it is a reason not to describe it as broadly replicated.

## 4. The boundary case, and why it is the important one

**O'Donnell, Chan, Foster & Garry 2023**, *Experimental and meta-analytic evidence that source variability of misinformation does not increase eyewitness suggestibility independently of repetition of misinformation*, Front Psychol 14:1201674, [DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1201674](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1201674), PMID 37691811.

Two experiments orthogonally manipulating repetition and source variability, plus meta-analyses (k=19 repetition; k=8 source variability). Repetition increased suggestibility. **Source variability did not** — not in written transcripts, and not in Experiment 2 where each witness was made visibly and audibly distinct to raise source salience. The meta-analytic results agreed with the experiments. The authors' conclusion is a fluency account: participants respond to retrieval fluency, not to source-specifying information.

This is the single most consequential source in the tranche, for three reasons.

1. **It is the nearest paradigm to REE's construct and it points the other way.** The provenance branch is about memory descendants of one event being counted as several witnesses. This experiment presents misinformation from one versus several witnesses and measures its influence on memory. Cardinality did nothing; repetition did everything.
2. **It converges with Weaver 2007's own mechanism attribution.** The two studies that most directly manipulate *number of apparent sources* while holding content fixed both conclude the driver is accessibility/fluency rather than an inference over source count. The cardinality reading of the corrected row is therefore under-determined by the very studies that establish it.
3. **It supplies the rival hypothesis P1 has to beat**, and the supplement's four refinements (P1-R1 … P1-R4) do not contain a control for it. See §6.1.

Boundary honestly stated in the other direction too: this is eyewitness suggestibility with a misinformation-acceptance readout, not a confidence-calibration or source-count readout, and a null at k=8 is a small meta-analysis. It does not overturn the C3 result; it bounds it.

## 5. The clinical question

### 5.1 Has the consensus-illusion / source-independence paradigm ever been run in psychosis?

**No study located. The absence is now a properly searched absence rather than an unsearched one, and it should be recorded as the tranche's most actionable finding.**

Searched, all returning nothing on-target: `consensus independence schizophrenia delusion` (0 hits); `delusion proneness testimony source independence` (0); `schizotypy source credibility testimony` (0); `paranoia conspiracy belief epistemic trust testimony` (0); `psychosis misinformation repetition belief` (0); `schizotypy consensus social learning informant` (0); `wisdom of crowds psychosis` (0); `source independence delusion belief updating` (0). Forward-citation graphs of Yousif 2019 (45 citations) and Weaver 2007 (100) were read in full; neither contains a clinical psychosis study. The single clinically-flavoured citation of Yousif 2019 is Greenburgh et al. 2022 (below), which is not the paradigm.

**The three nearest neighbours, none of which is the experiment:**

- **Greenburgh, Liefgreen, Bell & Raihani 2022**, *Factors affecting conspiracy theory endorsement in paranoia*, R Soc Open Sci 9(1):211555, [DOI 10.1098/rsos.211555](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.211555), PMID 35116159. Preregistered, n=1000. Paranoia predicted conspiracy endorsement, especially for intentional and self-referential harm. Relevant negative detail: participants endorsing conspiracy items also judged that similar others would endorse them, **and this consensus-projection effect was not amplified in paranoia, counter to the authors' prediction**. A null on a consensus-estimation variable in paranoia — suggestive, and no substitute for the manipulation.
- **Wellstein et al. 2020**, *Inflexible social inference in individuals with subclinical persecutory delusional tendencies*, Schizophr Res 215:344–351, [DOI 10.1016/j.schres.2019.08.031](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.schres.2019.08.031), PMID 31495701. N=151 split on Freeman's Paranoia Checklist; probabilistic advice-taking under dispositional versus situational framing. High scorers were less sensitive to the attributional frame. The manipulated variable is the adviser's **intention**, not source **independence** — adjacent architecture, different question.
- **Moritz et al. 2012**, PMID 22683551 — §5.2.

**This is a clean, well-specified gap.** The reframed question of supplement §5.2 — whether the discrimination capacity that Connor Desai 2022 shows is recoverable under explicit cueing is selectively unavailable or merely not deployed in psychosis — maps onto an existing, validated, cheap paradigm that has simply never been given to a clinical sample. It is answerable, and the moderator structure in §3.2 tells you exactly which conditions to run: ambiguous ancestry versus explicit ancestry, with the explicit condition as the within-subject test of whether the capacity is present but undeployed.

### 5.2 Moritz 2012 replication status: **not replicated; no clinical follow-up in 13 years**

Moritz, Köther, Woodward, Veckenstedt, Dechêne & Stahl 2012, J Behav Ther Exp Psychiatry 43(4):1058–1063, [DOI 10.1016/j.jbtep.2012.04.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbtep.2012.04.004), PMID 22683551. Forward-citation graph read in full: **15 citations, none a replication attempt in a clinical sample**, and no other illusory-truth-in-schizophrenia study exists in PubMed (`illusory truth schizophrenia` returns this paper alone). The citations are conceptual (Corlett's doxastic shear pin, 2016), methodological (remote cognitive assessment scoping review, 2022), or general truth-effect work with no clinical arm.

Two further reasons it cannot bear load, on top of the limits the authors themselves stated (online recruitment, unvalidated diagnoses, no psychiatric control):

- **Calio, Nadarevic & Musch 2025**, *Assessing the truth effect's reliability and test–retest stability*, Conscious Cogn 135:103923, [DOI 10.1016/j.concog.2025.103923](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2025.103923), PMID 40876151, examines split-half reliability and test–retest stability of the truth effect precisely because individual-difference correlates of it have been scarce and mixed. Moritz 2012's load-bearing result is an individual-difference claim (an *excessive* truth effect in patients high on positive symptoms, for delusion-relevant emotional items) — the class of claim whose measurement reliability is under active question.
- It manipulates repetition of the *same statement*, with no ancestry variable, as the supplement already notes.

**Recommendation: cite Moritz 2012 as the only located clinical repetition-belief bridge and as unreplicated, and do not let any assay design depend on its individual-difference finding.**

## 6. Consequences for the P1/P3 design chips

Addressed to `chip-20260910-provenance-harness-design-v2` (P1, generated ancestry loss) and `chip-20260910-provenance-p3-replay-design-v2` (P3, replay amplification). **No artifact of either chip is edited here.** The corrected row is **qualified, not overturned** — neither chip's premise fails, but each acquires one requirement it did not have.

### 6.1 P1 — premise holds; one new mandatory control (fluency versus cardinality)

The supplement's P1-R1 (descendants generated, not stipulated), R2 (adaptive-linking positive control), R3 (legitimate-computation control) and R4 (independent-observation control) all stand.

**Missing: a fluency/accessibility control.** Weaver 2007 and O'Donnell 2023 both conclude that the human over-weighting of apparently multiple sources runs on retrieval fluency rather than on a representation of source count. If REE's architecture shows confidence inflation under `FALSE_SPLIT`, the fluency rival must already be excluded by design, because it will otherwise explain the result — a corrupted genealogy that produces four separately-retrievable descendants also produces four retrieval events.

Required arm, in the P1 idiom: hold the ancestry representation **intact and correct** while varying the number of retrieval/reinstatement events over the same descendant set. Predeclared signatures:

```text
cardinality effect:  confidence tracks represented source COUNT,
                     flat across retrieval-event count at fixed count
fluency effect:      confidence tracks RETRIEVAL EVENTS,
                     present even with ancestry correctly represented
```

An architecture in which these are not separable has the same interpretive problem the human literature has, and a positive `FALSE_SPLIT` result would be uninterpretable.

**A second, smaller consequence.** P1's scientific value is higher than the supplement concluded, and for a reason worth stating in the design: the human baseline may not be a genealogy phenomenon at all. If the human effect is fluency, then "can corrupting an internal genealogy representation inflate evidence cardinality?" is not merely the *route* to a known endpoint (supplement §5.3) — it is an open question about whether the endpoint is reachable that way in any system. That raises the information value of both a positive and a negative result.

### 6.2 P3 — premise strengthened, and Break B needs one correction

The supplement records step 5 (recursive amplification across replay) as "untested in every class". **That is right for cardinality and wrong for strength**, and the distinction changes P3's control structure.

- **Corlett et al. 2013**, *Ketamine effects on memory reconsolidation favor a learning model of delusions*, PLoS One 8(6):e65088, [DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0065088](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0065088), PMID 23776445. Human, placebo-controlled, within-subjects (n=18). A single unreinforced re-presentation of a conditioned stimulus **under ketamine** produced a stronger memory 24h later than under placebo; the degree of strengthening correlated with individual vulnerability to ketamine's psychotogenic effects and with prediction-error brain signal. Partially replicated in an independent appetitive sample (n=8).
- **Honsberger, Taylor & Corlett 2015**, *Memories reactivated under ketamine are subsequently stronger*, Schizophr Res 164(1–3):227–233, [DOI 10.1016/j.schres.2015.02.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.schres.2015.02.009), PMID 25728834. Rodent homologue: pre- but not post-reactivation ketamine enhanced fear memory at 24h, absent at 3h, not generalising to a closely related contextual memory, and blocked by prior inhibition of a BLA destabilisation mechanism.

So a **reactivation-without-new-evidence → increased memory strength** effect exists, in two species, with a direct psychotomimetic link and a named mechanism (aberrant prediction error during reconsolidation). Break B stands **for cardinality**: neither study measures effective source count or calibration, and neither concerns ancestry. But P3 can no longer be described as having no evidence in any direction for anything in its region.

**What this requires of P3.** The supplement's P3-R2 mandates a healthy-replay arm in which replay improves fidelity while source count stays flat. Corlett/Honsberger supply a *third* possibility that neither the dangerous signature nor the healthy arm covers: replay raises confidence by **strengthening the representation**, with no ancestry involvement and no cardinality change, and does so more under conditions that model psychosis. P3 must be able to distinguish:

```text
strength route:      confidence up, effective source count FLAT, calibration degrades
                     (Corlett/Honsberger analogue -- no ancestry variable)
cardinality route:   confidence up, effective source count RISES, calibration degrades
                     (the branch's H2)
fidelity route:      confidence up, count flat, calibration IMPROVES
                     (healthy replay, P3-R2)
```

A replay-count main effect that raises confidence and degrades calibration is consistent with the strength route alone and is **not** evidence for H2. Since the ancestry × replay-count interaction is already P3's declared falsifier, this mostly hardens an existing commitment — but it names the specific confound and gives it a literature anchor and a predicted signature.

## 7. Recommendations to the ladder (recommendations only — no edit made)

1. **P1:** add the fluency-versus-cardinality control of §6.1 as a required arm alongside P1-R1…R4.
2. **P2:** the promotion of `ABSENT`/`SOFT` over `FALSE_SPLIT` (supplement §9) is confirmed and can be stated more strongly — ambiguity is the empirically identified trigger. Add a fourth comparator suggested by Connor Desai 2026: **explained** dependence, where the system is given a reason for the repetition. Human discounting moves in both directions on that reason, so an architecture whose response to dependence is a fixed discount already diverges from the human pattern.
3. **P6:** Pilditch 2020 joins E30 as a named competitor, and is a different *kind* of competitor — E30 shows lineage-free tracking can match explicit genealogy, whereas Pilditch shows maximal discounting of dependent evidence is sometimes normatively **worse** than treating it as independent. P6's scoring should therefore include a structure in which dependent-but-partial observations are more informative than independent ones, so that an over-discounting architecture is penalised rather than rewarded.
4. **Engineering rule:** adopt the §3.3 restatement (represent the structure and condition on it; never infer independence from an absent dependency record) in place of any formulation implying descendants collapse to one vote.
5. **Clinical:** the §5.1 gap is the branch's cheapest external test and does not need REE substrate. Recorded here as an observation, not a proposal — this tranche queues nothing.

## 8. What this tranche does NOT establish

- Nothing here is evidence about psychosis. §3 and §4 are healthy-adult studies; §5.1 is a documented absence; §5.2 is one weak, unreplicated study.

## Compact statement

> The corrected row holds: under ancestry ambiguity, healthy adults do not discount dependent testimony, and the sweep strengthens this by finding the same insensitivity in an abstract inference task with no consensus framing. Three qualifications follow. The effect is bounded — orthogonally manipulating source variability against repetition in eyewitness memory produces no source-variability effect at all. Its mechanism is under-determined — the two studies that most directly vary apparent source number both attribute their effect to retrieval fluency rather than to any inference over source count. And its normative reading is wrong as usually stated — dependent evidence is provably sometimes worth *more* than independent evidence, so the branch's rule must be about representing dependency structure, not about discounting descendants. For the assays this means P1 needs a fluency control it does not have and is worth more than the audit concluded, and P3 must separate a replay-strengthens-the-memory route — which does exist, in humans and rodents, with a psychotomimetic link — from the cardinality route it is actually testing. The consensus-illusion paradigm has still never been run in psychosis; after eight query formulations and two full citation graphs, that absence is now searched rather than assumed, and it is the cheapest external test the branch has.
