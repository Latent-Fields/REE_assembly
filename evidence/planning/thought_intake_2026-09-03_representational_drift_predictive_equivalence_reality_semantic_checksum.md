## Verbatim core proposal

> Scientific claims can be represented in multiple coordinate systems, but representational equivalence should be earned empirically rather than assumed linguistically. Two views remain the same claim only within a declared envelope where their experimentally relevant predictions remain compatible. Rotations may sharpen prediction without changing truth state; when they require new empirical assumptions or diverge under discriminating intervention, the difference must be made explicit. Reality functions as a semantic checksum by exposing where apparently equivalent representations stop constraining the same world.

> When a claim stalls, rotate it. When two rotations disagree, experiment where the disagreement is sharpest. Let reality tell us whether we changed coordinates or changed the claim.

The thought is a governance/epistemics thought, not a scientific claim about the organism. It is the direct follow-on to the claim-rotation thought (GOV-ROTATE-1) and supplies the question that one leaves open: **how do we know a rotated claim is still the same claim?** Its answer is prediction under intervention over a declared domain, not paraphrase.

## Key formulations (verbatim, load-bearing)

> Empirical equivalence is not absolute. It has an envelope.

> predictive equivalence != predictive precision

> A representation may expose consequences of a claim, but it may not smuggle new empirical assumptions into the old truth state.

> A useful semantic checksum therefore asks not only whether two representations predict the same things, but which auxiliary assumptions are required to make them do so.

> When two representations appear equivalent observationally, look for an intervention under which their implied causal structure differs.

> Representational equivalence earns its scope by surviving discriminating contact with the world.

> Where do the current representations disagree most sharply while remaining experimentally tractable?

> rotate freely, but keep the rotations answerable to the same world.

Section 16's two candidate principles are registered essentially verbatim as the titles of GOV-EQUIV-1 and GOV-SHARPEN-1.

### GOV-EQUIV-1 -- drafted `what_would_answer`

```
NOT an empirical pass/fail falsifier -- GOV-EQUIV-1 is a governance_rule about how the Assembly
decides whether two representations of one claim may share a truth state. It specifies the
REASSESSMENT TRIGGER, the recorded-trial evidence that would inform it, and what would revise
it. No EXP-#### is to be minted against it.

NON-DEGENERACY PRECONDITION: at least one canonical claim must carry two representations that
are BOTH in live use with an explicitly declared equivalence envelope (contexts, interventions,
observables, scales, auxiliary assumptions, known divergence points), so that a discriminating
intervention inside that envelope is possible. As of registration the only recorded rotation is
INV-088 architectural vs transformation (GOV-ROTATE-1 note (v), V3-EXQ-978 -> 1002) and it
carries NO declared envelope, so this rule currently has nothing to act on; a "trial" run before
any envelope is declared is vacuous and must not be counted either way.

TRIGGER: (a) the next rotation recorded under GOV-ROTATE-1 whose author declares an envelope;
(b) any session about to credit a result obtained under one representation to the canonical
claim as read through another representation; (c) opportunistically, a retrospective search of
claims.yaml for a pair of formulations that DID share a truth state and were later shown not to
(this search is the first owed trial and has not been run).

CONFIRMING (the rule does work): a recorded case in which (i) two representations were treated as
the same claim, (ii) a discriminating intervention within the declared envelope produced
incompatible predictions, and (iii) applying the classification step (scope condition / hidden
auxiliary assumption / underspecified parent / invalid rotation / new proposition) CHANGED what
evidence was allowed to transfer -- i.e. without the rule a result under R_B would have been
credited to C as read under R_A, and with the rule it was withheld or split. The strongest form
is retrospective: a historical pair in claims.yaml where evidence was in fact mis-transferred and
the rule, applied at the time, would have withheld it. A weaker confirming signal, for the
generative corollary: an envelope's named divergence point becoming the next queued experiment.

FALSIFYING / "UNNECESSARY" (source thought section 17): after >=3 rotations with declared
envelopes, (i) no divergence ever appears inside a declared envelope (the envelope never withholds
anything -- paperwork without decisions), or (ii) every divergence that did appear was already
caught by GOV-ROTATE-1's safeguard plus ordinary claim-splitting without the predictive criterion,
or (iii) sessions consistently skip declaring envelopes because the per-rotation cost is judged
too high (the same "honest counterweight" GOV-HELDOUT-1 records for itself).
"MISLEADING": a case where the checksum PASSED -- predictions compatible over the declared
envelope -- and the claim was nonetheless laundered, because observational agreement was
mistaken for causal equivalence or an undeclared intervention was the one that mattered. One
such case does not demote the rule but must be recorded as a scope failure of the envelope
concept; two argue for retaining the idea as a reasoning note only, per the source thought's own
success criterion ("Does explicitly checking predictive equivalence prevent semantic drift and
generate better discriminating experiments without multiplying governance overhead? If not,
retain the idea as a reasoning note rather than a formal rule.").

WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY REVISE: the dependency on GOV-ROTATE-1 is load-bearing -- if GOV-ROTATE-1 is
demoted under its own criterion (iv), this rule has no object and follows it. Independently, the
"unnecessary" branch above demotes the PRACTICE (declare envelopes, classify divergences before
transfer) toward a reasoning note without touching the underlying observation that paraphrase is
an unreliable test of claim identity, which stands on the 978/1002/1008 lineage regardless.

READER NOTE: do not read the predictive-processing resemblance in the source thought as evidence
for this rule (GOV-ANALOGY-1); the 978 -> 1002 -> 1008 lineage is the ILLUSTRATION of what the
rule names, run without it, and is not a trial of it.
```

### GOV-SHARPEN-1 -- drafted `what_would_answer`

```
NOT an empirical pass/fail falsifier -- GOV-SHARPEN-1 is a governance_rule (a judgement rule
applied when a session drafts a rotated view, a lettered successor, or a child claim that is
strictly more precise than its parent). It specifies the reassessment trigger, the recorded-trial
evidence, and what would revise it. No EXP-#### is to be minted against it.

NON-DEGENERACY PRECONDITION: a sharpening case must actually arise -- a new formulation that
names a sign, ordering, conditional dependence, timescale, locus, developmental window, or
inequality the parent did not -- AND the "accepted background B" must be fixable
non-circularly at that moment: B = what the registry already holds as stable/provisional or
as an ARC commitment the parent depends_on, plus the substrate as built; never the drafting
session's own plausibles. If B cannot be fixed without argument, the test is degenerate for that
case and must be recorded as such rather than forced.

TRIGGER: (a) the FIRST OWED TRIAL, per this claim's note (iii): a GOV-HELDOUT-1 check against
three historical split / no-split decisions the rule was not written from -- the MECH-074 ->
MECH-074a-d split (2026-04-21), the z_gamma -> z_self / z_world split, and the 2026-06-11
stance-split hygiene pass -- asking for each whether the C + B vs C + A test reproduces the call
the registry actually made, counting only cases where "split" and "keep as refinement" would
have given DIFFERENT answers (GOV-HELDOUT-1 non-degeneracy guard); (b) prospectively, any
session drafting a sharper formulation under GOV-ROTATE-1 or a lettered successor claim.

CONFIRMING: (i) the held-out check reproduces the registry's actual decision on >=2 of the 3
historical cases AND those decisions were later borne out (no evidence mis-credited, no
refinement orphaned from its parent's record); (ii) a prospective case where the rule PREVENTED a
fragmentation -- a sharper view kept on the parent's evidence record where the "when in doubt,
new letter" default would have minted an evidence-less candidate -- or FORCED a split by
surfacing an assumption A that a rotation was carrying unlabelled.

FALSIFYING: (i) the rule gives the WRONG call on >=2 of the 3 held-out cases, where "wrong" means
it would have merged something the registry correctly split (or split something correctly kept)
and the registry's decision was later vindicated by evidence; (ii) B cannot be fixed
non-circularly in practice -- sessions applying the rule to the same case disagree about what
counts as accepted background, so the rule reduces to the judgement it was meant to replace;
(iii) inert ceremony: across >=3 prospective sharpening cases the rule never changes a decision
relative to the existing "when in doubt, new letter" convention (CLAUDE.md, EXQ versioning) --
in which case demote toward rejected/superseded and let that convention stand.

WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY REVISE: this rule refines GOV-ROTATE-1's safeguard in the opposite direction
(anti-fragmentation vs anti-laundering); if GOV-ROTATE-1 is demoted, this rule's rotation-facing
half falls with it, but its lettered-successor half (a sharper EXQ-047b-style restatement is not
a new claim unless it needs A) is independent of rotation and would survive as a note on the EXQ
versioning convention. Distinguish from GOV-MATCHAUX-1: that rule says which auxiliary CONTROL
a result is admissible against; this one says which auxiliary ASSUMPTION makes a formulation a
different claim. Do not conflate.

READER NOTE: governance_rule / process claim about how the Assembly grains its own registry, not
an empirical mechanism about REE cognition. The E2 / E3 candidate-future-ordering example in the
functional_restatement is illustrative and is NOT a registered prediction of any MECH claim.
```
