# Provenance, source monitoring, confidence, and psychosis — dedicated literature pull

**Date:** 2026-09-09  
**Status:** external-evidence synthesis / literature pull; not a scientific claim registration, not a diagnostic model, not an experiment-queue mutation  
**Parent thought:** `docs/thoughts/2026-09-09_provenance_errors_false_evidence_multiplication_and_psychosis.md`

## 0. Question

The REE provenance thought proposes a specific extension beyond ordinary source monitoring:

```text
one causal lineage
→ several transformed descendants
→ shared ancestry becomes uncertain or wrong
→ descendants are treated as independent evidence
→ confidence rises without new world evidence
```

This literature pull asks what parts of that chain are already supported in psychosis research, what the closest computational neighbours are, and what remains genuinely untested.

The key distinction throughout is:

```text
source attribution error
!= confidence calibration error
!= evidence-integration error
!= false independence / ancestry multiplication
```

They can interact, but the literature does not justify collapsing them into one mechanism.

# 2. Evidence stream A — source / reality monitoring

## A1. Damiani et al. 2022 meta-analysis

**Damiani S et al. 2022. PMID 35124869. DOI 10.1111/pcn.13338.**

44 studies; 1,566 patients and 1,175 controls.

Main findings:

- source-monitoring performance was impaired in psychosis overall;
- internal source monitoring and imagined stimuli showed particularly marked impairment;
- patients with hallucinations versus those without showed greater externalising and imagined/auditory source-monitoring errors;
- associations with symptoms and general cognition were relatively sparsely measured and heterogeneous.

### What this supports

A real vulnerability in determining the origin of internally generated / imagined material, with a more specific relation to hallucination-related phenomena than to psychosis as one undifferentiated syndrome.

### What it does not support

It does not show that source errors increase the **number of evidence units** counted by a belief-updating system.

---

## A2. Damiani et al. 2024 signal-detection meta-analysis

**Damiani S et al. _The noise in our brain: A systematic review and meta-analysis of neuroimaging and signal-detection studies on source monitoring in psychosis._ J Psychiatr Res. 2024;169:142–151. DOI 10.1016/j.jpsychires.2023.11.036. PMID 38039688.**

Included 44 behavioural error/accuracy studies, 16 neuroimaging studies and 7 signal-detection studies; total psychosis N = 2,297 and control N = 1,745.

Important result for REE:

- accuracy and discrimination differentiated psychosis from controls;
- discrimination measures were argued to better represent the **bidirectional** internal/external confusion than simple error counts;
- response bias alone was less reliable.

### REE implication

Do not operationalise provenance pathology only as:

```text
internal → external
```

A better assay should separately measure:

- source discrimination;
- directional bias;
- confidence;
- ancestry uncertainty.

---

## A3. Suter et al. 2026 — current reality-monitoring review

**Suter G, Apperly I, Zhang L, Černis E. _Reality monitoring across disorders of reality: Systematic and narrative reviews of dissociation and psychosis._ J Psychiatr Res. 2026;195:21–30. DOI 10.1016/j.jpsychires.2026.01.021. PMID 41570403.**

The psychosis component was a narrative synthesis of prior systematic reviews and original studies.

Its useful current summary is:

- clinical psychosis is robustly associated with impaired reality monitoring and externalising bias;
- findings at non-clinical/subclinical levels are mixed;
- reality-monitoring abnormality is not unique to psychosis.

### REE implication

Reality-monitoring failure is a candidate **mechanistic dimension**, not a psychosis-specific diagnostic signature.

---

## A4. Brookwell, Bentall & Varese 2013

**Brookwell ML, Bentall RP, Varese F. _Externalizing biases and hallucinations in source-monitoring, self-monitoring and signal detection studies: a meta-analytic review._ Psychol Med. 2013;43:2465–2475. DOI 10.1017/S0033291712002760. PMID 23282942.**

Moderate-to-large effects linked hallucinations / hallucination proneness with externalising biases in source-monitoring and signal-detection paradigms.

### Constraint

The review predates the larger 2022/2024 syntheses and does not establish a unitary source-monitoring lesion.

---

## A5. Negative / heterogeneity evidence

**Varese et al. 2011, PMID 21498051** tested three source-monitoring accounts in first-episode psychosis. Hallucinating participants did **not** show a generalized external-misattribution bias; an effect appeared only for positive words. The authors concluded that broad external source-monitoring bias may not be central early in psychosis.

**Waters et al. 2008, PMID 18781492** meta-analysed associative-memory studies and found no evidence for a uniquely selective reality-monitoring deficit; more global associative-memory impairment could explain part of the apparent source deficit.

### Consequence

The literature does not justify:

```text
psychosis = source monitoring failure
```

or

```text
hallucination = internally generated event simply labelled external
```

Any REE analogy needs competing explanations and performance-matched controls.

# 3. Evidence stream B — self-monitoring, corollary discharge and distributed provenance

A provenance system should not be modelled as a decorative metadata field merely because source-monitoring tasks use explicit source labels. Biological self/non-self discrimination appears distributed across prediction, sensory attenuation, memory and prefrontal monitoring.

## B1. Skipper, Lametti & Green 2026

**Skipper JI, Lametti DR, Green DW. _Inner speech and the neurobiology of psychosis._ Neurosci Biobehav Rev. 2026;188:106805. DOI 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2026.106805. PMID 42331125.**

This review integrates inner speech, speech-production systems, efference copy, auditory attenuation and predictive processing.

Its relevant proposal is not a single failed “source tag”, but disruption in a distributed recurrent system in which self-generated speech-related predictions are insufficiently suppressed/monitored.

### REE implication

Provenance may need to be:

- probabilistic;
- distributed across transformations;
- cross-checked against expected consequences;
- available to downstream confidence/evidence calculations.

---

## B2. Whitford et al. 2025 — inner speech corollary-discharge experiment

**Whitford TJ et al. _Corollary Discharge Dysfunction to Inner Speech and its Relationship to Auditory Verbal Hallucinations in Patients with Schizophrenia Spectrum Disorders._ Schizophr Bull. 2025. PMID 41116234.**

Participants covertly produced timed syllables while hearing matched or mismatched audible syllables. Healthy controls showed the expected suppression of auditory N1 for matched inner speech. The current-hallucination group showed the opposite pattern, with enhanced N1 in the match condition; the non-current-hallucination group showed a different abnormal pattern.

### Relevance

This is stronger than a retrospective source-memory association: it directly probes a predictive self-generation signal during inner speech.

### Limit

It still tests self-generated prediction / sensory attenuation, not evidence ancestry or independence counting.

---

## B3. Samantaray et al. 2025

**Samantaray S et al. _Impaired corollary discharge as a predictor of reality monitoring deficits in schizophrenia: Implications for auditory verbal hallucinations._ Schizophr Res. 2025;283:67–76. DOI 10.1016/j.schres.2025.07.003. PMID 40633307.**

35 patients with persistent auditory verbal hallucinations and 35 controls completed Talk/Listen and reality-monitoring paradigms.

Patients showed reduced corollary-discharge index, impaired internal-source recognition and greater externalisation bias. Corollary-discharge index predicted externalisation bias (reported R² = 0.128).

### Relevance

This provides a direct bridge between a self-generation prediction signal and later reality-monitoring performance.

### Limit

The effect explains only a modest share of variance and does not imply that corollary discharge is the sole source of provenance information.

---

## B4. Prefrontal / network evidence

**Garrison et al. 2017, PMID 28203529** found reality-monitoring impairment in schizophrenia associated with medial anterior prefrontal dysfunction distinct from dorsolateral-prefrontal working-memory dysfunction.

**Wang et al. 2011, PMID 21147519** found altered medial-prefrontal / superior-temporal connectivity during self-other source monitoring.

These studies favour a distributed source-monitoring network rather than one local “provenance register”.

### Negative causal evidence

**Subramaniam et al./related tDCS source-monitoring study, PMID 30326206 (2018)** found no effect of medial-prefrontal or superior-temporal tDCS on reality-monitoring performance, with Bayesian evidence supporting the null in the tested protocol.

Therefore anatomical association should not be promoted directly into a causal implementation story.

---

# 4. Evidence stream C — source errors plus confidence errors

This is the first literature stream that approaches the bridge from “wrong source” to “wrong epistemic weight”.

## C1. Moritz, Woodward & Ruff 2003

**_Source monitoring and memory confidence in schizophrenia._ Psychol Med. 2003;33:131–139. DOI 10.1017/S0033291702006852. PMID 12537044.**

Patients made more source-attribution errors and were more confident than controls that false source attributions were true.

### Why this matters

It shows that source error and confidence error can co-occur within the same task.

### Why it is still not the REE mechanism

The study did not manipulate whether several memories shared one causal ancestor, so it cannot show **false evidence multiplication**.

---

## C2. Knowledge corruption / overconfidence in errors

**Moritz et al. 2005, PMID 15674129** and **Moritz & Woodward 2006, PMID 19817068** developed the “knowledge corruption” idea: false memories or judgments are not necessarily uniquely frequent, but false information may enter the knowledge system with excessive confidence.

**Moritz et al. 2006, PMID 16492092** found a reduced confidence gap in schizophrenia: greater confidence in errors and lower confidence in correct answers relative to psychiatric and healthy controls.

### REE relation

This is conceptually close to the idea that provenance corruption is dangerous because of its **downstream epistemic consequences**, not merely because the source label is wrong.

But these studies concern calibration of individual judgments, not the cardinality or independence of evidence.

---

## C3. Ultra-high-risk / first-episode evidence

**Gawęda et al. 2017, PMID 29107832** found that ultra-high-risk and first-episode groups more often misattributed imagined actions as performed, and made these false responses with greater confidence than controls.

This matters because the combination of self-monitoring error + high confidence is not confined to chronic illness.

---

## C4. Important metacognitive restraint

**Rouy et al. 2021, _Systematic review and meta-analysis of metacognitive abilities in individuals with schizophrenia spectrum disorders_, PMID 33757817** found an overall metacognitive deficit (g ≈ -0.57), but the deficit was substantially driven by studies that did not equate underlying task performance. In performance-controlled studies, the evidence for a specific metacognitive deficit was inconclusive.

### Direct assay consequence

REE provenance experiments must not infer a confidence-specific mechanism from a condition that simply makes the base task harder.

P1/P5 should therefore hold content and objective task information constant and verify matched base discrimination where the mechanism predicts only a provenance change.

---

# 5. Evidence stream D — delusional conviction, evidence integration and belief flexibility

The provenance hypothesis predicts a possible delusion-maintenance branch only if ancestry corruption changes epistemic weighting. The nearest clinical literature concerns evidence gathering, belief flexibility and confidence.

## D1. Jumping to conclusions — established group effect, uncertain mechanistic specificity

Older meta-analyses found a moderate tendency for people with psychosis / current delusions to make decisions with less evidence.

- **Dudley et al. 2016, PMID 26519952**: 55 studies; psychosis groups required less information than controls.
- **McLean et al. 2017 / detailed bias meta-analysis, PMID 27169465**: JTC, bias against disconfirmatory evidence (BADE), bias against confirmatory evidence and liberal acceptance co-varied with current delusions in cross-sectional samples.

However, the newer picture is more cautious.

**Doherty et al. 2025, PMID 40592007**, 42 studies / 7,604 participants, found no meaningful relationship between JTC and dimensional delusional ideation in the full dataset or subgroups, although the current-delusion subgroup showed relatively greater JTC than the general population. A corrigendum was published later in 2025 (PMID 41224630).

**Ashinoff et al. 2022, PMID 33676820** argue that classic draws-to-decision paradigms may not cleanly isolate inferential processes and that the standard JTC construct may be less delusion-specific than commonly stated.

### REE implication

Do not use “premature closure” as a proxy for false-independence. It should remain a separate dependent variable whose mechanism must be shown.

---

## D2. Bias against disconfirmatory evidence / belief inflexibility

**Zhu, Sun & So 2018, PMID 28805246** found belief inflexibility associated with delusion severity, especially conviction.

**Eisenacher et al. 2016, PMID 27086240** reported BADE in first-episode psychosis, with at-risk participants intermediate between controls and FEP.

But a recent umbrella review (**PMID 39729453**) judged the quality of evidence for several reasoning biases other than JTC to be weaker/inconsistent.

### REE implication

A provenance model should predict a *specific* correction phenotype:

```text
re-link genealogy
→ reduce inappropriate confidence
→ restore sensitivity to genuinely independent counterevidence
```

rather than merely predicting generic inflexibility.

---

## D3. Current first-episode metacognition synthesis

**Aguiar, Martins & Castelo-Branco 2026, PMID 42107828** reviewed 41 studies of metacognition and positive symptoms in first-episode psychosis. The review describes a complex, non-unitary relationship; cognitive-bias studies often reported associations with positive symptoms, while causal direction remains unresolved.

This supports studying component processes rather than treating “metacognition” as one latent deficit.

---

# 6. Evidence stream E — circular inference: the closest computational neighbour

The nearest established computational analogue to false evidence multiplication is **circular inference**, but the concepts are not identical.

## E1. Jardri & Denève framework

**Jardri & Denève 2013, _Circular inferences in schizophrenia_, PMID 24065721.**

The model proposes that messages can reverberate improperly through hierarchical inference loops so that priors contaminate sensory evidence and sensory evidence is fed back as if newly informative. The framework was proposed to explain overconfidence and fixed causal beliefs among other phenomena.

This is very close to the REE concern that recurrent information can become **epistemically counted more than once**.

## E2. Experimental evidence

**Jardri, Duverne, Litvinova & Denève 2017, _Experimental evidence for circular inference in schizophrenia._ Nat Commun. 8:14218. DOI 10.1038/ncomms14218. PMID 28139642.**

Participants performed a forced-choice task manipulating sensory evidence and prior information while reporting confidence. A circular-inference model fit behaviour better than simple Bayesian inference. Ascending loops were stronger in schizophrenia than controls and correlated with positive-symptom severity; descending loops correlated with negative symptoms; both related to disorganisation.

### Why this matters for provenance

Both theories identify a danger in **recurrent information returning as though it were fresh evidence**.

### Why they are not equivalent

Circular inference is formulated in terms of reverberant hierarchical message passing / prior-likelihood contamination. The REE provenance hypothesis is specifically about **causal genealogy and dependence**:

```text
one ancestor
→ multiple descendants
→ ancestry lost
→ descendants treated as independent witnesses
```

The literature pull found no direct clinical demonstration that psychosis involves this ancestry-cardinality failure as such.

That distinction should be preserved; the REE hypothesis is currently an experimentally testable extension, not a relabelling of circular inference.

---

# 7. Evidence stream F — dynamic belief updating and predictive-processing neighbours

## F1. Dynamic belief updating

**Katthagen et al. 2022, PMID 35492702** reviewed computational models of dynamic belief updating in psychosis. Across heterogeneous paradigms, psychosis/delusion-proneness was associated with difficulty separating environmental noise from true change, often expressed as excess updating / overestimated volatility. Correlations with positive symptoms were sparse and heterogeneous.

**Gibbs-Dean et al. 2023, PMID 36791933** similarly found psychosis-related difficulty adapting to changing contingencies and higher-order abnormalities in volatility estimation, but again across heterogeneous tasks and models.

### REE relation

These results make provenance potentially valuable because ancestry/dependence is one way to distinguish “new world evidence” from repeated internally generated variation.

They do not demonstrate that provenance is the missing variable biologically.

---

## F2. Developmental computational account

**Powers et al. 2025, _A Computational Account of the Development and Evolution of Psychotic Symptoms._ Biol Psychiatry. 97(2):117–127. DOI 10.1016/j.biopsych.2024.08.026. PMID 39260466.**

The model proposes a developmental sequence involving bottom-up noise, aberrant prediction-error-driven learning, reduced signal-to-noise ratio and compensatory relative overweighting of priors, potentially contributing to hallucinations and delusional crystallisation.

### REE relation

This provides a different route to self-confirming inference: noise can first generate inappropriate learning and later produce compensatory prior dominance.

It is a competing/complementary mechanism, not evidence for ancestry failure.

---

# 8. Evidence stream G — broad cognitive-bias synthesis

**Gawęda et al. 2024, _A systematic review of performance-based assessment studies on cognitive biases in schizophrenia spectrum psychoses and clinical high-risk states_, Clin Psychol Rev. 108:102391. DOI 10.1016/j.cpr.2024.102391. PMID 38301343.**

324 studies across five bias families were reviewed. Source monitoring and JTC were among the more consistently exaggerated biases in both clinical psychosis and clinical-high-risk groups, but the authors emphasize interactions/additive effects among biases rather than a single lesion.

### REE implication

The provenance proposal is most plausible as one component in a multi-process architecture involving source discrimination, confidence calibration, evidence integration, salience/precision and information seeking.

---

# 9. What the literature now permits us to say

## Established / comparatively well supported

1. **Source / reality monitoring is impaired on average in psychosis**, with particularly relevant associations to hallucination-related phenomena and internally generated / imagined material.
2. **The impairment is heterogeneous and not universal.** It is neither necessary nor sufficient for psychosis.
3. **Self-generation prediction / corollary-discharge abnormalities are plausible contributors** to auditory-verbal hallucination-related source disturbances; recent electrophysiology strengthens this link.
4. **Confidence calibration can be abnormal**, including excessive confidence in source-memory errors, although performance confounding is a major methodological concern.
5. **Evidence gathering / belief flexibility abnormalities occur in psychosis and delusions**, but their specificity and mechanisms are less clean than older summaries sometimes implied.
6. **Circular inference has both a formal model and experimental support** as a recurrent-inference abnormality in schizophrenia.

## Supported only as an adjacent bridge

1. Source errors can co-occur with high confidence.
2. Recurrent inference can produce overcounting-like effects in circular-inference models.
3. Belief updating can mis-handle noise versus genuine environmental change.
4. Multiple cognitive biases may interact in producing or maintaining psychotic experiences.

## Not established by the clinical literature located here

The following specific chain remains an REE hypothesis:

```text
shared causal ancestry is lost
→ dependent descendants are represented as independent evidence families
→ effective evidence cardinality increases
→ confidence inflates
→ replay/prediction recursively multiplies apparent corroboration
```

No paper located in this pull directly manipulates **ancestry/dependence while holding content constant** and measures consequent confidence inflation in psychosis.

That absence is important. It means the proposed P1 assay is not merely reproducing an existing psychosis paradigm; it tests a sharper computational construct.

# 10. Consequences for the provenance experiment ladder

## P1 — content-matched provenance corruption

Add explicit separate readouts for:

- source discrimination;
- source-direction bias;
- confidence calibration;
- effective independent-source count;
- task accuracy / content discrimination.

The experiment should only support false-independence if provenance corruption alters confidence/evidence count **without degrading the informational task itself**.

## P2 — unknown is not independent

The signal-detection literature strengthens keeping **uncertainty** separate from categorical source reassignment.

Compare:

```text
unknown
probably shared ancestry
probably independent
definitely shared
definitely independent
```

rather than forcing a binary source tag.

## P3 — replay amplification

The clinical literature currently gives no direct evidence that replay itself multiplies delusional evidence through provenance loss. Treat this as a deliberately novel REE test, not a biologically established psychosis mechanism.

Include a control in which replay improves memory fidelity but is prevented from adding independent evidential weight.

## P4 — counterevidence recovery

Measure whether genealogy repair alone restores calibration. This is more discriminating than generic BADE/JTC outcomes.

## P5 — hallucination-like source assignment versus delusion-like confidence

The literature strongly supports keeping these as separate readouts.

Recommended factorial design:

```text
self-generation prediction: intact / degraded
ancestry integrity: intact / corrupted
```

with two primary outcomes:

```text
P(external source)
P(H | evidence graph)
```

A double dissociation would be particularly informative.

## P6 — inferred genealogy

Given the distributed biological evidence, explicit immutable lineage IDs should be treated as an engineering ceiling/control rather than the only plausible mechanism. Compare explicit ancestry with inferred dependence from covariance, temporal coupling, shared prediction origin, or transformation history.

---

# 11. Stronger falsifiers after the literature pull

The REE false-evidence-multiplication account should weaken if:

1. source/provenance corruption changes source attribution but leaves evidence count and confidence calibrated;
2. confidence effects disappear once task performance is matched;
3. ordinary dependence/covariance estimation prevents double-counting without genealogy information;
4. circular-inference-like behaviour can be reproduced entirely by gain/precision abnormalities with no ancestry variable;
5. replay changes representational strength but never increases confidence in the absence of new external evidence;
6. re-linking ancestry does not improve response to disconfirmatory evidence;
7. effects require an explicit handcrafted “provenance error → confidence bonus” rather than emerging from the inference rule itself.

---

# 12. Architectural pressure that survives even if the psychosis analogy fails

The literature is sufficient to justify one conservative engineering concern:

> **Self-generated, remembered, simulated and externally observed material should not be forced into one categorical source code, and confidence should not be inferred from repeated internally dependent representations as though repetition itself were independent corroboration.**

The stronger proposed design rule remains to be tested:

> **A hypothesis should not be able to cite its own descendants as independent witnesses.**

This can be valuable for error-tolerant artificial cognition even if it turns out not to be a major biological mechanism of psychosis.

## Bottom line

> **The literature strongly supports treating provenance/source attribution, self-generation prediction, confidence calibration and evidence integration as real and partly dissociable computational dimensions in psychosis. Circular inference provides an established neighbour for recurrent over-use of information. What remains genuinely new is the REE proposal that corruption of causal genealogy can convert one ancestry family into several apparently independent witnesses, thereby multiplying evidence without new world input. That step is not established clinically and deserves a content-matched synthetic test before any biological claim is considered.**
