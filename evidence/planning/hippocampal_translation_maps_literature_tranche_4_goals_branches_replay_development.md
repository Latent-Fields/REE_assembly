# Hippocampal translation maps -- literature tranche 4: goals, branches, replay and development

**Search and synthesis date:** 2026-09-20  
**Status:** targeted literature intake; no claim registration, confidence update, substrate choice or experiment-queue mutation  
**Companion:** [mutual-legibility intake, 2026-09-20 addendum](thought_intake_2026-09-07_mutual_legibility.md#7-2026-09-20-addendum-boundary-indexed-reference-across-goals-hippocampus-and-predictive-systems)

## 0. Scope and result

This tranche asks what biological evidence supports boundary-specific reference across goal systems,
hippocampal representations, predictive systems and cortical consumers. It extends
[tranche 1](hippocampal_translation_maps_literature_tranche_1.md),
[tranche 2](hippocampal_translation_maps_literature_tranche_2_ca1_transformation.md) and
[tranche 3](hippocampal_translation_maps_literature_tranche_3_receiver_conditioning_temporal.md).
Previously cited studies are rechecked anchors, not counted as independent new evidence.

| Targeted question | Assessment from this search | What remains unestablished |
|---|---|---|
| Factorised goal/context dimensions outside navigation | Nonspatial abstraction, contextual valuation and task-specific geometry provide substantial adjacent evidence | One modality-general goal dimension, or a goal variable causally portable between arbitrary systems |
| Hippocampal–prefrontal branch identity and theta addressing | Prospective content, choice-sensitive coupling and phase-dependent readout are plausible components | A shared candidate/horizon identity causally selected by phase, independently of gain and general timing |
| Replay trains or realigns interfaces | Causal consolidation and subsequent coordination effects; computational demonstrations of downstream training | Selective repair of a measured mapping after drift with local information preserved and pairing controls |
| Development replaces hippocampal mediation with direct cortical interfaces | Adult skill consolidation offers a closer analogue than most ontogenetic data; childhood evidence is non-monotonic | A general developmental handover to directly necessary cortical mappings, or pruning of those specific routes |

“Not established” means not demonstrated by the sources examined here. This is a bounded narrative
search, not a systematic review or a claim that no such evidence exists.

## 1. Search and evidence protocol

Searches combined the named 2026 papers with topic queries and backward/forward citation leads.
Bibliographic identities, publication state and central findings were checked against publisher pages,
PubMed records of the primary papers, PMC manuscripts or author-hosted manuscripts. Secondary summaries
and search-result publication-age estimates were not treated as scientific evidence or authoritative
dates. Journal issue year is used below; early-online dates are noted where they can cause confusion.

Representative queries actually run on 2026-09-20:

| Track | Query |
|---|---|
| Nonspatial factors | `hippocampus prefrontal factorized context representations nonspatial task goal orthogonal` |
| Branch/phase | `hippocampal prefrontal theta phase nonlocal trajectory choice branch identity` |
| Replay/interfaces | `replay hippocampal cortical communication subspace learning realignment consolidation` |
| Development | `development hippocampal cortical connectivity children direct cortical schema learning` |

These were supplemented by exact-title/author searches, including Elston–Wallis, Bernardi, Flesch,
Robinson, Kim, Qin, Audrain, Bessières, Spens–Burgess and the newly surfaced Julian preprint. Direct DOI
and publisher checks verified the requested anchor papers. No numerical pooled effect or comprehensive
coverage claim is made.

**Access labels:** *Text* means relevant primary results/methods or indexed full-text passages were
inspected, not a full replication audit. *Abstract/figures* means the accessible primary abstract and
figure descriptions supported the stated finding. *Abstract* means the narrower published abstract
record; methods-dependent details are deliberately not inferred. These labels describe this pass's
access, not whether the paper has a paywall for every reader. The Julian item is explicitly a preprint.

Throughout: a decoded variable is not necessarily natively used; statistical directionality is not a
pathway intervention; replay coactivation is not demonstrated synaptic reassociation; a computational
model is not an animal experiment. REE interpretations are stated separately from observations.

## 2. Requested neuroscience anchors

### A1. Goal configuration in CA1 and prefrontal–thalamic input

Golipour Z, Mozaffarilegha M, Yen SF, Üstüner C, Ito HT (2026).
[Prefrontal–thalamic goal states organize spatially aligned hippocampal maps](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77240-6).
*Nature Communications* 17, 9233. **Text; published 29 August.**

In rats, CA1 goal-configuration and position dimensions are largely separable; mPFC and nucleus
reuniens carry persistent goal-state signals. Reuniens silencing reduces CA1 goal coding and
pre-navigation sequences while sparing spatial coding, without an apparent behavioural deficit in
this task. Crucially, “goal” denotes blockwise rewarded-location configuration, not the next chosen
destination. The causal result does not isolate the entire mPFC–reuniens–CA1 chain. The published paper
supersedes the April preprint; they are one evidence unit. Generalisation to nonspatial desires remains
an inference to test.

### A2. OFC prospective destination

Basu R et al. (2021).
[The orbitofrontal cortex maps future navigational goals](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04042-9).
*Nature* 599, 449–452. **Text; [author manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8599015/).**

OFC activity represents the future destination before movement and across navigation, including the
incorrect destination on error trials. Brief disruption at journey onset produces navigation errors.
This motivates distinguishing intended target from current position and reward configuration. It does
not show that the OFC code is transmitted directly into CA1 or constitutes a universal goal language.

### A3. Goal-directed theta sweeps and PFC coordination

Tang W et al. (2026).
[Goal-directed hippocampal theta sweeps during memory-guided navigation](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02364-3).
*Nature Neuroscience* 29, 2214–2224. **Abstract/figures; online 1 July.**

During learned open-arena navigation, hippocampal sweeps anticipate goal-directed trajectories,
coordinate with PFC activity and are preferentially represented in subsequent ripple replay. The
study links prospective content, interregional coordination and later replay. Its proposed circuit
account is not itself evidence for an E1/E2-style branch identifier or a receiver that selects that
identifier by theta phase.

Yu C, Ji Z, Ormond J, O'Keefe J, Burgess N (2026).
[Hippocampal theta sweeps indicate goal direction during navigation](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02365-2).
*Nature Neuroscience* 29, 2225–2236. **Text; online 1 July.**

Analyses of Honeycomb and Cheeseboard datasets find sweeps toward goals, distinguishable from current
heading/movement and predictive of correct choices. A continuous-attractor model offers a mechanism.
This supports prospective reference beyond immediate position; the model and decoding do not establish
the exact biological circuit or causal delivery of branch identity to a cortical consumer. These two
2026 papers are distinct studies, not two citations to the same result.

### A4. Partner-specific hippocampal–retrosplenial subspaces

Gonzalez J et al. (2026).
[Subspace communication in the hippocampal–retrosplenial axis](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10481-z).
*Nature* 655, 192–201. **Text; online 13 May; [published manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13317024/).**

Simultaneous recordings identify partner- and state-related shared population subspaces. Experience
and sleep affect hippocampal–hippocampal and CA1–RSC relationships differently, consistent with flexible
local processing alongside more stable cortical communication. This is already an anchor in earlier
tranches. Shared covariance and replay associations do not demonstrate a causal communication channel,
receiver-conditioned translation or reduced bridge complexity. In particular, differential stability
does not imply that replay realigns every interregional map.

### A5. Reference-frame transformation toward entorhinal cortex

Yang Y et al. (2026).
[Anterior and posterior retrosplenial cortex employ distinct strategies for egocentric-allocentric transformation in spatial coding](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2600565123).
*PNAS* 123(31), e2600565123. **Abstract; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42520117/); online 28 July.**

Mouse recordings distinguish anterior RSC egocentric boundary coding from posterior RSC conjunctive
head-direction/allocentric coding, with relevant coding enriched in the MEC-projecting population.
This is an anatomically situated reference-frame example, already covered in tranche 1. It does not
establish an arbitrary hippocampal translator, or prove every causal computational step of a serial
RSC-to-MEC transformation. Detailed pathway-intervention claims require more than the abstract accessed.

### A6. Reciprocal consensus

Javadzadeh M, Schimel M, Hofer SB, Ahmadian Y, Hennequin G (2026).
[Reciprocal connections dynamically build consensus between neocortical areas](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02437-3).
*Nature Neuroscience*. **Text; published 18 September.**

The authors reanalyse mouse V1–LM recordings and perturbations with constrained dynamical models;
compatible population patterns persist while disagreement decays more rapidly. This is evidence about
reciprocal integration in a visual circuit, not newly collected hippocampal goal data. The REE ordering
“first establish mutual legibility, then test consensus” is our methodological inference. The paper
does not demonstrate a learning stage following translation, universal convergence, or agreement as a
test of truth. Keep the existing [consensus thought](../../docs/thoughts/2026-09-19_dynamic_consensus_post_translation_integration.md) as owner.

## 3. Target 1 -- factorisation beyond spatial navigation

### N1. Abstract context, action and value geometry

Bernardi S et al. (2020).
[The geometry of abstraction in hippocampus and prefrontal cortex](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.09.031).
*Cell* 183, 954–967.e21. **Text; [manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8451959/).**

Macaques perform a nonspatial visual reversal task. Hippocampal and prefrontal representations support
cross-condition generalisation of task variables alongside mixed, high-dimensional coding. Context
geometry relates to errors in a way ordinary decoding can miss. The useful discriminator is transfer
across held-out *conditions*, not random held-out trials. This supports abstract contextual structure,
without establishing a single orthogonal goal axis or interregional causal translation.

### N2. Context-specific task geometry

Flesch T, Juechems K, Dumbalska T, Saxe A, Summerfield C (2022).
[Orthogonal representations for robust context-dependent task performance in brains and neural networks](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2022.01.005).
*Neuron* 110, 1258–1270.e11. **Text; [manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8992799/).**

Human visual categorisation, macaque-data reanalysis and neural-network modelling connect robust
contextual performance to separated task-relevant representations. Model gating is a mechanistic
candidate, not demonstrated biological synaptic implementation. The
[December 2022 correction](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2022.12.004) corrects swapped colour/motion
labels in macaque-analysis figures 4, 6, S4 and S5; use the corrected version. Task-factor separation
should not be relabelled as proof that all goals factorise identically.

### N3. Hippocampal context and OFC valuation

Elston TW, Wallis JD (2025).
[Context-dependent decision-making in the primate hippocampal–prefrontal circuit](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-024-01839-5).
*Nature Neuroscience* 28, 374–382. **Text; published 6 January 2025 despite “024” in the DOI.**

In two monkeys, a cued state changes picture values. Hippocampal neurons encode state and OFC neurons
express state-dependent value coding at choice, accompanied by hippocampal–OFC theta coordination.
Different cues can specify the same state, helping distinguish state from cue identity. This is a
particularly relevant nonspatial link between context and a consumer-specific value computation.
Directionality and phase analyses do not substitute for a phase-selective pathway intervention.

### N4. Compositional relations and generative replay

Schwartenbeck P et al. (2023).
[Generative replay underlies compositional inference in the hippocampal-prefrontal circuit](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2023.09.004).
*Cell*. **Abstract/figures and primary manuscript passages; [institutional record](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10179099/).**

Human puzzle-composition experiments connect relational representations and replay-like sequences to
inference about new combinations. This broadens the relevant reference relations beyond navigational
locations. It does not isolate a factorised goal dimension, prove offline synaptic training, or measure
repair of a cross-area map. Inference-time replay and consolidation-time learning must remain separate.

### N5. A causal context-reinstatement lead -- preprint

Julian JB, Kaminsky JC, Tank DW, Brody C (2026).
[Hippocampal engrams configure prefrontal context representations to guide flexible decisions](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.06.732916).
**bioRxiv preprint, posted 7 July; abstract accessed; not peer reviewed in the record checked.**

The authors report that reactivating tagged hippocampal engrams reinstates corresponding mPFC context
states and shifts context-dependent decision rules, rather than merely inducing one motor response.
Other aspects of prefrontal dynamics remain intact. This is a promising causal lead for context-based
configuration of a downstream consumer. It remains preliminary, and immediate reinstatement does not
show that hippocampus learns arbitrary translation maps or trains those mappings during replay.

**Track assessment:** The evidence justifies separating context, desired target and value in REE
assays. It does not justify replacing these with one universal “goal” coordinate. Nonlinear or mixed
representations can still support recoverable factors; orthogonality is an assay result, not a design
axiom. See intake discriminator 1 and the cue/native-completion rival in discriminator 2.

## 4. Target 2 -- branch identity and theta-phase addressing

### T1. Causal prefrontal–thalamic–hippocampal route

Ito HT et al. (2015).
[A prefrontal–thalamo–hippocampal circuit for goal-directed spatial navigation](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14396).
*Nature* 522, 50–55. **Abstract/primary publisher record.**

Trajectory-dependent hippocampal firing and reuniens interventions implicate a prefrontal–thalamic
route in prospective spatial processing. This supplies a causal circuit predecessor to A1. It does
not by itself establish a common identifier for alternatives across regions, distinguish every
receiver's horizon semantics, or demonstrate phase as an address rather than a communication window.

### T2. Alternatives versus selected outcomes across timescales

Tang W, Shin JD, Jadhav SP (2021).
[Multiple time-scales of decision-making in the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.66227).
*eLife* 10, e66227. **Abstract/figures; [primary article](https://elifesciences.org/articles/66227).**

Hippocampal theta sequences can sample alternatives while coordinated PFC sequences preferentially
represent the upcoming choice. Slower behavioural sequences and replay supply distinct information;
theta sequencing can remain present on errors despite impaired preceding replay. Coordination thus
need not mean identical content at every instant. A branch-identity assay must test correspondence
between roles without requiring the two populations to represent the same distribution over options.

### T3. Prefrontal sensitivity to nonlocal hippocampal content

Berners-Lee A, Wu X, Foster DJ (2021).
[Prefrontal Cortical Neurons Are Selective for Non-Local Hippocampal Representations during Replay and Behavior](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1158-20.2021).
*Journal of Neuroscience* 41, 5894–5908. **Primary abstract/results passages; [manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8265798/).**

Prefrontal activity distinguishes trajectories represented in hippocampal replay and relates to
nonlocal theta content during behaviour; late-theta population information predicts choice. This is
closer to a consumer tracking *which* prospective content is active than coherence alone. Selectivity
and prediction still leave common input and learned association alternatives; causal reassignment of
the same content between phase-specific recipient states remains an open discriminator.

### T4. Communication subspaces need not track theta coherence

Young RA, Shin JD, Guo Z, Jadhav SP (2025).
[Hippocampal–Prefrontal Communication Subspaces Align with Behavioral and Network Patterns in a Spatial Memory Task](https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0336-24.2025).
*eNeuro* 12(9). **Text; [final article](https://www.eneuro.org/content/12/9/ENEURO.0336-24.2025).**

Shared CA1–PFC population activity relates to behavioural trajectories, learning and oscillatory
state. In this task its relationship to theta power is stronger than to theta coherence. This is a
useful constraint on an overly simple “more coherence means more communication” story. Reduced-rank
regression/shared covariance is an observational estimator, not proof of causal signal routing; phase
addressing must beat power, speed, common-drive and static-map explanations.

### T5. Human planning and theta–gamma organisation

Huang Z, Bisby JA, Burgess N, Bush D (2026).
[Human hippocampal theta–gamma coupling coordinates sequential planning during navigation](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2513547123).
*PNAS* 123(9), e2513547123. **Abstract; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41758661/).**

Human MEG relates hippocampal theta–gamma coupling to goal proximity and successful planning in an
abstract navigation task, with differences between novel and familiar routes. This is compatible with
phase-organised sequence processing. It does not decode an interregional branch identifier or causally
demonstrate that a receiver selects one branch by phase.

**Track assessment:** Combine A3 with N3 and T1–T5, without collapsing three levels: prospective
content, coordinated availability, and causal phase-addressed use. Only the third would settle the
strongest REE addressing interpretation. The joint manipulation must preserve content and phase
power while altering correspondence, as in intake discriminators 3–4. MECH-561 owns addressing;
MECH-556 owns the more permissive timing requirement.

## 5. Target 3 -- replay as downstream training or interface realignment

### R1. Timed coupling can causally improve consolidation

Maingret N et al. (2016).
[Hippocampo-cortical coupling mediates memory consolidation during sleep](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4304).
*Nature Neuroscience* 19, 959–964. **Primary abstract; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27182818/).**

Closed-loop coordination of hippocampal ripples with cortical sleep events improves later memory
and alters prefrontal task-related activity. This supports a causal role for appropriately timed
hippocampo-cortical interaction in consolidation. It does not isolate repair of an old mapping from
ordinary cortical learning, or hold memory quality fixed while manipulating interregional pairing.
This anchor already appears in the repository's replay-maintenance supplement.

### R2. Large ripples, later coactivation and waking coordination

Robinson HL et al. (2026).
[Large sharp-wave ripples promote hippocampo-cortical memory reactivation and consolidation during sleep](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2025.10.003).
*Neuron* 114(2), 226–236.e6; **online 7 November 2025, issue 21 January 2026. Abstract;
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41205608/).**

Large sleep ripples preferentially associate with hippocampal–prefrontal memory-ensemble coactivation.
Closed-loop enhancement strengthens reactivation, subsequent memory and later waking coordination.
This adds a useful causal downstream consequence beyond simply observing replay. The accessible
abstract does not establish which synapses/mapping parameters changed or show preserved endpoint
information with content-matched, pairing-shuffled replay. The earlier preprint is not a second study.

### R3. Cortical interface development during adult skill learning

Kim J, Joshi A, Frank L, Ganguly K (2023).
[Cortical–hippocampal coupling during manifold exploration in motor cortex](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-05533-z).
*Nature* 613, 103–110; online 14 December 2022. **Text; [author manuscript](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/30z4p88r).**

During rat motor learning, sleep slow-oscillation coupling between PFC and motor cortex relates to
later reduction in hippocampal ripple coupling as motor population structure stabilises. A changed
skill demand re-engages hippocampal coupling. This is a close candidate analogue for transient
mediation and cortical coordination. It remains adult skill learning, not ontogenetic pruning; the
observations do not independently demonstrate a repaired translation map or direct cortical pathway
necessity. See also developmental track D4 below without counting it twice.

### R4. Replay can be a reciprocal loop

Rothschild G, Eban E, Frank LM (2017).
[A cortical–hippocampal–cortical loop of information processing during memory consolidation](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4457).
*Nature Neuroscience* 20, 251–259; online December 2016. **Primary abstract;
[manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5783826/).**

Auditory cortical activity preceding ripples predicts hippocampal activity, which relates in turn to
subsequent cortical activity; sounds can bias this loop. This constrains a purely one-way teacher
story. Bidirectional content selection can support reinstatement without demonstrating that an
interregional transformation has been learned or repaired. This is another previously owned anchor.

### R5. Explicit downstream training -- computational evidence

Spens E, Burgess N (2026).
[Hippocampo-neocortical interaction as compressive retrieval-augmented generation](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-74357-6).
*Nature Communications* 17, 7971. **Text; computational model; publisher lists first publication
25 June and version of record 7 August.**

Compressed episodic traces support reconstruction by a generative cortical model; replay trains
the latter on episodic statistics, enabling generalisation and reducing dependence on particular
traces. Retrieval also supplies context for current inference. This explicitly distinguishes
downstream parameter learning from sending a vector for immediate use. It is a constructive modelling
result, not evidence that biological cortex uses the implementation or that sleep uniquely repairs
interfaces. It also permits schema-driven reconstruction errors.

**Track assessment:** There is credible evidence for timed cross-area consolidation and increasingly
strong causal evidence for later coordination. A4 supplies communication-subspace observations; R5
supplies a worked training account. None of the examined empirical studies meets the full, stricter
interface-repair standard already owned by INV-110.

Do not reopen that standard as a new claim: consult the
[replay-maintenance supplement](../../docs/thoughts/2026-09-09_hippocampal_replay_interface_maintenance_supplement.md),
including its earlier reassociation-model audit and local-adaptation rival. Required evidence remains
measured drift, preserved local information, lost receiver use, correct-versus-shuffled pairing,
held-out causal restoration, and matched-budget comparison with ordinary replay/local adaptation.
Ripple occurrence, replay content and plasticity opportunity must also be measured separately.

## 6. Target 4 -- development toward more direct cortical interfaces

### D1. Childhood arithmetic has an increasing hippocampal phase

Qin S et al. (2014).
[Hippocampal-neocortical functional reorganization underlies children's cognitive development](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3788).
*Nature Neuroscience* 17, 1263–1269. **Text; [manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4286364/).**

Longitudinal arithmetic learning at ages 7–9 associates increasing retrieval with increased
hippocampal recruitment/connectivity and reduced frontoparietal activation. Older-group comparisons
show a later reduction in hippocampal activation with more stable representations. The early within-child
increase must not be omitted to manufacture a monotonic handover; the later contrast is cross-sectional.
Neither lower activity nor functional connectivity establishes anatomical bypass or a directly
necessary cortical mapping.

### D2. Early hippocampal connectivity grows and differentiates

Audrain S et al. (2026).
[The Development of Hippocampal-Cortical Functional Connectivity in Infants and Toddlers](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.70475).
*Human Brain Mapping* 47(4), e70475. **Abstract; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41795623/).**

Data from 212 infants across the first two years show pronounced early connectivity growth and
differentiation, with subsequent refinement. These observations constrain a story in which mediation simply
declines from birth. Resting functional connectivity is not a test of task-specific causal routing,
synaptic pruning or the competence of a direct cortical replacement.

### D3. Infant schemas can retain hippocampal involvement in adulthood

Bessières B et al. (2026).
[Infant learning forms lasting memory schemas that influence adult behavior](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.03.018).
*Neuron* 114(16), 3094–3110.e4; online 10 April, issue 19 August. **Abstract/publisher summary;
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41966691/).**

Infant learning in mice leaves latent memories that facilitate later behaviour. Adult reminders
and congruent new learning re-engage prefrontal and hippocampal circuitry, including prefrontal-to-
hippocampal involvement. This argues against equating developmental persistence with irreversible
hippocampal replacement. It does not rule out selective cortical independence for familiar relations;
retention, retrieval and learning a new congruent relation are different demands.

### D4. Adult learning and computational handover are adjacent, not ontogenetic proof

R3 supplies the closest examined longitudinal change in hippocampal versus cortical coordination at
the level of population manifolds. R5 shows how a model can acquire cortical competence from replay.
These motivate a developmental assay but do not establish its biological claim. Three clocks must be
separated: organism age, time/experience learning a task, and age of a particular memory.

**Track assessment:** A selective, reversible reduction in mediator dependence is better supported
than a universal permanent replacement. A convincing developmental test would combine longitudinal
recording with early/late hippocampal and cortical-pathway perturbations, matched experience, and
familiar/novel/remapped tasks. A direct route must become necessary or sufficient in the appropriate
causal comparison; lower hippocampal activation is not enough. Dense-to-sparse interface learning,
replay-coupled pruning and historical advantage at matched final capacity remain the hypotheses
owned by ARC-143 and MECH-549/550/551, not conclusions of this tranche.

## 7. Synthesis and routing

The useful systems-level hypothesis is a collection of boundary-specific reference relations:
goal/context -> relevant episode -> candidate trajectory -> receiver-native consequence or value,
with timing and recurrence determining whether the relation can actually be used. The arrows denote
assay questions, not a universal anatomical pipeline. Retrieval can configure present computation;
replay can change future competence; consensus can integrate already-legible estimates. These jobs
must be measured independently.

The [companion intake](thought_intake_2026-09-07_mutual_legibility.md#75-falsifiable-discriminators-not-queued-experiments)
contains eight falsifiable discriminators and the explicit ownership table. In particular:

- MECH-555/556 retain frame/gate ownership; MECH-561 retains phase addressing.
- ARC-142/144 retain relational and boundary-contract ownership; MECH-537/547/548 retain subspace,
  receiver-state and recurrent-stability ownership.
- MECH-540 and INV-109/110 retain interface-maintenance and evidence-standard ownership; MECH-562
  remains a live local-adaptation rival.
- ARC-121 remains a common-object rival. Neither heterogeneity nor a universal coordinate system
  has been selected by this literature pass.

No paper in this tranche licenses a claim-confidence update by resemblance. No claim, scored
literature evidence record, experiment, version-routing decision or substrate mutation is created.
The resulting documents are the requested thought-intake update and this supporting literature record.
