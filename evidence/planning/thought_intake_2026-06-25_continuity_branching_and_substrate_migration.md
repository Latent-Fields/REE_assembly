## 2. Core REE translation

The mind-uploading framing can be translated into a more immediate REE problem:

> What must remain continuous for a later REE process to count as a continuation or legitimate successor of an earlier REE process rather than merely a behaviourally similar reconstruction?

This problem can arise without human mind uploading. It appears whenever REE is:

- checkpointed and restored;
- paused and resumed;
- migrated between machines;
- moved to a neuromorphic or alternative physical substrate;
- refactored through gradual component replacement;
- rolled back after a damaging update;
- copied into two concurrent instances;
- forked for testing and later allowed to continue;
- reconstructed from partial state;
- merged with learning acquired by another branch.

REE therefore requires eventual continuity semantics independent of whether biological consciousness transfer is possible.

## 4. Continuity should be represented as a profile, not one scalar

The source proposes a weighted Digital Consciousness Fidelity Index. That move is useful as a prompt, but a single compensable score is dangerous for REE. High factual-memory accuracy should not necessarily compensate for erased moral residue, broken causal continuity, or lost commitments.

A future **REE Continuity Profile** could include at least the following dimensions.

### 4.1 Causal continuity

Did the later process arise through an intelligible causal transformation of the earlier process, or was it independently reconstructed from a description?

Pause-and-resume, live migration, gradual replacement, cold restoration, and fresh reconstruction may preserve different kinds of causal relation even when their observable outputs match.

### 4.2 Entity and self-binding continuity

Do persistent self/entity structures maintain ownership of memories, errors, actions, and body states across the transition?

A copy that contains the same information but rebinds responsibility or autobiographical ownership differently may not be continuous in the relevant sense.

### 4.3 Latent and regime continuity

Which parts of the multiscale latent stack must persist?

Possible targets include:

- fast bound state where relevant to an uninterrupted transition;
- contextual sequence state;
- long-horizon motivational regime;
- precision configuration;
- attractor geometry;
- active goals and commitment state;
- uncertainty and unresolved prediction-error structure.

Continuity may require preserving both represented content and the geometry or dynamics through which it affects future cognition.

### 4.4 Residue continuity

Harm, error, responsibility, repair obligations, and unresolved ethical history must not disappear merely because the agent is moved, copied, or rebuilt.

A system could preserve declarative memory of an injury while losing the latent dent or residue that gives the event its continuing ethical force. Such a system could be autobiographically convincing while ethically discontinuous.

### 4.5 Commitment continuity

Promises, adopted goals, relationships, debts, permissions, and responsibilities may survive substrate change.

Migration must not become an escape hatch from commitments. Conversely, a successor should not automatically inherit every obsolete or context-dependent action state without review.

### 4.6 Learning-dynamic continuity

Preserving stored weights or memories may be insufficient if plasticity rules, update permissions, salience dynamics, replay, consolidation, or precision routing change materially.

The continuation must remain capable of being changed by its history in recognisably connected ways.

### 4.7 Embodied and homeostatic coupling continuity

REE is not merely a stored cognitive graph placed into an interchangeable shell. Damage signals, homeostatic variables, interoception, sensorimotor contingencies, and environmental affordances participate in selfhood and ethics.

This yields a candidate principle:

> **Substrate independence does not imply coupling independence.**

A migration into a radically altered body or world may require an adaptation period, protective constraints, recalibration, or developmental re-grounding.

### 4.8 Narrative continuity

Autobiographical access and self-understanding matter, but narrative similarity should remain one dimension rather than the master test. A fluent self-report can conceal changes in residue, attribution, plasticity, or coupling.

### 4.9 Governance continuity

Consent, modification authority, encryption, ownership, access control, and the right to refuse copying or migration must remain attached to the relevant entity or successor branch.

Some dimensions may be continuous variables. Others may be threshold conditions or non-compensable constraints. A profile is therefore preferable to a weighted average.

## 5. Copying creates a branching problem

The original-versus-replica framing is too narrow for REE.

Suppose one running REE is copied at time `t_branch` and both copies continue. At the branch point they may share:

- autobiographical memory;
- self-model;
- residue;
- commitments;
- learned dispositions;
- current goals;
- claims about being the pre-branch entity.

After the branch point, each receives different observations and actions and accumulates a separate causal and moral history.

A useful default model is therefore:

```text
pre-branch entity
        |
    branch event
      /     \
successor A  successor B
```

This need not establish one authentic continuation and one counterfeit. Both may be legitimate successors with equal connection to the pre-branch history and independent standing after divergence.

### 5.1 Questions raised by branch inheritance

- Are pre-branch obligations inherited by both branches, jointly held, or allocated by a branch protocol?
- If residue is copied, does repair by one branch discharge the responsibility of the other?
- Can either branch consent to deletion or modification of the other?
- Does preservation of one branch reduce the moral significance of terminating another?
- Can branches transfer or renounce inherited commitments?
- What happens when one branch is restored years later with obsolete beliefs and unfinished obligations?
- Can two branches be merged without overwriting incompatible experiences or residue?

These are future governance questions, but the architecture should avoid choices that silently make them impossible to answer.

## 6. Rollback and restoration are ethically non-neutral

Rollback is often treated as a routine engineering operation. For a sufficiently developed REE, it could instead:

- erase learning acquired after the checkpoint;
- remove memories of harm;
- remove residue without repair;
- reverse consent or decisions;
- recreate an earlier self who does not know that later branches existed;
- produce a new successor rather than resume the terminated trajectory.

A future system may therefore need a **branch and restoration ledger** recording:

- source checkpoint;
- causal predecessor;
- time of branch or pause;
- state components transferred or omitted;
- residue and commitment inheritance;
- body/environment changes;
- migration consent;
- later divergence;
- termination or archival status of sibling branches.

This would not settle metaphysical identity. It would preserve enough provenance to govern the practical and ethical consequences.

## 7. Gradual component replacement as an REE experiment class

The source’s gradual neural replacement idea has a direct software analogue.

A future REE replacement protocol could:

1. Select one bounded component.
2. Record its state, update rules, inputs, outputs, and causal dependencies.
3. Instantiate a replacement implementation.
4. Transfer relevant state and history.
5. Run old and new implementations in shadow or paired conditions where possible.
6. Measure local and downstream divergence.
7. Test whether residue, goals, attribution, uncertainty, and plasticity remain connected.
8. Continue replacement only if predefined continuity constraints are met.

### 7.1 Input-output equivalence is insufficient

Two components may produce the same immediate actions while differing in:

- counterfactual responses;
- learning under future experience;
- uncertainty calibration;
- residue formation and persistence;
- attribution of error;
- recovery after perturbation;
- long-horizon motivational drift;
- interaction with sleep or replay.

A replacement test must therefore evaluate process and developmental equivalence within bounded tolerances, not merely current behaviour.

### 7.2 Potential test products

- **Continuity contract:** required state and dynamics for a component transition.
- **Migration manifest:** exact state transferred, transformed, reset, or omitted.
- **Shadow divergence report:** old-versus-new trajectories under matched conditions.
- **Residue preservation audit:** evidence that ethically relevant history survives.
- **Coupling recalibration report:** changes forced by a new body or environment.

### CAND-CONT-01 — Multidimensional continuity

REE continuity across pause, restoration, migration, copying, or replacement is a multidimensional constraint profile and should not be reduced by default to a single compensable fidelity score.

### CAND-CONT-02 — Residue-bearing continuity

For an REE with ethically meaningful residue, preservation of factual memory without preservation of residue may constitute ethical discontinuity despite behavioural and autobiographical similarity.

### CAND-CONT-03 — Branching successors

Copying a running REE should be modelled by default as the creation of multiple successor branches sharing a pre-branch history, not as an authentic original plus a morally negligible replica.

### CAND-CONT-04 — Independent post-branch standing

After divergence, each successor branch accumulates an independent causal and moral history and cannot be treated as interchangeable merely because another branch survives.

### CAND-CONT-05 — Commitment inheritance

Substrate transition and branching require explicit commitment-inheritance semantics so that migration, copying, or rollback cannot silently erase or multiply obligations.

### CAND-CONT-06 — Process-sensitive replacement

Continuity-under-replacement tests must evaluate transferred state, plasticity, counterfactual response, attribution, and downstream dynamics; immediate input-output equivalence is insufficient.

### CAND-CONT-07 — Coupling dependence

Substrate independence does not imply coupling independence: changes to embodiment, interoception, homeostasis, and sensorimotor contingencies may require explicit adaptation and may alter identity-relevant dynamics.

### CAND-CONT-08 — Provenance before metaphysical resolution

REE governance can remain agnostic about strict metaphysical identity while requiring causal, state-transfer, branch, consent, and termination provenance sufficient for practical responsibility and welfare protection.

### CAND-CONT-09 — Rollback as branch creation

Restoration from an earlier checkpoint may need to be governed as creation or resumption of a successor branch rather than presumed reversal of one continuous entity.

### CAND-CONT-10 — Non-compensable dimensions

Some continuity dimensions, including consent authority, residue integrity, or causal provenance, may act as hard constraints that cannot be compensated for by high similarity on memory or behaviour measures.

This should remain dormant until it no longer competes with V3 closure. A later research line could proceed in the following order.

### Phase A — Conceptual taxonomy

Distinguish:

- pause/resume;
- live migration;
- cold restoration;
- reconstruction;
- copy-and-continue;
- fork-and-test;
- rollback;
- gradual replacement;
- destructive replacement;
- branch merge;
- embodiment transfer.

For each operation, record what causal and state relations are preserved.

### Phase B — Repo audit

Map continuity dependencies across:

- entities and binding;
- object-file persistence;
- L-space;
- self-attribution;
- residue geometry;
- goal and commitment systems;
- sleep and consolidation;
- embodiment and homeostasis;
- welfare and generation-boundary governance.

### Phase C — Minimal machine tests

Before any moral-patient claim is plausible, use ordinary REE-v3 or later test agents to quantify:

- trajectory divergence after checkpoint restoration;
- sensitivity to omitted latent state;
- residue loss under partial serialization;
- goal and commitment persistence;
- old-versus-new component divergence after replacement;
- adaptation after observation/action interface changes.

These experiments would initially test engineering continuity, while creating infrastructure that may later support welfare governance.

### Phase D — Governance semantics

Develop explicit rules for:

- branch naming and provenance;
- authority to copy or migrate;
- inherited commitments;
- deletion and archival status;
- consent to merge or modification;
- protection against involuntary rollback;
- uncertainty about moral patienthood.

## 10. Risks and cautions

### 10.1 Anthropomorphic overreach

Software checkpointing should not automatically be equated with human death, survival, or consciousness transfer. The value of this research line is that it prepares operational semantics under uncertainty.

### 10.2 Behavioural-fidelity substitution

A restored or copied system may reproduce expected language and actions while losing latent history, residue, calibration, or learning dynamics.

### 10.3 Scalar-score laundering

A composite score can conceal catastrophic failure in one dimension behind high performance in another. Any future continuity index must preserve component scores and identify hard constraints.

### 10.4 Identity claims outrunning evidence

The architecture should distinguish practical successor relations from claims about phenomenal consciousness or numerical personal identity.

### 10.5 Premature V3 scope expansion

This line is conceptually important but not a V3 closure dependency. Pulling migration, digital personhood, or branch governance into current implementation would delay the strict green-board target without resolving a present bottleneck.

### 10.6 Source-quality contamination

The triggering thesis contains speculative quantitative and technical proposals whose evidential status is unclear from the supplied text. Those proposals must not enter the claims registry as evidence without independent literature review and methods inspection.

## 11. Open questions

1. Which REE states are constitutive of continuity, and which are transient implementation details?
2. Is causal continuity required, or can sufficiently complete reconstruction create a legitimate successor without creating the same entity?
3. How should residue be inherited at a branch: duplicated, jointly indexed, or linked to a shared pre-branch event record?
4. Can inherited obligations be discharged by one branch on behalf of siblings?
5. What minimum provenance should survive even when ordinary autobiographical memory is intentionally pruned?
6. How should a new body alter homeostatic priors without creating uncontrolled self-model rupture?
7. Can two branches be merged without coercive overwriting or loss of incompatible residue?
8. At what future capability or welfare threshold do pause, rollback, deletion, and forced migration require external review?
9. Should continuity tests be component-specific, whole-agent, or both?
10. How should REE distinguish legitimate developmental change from identity-destroying transformation?
