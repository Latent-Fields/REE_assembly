## 2. Method

- **World head training:** `probes/w3/w3_l2r_member_probe.py`'s exact recipe, byte-identical to `probes/aspc/gate_c_probe.py` (`E2WorldMember` via `WakingTrainer`, W2a `StructuredBabbler` babbling over 12 Phase-0 episodes into a FROZEN retained set, 3000 pre-updates, then 1200 native closed-loop on-policy steps at 8 member updates/step, 25% retained replay, re-encoded). Seeds 106-110 (same 5 seeds as the ASP readout).
- **Codec training (new):** a `CodecMember` (`ree_core/utils/waking_trainer_codec.py`, lr 1e-3, batch 16, buffer 2000, `code_l2` 1e-3 -- all defaults) registered on the **same** `WakingTrainer` alongside the world member, so both read the same tick stream. `codec.updates_per_step` is pinned to 0 so the per-tick `on_waking_step` never auto-trains it (only fills its buffer); babble-phase `z_world` states are additionally fed via `CodecMember.add_states()` (its own documented manual path) because the babble loop does not call `on_waking_step`. Exactly 1000 manual `tr._update("codec", codec)` calls after the babble pre-phase -- matching `test_w1_codec.py`'s GB gate recipe ("1000 member updates" on recorded `z_world`). The two members' parameter groups (`e2.world_transition`/`world_action_encoder` vs `e2.action_object_head`/`hippocampal.action_object_decoder`) are disjoint, so joint registration does not confound either training signal.
- **Readout head:** the trained world-head state dicts are loaded into `ref`, the SAME full production `REEAgent` construction aspc used (`rollout_fidelity_probe.build_B`, `REEConfig.from_dims`, `CausalGridWorldV2` 8x8, world_dim 32).
- **Held-out states:** identical protocol and identical per-seed RNG draws to `gate_c_probe.py` (`BB.gen_policy` at k0=300+, disjoint from every training k-range; one `(z_world, z_self)` per generated segment at a per-episode-random tick). Per-seed state counts: 166/173/183/177/179 (seeds 106-110) -- identical to aspc's own counts for the same seeds, confirming the SAME states were drawn (same RNG calls, same seeding scheme).
- **Four pools, same states, same trained world head, per seed:**
  1. **`native_codec_w1_on`**: `use_action_space_proposals=False`; `use_codec_bounded_decode=True`; `use_codec_iter0_image_match=True`; the codec (encoder+decoder) weights are the ones just trained. This is the native (non-ASP) proposer's decode path with the W1 codec repair active.
  2. **`native_default`**: `use_action_space_proposals=False`; both codec knobs `False`; codec weights left at `ref`'s own UNTRAINED construction (P3 above) -- today's actual deployed default.
  3. **`control_random_per_step`**: no proposer at all. `K=32` (matching `num_candidates`) one-hot action sequences per held-out state, each of the `H=30` per-candidate actions drawn i.i.d. uniform over the 5 classes, rolled out directly via `agent.e2.rollout_with_world(z_self, z_world, actions, compute_action_objects=False)`.
  4. **`control_constant_per_class`**: same direct-rollout path, but each of the 32 candidates holds ONE class constant for all 30 steps, cycling `i % 5` to cover every class 6-7 times per state.
  - Pools (3)/(4) touch neither `propose_trajectories`, the CEM loop, nor the codec at all -- `compute_action_objects=False` means the decoder is never called.
- **Gate (c) computed exactly as `gate_c_probe.py`/the design defines it**: per pool, per seed, pool every candidate's ratio (`||z_world_H|| / ||z_world_0||`) and per-step growth (`||z_world_i|| / ||z_world_{i-1}||`, max over all i) across all held-out states x candidates; PASS iff pooled median ratio in [0.5, 2] and pooled max growth <= 1.05.
- **Two alternative readings** (aspc sec 4, not measured there), computed on the SAME per-candidate world-state sequences for every pool: (a) late-window MEAN growth over steps 21-30 (W3 gate (e)'s own convention), pooled median and max; (b) max growth restricted to the first D in {1, 3, 5} steps, pooled max.
- Script: `probes/aspc2/gate_c_pool_controls_probe.py` (committed alongside this record). Raw per-seed JSON: `probes/aspc2/gatec2_s{106,107,108,109,110}.json`.

| pool | seed 106 | seed 107 | seed 108 | seed 109 | seed 110 | gate (c) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| `native_codec_w1_on` (ASP off, W1 codec ON, codec trained) | 1.4466 / **1.7238** | 2.8831 / **1.7940** | 1.6707 / **1.7372** | 1.5058 / **1.7243** | 1.1518 / **1.7384** | **FAIL 5/5** |
| `control_random_per_step` (no proposer) | 1.5150 / **1.7254** | 2.0275 / **1.7942** | 1.7488 / **1.7372** | 1.4780 / **1.7243** | 1.1097 / **1.7384** | **FAIL 5/5** |
| `control_constant_per_class` (no proposer) | 1.9480 / **1.7254** | 4.3894 / **1.7942** | 2.7729 / **1.7372** | 1.7070 / **1.7243** | 1.2495 / **1.7384** | **FAIL 5/5** |

All 20 pool x seed cells FAIL the growth leg (bound <= 1.05); the ratio leg PASSes on most cells (median in [0.5,2]) but is not the gate-deciding leg, exactly as in the ASP readout. **The two controls -- which involve no optimization, no CEM, no codec, no ASP, and cannot express a "the proposer found an adversarial sequence" story -- fail at the SAME magnitude (1.70-1.79) as the native proposer pools.**

- **The worst-case growth is driven entirely by STEP 1.** `pooled_max_first1_max_growth` (max growth restricted to only the FIRST simulated step) equals `pooled_max_max_growth_all` (the literal all-30-steps max) to 4 decimal places, in every one of the 20 pool x seed cells, and D=3 and D=5 give the identical number too. This was not visible in the ASP-only readout and refines its finding: the instability is not a compounding-over-many-steps phenomenon, it is a single poorly-conditioned `world_forward` step from a (state, first-action-class) pair, and once that first step lands on such a pair the growth measured over the rest of the horizon never exceeds it.
- **`control_random_per_step` and `control_constant_per_class` hit an IDENTICAL pooled max growth within each seed** (e.g. seed 107: 1.7942/1.7942; seed 110: 1.7384/1.7384) -- consistent with the step-1-only finding above: since both control pools' 32 candidates collectively cover every one of the 5 action classes as the FIRST action at every held-out state, both pools necessarily contain the same worst (state, class) pair, regardless of what either pool does at steps 2-30.
- **Late-window (steps 21-30) mean growth reading (W3 gate (e)'s own convention) passes comfortably in every pool, every seed** -- pooled median 1.002-1.110, pooled max 1.036-1.158, all well under gate (e)'s own "no x1.2/step" bound and in the same range aspc reported for ASP alone (median 1.001-1.012, max 1.011-1.047 -- slightly tighter there, but the same regime). This reconfirms aspc's sec 4 finding (a): a late-window/mean statistic and a literal all-step/max statistic disagree sharply on the SAME rollouts, and the disagreement is not specific to ASP.
- Full per-seed pooled numbers (all readings, all pools): `probes/aspc2/gatec2_s{106..110}.json`.

## 4. Verdict: GENERAL

**All four pools breach the growth leg at comparable magnitude, 5/5 seeds, including the two controls that contain no proposer, no optimizer, and no codec/ASP mechanism whatsoever.** Per the decision node this closes: the growth-leg failure is a property of the trained W3 `E2` world head's `rollout_with_world` at the deployed `H=30`, for candidate action sequences drawn from ANY distribution over the 5-class action space at these held-out states -- not a defect specific to ASP's proposal mechanism, not specific to the native codec-based CEM proposer, and not evidence that an *optimizing* proposer finds unusually destabilizing sequences (the constant-and-random controls, which do no optimization, reproduce the same worst-case magnitude). This is **GENERAL**, not PROPOSER-SPECIFIC and not MIXED: no pool -- including either control -- stayed under the 1.05 bound.

This sharpens (does not merely confirm) aspc sec 4's two candidate readings: reading (a) ("the design's 1.05-per-any-step-anywhere bound was calibrated against an obviously-bad untrained-head signature and may be the wrong bound for a genuinely trained head at H=30") is far more consistent with what was measured here than reading (b) ("CEM/ASP finds sequences more destabilizing than random") -- (b) is now directly falsified by the controls. The refined finding (sec 3, step-1-only) further narrows reading (a): if the bound is revisited, the revisit should look at the FIRST simulated step's conditioning specifically (a property of `world_transition`/`world_action_encoder` at that state), not at multi-step compounding or at anything proposer-side.
