## 1. Verbatim core proposal

> **The more it rewards agreement, the more it risks destroying the independence that made agreement
> valuable.**

> **Convergence should be something the system detects when diverse routes happen to align, not
> something it trains all routes to do continuously.**

> **agreement among clones is cheap; agreement among competent differences is valuable.**

> The target is not maximum diversity or maximum agreement, but a system capable of generating different
> answers, noticing when those answers independently converge, and re-opening diversity when reality
> exposes the convergence as premature.

## 3. Key formulations (verbatim)

- "This is not a side constraint. It may be the central design problem of any convergence-aware
  architecture."
- "A system in which every evaluator has been trained to produce the same representation may appear
  beautifully coherent while being fragile to exactly the errors all of them share."
- "These are all cases where apparent convergence can rise while **effective independent support
  falls**."
- "A system can maximise representational difference by producing arbitrary noise, contrarian outputs or
  useless specialisation."
- "preserve competent, non-redundant routes."
- "Independent methods are valuable because they can disagree. Their later agreement is strong precisely
  because they were capable of producing different answers."
- "avoid turning the convergence detector into a teacher that homogenises its own evidence base."
- "the optimal mature state may therefore contain **less raw diversity than the immature system but more
  useful diversity than a system trained directly for one solution**."
- "For convergence evidence, **error/causal diversity** may matter more than geometric diversity."
- "If consensus receives extra attention, dissent risks being silenced precisely when it is most
  valuable."
- "high convergence normally lowers further-deliberation value, EXCEPT when dissent is high-reliability,
  protected, or novel in a diagnostic way."
- "C should preserve more robust cross-view evidence than B even if B has higher raw agreement."
