## 1. Verbatim core proposal

> A system can preserve useful information while failing because another system cannot interpret,
> index, retrieve, or use that information in the form required at the relevant moment.

The strongest synthesis, stated as an explicit rejection of the stronger reading:

> not: `hippocampus = universal translator`
> but: **hippocampal-like machinery may provide relational indices, temporally ordered
> correspondences, replayed paired examples, and context reinstatement that allow specialised systems
> to learn, select, maintain, or re-engage low-complexity translations between their otherwise
> different representations.**

And the bounded unifying hypothesis (section 14), which differs from the sibling's equation by being a
CONTEXT-BOUND READ SURFACE rather than a state-to-state map:

```
m_(A->B,t) = T_(A->B)(x_A ; h_t, x_B, D_t, p_t)
```

where `h_t` is a hippocampal relational/episodic index, `x_B` the receiver state, `D_t` the directive
context, `p_t` the temporal phase, and `m` "a consumer-facing message or temporary projection, **not a
rewrite of B's whole representation**."

## 3. Key formulations (verbatim)

- "A system can preserve useful information while failing because another system cannot interpret, index, retrieve, or use that information in the form required at the relevant moment."
- "an interface can be live and computationally connected yet remain functionally poor because sender and consumer disagree about what kind of relation is being communicated."
- "**a live connection is not sufficient; what crosses the connection must preserve the relational object the receiver's computation requires.**"
- "CITM does not necessarily give A and B a common representation. It can give them a common **indexing relation** against which different representations can be aligned."
- "**The crucial point is that shared reference does not require shared code.**"
- "the useful hippocampal index need not remain fixed across development or learning."
- "even if hippocampal indexing is important to translation, TCRT does not imply that the hippocampus performs the whole transformation."
- "A successful linear or low-rank bridge should defeat the need for a more elaborate hippocampal translation story at that boundary. Failure of an over-capacity bridge should move the question upstream rather than invite an even larger translator."
- "Calling all eleven 'translation' would obscure rather than clarify the science."
- "That recurrence makes the synthesis worth testing, but **recurrence inside one research programme is not independent empirical evidence.**"
