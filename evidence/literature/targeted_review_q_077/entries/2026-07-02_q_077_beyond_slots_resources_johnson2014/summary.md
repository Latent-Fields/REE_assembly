# Johnson, Simmering & Buss (2014) — beyond the dichotomy

**Claim tested:** Q-077 — is SD-046's multi-slot GoalState discrete fixed-capacity slots or a flexible shared resource?

According to PubMed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-013-0596-9)), Johnson, Simmering & Buss decline to pick a side of Q-077's fork and instead argue that the fork itself is the problem. They grant that both the fixed-slot account and the shared-resource account have "generated and explained impressive amounts of empirical data" — neither is simply wrong. Their claim is that both accounts are *representation-centric*: they argue about what is stored (a fixed number of discrete representations, or a continuously-divided resource) rather than about the *processes* that produce the capacity limit. As an alternative they present dynamic field theory (DFT), a neurally-grounded, process-based model in which working-memory items are self-sustaining peaks in a neural field held apart by lateral inhibition. They show DFT reproduces the key change-detection phenomena, extends to recall, feature integration, cognitive development, individual differences, and functional imaging, and — importantly — treats the capacity limit not as a stipulated number but as an *emergent* property of the field dynamics.

This is the adjudicating entry for the Q-077 directory, and I have marked it **mixed** because that is exactly what it is: it validates the fork as a real, data-supported tension while arguing it is posed at the wrong level. For REE this is the most *constructive* of the three entries, because it hands SD-046 a third design option. Rather than committing SD-046 to either N discrete goal slots or one scalar precision budget, the DFT framing suggests implementing a set of *mutually-inhibiting goal fields* whose effective capacity — and whether it degrades as a step or as a smooth dilution — falls out of the competition dynamics (field coupling, lateral-inhibition strength, resting level). That is a natural fit for a substrate that already thinks in terms of latent fields and competition, and it aligns with REE's broader ARC-106 preference for building the mechanism and letting the phenomenon emerge rather than stipulating the phenomenon.

The two failure signatures this entry contributes are consequential for how Q-077 should eventually be tested. First, DFT can produce *either* a quasi-step or a smooth-dilution capacity curve depending on its parameters — so the step-vs-smooth behavioural signature that Q-077's `what_would_answer` relies on to discriminate slot from resource may be *underdetermined*. Two systems with the same fidelity-vs-N curve could differ in their underlying dynamics, and the same dynamics could yield either curve. The practical implication is that REE may not be able to settle Q-077 from the degradation curve alone; it may need to instrument the goal-competition dynamics directly (field interaction terms, inhibition among slots) rather than inferring structure from behaviour. Second, if REE forces SD-046 into one horn to "answer" Q-077, it risks inheriting a false dichotomy instead of the process-level mechanism that actually generates the limit.

I hold confidence at 0.58. Source quality is solid and the paper addresses Q-077's exact wording more directly than either primary horn, which is why mapping fidelity (0.60) is unusually high for a lit entry; the discount is that DFT-to-goal-substrate is a design proposal rather than an established result, and the paper deliberately does not tell REE which horn SD-046 occupies — it tells REE the question may be malformed, and offers a better-posed one.
