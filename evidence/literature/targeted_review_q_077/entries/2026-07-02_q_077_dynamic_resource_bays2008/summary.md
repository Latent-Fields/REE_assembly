# Bays & Husain (2008) — a flexible, dynamically-shared resource

**Claim tested:** Q-077 — is SD-046's multi-slot GoalState a set of discrete fixed-capacity slots or a single *flexible precision resource* shared across goals?

According to PubMed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1158023)), Bays & Husain make the case for the resource horn of exactly this fork. Using analogue (continuous) report measures rather than binary change-detection, they show that visual memory capacity is not fixed by a number of objects. Instead it behaves like a limited resource shared out dynamically across all items in a scene: the more items, the less resource each receives, and the *proportion* of resource allocated to an item determines the *precision* with which it is remembered — a relation they characterise with a simple power law. Critically, allocation is not uniform; it is biased by selective attention and toward the targets of upcoming eye movements. Some items are remembered well at the expense of others.

This is the direct evidential anchor for Q-077's resource reading, and it maps onto SD-046 in two specific ways. First, the power-law degradation is the *smooth-dilution* signature Q-077's `what_would_answer` assigns to the resource model — each added goal dilutes a shared precision budget continuously, with no fixed slot count and no hard cutoff. Second, and more precisely, Bays & Husain's finding that attention and saccade targets draw disproportionate resource maps almost one-to-one onto Q-077's sub-prediction that "the acted-on goal draws disproportionate fidelity." If SD-046's goals share a precision budget the way Bays & Husain's visual items share theirs, the currently-acted-on goal should be represented sharply while dormant goals degrade gracefully.

I have marked this **mixed** rather than *weakens*, deliberately, and consistent with Q-077's own registration note (which records that the Bays & Husain and Heilbronner entries are "marked MIXED against SD-046 precisely because they problematise that framing"). The nuance is that Bays & Husain undercut the *discrete-slot parameterisation* while remaining fully consistent with the *multi-slot design decision itself* — SD-046 holding N≥2 goals at graded fidelity is exactly what a resource model predicts. So the paper does not refute SD-046; it argues its internal parameterisation should be resource-like rather than slot-like.

Two caveats hold confidence at 0.57. First, this is visual working-memory *precision* for briefly-remembered items, not maintenance of active goals with drive coupling, age, and persistence — the transfer from per-item visual precision to per-goal behavioural fidelity is a real gap. Second, and importantly for honesty, the result is *contested within its own field*: Zhang & Luck (2008, Nature) and Cowan & Rouder (2009, a direct Science comment on this very paper, with a Bays & Husain reply) argue that a slots-plus-averaging / item-limit model fits the same data at least as well. So Bays & Husain cannot unilaterally settle the fork — which is the whole reason Q-077 exists as an open question. The discriminating escalating-load experiment has to be run on REE's own SD-046 substrate; the source literature is genuinely split, and this entry represents the strongest single statement of one side of that split.
