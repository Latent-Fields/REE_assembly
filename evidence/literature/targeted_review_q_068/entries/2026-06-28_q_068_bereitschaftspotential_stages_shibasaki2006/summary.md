# What is the Bereitschaftspotential?

**Source:** Shibasaki & Hallett (2006), *Clinical Neurophysiology*. [DOI: 10.1016/j.clinph.2006.04.025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2006.04.025)
**Claim tested:** Q-068 (graded action-status vocabulary — simulated/rehearsed/intended/committed/acted)
**Evidence direction:** supports · **Confidence:** 0.60

According to PubMed, this is the canonical review of the readiness potential — the slow negative EEG drift that precedes self-initiated movement. Its relevance to Q-068 is that it does not treat "getting ready to act" as a single state. The Bereitschaftspotential decomposes into two neurally distinct stages: an *early BP* that begins roughly two seconds before movement onset, generated in the pre-SMA and SMA, site-non-specific, reflecting a slowly increasing cortical excitability that the authors gloss as subconscious readiness; and a *late BP* (also called NS′) that begins about 400 ms before onset, generated in contralateral M1 and lateral premotor cortex with precise somatotopy, and — crucially — selectively enhanced by movement complexity while the early BP is not. Two stages, two generators, two modulation profiles. The transition from being-ready to executing is structured, not a step function from "off" to "moving."

Q-068 asks whether REE needs an explicit graded action-status vocabulary as a first-class annotation, or whether MECH-094's simulation/commit write-profiles plus MECH-061's commit-boundary token already carry the distinction implicitly. This paper speaks to the *premise* underneath that question: that the later action statuses — intended, committed, acted — are real, separable stages in the substrate rather than arbitrary slices of a continuum. The early-BP / late-BP dissociation, with the late stage specifically gated by task complexity, is exactly the kind of structure that would justify placing a commit boundary (MECH-061) at a genuine transition rather than at a convenient label. If the brain itself marks distinct, differentially-modulated stages on the road to action, then an annotation scheme that records those stages is tracking something, not inventing it.

But I want to be careful about how far this carries, because it is the easier half of Q-068. The BP indexes the *preparation-to-execution tail* of the gradient: roughly intended → committed → acted. It is largely silent on the covert end — the simulated vs rehearsed vs intended distinctions — which is where the genuinely hard adjudication lives (those are the statuses MECH-094 write-profiles would have to disambiguate without an explicit token). And it says nothing at all about the parallel `self_reference_frame` axis (first_person / system_state / third_person_model) that Q-068 bundles in. So the paper supports "the later statuses are neurally distinct stages" with good authority, and does not reach "REE must annotate the full five-way vocabulary as first-class rather than deriving the finer distinctions."

Confidence is 0.60. Source quality is high — a definitive review of a well-replicated phenomenon. But mapping fidelity is the limiting factor: it covers maybe the last two-fifths of Q-068's gradient and none of the self-reference-frame question, so it can confirm the existence of graded structure without settling whether REE needs an *explicit* vocabulary or whether write-profiles suffice. Transfer risk is moderate, the usual human-EEG-to-agent-annotation jump. Read against the two motor-imagery entries in this directory, the picture is a deliberate tension: the BP shows the later statuses are separable (supporting an explicit graded scheme), while the simulation-theory literature shows the covert statuses share a common core differentiated by gating (which is closer to the "write-profiles suffice" view) — Q-068 is genuinely open, and these entries map the two sides rather than closing it.
