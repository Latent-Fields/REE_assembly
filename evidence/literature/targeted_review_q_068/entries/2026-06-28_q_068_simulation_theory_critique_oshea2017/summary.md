# Does motor simulation theory explain motor imagery? A critical review

**Source:** O'Shea & Moran (2017), *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*. [DOI: 10.3389/fnhum.2017.00072](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2017.00072)
**Claim tested:** Q-068 (graded action-status vocabulary — simulated/rehearsed/intended/committed/acted)
**Evidence direction:** mixed · **Confidence:** 0.55

According to PubMed, this is the skeptic's entry in the motor-simulation literature, and I have included it deliberately as a counterweight to the Munzert review. Where the standard story (Jeannerod's simulation theory) says imagined and executed actions share representations and mechanisms, O'Shea and Moran step back and ask whether the evidence actually licenses that confidence. Their verdict is cautionary: the psychological mechanisms underlying motor imagery are poorly understood; the key terms — simulation, emulation, functional equivalence — are used loosely and need sharper conceptual and empirical delineation; and the assumed functional equivalence between imagery and execution is less settled than the field tends to assume. They are not refuting the gradient so much as saying the field has not earned the right to treat its action-status vocabulary as established.

For Q-068 this is genuinely double-edged, which is why I mark it mixed. Q-068 weighs an explicit, first-class graded action-status vocabulary against the alternative that MECH-094's write-profiles plus MECH-061's commit token already carry the distinction implicitly. O'Shea and Moran's central complaint — that action-related cognitive states are described too vaguely to be tested — actually supports the *explicit* side: if you leave the statuses implicit and expect write-profiles to "sort it out," you reproduce exactly the under-specification they fault simulation theory for. A vocabulary you can name, define, and falsify is the antidote to that vagueness. But their other conclusion — that the simulated-vs-executed relationship is not cleanly resolved — warns against the opposite error of hard-coding a particular five-way taxonomy as settled fact. The evidence does not license treating any fixed partition as established truth.

The resolution those two pulls suggest, and the one I think is faithful to the source, is: make the action-status vocabulary *explicit and well-specified* (against leaving it to implicit write-profiles), but *revisable* (against freezing a taxonomy the science has not settled). That is, satisfyingly, consistent with Q-068's own metadata — it is flagged substrate_conditional, implementation_phase v4, needing the ARC-090 self-narration surface to actually probe. The literature is telling us the same thing the claim already says about itself: this is not yet adjudicable, and the right move is to define the distinctions clearly enough to test them later, not to pretend they are resolved now.

Confidence is 0.55, the lowest of the three Q-068 entries, and appropriately so: this is a conceptual critique that adjudicates nothing empirically, addresses imagery-vs-execution rather than the full vocabulary or the self-reference-frame axis, and bears on the *meta*-question (should the distinctions be explicit and well-defined?) more than on the object-level partition. Source quality is fair, mapping fidelity moderate, transfer risk the usual. Taken with the other two entries, the three map a coherent picture: distinct neural stages exist at the action end (BP), a shared core differentiated by gating exists at the covert end (Munzert), and the conceptual house is not yet in order (O'Shea & Moran) — so Q-068's honest status is open, with literature favouring an explicit-but-revisable vocabulary over both implicit write-profiles and a frozen taxonomy.
