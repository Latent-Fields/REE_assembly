# Cognitive motor processes: motor imagery and motor representations

**Source:** Munzert, Lorey & Zentgraf (2009), *Brain Research Reviews*. [DOI: 10.1016/j.brainresrev.2008.12.024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brainresrev.2008.12.024)
**Claim tested:** Q-068 (graded action-status vocabulary — simulated/rehearsed/intended/committed/acted)
**Evidence direction:** mixed · **Confidence:** 0.58

According to PubMed, this review synthesises the motor-imagery evidence for Jeannerod's mental-simulation theory, which holds that imagined, observed and executed actions draw on the same underlying motor representations. The supporting data are substantial: motor areas — including, strikingly, primary motor cortex — activate during pure imagery; mental performance durations track actual movement durations closely (the so-called isochrony of imagined and real action); and the chief thing that distinguishes imagining from doing is whether overt execution is *gated*. On this account the difference between a simulated action and an executed one is not two separate machines; it is one representation with the output valve closed or open.

This is why I have marked the entry *mixed* rather than supports or weakens, and the mixedness is the interesting part for Q-068. Q-068 asks whether REE needs an explicit, first-class graded action-status vocabulary (simulated ≠ rehearsed ≠ intended ≠ committed ≠ acted), or whether MECH-094's simulation/commit write-profiles plus the MECH-061 commit-boundary token already carry the distinction. The shared-representation evidence cuts toward the *second* answer. If simulation and execution are one representation differentiated mainly by an execution gate, then MECH-061's commit boundary — which is essentially a marker on that gate — together with write-profiles that record *how* a representation was written, may already encode the action status without storing it as a separate annotation. In other words, the biology offers a worked example of "one core representation, status read off the gating," which is precisely the architecture that would make an explicit vocabulary redundant.

And yet the same review keeps the distinction real. Imagery and execution are reliably *different* — measurably so in timing and performance — so a model that collapsed simulation and action into a single indistinguishable state would also be wrong. The evidence supports the existence of the statuses while undercutting the need to store each as independent machinery. That is the honest shape of it: graded statuses are real, but they may be derivable from gating rather than annotated as first-class. The review does not tell us how many discrete statuses are warranted, and — a real limit — its axis is imagery vs observation vs execution, so it speaks to the *endpoints* of Q-068's gradient (simulated, acted) and not to the middle distinctions (rehearsed vs intended vs committed), nor to the parallel self-reference-frame axis at all.

Confidence is 0.58. Source quality is good for a synthesis review; mapping fidelity is moderate because it covers only the endpoints of the vocabulary; transfer risk is the usual human-cognition-to-agent jump. The most useful way to read this entry is in tension with the Bereitschaftspotential entry in the same directory: the BP shows the *later* statuses are neurally separable stages (a point for an explicit graded scheme), while this review shows the *covert* statuses share a common core differentiated by gating (a point for "write-profiles plus a commit token suffice"). Both are true, which is exactly why Q-068 is still open — and why the substrate-conditional resolution it calls for needs the ARC-090 self-narration surface (V4), not a V3 experiment, to actually adjudicate.
