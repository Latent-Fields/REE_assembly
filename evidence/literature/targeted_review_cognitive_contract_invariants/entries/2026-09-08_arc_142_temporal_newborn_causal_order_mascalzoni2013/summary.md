# Mascalzoni et al. 2013 -- causal order, hours after birth

If you want the earliest possible evidence that a mind cares about which thing came first, this is it. Newborns a few hours old look longer at a launching event, where one object strikes another and the second moves off, than at the same motion with a delay or without contact. The preference is not about motion energy, which was matched. It is about the before-after structure that makes an event look caused.

GOV-CONTRACT-1 says developmental precedence is the strongest single-domain signal for an architectural invariant, and the earlier the better. It is hard to be earlier than this. MECH-545's temporal lesion predicts cause/effect inversion when the relation is dropped at a boundary; the newborn result says the relation is available before anything much has been learned.

I would not overstate it. A preference is not a representation an engine could carry or lose. And the developmental record splits: order that is causally or enabling-constrained is early, while arbitrary order becomes reliable only around 28 months and is helped along by verbal reminders (Bauer, Hertsgaard, Dropik & Daly 1998). That split is itself informative for the contract. It suggests the architectural part is the relation between cause and effect, and the cultural part is remembering an arbitrary list.
