# Goupil & Kouider 2016 -- knowing you might be wrong, before you can say so

Ask an eighteen-month-old where the toy went, and then watch how long she keeps looking. If she was right she persists; if she was wrong she gives up sooner. She could not tell you this, but the EEG says her brain marks the error with the same negativity an adult's does. A few months later, in the companion PNAS study, twenty-month-olds given a way to ask an adult for help use it selectively on the trials where their own memory is weak. Confidence is not only computed before language; it is exported across an agent boundary before language.

That last point is why this entry matters for the contract rather than merely for metacognition. MECH-545's confidence lesion deletes uncertainty at a boundary while the content goes through. The help-seeking result is the closest developmental analogue of uncertainty crossing a boundary with its content, and it is in place at twenty months.

The dissent is developmental too, and I have made it its own entry. Gori, Del Viva, Sandini and Burr (2008) found that children below about eight do not combine visual and haptic estimates by reliability; one modality simply wins. So confidence as a flag (persist, seek help) is early, and confidence as a weight (precision-weighted integration) is late. The candidate as written in MECH-545 runs the two together. The literature says they come apart, and a lesion assay that does not say which one it is deleting will not be interpretable.

Confidence 0.7. Motivation, not support.
