# Onishi, Baillargeon & Leslie 2007 -- two ledgers at fifteen months

An adult pretends to pour from an empty jug into one cup and then pretends to drink from the other. Fifteen-month-olds look longer. They are not confused about the cups, which are empty and stay empty; what they are tracking is the consistency of the pretend episode on its own terms. An imagined state is being held as a separate ledger from the observed one, and violations within that ledger are noticed.

That is the earliest evidence I could find for the REALITY-STATUS candidate, and it comes from the domain GOV-CONTRACT-1 weights most heavily. MECH-545 predicts that dropping this relation at an engine boundary produces confabulation-like commitment to imagined states; the infant result says the relation exists as a separate carrier before language and before anyone has explained what pretending is.

The caveats are real and I have given them room. Only the pretend/actual cut is tested here. The reliable assignment of reality status is a long, knowledge-dependent achievement that continues into the school years (Woolley & Ghossainy 2013) and degrades under emotion (Carrick & Quas 2006). And two other domains in this pull suggest reality status may not be one relation: the typology splits possible from counterfactual, and Dijkstra and Fleming show the perception/imagination judgement in adults is a threshold on a shared signal rather than a protected tag. So what is early is a coarse actual/not-actual marker, and the four-way provenance distinction MECH-545 names is either derived later or is several invariants wearing one name.

Confidence 0.6. Motivation, not support; the candidate's classification stays 'uncertain, architectural-leaning'.
