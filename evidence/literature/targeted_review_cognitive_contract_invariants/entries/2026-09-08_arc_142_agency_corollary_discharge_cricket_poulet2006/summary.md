# Poulet & Hedwig 2006 -- one neuron that knows the song is its own

A cricket sings at an intensity that would deafen its own auditory system if nothing intervened. Something does. Poulet and Hedwig found a single identified interneuron that fires in time with the song motor pattern and inhibits the auditory pathway at two stages, so the animal's own chirps are discounted while an external chirp is still heard. Drive the neuron without any sound and the inhibition still appears. The self-caused/not distinction is carried by one cell from the motor side to the sensory side.

This is what a contract relation looks like when it is implemented by a translation mechanism rather than read off content, which is the neuroscience criterion in GOV-CONTRACT-1. It is also the comparative criterion at its strongest: the same solution, a copy of the outgoing command routed to the receiving subsystem, appears in mormyrid electric fish for a sense humans do not have (Bell, Han, Sugawara & Grant 1997) and in the primate visuomotor system (Crapse & Sommer 2008). Three lineages, three unrelated modalities, three unrelated bodies. Shared embodiment does not explain that.

Two things keep the classification honest. What the cricket carries is the coarse primitive, self-caused or not; ARC-142's developmental clause says the derived structure (intention, control, responsibility) differentiates from it later, and no insect study speaks to that. And Reznik and colleagues (2014) show that in humans the agency signal is implemented per effector and per modality, and can enhance rather than suppress. The relation recurs; the implementation is local. That is consistent with ARC-142's own insistence that engines may encode an invariant differently as long as the relation is recoverable at the boundary, and it is also consistent with the embodied reading of the mechanism.
