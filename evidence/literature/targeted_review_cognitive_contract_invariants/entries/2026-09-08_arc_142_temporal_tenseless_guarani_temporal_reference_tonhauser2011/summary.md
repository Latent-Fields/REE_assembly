# Tonhauser 2011 -- what a language without tense still cannot avoid saying

The typology of tense is a caution against reading grammar as cognition. Dahl and Velupillai's WALS chapter finds that roughly forty percent of sampled languages have no grammatical past/non-past marking at all, and that tenseless languages cluster by area rather than by family. So the grammatical category is a convention that diffuses. Under GOV-CONTRACT-1 that is exactly the finding that should stop us treating tense as evidence for a contract invariant.

Tonhauser's study of Paraguayan Guarani is what makes the cell interesting anyway. Guarani has no tense morphology, yet its sentences are not temporally vague. Reference time is fixed compositionally: telic predicates default to past, statives to present, and adverbials and discourse anaphora do the rest. The relation is obligatorily recovered even when its dedicated channel is absent. That has the shape of a lesion result: delete the carrier and the relation survives by other routes.

For MECH-545 this cuts both ways, and I want both recorded. It supports the claim that the TEMPORAL RELATION is something a system will not do without. It also shows that a single-channel deletion can be translated around, which is the possibility GOV-CONTRACT-1 says must be preserved as a negative result whenever it appears. Whether an engine boundary in REE behaves like a Guarani hearer, reconstructing order from aspect and context, is the REE-internal question and not one literature can settle.

For MECH-546 it is direction (b) evidence: a recurrently grammaticalised distinction that classifies as cultural, sitting on top of a relation that does not.
