# Gori, Del Viva, Sandini & Burr 2008 -- precision as a weight arrives late

Adults combine what they see and what they feel by reliability: the more precise cue gets the larger say. Gori and colleagues showed that children do not do this until about eight. Below that age one modality simply dominates, touch for size and vision for orientation, regardless of which is more reliable on the trial. The machinery that weighs sources by their precision is a late, presumably calibration-dependent achievement.

I have recorded this as a weakening finding on purpose, because GOV-CONTRACT-1 says negative results must be kept as first-class and because the temptation with a five-by-five matrix of supports is to let a cell like this disappear into a footnote. It should not. The CONFIDENCE candidate in MECH-545 bundles two things: a confidence flag attached to a decision, which Goupil and Kouider show at twelve months, and a precision weight attached to a representation for integration, which this paper shows is absent until eight years. The developmental record splits them.

What follows for the contract is not that precision is cultural. It is that if precision is an invariant, it is one that differentiates, in the sense of ARC-142's developmental clause, from an early flag to a late weight. And a boundary lesion that deletes "confidence" without saying which of the two it deletes will be uninterpretable, because one of them is normally absent in a five-year-old who is otherwise a unified mind.

The caveat cuts the other way: the paper shows the weight is not used optimally, not that it is not carried. A preserved signal with uncalibrated receiving weights would look the same. That is the within-boundary control question again.
