# Dijkstra & Fleming 2023 -- reality as a threshold, not a label

The question is how a mind decides that what it is representing is actually out there. The tidy answer for a contract invariant would be a tag: observed, remembered, imagined, counterfactual, riding with the content wherever it goes. Dijkstra and Fleming's data argue for something less tidy. Reality judgements track whether a combined signal, perception plus imagery, crosses a subjective strength threshold. Make the imagery vivid enough and it is judged real.

I have recorded this as mixed rather than as weakening, and the reason matters. The paper does support the candidate in one way: there is a reality-status decision, it has its own failure mode, and that failure mode is the one MECH-545 predicts, commitment to an imagined state as real. Garrison and colleagues (2015) add that the substrate of this decision, the paracingulate sulcus, predicts hallucination regardless of the sensory modality involved, which is the subsystem-independence GOV-CONTRACT-1's neuroscience domain asks for.

What the paper contradicts is the separable-tag form. If provenance travelled as a protected label, vividness should not move the judgement. It does. So in humans the relation looks reconstructed at the point of decision from a graded variable that observed and imagined content share. Under GOV-CONTRACT-1 that is exactly the kind of finding we are told not to discard: an apparently fundamental invariant that may be inferred rather than preserved.

Whether an engine boundary in REE carries a tag or a signal strength is not something this paper can decide. It does tell us the within-boundary control in MECH-545 is not optional: the assay must distinguish a lost tag from a degraded signal, because the human brain seems to run on the second.

Confidence 0.65, direction mixed. No claim's status changes.
