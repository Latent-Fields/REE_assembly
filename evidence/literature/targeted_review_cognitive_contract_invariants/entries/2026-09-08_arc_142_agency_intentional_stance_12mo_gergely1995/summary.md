# Gergely, Nadasdy, Csibra & Biro 1995 -- agency without a body

The cleanest way to test whether infants attribute agency by reading bodies is to remove the body. A small circle jumps over a barrier to reach a large circle; the infant habituates. The barrier is removed. Twelve-month-olds now look longer when the circle repeats its jump, which has become pointless, than when it goes straight, which it never did before. They had encoded the jump as an efficient means to an end, not as a shape of motion. Nine-month-olds had not.

For the AGENCY candidate this is the datum that most directly separates the architectural reading from the embodied one. The embodied story says agency recurs because minds share bodies and action problems. Here the agent has no body, no face, no biological motion, and the attribution goes through anyway, on the basis of rational action-to-goal structure. GOV-CONTRACT-1 asks for developmental precedence and body-independence; this gives both at once.

It is the social half of the candidate, the who-is-the-actor half. The self-caused versus externally caused half rests elsewhere: infants bind their own actions to their effects by nine months (Verschoor, Weidema, Biro & Hommel 2010) and discriminate their own contingent mirror image from a mimicking other at four months (Rochat & Striano 2002). Together with the cricket corollary-discharge cell recorded separately, the agency candidate has the widest and most body-independent support of the five.

Confidence 0.72. Motivation for the candidate, not support for a claim.
