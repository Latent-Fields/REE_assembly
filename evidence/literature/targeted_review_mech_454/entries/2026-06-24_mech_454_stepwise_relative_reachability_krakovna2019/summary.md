# Reachability-preservation as an explicit cost (Krakovna, Orseau, Kumar, Martic & Legg 2019)

## What the paper did

This DeepMind safety paper asks a deceptively narrow question: how do you stop a reinforcement-learning agent from wrecking its environment as a side effect of pursuing its task, without giving it some new pathology in exchange? The authors decompose any "side-effect penalty" into two choices -- a *baseline* state to compare against, and a *deviation measure* from that baseline -- and show that the usual choices smuggle in bad incentives. Their fix is a deviation measure based on *relative reachability*: penalize an action to the extent that it reduces the set of states still reachable from the current state, measured against a stepwise inaction baseline. The penalty is added straight onto the agent's objective. Crucially, they engineer it specifically to avoid the perverse incentive -- which simpler penalties create -- to prevent *all* irreversible change, including other agents' actions.

## Why it is the strongest anchor in this pull

Of the five papers, this one maps most faithfully onto what MECH-454 actually proposes. MECH-454 does not want a new maximization drive; it wants a *cost term* sitting in E3 alongside harm, benefit, goal and residue, penalizing the foreclosure of the agent's own reachable-option space. Relative reachability is exactly that shape: a penalty for shrinking the reachable set, added to the objective. And it speaks INV-026's native language -- foreclosure of reachable state-space -- which matters, because MECH-454 is explicitly the SELF-directed, non-harm analog of INV-026's foreclosure-of-OTHERS. The paper's own anxiety about *not* penalizing other agents' state-reductions actually sharpens the self/other boundary that INV-026 and MECH-454 partition between them.

## The honest limit of the mapping

What this paper does not have is the uncertainty-gate, and that is precisely the half that makes MECH-454 more than a re-skin of relative reachability. Krakovna et al.'s penalty fires by the same rule whether the agent is confidently right or blindly guessing about what the irreversible action buys it. MECH-454 generalizes DR-12's prediction-error-conditioned E3 confidence: the foreclosure cost should *scale with forecast uncertainty* -- heavy when the justification is a guess, absent when it is confident. An un-gated reachability penalty is exactly the "deranged gating" failure mode (cost present but not uncertainty-scaled) the claim names. The paper is also honest that reachability penalties flirt with the over-preservation pole -- the incentive to freeze the world -- and their stepwise-relative design mitigates rather than eliminates it; a SELF-scoped E3 port would need its own guard against pathological option-hoarding. Finally, relative reachability assumes an enumerable state space with a clean baseline, which a latent-space E3 foreclosure cost does not have -- so this transfers as a *design pattern*, not a drop-in formula.
