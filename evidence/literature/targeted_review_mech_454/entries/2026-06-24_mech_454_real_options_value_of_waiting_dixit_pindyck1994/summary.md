# Real options: the value of waiting rises with uncertainty (Dixit & Pindyck 1994)

## What the paper did

*Investment under Uncertainty* is the book that made real-options thinking standard in economics. Its central observation is simple and sharp: when an investment is irreversible and its returns are uncertain, the *opportunity* to invest is itself an option, and that option has value. Making the irreversible expenditure "exercises" -- kills -- the option, forgoing the chance to wait for better information. So the true cost of an irreversible commitment is its direct cost *plus* the option value you destroy by committing now. And because option value rises with uncertainty, the optimal rule defers irreversible commitment more, the greater the irreversibility and the greater the uncertainty.

## Why it is relevant to MECH-454

This is the gating logic of MECH-454, stated in decision theory rather than environmental economics. MECH-454 wants E3 to charge a cost for foreclosing the SELF's reachable options, *scaled by forecast uncertainty* (generalizing DR-12's prediction-error-conditioned E3 confidence). Dixit & Pindyck's "exercising the option kills it, and the lost option value is an opportunity cost" is, almost word for word, the foreclosure cost MECH-454 wants represented; their "and that cost rises with uncertainty" is the gate. The reason I pull this *in addition to* Arrow & Fisher is robustness: the gating principle is not a quirk of the wilderness-preservation case -- it recurs, fully developed, in the canonical treatment of capital-investment timing. That a self-foreclosure-under-uncertainty premium shows up independently in two separate economic literatures is a reason to trust the gate as a general decision principle worth importing into E3.

## The honest limit of the mapping

This entry deliberately anchors the *same half* of MECH-454 as the Arrow & Fisher entry -- the uncertainty-gate -- and I weight it below that entry precisely so two economics sources do not silently inflate one component into apparent independent strength. The same transfer caveats apply, plus one the firm-investment framing makes vivid: the value of waiting presupposes that deferral is both *feasible* and *informative*. Where the agent cannot wait, or no information will arrive before it must act, the option-value-of-waiting argument does not license preserving options at all -- and a naive E3 cost built on it would over-defer into chronic indecision. The formalism also assumes a known stochastic process with quantified volatility; it tells you what to do *given* calibrated uncertainty and is silent on how an E3 selector should *estimate* its own forecast confidence -- which is exactly where MECH-454's surfacing failure (collapsing an unknown future into an over-confident point) lives. There is also a surface-overlap trap worth naming: the book literally frames the lost option value as an "opportunity cost," which sounds like ARC-068 -- but ARC-068 is opportunity cost of *time*, whereas MECH-454's cost is over reachable-option *space*. Same word, different quantity.
