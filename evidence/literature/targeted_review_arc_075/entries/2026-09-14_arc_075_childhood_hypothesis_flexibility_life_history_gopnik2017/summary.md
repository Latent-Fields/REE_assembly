# Gopnik et al. (2017) -- flexibility and hypothesis search across human life history

**What they did.** Gopnik and colleagues argue that a long childhood buys a period of wide hypothesis search before the narrower, exploitative search of adulthood. They tested it directly: participants from preschool to adulthood saw evidence for an unusual causal relation, physical or social, and the question was who would adopt it. In the physical domain preschoolers did best, then school-age children, then adolescents and adults, who preferred a familiar hypothesis even when it fit the evidence worse. In the social domain, preschoolers *and* adolescents were the most flexible.

**Why it bears on ARC-075.** ARC-075 is about asymmetry: the pathway that encodes what already works (F) should carry less weight early, so alternatives have a chance to become competitive. Gopnik's data are the behavioural fingerprint of exactly that asymmetry in humans -- the familiar hypothesis is weak early and strong later. It is a nice complement to the machine-learning entries in this directory, which show that *without* such an asymmetry one pathway captures the objective by default.

**The confound I can't get past.** Children may be flexible simply because they have weaker priors -- they have seen less -- not because anything is actively turning down how much their priors count. That is precisely the distinction ARC-075 depends on: "temporal scheduling (and the experience that comes with it) produces the asymmetry for free" versus "the asymmetry must be imposed as a magnitude gate". A cross-sectional behavioural design can't separate those. So this supports the *premise* that developmental phases differ in how strongly the favoured hypothesis dominates, and is silent on the mechanism.

**A wrinkle worth keeping.** Adolescents re-open in the social domain. One early window, then lock, is too simple; flexibility looks staggered and domain-specific, which sits better with MECH-335's staggered loop windows than with a single infant critical period.

**Confidence.** 0.52: supportive, descriptive, mechanism-ambiguous.
