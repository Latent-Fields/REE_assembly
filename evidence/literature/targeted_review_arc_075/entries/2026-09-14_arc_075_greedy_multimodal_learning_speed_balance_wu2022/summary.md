# Wu, Jastrzebski, Cho & Geras (2022) -- the greedy nature of multi-modal learning

**What they did.** Wu and colleagues asked a simple question about networks that receive two or more input streams: does the trained model actually use all of them? They defined a *conditional utilization rate* -- how much accuracy a modality adds given the others -- and found, across tasks and architectures, that jointly trained networks lean heavily on one modality and under-fit the rest, and that this hurts generalization. Because the utilization rate is too expensive to track during training, they introduced a proxy, *conditional learning speed*, and an algorithm that balances it between modalities. Balancing improved generalization on three datasets.

**Why it matters for ARC-075.** ARC-075 says that an infant curriculum which only schedules *when* things happen will still converge to monostrategy, because the scorer that learns fastest (F) captures E3 before the diversity circuits have competitive weight; it prescribes suppressing F's effective update magnitude during the open window. Wu et al. is a clean demonstration of the dynamic in a different substrate: shared objective, uniform update rules, one pathway wins by default. And what fixes it is not a change in data order but an asymmetric modulation of how fast each pathway learns. That is a magnitude lever, which is the thing ARC-075 claims is missing.

**Where the mapping strains.** This is supervised fusion of parallel input streams, not an agent choosing actions with a scorer and a set of diversity heads. There are no developmental phases, no release of the balancing, and no test of whether balance survives release -- so the paper speaks to the *open-window* half of ARC-075 (MECH-333) and says nothing about closure or crystallization. Nor do they run a curriculum baseline, so "scheduling alone is insufficient" is carried by the mechanism rather than by a head-to-head contrast. One uncomfortable practical point transfers well: the quantity you want to equalise can't be measured online, so you steer by a proxy. REE will have the same problem deciding when diversity circuits have "reached competitive weight".

**Confidence.** 0.62. Good venue and consistent effect across architectures; mechanistically close to MECH-333; developmentally silent. I would count this as solid support that update-magnitude asymmetry is the right *kind* of intervention, and as no support at all for the closure trigger.
