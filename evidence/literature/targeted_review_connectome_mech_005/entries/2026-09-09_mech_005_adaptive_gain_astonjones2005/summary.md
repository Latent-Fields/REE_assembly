# Adaptive gain: the strongest support for path authority, arriving with a prohibition attached

This is the paper MECH-005's first pillar most wants to lean on, and reading it carefully is
uncomfortable in a way I think is productive. Aston-Jones and Cohen's synthesis established the
phasic/tonic dissociation that has organised LC research ever since. Phasic LC activation, driven
by the outcome of task-related decision processes, facilitates the behaviour that follows and
optimises performance on the task at hand -- exploitation. Tonic activation accompanies
disengagement from the current task and a search for alternatives -- exploration. The evidence is
two decades of monkey and rat single-unit recording, supplemented by human pupillometry and by
computational work on gain modulation.

Map that onto MECH-005 and the correspondence is close. REE says high nu makes recent path endings
dominate, makes deviation costly, makes commitment sticky; low nu lets alternative paths gain
voice and tolerates branching. That is the phasic and tonic modes, described in a different
vocabulary. More importantly, adaptive gain theory demonstrates the structural point the claim
actually needs: a single neuromodulatory axis can set how tightly a system is bound to its current
trajectory without itself generating the trajectories. Gain modulation changes the influence of
representations, not which representations exist -- which is precisely REE's insistence that nu
does not select tau bands, that gamma, beta, theta and delta all keep running and nu only weights
their say in what gets committed.

The trouble is where the LC's mode comes from. In Aston-Jones and Cohen the answer is frontal:
anterior cingulate and orbitofrontal cortex monitor task utility and regulate LC mode so as to
optimise utility over short and long timescales. MECH-005 prohibits nu from determining value or
reward, and locates viability in coherence -- A(P_i) is explicitly coherence-based, not
reward-based. So the strongest empirical support for path authority arrives welded to a
value-driven control loop that the claim disavows. Either REE needs a non-reward regulator that
plays the ACC/OFC role -- some coherence-monitoring structure that sets nu the way utility
monitoring sets LC mode -- or the prohibition needs revisiting. I do not think this can be waved
through. It is the kind of tension that should be recorded now and adjudicated when MECH-005 next
comes up for status review, rather than discovered later by an implementation that quietly
smuggled a utility term into g(nu, context).

There is also a trap for whoever operationalises nu experimentally, and it is a sign error. The
correspondence between nu and noradrenaline is not monotone in level. High nu in REE means sticky
commitment, which is the PHASIC mode; high tonic noradrenergic tone produces more exploration, not
less. Anyone who implements nu as "amount of NE" and predicts stickier commitment at higher values
will get the opposite of what this literature reports. Related: every task in the reviewed corpus
has explicit reward structure, so this evidence cannot distinguish commitment pressure driven by
coherence from commitment pressure driven by utility. REE's claim is about the former and the
literature has never observed it in isolation.

Confidence 0.71, with source quality at 0.92 and mapping fidelity dragged down to 0.65. The
docking is deliberate and is not a comment on the paper's quality, which is exceptional. It
records that the best available support for pillar one comes packaged with a violation of one of
the claim's own stated prohibitions, and that a claim cannot bank the support while declining the
package.
