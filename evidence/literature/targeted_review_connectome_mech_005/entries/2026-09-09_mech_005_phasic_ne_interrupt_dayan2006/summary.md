# The interrupt, made formal -- and the boundary it blurs

If Bouret and Sara supply the biological intuition for MECH-005's interruptibility pillar, Dayan
and Yu supply the equation. Their proposal is that what activates the noradrenergic interrupt is
not novelty as such but unexpected change in the world evaluated within the context of the current
task. They formalise this in a Bayesian model of a well-studied visual discrimination task and
show it reproduces a rich repertoire of LC response profiles at sub-second resolution -- including
the awkward observations that had resisted simpler accounts, where LC activity is modulated not
only by sensory input and by the animal's level of engagement but by temporally extended internal
decision processes.

That last point is the one I would keep. It means the noradrenergic signal is not a perceptual
variable. It is sensitive to where the system is in its own deliberation, which is exactly what
MECH-005 requires of nu: a control-plane quantity that decides how hard the system must care right
now, rather than a report about the stimulus. The context-relativity is the second thing worth
keeping. REE does not want a startle detector; it wants escalation gated by relevance to the path
currently being committed to. Dayan and Yu's interrupt is defined against the current task, which
is the right shape.

And now the difficulty, which is substantial enough that I have filed this as supporting evidence
while flagging it as a challenge to the claim's own foundations. The currency of this model is
Bayesian uncertainty. The interrupt fires when the posterior over task state shifts unexpectedly.
MECH-005 states flatly that nu does not update precision registers, and REE's whole design
rationale rests on fragmenting precision -- dopamine-like, tau-scoped -- from path authority --
norepinephrine-like, urgency-scoped. If the interrupt is only definable as a function of posterior
uncertainty, that fragmentation looks less like a discovery about control architecture and more
like a redescription of one quantity in two vocabularies. I do not think this paper settles the
question against REE. I do think REE cannot cite it as clean support for the interrupt function
while ignoring what the interrupt is made of. The honest position is that the mapping holds at the
functional level -- when does the system reorient -- and strains at the representational level --
what quantity nu actually carries.

A narrower gap: nothing here touches MECH-005's third pillar. The claim privileges post-commitment
error, on the argument that errors after action are uniquely informative about model adequacy and
action viability in a way that errors before action are not. Dayan and Yu's interrupt is triggered
by unexpected change in the world and makes no distinction between surprise before and after the
system has committed. That asymmetry is REE's own, and this literature neither supports nor
weakens it.

Confidence 0.74. Mapping fidelity is the highest in this pull -- "a neural interrupt signal" is
close to a restatement of the claim -- and transfer risk is low, because the target is already a
computational abstraction and so is REE. What holds the number below 0.8 is source quality: a
modelling paper in a specialist venue, validating against previously published response
repertoires rather than generating new causal evidence, and carrying a formalism that quietly
contests one of the claim's prohibitions.
