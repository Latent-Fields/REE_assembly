# Median-raphe 5-HT tunes hippocampal representational stability (Wu et al. 2021, Nature)

According to PubMed, Wu and colleagues ([10.1038/s41586-021-03956-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03956-8)) dissected how serotonin shapes hippocampal social memory. They identified the medial septum (MS) as a critical dorsal-CA2 input, showed that novel social interaction increases activity in dCA2-projecting MS neurons and induces glutamatergic plasticity at MS→dCA2 synapses, and — the part that matters here — demonstrated that median-raphe serotonergic input *bidirectionally* controls social-memory stability. 5-HT enhances the relevant MS cells' activity, and optogenetic manipulation of median-raphe 5-HT terminals in MS pushes memory stability up or down. In other words, a median-raphe serotonergic signal sets *how firmly a hippocampal representation holds*.

I include this as principle-level support for MECH-085. The receptor layer's terrain account is, at bottom, a claim that median-raphe serotonin governs the depth and stability of hippocampal attractor basins — how strongly a representation, once entered, captures the rollout. Wu et al. give a clean, causal, mechanistically-resolved example of exactly that *type* of computation: median-raphe 5-HT acting on a hippocampal circuit to dial a representational-stability parameter. That the lever is bidirectional is itself congenial to a basin-depth framing.

But I have set confidence deliberately low (0.55, mapping_fidelity 0.40) because the specifics diverge from the layer's claim in two ways that an honest governance record must foreground. First, the receptor implicated is **5-HT1B on medial-septum neurons**, not 5-HT1A on CA1/DG. Second, the modulated quantity is **social-memory stability via the MS→dCA2 circuit**, not aversive-basin depth in CA1/DG. So this paper supports the *generic* principle "median raphe 5-HT tunes hippocampal representational stability" rather than the layer's *specific* "5-HT1A sets aversive-terrain basin depth." Read uncharitably, it is even a mild competing account — it shows a different serotonin receptor doing terrain-like work in a different hippocampal subcircuit, a reminder that the hippocampal serotonergic story is multi-receptor and the layer's single-receptor assignment is a simplification.

That tension is precisely why it earns a place: strand 1's job is biology-first grounding, and good grounding includes the evidence that complicates the tidy mapping. Tagged to MECH-085 only, as principle-level support with an explicit failure signature on the 5-HT1B-not-5-HT1A divergence.
