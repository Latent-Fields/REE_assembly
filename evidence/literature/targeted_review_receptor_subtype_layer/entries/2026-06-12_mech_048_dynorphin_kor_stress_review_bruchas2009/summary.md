# Dynorphin/KOR as the aversive-state controller (Bruchas, Land & Chavkin 2009, Brain Res)

According to PubMed, Bruchas, Land and Chavkin ([10.1016/j.brainres.2009.08.062](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brainres.2009.08.062)) review the dynorphin/kappa-opioid system as a modulator of stress-induced and pro-addictive behaviour. The consolidated picture: activation of the dynorphin/KOR system modulates mood, induces dysphoria, increases the rewarding impact of abused drugs, and drives both drug-seeking and stress-induced reinstatement — all in tight interaction with corticotropin-releasing factor. They argue that KOR-system activation is a major route by which stress becomes pro-addictive, and they flag KOR antagonists as promising therapeutics for affective dysregulation.

This review is the synthesis complement to the Land et al. (2008) primary study in strand 3. Its value to the receptor layer is that it consolidates, across many experiments, the premise the commitment-entropy axis depends on: that the brain has a *dedicated* neuropeptide system whose function is to install and modulate negative affective state, downstream of the CRF stress controller. Section 3C's kappa pole — the destabiliser that "carries dysphoric/aversive state" and opposes the mu stabiliser of MECH-048 — needs exactly this: not a one-off finding but a coherent body of work treating dynorphin/KOR as an aversive-state machine. The therapeutic framing (KOR antagonism for affective dysregulation) is also the conceptual ancestor of PHARM-018's prediction that kappa antagonism normalises pathological aversive-state-lock.

The caveats track those of the Land entry and keep confidence at 0.66. This is a review, not primary data, and it shares authorship and substance with Land et al. (2008), so it is a *synthesis/therapeutic-framing* complement rather than independent corroboration — I have weighted it accordingly rather than double-counting. More importantly, the review is organised around dysphoria and addiction/relapse, not around commitment or option-selection; it gives no independent support for the specifically *computational* half of the layer's claim — that kappa "raises policy entropy" and destabilises commitments — beyond the general affective-state framing. That commitment-specific reading is REE's, and is grounded directly only by the Bari et al. (2025) computational entry.

I record this as *supports* for the aversive-state-controller substrate (MECH-048 kappa pole) and the vulnerability-axis premise (ARC-086), at confidence 0.66 with mapping_fidelity 0.60 and an explicit failure signature noting the dysphoria/addiction (not commitment) framing.
