# M4 dampens striatal dopamine without touching D2 (Foster et al. 2016, Neuron)

According to PubMed, Foster and colleagues ([10.1016/j.neuron.2016.08.017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.08.017)) asked how muscarinic modulators achieve antipsychotic effects, and gave an unusually clean mechanistic answer. Using fast-scan cyclic voltammetry to measure dopamine release in striatal slices, M4 and CB2 knockouts to isolate the pathway, and antipsychotic-predictive behaviour, they showed that activating M4 receptors on striatal spiny projection neurons produces a *sustained* depression of striatal dopamine release — lasting more than 30 minutes after the agonist is removed — and that both this dopamine-release inhibition and the antipsychotic-like behaviour require intact CB2 cannabinoid signalling. The mechanism is a retrograde endocannabinoid relay: M4 on the spiny neurons drives CB2-dependent suppression of dopamine output.

This is the mechanistic heart of strand 2 and the load-bearing biology under PHARM-017. The receptor-subtype layer's Section 3B claims that xanomeline is antipsychotic *from the muscarinic side* — that its M4 arm dampens the MECH-086 dopamine trajectory-selection plane without any D2 blockade, attacking the "selection" arm of MECH-088 psychosis (dopamine stamping salience onto noise) by a non-dopaminergic route. The strong version of that claim needs a demonstration that M4 activation actually reduces striatal dopaminergic drive and actually yields antipsychotic-predictive behaviour, by a pathway separate from postsynaptic D2 antagonism. Foster et al. is precisely that demonstration. It also concretely instantiates the cross-plane (MECH-087) logic: an action originating on the acetylcholine/muscarinic side reaches down into the dopamine-selection plane.

The caveats are real and I have left confidence at 0.75 rather than higher. First, "striatal dopamine release" is a *presynaptic dopamine-availability* measure; MECH-086's "trajectory-selection gain" is a computational construct about how dopamine weights option/action selection, which this paper does not measure. Reduced release is consistent with reduced selection-gain but is not the same quantity. Second, the M4→dopamine effect is *not direct*: it obligately runs through CB2/endocannabinoid signalling, an intermediary the REE account does not represent at all. Any REE model that treats M4 as a direct dopamine-selection damper is omitting a required relay — and that relay is a concrete place the mechanism could fail to translate to humans.

I record this as *supports* with good mapping_fidelity (0.70), tagged to MECH-086 (the dopamine-selection plane it directly grounds) and MECH-087 (the cross-plane interaction it instantiates). It pairs with the EMERGENT-3 clinical entry (Kaul et al. 2024) and the McKinzie & Bymaster mechanism review to make the M1/M4 interaction-effect case. PHARM-017 stays out_of_domain for the V3 substrate, which has no muscarinic subtypes; this grounds the design and promotes nothing.
