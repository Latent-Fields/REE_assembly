# Kappa antagonism restores optimal perseveration — the commitment-entropy anchor (Bari et al. 2025)

According to PubMed, Bari, Krystal, Pizzagalli and Gershman ([10.1016/j.bpsc.2025.05.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bpsc.2025.05.011)) bring computational psychiatry to bear on anhedonia and its treatment by kappa-opioid antagonism. They apply a theory of optimal decision-making under memory constraints that decomposes behaviour into a *memory* component and an *efficiency* component, fit a resource-bounded reinforcement-learning model to Probabilistic Reward Task data, and analyse the secondary data from the KOR-antagonist RCT (n=24 KOR, n=31 placebo) alongside three other datasets. Two results are striking. First, across clinical and non-clinical populations, anhedonia was associated with deficits in *efficiency*, not memory — and those efficiency deficits arose specifically from an *inability to perseverate optimally* for a given memory capacity. Second, KOR antagonism increased both memory and efficiency, which the model attributes to increased reward sensitivity *and* increased perseveration.

This is, of the eleven entries in this pull, the most architecturally on-point for the layer's boldest move — and it is why strand 3 can support a *proposal*, not just a vocabulary. The receptor layer reads mu- and kappa-opioid as opposing poles of a commitment-entropy axis: mu stabilises commitments and reduces the urge to switch (MECH-048), kappa raises policy entropy and destabilises them. The conceptual worry has always been that "commitment entropy" is REE jargon with no empirical handle. Bari et al. give it one. In their resource-rational model, *perseveration* is precisely commitment-to-an-action / resistance-to-switching, and "deviation from optimal perseveration" is exactly the policy-instability the layer attributes to pathological kappa drive. Their finding that KOR antagonism *restores optimal perseveration* is, translated, "normalising kappa-driven commitment-destabilisation" — the ARC-086 / PHARM-018 prediction stated in the language of computational psychiatry by an independent group with no knowledge of REE.

I have nonetheless held confidence at 0.66, because the convergence is suggestive rather than decisive and the caveats are the kind that matter for a *proposal*. "Perseveration" is an operational RL proxy, not identical to REE "commitment entropy." Crucially, the optimum is *bounded by memory capacity* — more perseveration is therapeutic only up to the resource-rational optimum, an optimality constraint the REE "kappa antagonism stabilises commitment" framing omits and would do well to absorb. And the paper is explicit that KOR antagonism has *distinct* cognitive effects, only one of which relates to anhedonia — so attributing the whole drug effect to a single commitment-entropy axis over-simplifies a multi-component mechanism. It is also a secondary analysis with modest KOR n. I have logged both the multi-component and the optimality constraints as failure signatures, because they are exactly the refinements the proposed axis should be stress-tested against before any claims.yaml registration.

I record this as *supports* with the highest mapping_fidelity in strand 3 for the commitment-entropy construct specifically (0.70), tagged to ARC-086 (the proposed kappa axis) and MECH-048 (the commitment-stability mechanism it operationalises). Together with Land (substrate), Bruchas (synthesis) and Krystal (human target engagement), it makes strand 3 strong enough to justify *proposing* the kappa commitment-entropy axis to the user — while leaving the actual registration, and the optimality/multi-component refinements, to the proposal step.
