# The reciprocal M4-dopamine relationship and the M1/M4 two-plane rationale (McKinzie & Bymaster 2012)

According to PubMed, McKinzie and Bymaster ([10.1007/978-3-642-25758-2_9](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-25758-2_9)) — investigators close to the original xanomeline programme — review the muscarinic cholinergic hypothesis of psychosis. The pieces relevant to strand 2: postmortem and in-vivo imaging show decreased M1 and M4 receptors in schizophrenia-associated regions; M4-knockout studies reveal a *reciprocal relationship between M4 and dopamine-receptor function*; muscarinic agonists display antidopaminergic activity in assays predictive of antipsychotic efficacy; and the M1/M4-preferring partial agonist xanomeline shows both antipsychotic-like and pro-cognitive activity preclinically, and reduces psychotic symptoms clinically. Their synthesis: an agonist engaging *both* M1 and M4 should address core symptom clusters that D2 antagonism leaves untouched.

This review is the connective tissue of strand 2 because it states, in the pharmacology's own terms, the two-plane interaction the receptor layer asserts. Section 3B partitions xanomeline's action across two MECH-087 planes: M4 dampens the MECH-086 dopamine trajectory-selection gain (the antidopaminergic, antipsychotic arm), while M1 acts on the cortical plasticity/encoding plane (the pro-cognitive arm). McKinzie & Bymaster ground both halves — the reciprocal M4↔dopamine relationship is exactly the dopamine-selection arm, and the M1/M4 pro-cognitive rationale is the second plane — and they connect the antidopaminergic-without-D2 mechanism to psychosis (MECH-088). Where Foster et al. (2016) give the cellular mechanism and Kaul et al. (2024) give the clinical endpoint, this review supplies the integrative argument that the two receptor actions sit on functionally distinct axes.

The caveats are the standard ones for a mechanistic review and hold confidence at 0.64. The evidence is a synthesis of preclinical-model, knockout, and postmortem data rather than a single isolated dissociation; "antidopaminergic activity in predictive assays" is a behavioural proxy, not a direct measurement of REE's trajectory-selection gain; and the M1-vs-M4 functional split is presented as a well-argued *rationale*, not a fully causally-isolated result (which is why PHARM-017 still carries "M1-selective vs M4-selective dissection" as a needed anchor). There is one genuinely useful complication the review flags: schizophrenia is a syndrome, and muscarinic agonists — like D2 antagonists before them — will not help everyone, with non-response possibly tracking cortical M1 deficits. That predicts response heterogeneity which the REE single-mechanism plane story, taken literally, does not capture; I have logged it as a failure signature.
