# Kappa antagonism re-engages human reward circuitry in anhedonia (Krystal et al. 2020, Nat Med)

According to PubMed, Krystal and colleagues ([10.1038/s41591-020-0806-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-020-0806-7)) report the first comprehensive application of the NIMH "fast-fail" approach: a multicentre, double-blind, placebo-controlled randomised trial of the selective kappa-opioid receptor antagonist JNJ-67953964 (10 mg; n=45) versus placebo (n=44) in patients with anhedonia and a mood or anxiety disorder. The pre-registered primary endpoint was a circuit biomarker — ventral-striatum activation during reward anticipation on fMRI. The KOR antagonist significantly increased that activation relative to placebo (effect size 0.58), and was well tolerated with no serious adverse events. The design's logic is proof-of-*mechanism*: does blocking KOR favourably move the brain circuitry hypothesised to mediate anhedonia? It does.

This is the human anchor on which the layer's most consequential proposal rests. Strand 3 culminates in a *proposed* new vulnerability axis — a kappa-driven commitment-entropy axis (a candidate amendment to ARC-086), distinct from the serotonin-terrain and dopamine-selection axes already in the failure-axes taxonomy. PHARM-018 makes that proposal falsifiable, predicting that kappa antagonism normalises pathological aversive-state-lock / anhedonia via the kappa-antagonist arm. Krystal et al. supply the target-engagement half of the case in humans: pharmacologically removing kappa tone demonstrably re-engages the reward-anticipation circuitry whose hypofunction REE reads as the "aversive attractor dominant, nothing holds" state. Where Land (2008) and Bruchas (2009) ground the rodent substrate, this is the human demonstration that the lever moves the predicted circuit.

The caveat is exactly the one the fast-fail design is honest about, and it keeps confidence at 0.74. The endpoint is a reward-anticipation *biomarker*, not clinical anhedonia remission, and certainly not any commitment or perseveration measure. A positive biomarker establishes that kappa antagonism engages reward circuitry; it does **not** establish that the kappa axis is *dissociable* from the serotonin axis on clinical endpoints, nor does it validate the "commitment-entropy" construct as such. The PHARM-018 falsification condition — that kappa antagonism produces the same response profile as an SSRI on anhedonia-specific endpoints, which would collapse the axis — therefore remains genuinely open; this trial is consistent with the axis but does not close the test. (The companion review on aticaprant, Demyttenaere 2024, [10.1007/s00406-024-01851-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00406-024-01851-7), notes the self-reported anhedonia changes in this programme were "rather inconsistent," which is the live tension.)

I record this as *supports* for the kappa-axis proposal (ARC-086) and the kappa pole of the opioid commitment axis (MECH-048), at confidence 0.74 with transfer risk low (human anhedonia) and mapping_fidelity the limiter (biomarker, not clinical or commitment endpoint).
