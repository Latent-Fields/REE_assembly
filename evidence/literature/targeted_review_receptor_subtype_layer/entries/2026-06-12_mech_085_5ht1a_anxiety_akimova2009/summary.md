# 5-HT1A and the aversive/anxiety arm of the serotonin split (Akimova et al. 2009, Biol Psychiatry)

According to PubMed, Akimova, Lanzenberger and Kasper ([10.1016/j.biopsych.2009.03.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2009.03.012)) review the case that the inhibitory 5-HT1A receptor is central to the neural processing of anxiety. They draw three converging lines: preclinical 5-HT1A knockout and transgenic mice show robust anxiety-like behavioural, autonomic and endocrine phenotypes; human genetic polymorphisms in the 5-HT1A system associate with higher anxiety scores; and PET imaging with [carbonyl-11C]WAY-100635 shows reduced 5-HT1A binding in panic disorder and social anxiety disorder, with lower binding tracking higher trait anxiety even in healthy subjects.

This is the functional counterpart to the Weber & Andrade localisation in strand 1. The receptor-subtype layer's Section 3A assigns MECH-085's hippocampal-terrain account — aversive attractor basin depth, fear-consistent rollout capture — to 5-HT1A on median-raphe-driven hippocampal cells, deliberately distinct from the 5-HT2A cortical/narrative arm carried by MECH-006. Akimova et al. supply what anatomy alone cannot: a causal and clinical tie between 5-HT1A tone and aversive/anxiety state. That is the functional grounding for treating 5-HT1A as the "terrain" lever and for PHARM-016's prediction that 5-HT1A partial agonism (buspirone) should flatten aversive basins — i.e. act as an anxiolytic / terrain-component augmentation distinct from the 5-HT2A narrative axis.

Two caveats are load-bearing and I have let them pull confidence down to 0.64. First, the review supports "5-HT1A modulates aversive/anxiety state," not the *specific* REE construct of hippocampal-CA1/DG basin depth; the human binding signal localises to mesiotemporal cortex and raphe broadly, so the layer's tidy CA1/DG attribution outruns the imaging resolution. Second — and this is a genuine partial counter, not a quibble — the PET pattern shows reduced 5-HT1A binding in panic and social anxiety but explicitly **not** in PTSD. The receptor layer attributes a "MECH-088 PTSD terrain component" to 5-HT1A, and the human data sit awkwardly with that sub-claim. There is also the perennial 5-HT1A complication that pre-synaptic raphe autoreceptors and post-synaptic limbic receptors have opposite functional signs, which a single "5-HT1A = terrain" label papers over.

I record this as *supports* for the broad 5-HT1A-aversive-state assignment (MECH-085) with mapping_fidelity capped at 0.60 and an explicit failure signature on the PTSD-null result. Tagged to MECH-085 only — it speaks to the terrain/aversive arm and is silent on the cortical/narrative arm. Together with Ren (anatomy) and Weber & Andrade (5-HT2A localisation), it completes the receptor-resolved reconciliation the layer proposes, while PHARM-016 stays out_of_domain for the V3 substrate. The companion review by Smith, Harmer, Cowen & Murphy (2023, CNS Drugs, [10.1007/s40263-023-01014-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40263-023-01014-7)) — pre- vs post-synaptic 5-HT1A and the raphe-autoreceptor basis of SSRI onset delay — is the natural next anchor for the audit-link 6.4 / PHARM-016 onset-delay falsification, and is noted here rather than entered separately to keep the strand tight.
