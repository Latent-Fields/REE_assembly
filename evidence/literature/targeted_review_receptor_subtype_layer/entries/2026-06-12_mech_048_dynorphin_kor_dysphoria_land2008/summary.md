# Dynorphin/KOR encodes the dysphoric component of stress (Land et al. 2008, J Neurosci)

According to PubMed, Land and colleagues ([10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4458-07.2008](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4458-07.2008)) asked what, mechanistically, makes stress feel *bad* — and isolated a dedicated answer. Across repeated forced-swim and inescapable-footshock paradigms, the aversive behavioural consequences (conditioned place aversion, immobility) were blocked by a kappa-opioid receptor antagonist and were absent in dynorphin-knockout mice. Corticotropin-releasing factor and urocortin III, the key stress mediators, produced place aversion that was likewise abolished by dynorphin deletion or KOR antagonism; this CRF-induced aversion was mediated by CRF2 (not CRF1) receptors driving dynorphin release. Using a phospho-selective antibody against the activated receptor, they mapped dynorphin-dependent KOR activation to the basolateral amygdala, nucleus accumbens, dorsal raphe and hippocampus. The interpretation: a CRF2 → dynorphin → KOR cascade is the substrate that encodes the dysphoric *valence* of stress.

This is the foundational primary anchor for strand 3 and for the new vulnerability-axis the layer proposes. Section 3C reads mu- and kappa-opioid as opposing poles on a commitment-entropy axis: mu stabilises selected commitments (the MECH-048 account, "reduced urge to switch"), while the kappa/dynorphin system destabilises and carries aversive state. REE reads anhedonic, dysphoric, aversive-state-locked depression as *pathological kappa-driven destabilisation* — the condition in which "nothing holds and the aversive attractor wins by default." Land et al. give that reading its biological substrate: dynorphin/KOR is a dedicated, genetically-dissectible system whose job is to install aversive state, and it acts in precisely the limbic structures (amygdala, accumbens, hippocampus, dorsal raphe) that the terrain and harm planes (MECH-088) implicate. The CRF2 gating is a bonus — it places the kappa system downstream of the canonical stress controller, consistent with a stress-vulnerability axis.

The honest limit, which holds confidence at 0.72, is that this is a study of aversive *valence encoding*, not of commitment dynamics. Nothing here measures policy-competition entropy or shows that kappa activation destabilises selected commitments; the "commitment-entropy destabiliser" framing is REE's overlay on an aversive-valence finding, and I have logged that as the explicit failure signature. The assays are rodent place-aversion and immobility, and the mu-opioid stabiliser pole (the other half of MECH-048) is not examined in this paper at all. The commitment-specific reading is grounded much more directly by the Bari et al. (2025) computational entry; Land et al.'s contribution is the valence substrate.

I record this as *supports*, tagged to MECH-048 (the commitment/opioid axis it grounds the aversive pole of), MECH-088 (the limbic/terrain localisation of stress-driven KOR activation), and ARC-086 (it supports a kappa-dysphoria vulnerability axis — the proposed new axis discussed in the strand synthesis).
