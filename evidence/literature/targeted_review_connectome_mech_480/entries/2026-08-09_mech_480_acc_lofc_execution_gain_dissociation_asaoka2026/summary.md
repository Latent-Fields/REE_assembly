# Summary: Asaoka, Pagano & Hayashi (2026) — Dissociable roles of prefrontal plasticity in decision making strategy and execution of habitual behavior

## What the paper did

There's a puzzle sitting at the centre of habit formation that this paper takes seriously rather than explaining away: when a behaviour shifts from goal-directed to habitual, the animal's motivation to perform it visibly declines — and yet the behaviour keeps happening at roughly the same frequency and duration. If motivation is falling, why doesn't execution fall with it? Asaoka, Pagano and Hayashi built a two-stage training paradigm in male mice — an initial period of goal-directed learning followed by a short, accelerated window designed to force the transition to habitual responding — that lets them track this shift within the same animal rather than across separately-trained cohorts. Then they went looking, circuit by circuit, for what actually controls each half of the puzzle: the strategy shift itself, and the execution level that persists through it.

## Key findings

The answer they found is a clean double dissociation, and it's the kind of result that earns its confidence from the manipulation, not just the correlation. Plasticity in a projection from anterior cingulate cortex to retrosplenial cortex weakens as the animal transitions into habitual responding — and when the authors selectively erased that plasticity, they altered whether the behaviour became habitual at all, while execution level stayed put. Plasticity in a separate projection, from lateral orbitofrontal cortex to central striatum, does the opposite job: erasing it changed how vigorously the habit was executed — frequency, duration — while leaving the decision-strategy transition untouched. Two pathways, two independently-erasable jobs, no crosstalk in either direction. That's the paper's core claim, and it's the kind of finding you only get to make once you've shown that removing one doesn't touch the other.

## REE translation

MECH-480 was registered on the strength of a half-remembered citation — the raw thought that seeded it named "a recent Nature Communications paper" without pinning it down, and the claim carried that gap forward as a self-declared TODO. The claim itself proposes that REE's dACC-analog module (SD-032b) computes something you might call *strategy authority* — whether accumulated action-outcome evidence still gets to steer the policy — as a channel distinct from a proposed LOFC-analog *execution-gain* channel, controlling how strongly a persisting policy is expressed once it's no longer being steered. Put those two sentences next to the paper's finding and the match is hard to miss: ACC-to-RSC plasticity gates *whether* the behaviour becomes habit; LOFC-to-striatum plasticity gates *how strongly* it's executed once it has. That is close to a direct biological instance of the whether/how-strongly split MECH-480 is betting REE's architecture needs.

But "close" is doing real work in that sentence, and it's worth being precise about where the translation strains rather than folding the strain into the confidence number and moving on. The paper's ACC involvement is a specific efferent — the projection to retrosplenial cortex — not ACC activity in general, whereas SD-032b isn't currently scoped to any particular downstream target; the paper constrains a narrower biological claim than what SD-032b computes. The paper's "strategy shift" unfolds over a multi-day training window and is a categorical habit-formation transition, while REE's strategy-authority signal is meant to operate trial-by-trial, continuously, as evidence accumulates — related constructs, not the same clock. And the paper's execution-level readout is behavioural frequency and duration, not the softmax logit margin MECH-480 proposes as REE's execution-gain metric — both are "how strongly expressed," but nobody has shown they're the same quantity.

## Limitations and caveats

I have to be honest about what I could and couldn't read. Nature.com redirected to an authentication wall, and the bioRxiv HTML view returned a flat 403 — so nothing here comes from the paper's actual Methods or Results. What this entry is built from is the verified bioRxiv API abstract (which does state the core finding in the authors' own words: "shift in decision-making and amount of habit execution are independently controlled by plasticity in distinct cortical pathways") plus three independent secondary accounts — Kyoto University's own research-news page, EurekAlert, and Neuroscience News — all of which converge on the same circuit-level story. None of them report sample sizes, effect sizes, or which specific technique was used to erase the plasticity (optogenetic, chemogenetic, pharmacological — the press coverage is silent on this). That's a real gap. I'd want a session with journal access to read the actual statistics before this entry gets leaned on for anything beyond the qualitative shape of the dissociation. There's also the irony the abstract itself names: on the surface, before you erase anything, execution level looks coupled to strategy — it's only the causal manipulation that reveals independence. MECH-480's own falsifier already anticipates this trap (the NON-DEGENERACY guard against floor/ceiling artifacts masquerading as a real dissociation); it's reassuring that the paper's authors built their whole paradigm around dodging exactly that confound, but it's also a reminder that surface behavioural correlation is not evidence either way here.

## Confidence reasoning

Confidence lands at 0.68 — meaningfully above the ambiguous midpoint but well short of the 0.8+ range this design would earn with primary-source verification. Source quality (0.8) reflects a genuinely causal, double-dissociation design in a high-profile venue, marked down from the 0.9+ tier only because I couldn't check the manipulation's specificity or the statistics directly. Mapping fidelity (0.6) reflects a good conceptual match on the whether/how-strongly split, discounted for the circuit-specificity, timescale, and metric gaps laid out above — this paper licenses "the biological substrate can support this kind of split," not "REE's specific SD-032b authority signal and a logit-margin execution-gain channel will separate this cleanly." Transfer risk (0.45) is the standard cost of reading rodent circuit-lesion causal evidence into an RL-control architecture claim — ordinary for this literature genre, not unusually high, but real. This is supporting evidence for MECH-480's central architectural bet, not confirmation of its specific computational instantiation.
