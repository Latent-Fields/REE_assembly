# Horton & Adams (2005) -- The cortical column: a structure without a function

**Claim tested:** MECH-050 (functional locality supports attribution without requiring anatomical columns)
**Direction:** supports (asymmetrically -- see below) | **Confidence:** 0.80

## What the paper did

This is the fiftieth-anniversary audit of Mountcastle's column, and it is written by two people
who spent their careers looking for the thing. Horton and Adams take the whole accumulated
literature -- single-unit recording, tract tracing, cytochrome oxidase histochemistry, principally
in cat and primate V1/V2 and somatosensory cortex -- and ask the question that ought to have been
settled decades earlier: what does a column *do*? Their answer is that nobody knows, and that the
concept has degraded in the attempt to find out. "Column" began as a discrete modality-specific
structure spanning somatosensory laminae; it now denotes any vertical cluster of cells sharing
tuning for any receptive-field attribute whatsoever. A term that broad has stopped constraining
anything.

The load-bearing evidence is the ocular dominance column, because it is the case where the natural
experiment is available. Ocular dominance columns are present in some species and absent in others.
Worse for the functional reading, their expression varies among members of the *same* species, and
even between portions of V1 within a single individual. Horton and Adams take the obvious next step:
if columns do something, find the visual faculty that tracks their presence. No such faculty
emerged. They call this a fatal blow to the idea that ocular dominance columns serve a purpose, and
conclude that the column has failed as a unifying principle -- that understanding cortex will
require describing the circuits, projections and response properties of each area on their own
terms.

## How this bears on MECH-050

MECH-050 makes a two-part assertion: that *functional* locality -- modular recurrent substructure,
limited lateral spillover, sparse routing, bounded update regions -- supports error attribution, and
that this needs no anatomical column geometry, columnar bundling being a metabolic or developmental
optimisation rather than a computational primitive. The second part is close to a paraphrase of this
paper's conclusion, arrived at independently and from a far larger evidence base than REE has. That
matters: it means the claim's most contestable-looking move (declining to reproduce the most famous
structural motif in cortex) is the mainstream reading among the anatomists who looked hardest.

But I want to be exact about what this licenses, because the temptation to over-read it is strong.
The paper removes an *objection* to MECH-050; it does not supply MECH-050's mechanism. Horton and
Adams argue that columns are not necessary. They do not argue that bounded update regions are
sufficient, and the words "attribution" and "corrigibility" have no counterpart anywhere in their
evidence. A reading that treats this entry as evidence for the whole conjunction would be counting
the absence of one objection as the presence of a mechanism -- which is exactly the move REE's
governance is supposed to catch.

## Limitations and the direction they cut

Three caveats, and the second is the one I find genuinely uncomfortable.

First, the null result has the standard shape of a null result. "No visual faculty correlates with
ocular dominance columns" is compatible with "columns do nothing" and equally compatible with "the
discriminating assay was never run". Horton and Adams are candid about this; it is why the paper
reads as deflation rather than discovery.

Second, and more pointed for REE: the paper's own positive recommendation is *anti*-unifying. Its
closing move is that cortex will yield only to painstaking area-by-area description. That is not
support for substituting one canonical functional-locality motif everywhere in REE's representational
stack -- it is an argument that no single motif, columnar or otherwise, has earned that status. So
the paper simultaneously supports MECH-050's denial and undercuts the tidiness of MECH-050's
replacement. Both halves of that should be on the record.

Third, there is a direction-of-inference shift that the transfer_risk score is carrying. Biology's
question is whether an existing feature is functional. REE's question is whether an absent feature
is needed. Learning that cortex tolerates enormous variability in columnar expression tells us
biology never tested the low-locality regime; it does not tell us that arbitrarily low locality is
safe in a substrate that has neither columns nor the developmental history that produced them.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality 0.9: top venue, definitive authorship, exceptional breadth -- discounted slightly
because the contribution is synthesis, not measurement. Mapping fidelity 0.85 scored against the
claim's "without requiring anatomical columns" clause, where the correspondence is near-verbatim;
it would be near zero scored against the "functional locality supports attribution" clause.
Transfer risk 0.3 for the sufficiency-to-necessity shift above. Aggregate 0.80, weighting mapping
fidelity heavily as the calibration guide directs for an architectural claim, and deliberately not
higher: this is strong evidence for half a conjunction.
