# da Costa & Martin (2010) -- Whose Cortical Column Would that Be?

**Claim tested:** MECH-050 (functional locality supports attribution without requiring anatomical columns)
**Direction:** supports | **Confidence:** 0.80

## What the paper did

Where Horton and Adams audit the column's *function*, da Costa and Martin audit its *existence*.
This is a historical and conceptual review from Kevan Martin's group in Zurich -- Martin being one
of the two people who put the canonical microcircuit into the literature in the first place -- and
its central observation is deceptively simple: no one has ever seen the anatomy of a column. What
has actually been established, repeatedly and robustly, is a physiological fact: record along a
trajectory perpendicular to the cortical surface in a primate, ungulate or carnivore and you find
remarkable constancy in one set of receptive-field properties. The column was the structure
*inferred* to explain that constancy. The paper's point is that the inference never cashed out --
there is no obvious morphological analog for the functional architecture, and a good deal of the
anatomical data (their own work on bouton clusters and the lateral "daisy" connectivity included)
actively cuts against it.

They then make a constructive move rather than stopping at the deflation. The concept that can
reconcile the structure of neocortex with its functional architecture, they propose, is the
canonical circuit: a microcircuit that respects the neocortex's known connectivity and is flexible
enough to transiently change its network architecture to perform whatever computation is required.

## How this bears on MECH-050

This is the entry in the pull with the highest mapping fidelity, and it is worth being precise
about why. MECH-050 does not merely deny columns; it performs a specific two-term split --
functional motif is computationally relevant, anatomical bundling is contingent packaging -- and
then locates the computation in the motif. That is, term for term, this paper's argument. The claim
was registered from a thought document about the columnar motif versus columnar geometry; here is
the same distinction, drawn independently by cortical anatomists, on a far larger evidence base,
with the same resolution.

It is also the only entry here that speaks to *both* halves of MECH-050. Horton and Adams remove
the objection; this paper additionally nominates a positive replacement, which is what MECH-050
needs if it is to be more than a licence to omit something.

## Limitations and where the agreement stops

The agreement is at the level of framing, not measurement. This is a review with no new data, and
the canonical-microcircuit reconciliation is offered as a promising direction, not a demonstrated
result. For an architectural claim that is not disqualifying -- framing is most of what an
architectural claim is -- but it caps how much weight the entry can carry.

Two boundaries matter more than the venue question, and the first is genuinely awkward.

The canonical circuit da Costa and Martin propose is *dynamic*. Its selling point, in their own
words, is that it is flexible enough to change its network architecture transiently in order to
perform the required computations. MECH-050's functional locality is the opposite kind of object:
limited lateral spillover and bounded update regions are *static* structural constraints, and the
reason REE wants them is that a fixed locus of update is what makes error attribution tractable. A
motif whose effective connectivity reconfigures per computation satisfies this paper while
potentially failing the thing MECH-050 is actually for. So agreeing on "motif, not geometry" does
not settle *which* motif, and this paper's candidate may not be REE's. I would call that an open
question rather than a contradiction, but it should not be smoothed over.

Second: the vertical functional constancy being reconciled here is reported for primates, ungulates
and carnivores -- the taxa that have maps. The paper does not extend its account to the rodent
salt-and-pepper regime, which is the organisation REE's architecture more nearly resembles. The
canonical microcircuit is being fitted to the map-having case; whether it also explains the
map-lacking case is left open, and that is the case REE is betting on.

There is a third, more reflexive consequence worth recording. If the mapping from physiology to
anatomy failed in biology -- if the column was inferred and never found -- then REE cannot defend
its own functional locality by anatomical analogy either. "Our modules resemble cortical modules"
was never available as an argument, because the cortical modules were the thing that turned out not
to be there. MECH-050 has to be validated behaviourally, on attribution and corrigibility, not by
resemblance.
