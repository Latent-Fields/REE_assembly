# Cai, Mednick, Harrison, Kanady & Mednick (2009) -- REM, not incubation, improves creativity by priming associative networks

**PNAS 106(25):10130-10134. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.0900271106. PMID: 19506253.**

## What the paper did

Healthy adults were pre-exposed ("primed") to the target words of Compound Remote Associates Test (RAT) problems they had previously failed to solve, then took either a REM-rich nap, a NREM-only nap, or an equivalent period of quiet wakeful rest, with sleep stage confirmed by polysomnography. After the interval, everyone was retested on both primed and unprimed RAT problems, plus a separate recognition-memory test for the primed words themselves.

## Key findings

Only the REM-nap group showed a specific improvement on the primed problems relative to unprimed ones -- roughly a 40% relative gain -- while the NREM-nap and quiet-rest groups showed no such advantage. Critically, the REM group's recognition memory for the primed words was no better than the other groups': the effect was not "REM makes you remember the hint better," it was REM enabling a new associative link between the hint and the solution that the other conditions did not produce.

## How this maps to REE (ARC-092)

ARC-092 states that imagination/simulation may license consistency-checking, plan optimisation, and schema integration, but not durable world-model updates, prediction validation, or new-fact generation. This paper is a clean human instance of the schema-integration side: REM sleep -- the sleep stage most associated with internally generated, dream-like, imagination-adjacent content -- reorganizes associative structure among material the person already encoded while awake, producing a new *relation* between existing primitives rather than injecting new item-level knowledge. That the recognition-memory control shows no explicit-memory gain is exactly the dissociation ARC-092 needs: the LICIT operation (recombining waking-derived content into a better schema) is empirically separable from the FORBIDDEN one (treating imagined/offline processing as a channel for acquiring new durable facts). This gives REE's MECH-272/MECH-273 offline aggregation a concrete empirical target to reproduce -- restructure, don't fabricate.

## Limitations and caveats

The RAT is a laboratory proxy for "schema integration"; it measures verbal associative restructuring, not a computational schema/prototype update of the kind MECH-272/273 perform. The study says nothing about the FORBIDDEN side of ARC-092 -- it has no analogue of a world-model-update or prediction-validation channel to test, so it cannot speak to whether REM-like processing ever leaks into one. It is single-domain (verbal creativity) and does not establish that the same REM-dependent restructuring generalizes to procedural or spatial schemas.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality is high: PNAS, polysomnographically staged, with an explicit memory-strength control that rules out the most obvious confound. Mapping fidelity is moderate rather than high because the RAT is behaviorally indirect relative to REE's own schema substrate, and transfer risk is non-trivial (human verbal cognition to an artificial agent's schema/prototype mechanism). Net confidence 0.68: solid LICIT-side support, silent on the FORBIDDEN side.
