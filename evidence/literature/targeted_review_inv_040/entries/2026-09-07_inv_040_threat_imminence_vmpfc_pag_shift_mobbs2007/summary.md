# Mobbs et al. (2007) -- threat imminence shifts the defensive configuration

Healthy adults were pursued through a virtual maze by a predator that could chase, capture and
deliver a real electric shock, while fMRI tracked what happened as the threat closed. The finding is
a migration rather than an intensification: at distance, activity sat in ventromedial prefrontal
cortex; as the predator approached, it shifted toward periaqueductal gray. The shift was maximal when
a high degree of pain was anticipated, and imminence-driven periaqueductal gray activity tracked
rising subjective dread and falling confidence of escape.

This is the closest thing in the human literature to watching a harm terrain being configured, and it
cuts both ways for INV-040, which is why I have registered it as `mixed` rather than picking a side.
The supporting half is straightforward and worth stating: at distal threat -- predator visible,
nothing yet accumulated, no contact -- a defensive configuration is already engaged, and engaged in
ventromedial prefrontal cortex. Preparation demonstrably begins from sensory evidence about a hazard
that has not yet done anything. That is INV-040's core assertion, observed in humans.

The weakening half is the one I think matters more, and it bears on a word in the claim that is easy
to read past. INV-040 says a minimal cue is sufficient to activate the *appropriate* terrain
configuration. Mobbs et al. show that which configuration is appropriate is itself a function of the
accumulating variable -- the distal configuration and the proximal configuration are qualitatively
different, not scaled versions of one another, and the anticipated *magnitude* of harm modulates the
switch. So a cue carrying hazard presence but not hazard magnitude or proximity can activate some
preparation while being unable to select the right one. If REE's `z_world` hazard-gradient cue
carries only presence, INV-040's sufficiency claim may hold for the weaker reading (some terrain
preparation begins) and fail for the stronger one (the correct terrain configuration is selected).
That distinction should be pre-registered in any experiment, not discovered afterwards.

The mapping caveat is real and I do not want to bury it. Spatial proximity of a visible predator is a
continuously available, continuously updating sensory variable -- it behaves much more like a graded
accumulator than like the minimal cue INV-040 posits. The study therefore cannot isolate the
cue-only regime, and, more awkwardly, it cannot distinguish "the cue activated the configuration"
from "the cue initiated an accumulation that reached the configuration quickly". That is the exact
distinction the claim turns on. Confidence 0.60: a well-designed study, conceptually extended by the
same group's later work, bracketing the right regime with the wrong independent variable.
