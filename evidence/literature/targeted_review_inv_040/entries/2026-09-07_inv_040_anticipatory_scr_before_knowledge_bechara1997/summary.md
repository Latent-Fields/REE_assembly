# Bechara, Damasio, Tranel & Damasio (1997) -- anticipatory SCR before declarative knowledge

Ten healthy adults and six patients with bilateral ventromedial prefrontal damage played the Iowa
Gambling Task while behaviour, skin conductance and periodic self-report were recorded in parallel.
The headline result was ordering: healthy participants began selecting from the advantageous decks,
and began emitting anticipatory skin conductance responses when hovering over a risky deck, before
they could articulate which strategy was correct. The lesion group emitted no anticipatory SCRs at
all, and continued to choose badly even in the subset who eventually could state which decks were
punishing. The authors read this as a nonconscious biasing step preceding overt reasoning.

The part of this that matters for INV-040 is not the consciousness argument -- it is the within-trial
timing. The anticipatory SCR fires while the participant is inspecting a deck: after the cue, before
the card is turned, before any loss has been sustained on that trial. That is the same temporal
signature INV-040 asserts for E3's `terrain_weight` w_harm: elevated by a hazard-relevant cue in
`z_world` while `z_harm_a` is still low, because an EMA at tau of roughly twenty needs ten to thirty
steps to accumulate and a system that waits for it prepares too late. The lesion group is, quite
precisely, the failure mode the claim's own notes name -- harm weighting that never rises from cue
evidence, in an agent whose declarative representation of the hazard is otherwise intact.

I want to be careful about two things, because both are easy to elide. First, the deck-level cue that
drives the anticipatory SCR is itself the product of roughly ten prior trials of experience. It is
minimal *within* a trial and not minimal at all across the task. INV-040 should be read as a claim
about the within-episode regime, and this paper supports only that reading. Second, skin conductance
is undifferentiated autonomic arousal. It can tell us that something aversive is anticipated; it
cannot tell us that the *appropriate* terrain configuration was selected, and "appropriate" is a real
part of what INV-040 asserts. On this evidence the claim's cue-sufficiency half is supported and its
configuration-specificity half is untouched.

Confidence is set at 0.58, well below what the venue alone would suggest, and deliberately so. The
sample is small. More importantly, the interpretation the claim's notes actually lean on -- that the
bias precedes knowledge -- is the part that did not survive scrutiny; Maia & McClelland (2004) is
registered alongside this entry and argues the opposite. Using this paper as support for INV-040
requires keeping its timing result and setting aside its consciousness result, and honesty demands
saying that out loud rather than citing the paper as though it were undisputed. It remains genuine
evidence for the temporal claim. It is not the settled foundation the phrase "biological grounding"
in the claim notes implies.
