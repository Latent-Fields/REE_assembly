# Maia & McClelland (2004) -- what participants really know in the Iowa Gambling Task

This is the registered disconfirming source for INV-040, and it earns the label. Maia and McClelland
re-ran the Iowa Gambling Task with substantially more sensitive and more frequent knowledge probes
than the original self-report schedule, and found that participants know far more about the game than
Bechara et al. had detected. Not marginally more: participants reported the advantageous strategy
*more* reliably than they behaved advantageously, and when they did choose well, their reports nearly
always revealed quantitative knowledge of deck outcomes sufficient to have guided that choice on its
own. Their conclusion is deflationary -- both the behaviour and the reports can be explained by
sampling from consciously accessible knowledge, with no nonconscious somatic marker required.

The direct effect on INV-040 is to thin its cited evidential base. The claim's notes name three
biological groundings, and this paper substantially undercuts one of them. That is worth recording
plainly, because a claim carrying three named groundings reads stronger than a claim carrying two and
a contested one, and governance should see the difference.

The indirect effect is more interesting, and I think more useful. What Maia and McClelland actually
demonstrate is a measurement artefact: an apparent precedence result -- X happens before Y -- was
manufactured by an instrument too coarse to detect that Y had already begun. That is a general
hazard, and it lands squarely on any future INV-040 experiment. The claim's testable prediction is
that w_harm rises on cue-present steps *before* z_harm_a accumulates. If the accumulator is read with
a coarse or lagging instrument, or if "before accumulation" is operationalised as "early in the
episode" rather than as an explicit threshold condition on z_harm_a itself, then the experiment can
produce a clean-looking positive result that means nothing at all. The design must condition on the
accumulator's measured value, not on elapsed time.

I have registered this as `weakens` rather than `mixed`, but the mapping fidelity is deliberately held
at 0.55 and the reason should not be glossed. What this paper falsifies is the *nonconscious* half of
Bechara's interpretation. INV-040 asserts cue-sufficiency and temporal precedence; it says nothing
whatever about consciousness, which is not a property the REE substrate has or needs. So the
disconfirmation is adjacent to the claim rather than aimed at it. It does not show that cue-driven
anticipatory states are absent, and it does not test whether a partial sensory cue can configure a
defensive terrain -- it shows this particular paradigm does not *require* the nonconscious story.
Reading it as a refutation of INV-040 would be overreach. Reading it as leaving INV-040's cited
grounding weaker than advertised, and as a sharp methodological warning about how to measure the
claim, is the defensible reading and is what I have recorded.
