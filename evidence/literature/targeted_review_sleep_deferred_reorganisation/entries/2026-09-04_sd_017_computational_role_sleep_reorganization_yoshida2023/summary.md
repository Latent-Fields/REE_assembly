# Yoshida & Toyoizumi (2023) -- "Computational role of sleep in memory reorganization"

## What the paper did

This is a theoretical/computational review that surveys recent modeling approaches to sleep's role in memory processing, with a specific focus the parent thought's SWS/REM asymmetry directly bears on: the authors review the proposal that NREM (non-rapid eye movement, including slow-wave) sleep selectively CONSOLIDATES memory -- via mechanisms like sequential firing patterns and cortical up/down states that support memory selection -- while REM sleep REORGANIZES memory representations, connected to the developing neuronal representations thought to underlie dreaming.

## Key findings relevant to SD-017 / ARC-137

The review endorses and elaborates the general shape of a phase-specific division of labor: NREM handling selective consolidation, REM handling representational reorganization. This maps closely onto SD-017's SWS/REM functional-specialization claim and supports ARC-137's broader insistence that representational REORGANIZATION is a distinct offline outcome from mere retention/consolidation -- not two names for the same underlying process. Importantly, the review is candid about an asymmetry in how well-developed each half of this picture is: the NREM/consolidation side has considerably more developed computational models and stronger empirical grounding than the REM/reorganization side, which the authors explicitly flag as needing further theoretical development.

## Translation to REE

This is directly relevant literature for two REE claims. For SD-017 (the SWS/REM functional split), it is broadly supportive but with an important qualifier carried forward faithfully: the REM half of the split is the less-established half, in the field's own assessment, not just in REE's internal caution. For ARC-137 (the typed offline-transformation taxonomy, which treats reorganisation as a distinct outcome from retention repair), this review's willingness to treat "reorganization" as a genuinely separate computational problem from "consolidation" is itself supporting evidence -- it validates ARC-137's core methodological point that a phase label (REM vs. NREM) is not by itself proof of a distinct computation; the distinct computation has to be independently characterized, which is exactly what this review is attempting and candidly says remains incomplete on the REM side.

## Limitations and caveats

This is a review/synthesis article rather than new empirical data -- it reports and interprets the state of theoretical modeling in the field, and does not itself test SD-017 or ARC-137 with a fresh experiment. Its own candor about the underdeveloped state of REM-reorganization theory is the most important thing to carry forward: if REM's reorganizing role never gets a comparably rigorous computational account as NREM's consolidating role has, SD-017's REM-side claim specifically would remain the weaker half of the split, independent of anything REE does.

## Confidence reasoning

Solid, recent, and directly on-topic review from a respected venue. Confidence and evidence direction are both held to a moderate/mixed level deliberately: the review genuinely supports the general SWS/REM asymmetry SD-017 and ARC-137 need, but its own explicit statement that the REM-reorganization side is theoretically thinner than the NREM-consolidation side is a real complication, not a formality, and should not be smoothed over into a clean "supports" tag.

## Addendum: MECH-462 (first-order vs. second-order consolidation readout)

This entry also tags MECH-462, which distinguishes consolidation phases whose output is a first-order, content-bearing quantity (readily content-scorable) from phases whose output is a second-order quantity computed over representations (content-free by type, readable only through downstream behavioral consequence). The review's NREM/REM split maps onto exactly this distinction: NREM's memory-selection function (via slow-wave sequential firing) is a first-order operation over content, while REM's representation-reorganization function (via dream-related representation development) is a second-order operation over the representations themselves. The review's own candor that REM-reorganization theory is less developed than NREM-consolidation theory is consistent with MECH-462's point that second-order phases are inherently harder to score directly.
