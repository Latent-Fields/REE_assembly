# Oyarzun, Moris, Luque, de Diego-Balaguer & Fuentemilla (2017) -- "Targeted Memory Reactivation during Sleep Adaptively Promotes the Strengthening or Weakening of Overlapping Memories"

## What the paper did

This study used targeted memory reactivation (TMR) -- selectively cueing reactivation of one specific memory during sleep -- in a paradigm where memories were deliberately constructed to overlap: participants learned associations that shared common elements, forming a network of associatively related memories. The question was what happens to the OTHER, non-cued memories in that network when one member of the network is selectively reactivated during sleep.

## Key findings relevant to MECH-529

The associated, non-cued memories were not uniformly strengthened by the reactivation of their network neighbor. Instead, the brain adaptively regulated them -- strengthening some and weakening/pruning others -- as a function of each memory's relative associative strength with the shared element. This is a genuinely bidirectional, relationally-sensitive form of offline reorganisation: sleep-dependent reactivation is not simply "more reactivation, more strength" but a regulated process that can push associated memories in either direction depending on the network's relational structure.

## Translation to REE

MECH-529 predicts that offline replay under a shared evaluative frame drives split/merge/feature-reweighting operations over equivalence classes -- and critically, that this reorganisation is not a uniform consolidation multiplier but can move in either direction (strengthening a valid grouping, weakening or splitting an invalid one) depending on accumulated relational evidence. Oyarzun et al. supply close to the cleanest existing behavioral demonstration of exactly this bidirectional signature in humans: reactivating one memory causes its network neighbors to be adaptively strengthened OR weakened based on their associative relationship to it, not amplified indiscriminately. This is the strongest single piece of support in this pull for MECH-529's core empirical prediction.

## Limitations and caveats

The overlapping-memory network here is explicitly constructed by the experimenters through a paired-associate learning task with deliberately shared cue elements -- an oracular, externally-supplied relational structure, not one discovered non-oracularly from accumulated consequential-divergence evidence the way MECH-529's mechanism specifies. This is the same caveat that applies to the Solomon (2026) entry already in this directory: the paper demonstrates the right FUNCTIONAL SIGNATURE (bidirectional, relationally-adaptive reorganisation) without testing the specific non-oracular DERIVATION process MECH-529 proposes for how such relational structure would be discovered from experience rather than taught directly.

## Confidence reasoning

High source quality: a well-cited, causally-manipulated (TMR, the gold-standard technique for testing sleep-dependent memory effects causally) peer-reviewed study in a strong venue (Journal of Neuroscience). Mapping fidelity is good -- the bidirectional strengthen-or-weaken signature is precisely what MECH-529's split/merge framing predicts -- capped somewhat by the shared oracular-structure gap that also limits the Solomon 2026 entry in this same directory.

## Addendum: ARC-137 (typed offline-transformation taxonomy)

This entry also tags ARC-137, which distinguishes representational REORGANIZATION from retention/interference repair as separate offline outcomes requiring different readouts. Oyarzun et al.'s bidirectional finding -- some overlapping memories strengthened, others weakened, depending on relational structure -- is itself the kind of readout ARC-137 says reorganization should show: changed relational transfer and differential treatment of related content, not simply preserved or recovered competence after interference (which would be the retention-repair signature instead). A uniform strengthening result would not have distinguished the two outcomes; the bidirectional result does.
