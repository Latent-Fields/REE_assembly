# Oudiette, Antony, Creery & Paller (2013) -- "The role of memory reactivation during wakefulness and sleep in determining which memories endure"

## What the paper did

This study directly compares covert memory reactivation during WAKEFULNESS against reactivation during SLEEP, using targeted memory reactivation (TMR) -- sound cues associated with learned object-location associations -- in the same experimental design so the two conditions are directly comparable. Participants first learned associations of varying reward value (indicated by a point value on each object), then either stayed awake for 90 minutes or napped for 90 minutes, with a subset of their low-value associations targeted for cued reactivation during that interval.

## Key findings relevant to MECH-092 / MECH-272

Recall for low-value associations declined more than for high-value associations across both wake and sleep intervals -- covert reactivation (driven by the value signal itself, even without explicit cueing) shapes what gets retained. But the causally interesting result is about SCOPE: when the experimenters used sound cues to explicitly reactivate specific low-value associations, cueing during wakefulness rescued only the specifically cued item from forgetting, while cueing during SLEEP rescued the ENTIRE set of related low-value associations -- reactivation during sleep spread associatively beyond the directly cued item in a way waking reactivation did not.

## Translation to REE

REE already treats waking quiescent replay and sleep-phase replay as two licit but architecturally distinct reactivation channels (MECH-092's provenance framework), and the parent sleep-reorganisation thought insists explicitly that replay is a shared transport/sampling operation, not by itself a sufficient description of what a given reactivation event accomplishes -- wake and sleep replay are not interchangeable just because both involve "replay." Oudiette et al. supply direct causal human evidence for exactly this non-equivalence: both channels can rescue memories from forgetting via covert reactivation, but they differ qualitatively in scope, with sleep reactivation reaching an entire associative network and waking reactivation staying narrowly targeted. This grounds MECH-092's wake/sleep channel distinction as an empirically-supported architectural choice rather than an assumption, and is relevant context for MECH-272's account of what determines memory persistence.

## Limitations and caveats

The value signal that drives differential forgetting and rescue in this study is an explicit REWARD VALUE (point value assigned to each association), not REE's specific hypothesis-tag / provenance marker (real vs. simulated) that MECH-092 is centrally about -- this paper is strong evidence for keeping wake and sleep replay architecturally distinct in general, but it does not directly test the provenance-tagging mechanism itself. The paradigm is also healthy young-adult declarative object-location learning, a domain gap from REE's operational hypothesis-tagged trajectory replay architecture.

## Confidence reasoning

High source quality: a well-cited, causally-manipulated (TMR in both wake and sleep, directly compared within one design) peer-reviewed study from a leading memory-reactivation research group. Mapping fidelity is good for the specific architectural claim it bears on (wake and sleep replay are not interchangeable, and differ in associative scope), and moderate overall because the value signal tested differs from REE's provenance-tag construct specifically.
