# Lewis & Durrant (2011) -- "Overlapping memory replay during sleep builds cognitive schemata"

## What the paper did

This is a foundational theoretical paper in the sleep/memory literature, proposing a specific mechanism for how abstract cognitive schemata (organized relational knowledge structures) get built during sleep. The core proposal: when memories that share common elements are replayed in overlapping combinations during sleep, the SHARED elements get selectively strengthened relative to the memory-specific details. Repeated across many episodes and many different overlapping combinations, this selective strengthening progressively builds an abstract schematic representation of how the stimuli relate to one another -- not read out fully-formed from any single experience, but accumulated across many replay events.

## Key findings relevant to MECH-166 / ARC-045 / MECH-529

The model directly proposes the mechanism MECH-166 and ARC-045 need a theoretical anchor for: schema (or "slot") structure is built up cumulatively through overlapping replay, which is a natural theoretical grounding for MECH-166's claim that slot FORMATION must precede slot FILLING -- the abstract relational structure has to accumulate before specific content can be meaningfully organized within it. It is also the classical theoretical seed for MECH-529's split/merge/reweighting framing, proposing that offline replay across multiple episodes reorganizes representations rather than merely consolidating them individually. The authors extend the model to explain both insight (novel solutions emerging once a schema is sufficiently complete) and false-memory formation (over-generalization when shared-element strengthening goes too far) -- both plausible readouts of a REE-relevant offline reorganisation mechanism.

## Translation to REE

This paper predates and anticipates the empirical confirmations already banked or newly added in this same directory: Solomon (2026) shows sleep-phase reactivation dose driving surface-independent structural transfer, and Oyarzun (2017) shows bidirectional (strengthen-or-weaken) regulation of overlapping memories based on associative structure. Lewis & Durrant is the classical theoretical grounding those later empirical papers test and partially confirm -- it is worth banking here specifically because it establishes the THEORETICAL LINEAGE this cluster of MECH-166/ARC-045/MECH-529 claims sits within, not merely a loose family resemblance.

## Limitations and caveats

As a 2011 theoretical review, this paper provides no new empirical data of its own -- its value is as the classical anchor rather than as fresh evidence. Its original formulation is also narrower than MECH-529's full framing: it emphasizes selective STRENGTHENING of shared elements through overlapping replay, without proposing the bidirectional strengthen-OR-weaken mechanism that MECH-529 requires and that the later Oyarzun (2017) work empirically demonstrates. Read this entry together with Oyarzun (2017) and Solomon (2026) in this same directory for the fuller picture: theoretical grounding here, empirical confirmation (with the bidirectional extension) there.
