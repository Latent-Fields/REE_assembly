# Solomon, Krishnamurthy, Siefert, Gonciulea & Schapiro (2026) -- Memory Reactivation during Sleep Promotes Structure Abstraction

**Verification note:** bioRxiv preprint (doi 10.64898/2026.04.10.717748, posted 11 Apr 2026). Verified 2026-09-03: the DOI resolves via a bioRxiv lookup redirect; title, five-author list, and abstract matched the citation as given. Not peer reviewed.

## What the paper did

Solomon and colleagues ask when and how the brain manages to strip structural knowledge away from the superficial features it was first learned with -- a prerequisite for using that structure to generalize to something new that shares no features with the original at all. Their design is two experiments. Participants first learn a category defined by a particular pattern of feature covariation (a graph structure), then learn a second, entirely feature-disjoint category whose structure either matches or mismatches the first. In Experiment 1, with the two learning phases back to back, there is no transfer benefit at all -- structural knowledge stays glued to the features it was encoded with. Experiment 2 introduces an offline gap: learning phases are separated by three hours, during which participants either stay awake or take a nap in which cues from the first category are targeted for reactivation (TMR) during slow-wave sleep. Now a transfer benefit appears, but only when the reactivated and to-be-learned structures are congruent, and the size of the benefit scales with how many reactivation cues were delivered during slow-wave sleep. That dose-response relationship is the strongest part of the result -- it is hard to explain by anything other than the reactivation itself doing causal work.

## Findings relevant to MECH-529 and ARC-011

MECH-529 proposes a fairly specific mechanism: replay of non-contiguous episodes under a common evaluative frame, running preferentially in a decoupled sleep phase, drives split/merge/feature-reweighting and abstraction-change over the equivalence classes E1 uses to represent the world -- and the driving evidence should be typed insufficiency signals from decision outcomes, never bare category labels. This study's dependent variable is genuinely close to what such a mechanism should predict: surface-feature-independent structural transfer that appears specifically after sleep-phase reactivation, does not appear from mere elapsed time or exposure (Experiment 1 rules that out), and scales with reactivation dose. That is about as clean a human demonstration as exists that offline reactivation causally drives abstraction away from surface features, and the dose-response curve is exactly the kind of finding a rebucketing account should want. For ARC-011 (offline integration required for stability), the same result offers a looser but real form of support: without the offline nap-and-reactivation phase, structural knowledge simply fails to generalize, which is a demonstration that removing offline processing produces a measurable functional deficit, even though it is not framed in ARC-011's own vocabulary of representation drift, diversity erosion, or residue-field runaway.

## REE mapping and limitations

The honest limitation, and the one that matters most for how much weight this should carry, is that the category structures in this study were explicit graphs taught directly to participants -- exogenous, experimenter-supplied structure -- rather than equivalence classes that emerged from accumulated consequential-divergence evidence the way MECH-529 specifically requires (typed insufficiency signals from decision outcomes, never category labels). MECH-529's own falsifier menu asks for non-oracular learning (no downstream signal supplies the category label) and index continuity (episodic traces remain addressable after rebucketing); this study tests neither. It is evidence that sleep-phase reactivation CAN drive surface-independent structural transfer in humans, which is the right dependent variable, but it is not evidence about the specific non-oracular, consequence-driven mechanism MECH-529 proposes. It is also worth noting MECH-529 itself has no built REE substrate yet -- it is substrate_conditional, gated on the ARC-134 P0 merge/split operator -- so this entry is banked ahead of readiness rather than tested against a live mechanism.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality sits at the preprint ceiling (0.6): the targeted-memory-reactivation design is a well-established, causally interpretable sleep-manipulation technique, and the two-experiment structure with a dose-response result is methodologically strong, but the work is unreviewed. Mapping fidelity is moderate (0.55) -- the dependent variable lines up well, but the oracular/experimenter-supplied structure is a real mismatch with MECH-529's non-oracularity requirement. Transfer risk (0.55) reflects the domain gap between human declarative category learning and REE's E1 equivalence-class architecture, plus the untested index-continuity and object-correction/generalisation dissociation criteria. The aggregate, 0.55, is genuine but partial support: strong on "sleep-phase reactivation drives abstraction," silent on "abstraction is driven non-oracularly by decision consequences," which is the harder and more REE-specific half of MECH-529's claim.
