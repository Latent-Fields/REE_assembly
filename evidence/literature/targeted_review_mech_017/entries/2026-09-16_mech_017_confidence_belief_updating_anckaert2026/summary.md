# Sleep deprivation disrupts the gatekeeping role of confidence in belief updating (Anckaert, Peigneux & Gevers, 2026)

MECH-017 lists uncertainty recalibration as one of its three operations and then says nothing about
what it would look like if it failed. That silence is a problem for a claim meant to be testable,
and this paper is in the pull mainly because it fills it. Anckaert and colleagues ran two
preregistered within-subject studies (N=36 in 2023, N=49 in 2024) in which participants held
beliefs, received peer feedback, and could revise. The manipulation was twenty-four hours of total
sleep deprivation. The second study added a condition allowing confidence adjustment without belief
change, which is what lets the two quantities be separated.

The finding is not that sleep-deprived people became generally more or less confident. It is that
the *coupling* broke. Normally a belief held with high confidence resists revision -- confidence
acts as a gate on how much incoming feedback is allowed to move the posterior. Under deprivation
that gate stopped working: high-confidence beliefs, the ones ordinarily most stable, were revised
more often. Belief change increased regardless of feedback type, confidence drops were larger and
were largest where initial confidence was highest, and the size of the confidence drop predicted
whether the belief subsequently changed.

In REE's vocabulary this is a precision-weighting failure on the posterior, and that is the useful
translation. A recalibration mechanism that has stopped running does not produce uniform
overconfidence or uniform doubt -- it produces a system whose confidence no longer governs its
update magnitude, so precise beliefs behave like imprecise ones. That is a measurable dependent
variable. It gives MECH-017's third operation an experimental signature it currently lacks, and
one that a REE diagnostic could look for directly: not the level of posterior uncertainty, but
whether uncertainty still modulates how far a single observation moves the model.

The mapping is the weakest in this directory and I have scored it accordingly (mapping_fidelity
0.55, aggregate 0.58). The beliefs are social and propositional, revised under peer feedback;
MECH-017 concerns the generative model of a physical world. Confidence in a stated opinion may well
be regulated by machinery quite separate from precision on a sensory prediction, and nothing here
rules that out. There is a second and more structural limit: this is a deprivation design. It shows
that calibrated gating does not survive a night without sleep, which licenses the necessity
direction only -- sleep is needed for the gate to hold. It does not show that recalibration is
*performed* during sleep, and the alternative reading, that waking steadily degrades calibration
while sleep merely halts the degradation, fits the same data and is not MECH-017. Only 24-hour
total deprivation was tested, so the graded dose-response a genuine recalibration mechanism would
predict under partial restriction remains unexamined.
