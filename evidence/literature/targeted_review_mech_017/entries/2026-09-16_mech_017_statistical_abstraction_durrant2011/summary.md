# Sleep-dependent consolidation of statistical learning (Durrant, Taylor, Cairney & Lewis, 2011)

Of the human studies available for MECH-017, this is the one that measures the right thing.
Participants heard tones arranged by a probabilistic sequential structure, and were then tested on
their ability to recognise *novel* short sequences drawn from that same structure -- sequences they
had never heard. Performance on unheard material cannot be explained by retention of a trace,
because there is no trace to retain. It can only come from having a better model of the
distribution that generated the tones. That is what "fidelity of the generative world model" means,
operationalised, and it is why this entry carries more weight for MECH-017 than the far larger
literature on overnight retention of studied items.

The result has three parts. Participants who consolidated across a night of sleep improved
significantly more than those who consolidated across matched daytime wakefulness. The effect
reappeared in a second design: a four-hour afternoon interval containing a nap beat an equivalent
interval without one. And polysomnography showed a significant correlation between the amount of
slow-wave sleep obtained and the size of the improvement. The internal replication across two
retention designs is what makes this more than a single sleep-versus-wake contrast, and the SWS
dose-response is what locates the operation in a specific phase rather than in sleep generically --
the same phase ARC-011's offline-integration scheduling already privileges.

The authors are also straightforward about a fourth part, which cuts the other way: they report
evidence of a time-based consolidation process operating alongside the sleep-specific one. Some of
the abstraction gain comes from the interval, not from the sleep in it. That observation matters
here because it is the seam along which the Talamini 2022 null in this same directory pulls -- if
elapsed time does part of the work, a study whose design gives wakefulness enough time may find
sleep adding nothing detectable. The two results are not straightforwardly in contradiction and
should not be scored as though the later one simply overturns the earlier.

Two limits on how far this should be pushed. First, the SWS relationship is a correlation across
participants, not a manipulation: whoever got more slow-wave sleep improved more, which is also
what one would expect if some third factor -- prior sleep debt, baseline aptitude, time in bed --
drove both. Nothing here shows that adding slow-wave sleep would add abstraction. Second, an
auditory tone sequence is a thin world. There is one modality, no agent, no action and no
consequence, and MECH-017 ultimately claims fidelity gains for the model that keeps ethical
decisions grounded in reality. It is a real inferential step from tone statistics to that, and the
step is not licensed by anything in this paper. I should also record a retrieval limitation from
this pull: the sample sizes are not given in the abstract and the full text was paywalled
(ScienceDirect returned HTTP 403), so the study's power is unverified here and the effect size
should be treated as unknown rather than assumed adequate.
