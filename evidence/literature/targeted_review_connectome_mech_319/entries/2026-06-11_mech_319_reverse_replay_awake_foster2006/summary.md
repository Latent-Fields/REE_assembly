# Awake reverse replay -- Foster & Wilson 2006 (Nature)

According to PubMed. Source: Foster DJ, Wilson MA. "Reverse replay of behavioural sequences in hippocampal place cells during the awake state." *Nature* 440(7084):680-3, 2006. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04587)

**What the paper did.** Foster & Wilson recorded ensembles of CA1 place cells as rats ran a linear track and found that, during the brief awake pauses immediately after running, the recent spatial sequence was reactivated -- but in *temporally reversed* order. This was the first demonstration that internally-generated, temporally-structured replay occurs in the awake state (prior reports were of sleep replay) and, importantly, that it has a discriminable signature distinct from the on-line behavioural sequence. They proposed the reversed structure was suggestive of a role in evaluating event sequences in the manner of reinforcement-learning models.

**Why it matters for MECH-319.** This is the named substrate anchor in the claim's own notes (alongside the SWR-machinery review). MECH-319 presumes that the substrate hosts discriminable simulation/replay passes that a categorical write-gate could key on -- the "simulation tag" the gate reads. Foster & Wilson supply the foundational evidence that such internally-generated, temporally-tagged replay events exist and are distinguishable from real behaviour. In MECH-094's terms, this is the existence proof for a *tag-able* off-line mode; MECH-319 then proposes that the arbitration layer should condition its write policy on that tag.

**Where the mapping strains -- and this is the important part.** The paper supports only the substrate-availability *half* of MECH-319. It demonstrates that replay exists and is discriminable; it says nothing about whether arbitration-weight (or any synaptic) updates are categorically gated by a simulation tag. Worse for a naive reading, Foster & Wilson explicitly frame replay as a mechanism *for* learning -- reverse replay as an RL-style evaluation that drives plasticity. MECH-319's gate must therefore be selective: it suppresses updates only for *non-realized* (ghost/DMN) passes, while permitting real-experience replay to teach. A blanket simulation-mode write-block that caught all replay would directly contradict the learning role this paper assigns to it. So the entry is scored as supporting the substrate premise while being neutral-to-tension on the gating function.
