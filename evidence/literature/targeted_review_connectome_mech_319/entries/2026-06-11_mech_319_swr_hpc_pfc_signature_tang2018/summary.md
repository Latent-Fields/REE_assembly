# SWRs as context-tagged reactivation -- Tang & Jadhav 2018 (Neurobiol Learn Mem)

According to PubMed. Source: Tang W, Jadhav SP. "Sharp-wave ripples as a signature of hippocampal-prefrontal reactivation for memory during sleep and waking states." *Neurobiol Learn Mem* 160:11-20, 2018. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2018.01.002)

**What the paper does.** This review synthesises the evidence that hippocampal sharp-wave ripples (SWRs) are a *common* neurophysiological signature across both waking and sleep, organising coordinated reactivation of stored memory and facilitating communication between hippocampus and prefrontal cortex in both states. It also foregrounds the open question that is directly relevant here: do sleep and awake reactivation mediate *similar* memory processes, or do they have *different* mnemonic functions? The authors lay out the case that the same SWR machinery is deployed in different behavioural contexts to potentially different ends.

**Why it matters for MECH-319.** The claim's notes name a Joo & Frank SWR review as the substrate-machinery anchor; this Tang & Jadhav review is the cleanest current equivalent I retrieved, and it does two jobs for MECH-319. First, it consolidates the substrate-availability premise: SWRs are an established, well-characterised carrier of context-tagged reactivation at exactly the hippocampal-prefrontal interface REE abstracts into its arbitration layer. Second, and more interestingly, it frames the precise distinction MECH-319 operationalises -- that different reactivation *contexts* (sleep vs awake; by analogy, simulated vs realized) may warrant different functional treatment, and therefore plausibly different write policies. MECH-319 inherits from MECH-094 the principle that write policy should depend on the context of a reactivation; this review supplies the substrate and the framing for that principle.

**Where the mapping strains.** The review concerns consolidation and cross-regional communication -- SWRs as write-*enabling* for memory -- and the sleep-vs-awake axis. MECH-319's claim is the inverse-context case: a simulation-mode tag that *suppresses* arbitration updates from non-realized data. That specific gate is not evidenced here; what the review offers is the plausibility of context-dependent write policies, not a confirmed categorical simulation-tag gate. And whether sleep and awake reactivation truly differ in functional consequence is posed as an open question, not resolved -- so the support is for the scaffold, not the specific mechanism.

**Confidence.** 0.70. Solid current review from a leading lab; high for review-level evidence. Mapping fidelity moderate -- it anchors the SWR substrate and the context-matters framing but stops short of the write-gate. Transfer risk is the standard rodent-hippocampus to REE-arbitration gap, compounded by review-level generality. Its role in the cluster is to hold the substrate-availability and context-dependence premises while the Foster-lineage papers carry the discriminability evidence.
