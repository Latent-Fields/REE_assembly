# Separating the what and how of compositional computation to enable reuse and continual learning (Shan, Minni & Duncker, 2025, arXiv preprint)

This paper was not one of the three the source thought cited -- it turned up during the broader search this pull was instructed to run, looking for whether "reusable computational motifs" is a convergent pattern across multiple independent lines of work rather than a single paper's idiosyncratic framing. It is worth including precisely because it is the closest thing found to a direct computational test of MECH-503's SPECIFIC proposed bifurcation, rather than (like the other three entries) evidence that reuse happens without the architecture being explicitly split that way.

The architecture is a two-system design: a "what" system that uses a probabilistic generative model to infer shared structure across a stream of tasks and incrementally builds up a vocabulary of task components as it goes, and a "how" system -- a low-rank recurrent network -- that actually executes the inferred computation, composed based on the context the what-system has identified. That is structurally almost a direct restatement of MECH-503's proposed sequence: something decides WHICH reusable operation is needed (select), and a separate mechanism supplies HOW that operation actually gets carried out with the current content (bind/execute). The reported benefits are the ones you would want if you were trying to justify building this kind of split into REE's control plane: continual learning without catastrophic forgetting, forward and backward transfer between tasks, and rapid generalization to genuinely novel tasks by recomposing previously-learned low-rank components rather than learning each task's solution from scratch.

The honest caveat, and it is a significant one: this is an unreviewed arXiv preprint, posted 2025-10-23, with no biological data or grounding of any kind. It demonstrates that BUILDING IN an explicit what/how separation is a good engineering choice for a continual-learning system -- it does not demonstrate that brains, or REE's eventual substrate operating under MECH-481's existing typed-demand classification, need or benefit from the same separation. The "what" system here also builds its task-component vocabulary through unsupervised structure discovery across many episodes, which is a different (and arguably easier) problem than MECH-503's harder ask -- selecting the right motif under an ALREADY-typed demand classification, the way MECH-481's ControlDemandType taxonomy already frames it. That gap between "discover structure over time" and "select correctly given an existing type label" means this paper's what-system is not a direct proxy for the role-selection step MECH-503 proposes.

For REE, I'd weight this as suggestive convergent-design evidence rather than confirmatory evidence at the tier of the three peer-reviewed papers in this pull: it shows that when engineers deliberately go looking for an architecture that separates "which operation" from "how it's executed," they find real, measurable benefits of exactly the kind SD-101's falsifier logic predicts should exist if role/content separability is doing real work. That is a genuinely different kind of support than "the brain happens to reuse subspaces" (the other three entries) -- it is closer to "if you build the separation in on purpose, you get something useful," which is the more direct architectural argument SD-101/MECH-503 are actually making about REE. It should not, however, be read as evidence that this is how biological cognition -- or REE's eventual substrate -- is actually organized.

Confidence: 0.55, well below the three peer-reviewed papers, reflecting preprint status and the complete absence of biological grounding, while still weighted meaningfully above zero because the structural match to MECH-503's specific proposed mechanism is closer than anything else surfaced in this search.
