# Reusable modular architecture enables flexible cognitive operations in the mouse brain and artificial recurrent networks (Osako, Heller, Ährlund-Richter, Buschman & Sur, 2026, *Nature Neuroscience*)

This is the paper the source thought leans on hardest, and having pulled its actual abstract (via Semantic Scholar's indexed record, since PMC's full text was behind a CAPTCHA wall and the Nature.com page redirected to an authentication gate), it holds up. Mice perform a delayed match-to-sample task with a delay-and-report structure: hear a tone, hold it in memory, then at report time convert that memory into an action plan. The authors find a cluster of prefrontal cortex neurons that does something specific and useful for SD-101's argument -- it is not two separate populations, one for "remember the tone" and one for "remember the plan." It is the *same* population, occupying the *same* activity subspace, carrying different content at different points in the trial. A separate, distinct cluster in posterior parietal cortex, by contrast, holds onto the tone memory throughout and does not get repurposed. So the paper gives you both the phenomenon SD-101 is arguing REE's control plane needs to be able to represent (a reusable computational role with re-bindable content) and, in the PPC population, a contrast case of a fixed, non-reusable unit -- which is itself useful, because it shows the reuse is not simply "everything is fungible," it is selective.

The causal piece is where the paper is more interesting than the source thought's citation initially suggested, and worth being precise about. The thought's functional_restatement in SD-101 says the paper shows "computation-specific lesion effects in data-constrained RNNs," and that phrasing turned out to be exactly right once I checked it against three independent press summaries (AZoLifeSciences, MedicalXpress, TechExplorist) plus the indexed abstract -- but those same three press pieces, independently of each other, all flag that the *optogenetic or pharmacological silencing of the actual mouse circuit* is explicitly future work, not something reported in this paper. What the paper does report is silencing specific clusters within an artificial recurrent network that was constrained to reproduce the recorded population dynamics, and showing that silencing a cluster disrupts the computation that cluster's corresponding subspace implements, while leaving other computations intact. That is a real causal result, but it is causal-in-the-model, not causal-in-the-animal. For REE's purposes this is a meaningful distinction: it tells you the reuse phenomenon is at least dynamically self-consistent (a model that reproduces the recorded dynamics also reproduces a computation-specific fragility when you knock out the corresponding subspace), but it does not yet tell you the biological circuit is causally organized the same way rather than merely correlated with it.

Translating to REE: this is about as close as you currently get to a direct empirical anchor for SD-101's core move -- separating computational role from the content currently bound to it as things that do not have to co-vary. The PFC cluster literally performs "hold something in an accessible state" as its role, and what it holds changes from stimulus-identity to action-plan over the course of a trial without the role itself, or the neurons implementing it, changing. That is exactly the kind of dynamic re-binding MECH-503 proposes inserting an explicit binding step to support in REE's control plane, ahead of coalition/topology instantiation. What the paper does *not* give you is any evidence about the right way to represent that distinction architecturally -- it is a finding about what biological/RNN dynamics happen to do, not a claim about symbolic control-plane design, and SD-101/MECH-503 are explicit that they are not committing to any particular implementation schema for exactly this reason.

Two honest caveats limiting how far this should be leaned on. First, the causal-in-model-not-in-animal gap above means the strongest version of "this subspace really is doing the reusable-role work, and disrupting it really breaks the computation in the living circuit" is not yet established -- it is a reasonable, well-supported inference from a model fit to the data, not a direct demonstration. Second, this is one task, one species, two brain regions, and two subspaces -- the generality of "reusable role, rebindable content" beyond this specific delay/report structure is not tested here; a third, structurally different task would be needed to know whether the phenomenon generalizes the way SD-101's architectural claim would require, or whether it is specific to short-timescale within-trial memory-to-plan conversion.

Confidence: 0.68. Strong venue, a genuinely apt result for the specific architectural distinction SD-101 draws, and independently verified against the actual indexed abstract rather than taken on the source thought's characterization alone -- but held below 0.75 by the causal-in-model-not-in-animal gap and the single-task, single-species scope.
