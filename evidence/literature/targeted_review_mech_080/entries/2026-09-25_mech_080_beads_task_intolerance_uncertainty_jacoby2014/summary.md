# The Beads Task and intolerance of uncertainty in anxiety disorders (Jacoby et al., 2014)

## What the paper did

Jacoby and colleagues gave adults with a primary anxiety disorder diagnosis, and non-anxious
controls, the classic beads task -- the same information-sampling paradigm used by the
already-banked Hauser 2017a/b entries and by Rapp et al. 2024. Alongside task performance,
participants completed the self-report Intolerance of Uncertainty Scale. The question was
whether IU, a well-established self-report construct in anxiety research, actually tracks
behaviour on an uncertainty-induction task, or whether it is a purely verbal/attitudinal
measure disconnected from what people do when facing real sequential uncertainty.

## What is relevant to MECH-080

The anxious group reported significantly more distress during the task than non-anxious
controls, and within the anxious group, self-reported IU correlated with both
draws-to-decision and task distress. This is older and more modest evidence than Rapp 2024,
but it independently supports the same underlying link Q2 is asking about: IU -- the
construct behind MECH-080's original arm-2 mechanism (MECH-078, chronically high
uncertainty) -- has a real relationship to behaviour on this exact paradigm, not only to
self-report anxiety scales in isolation.

## Limitations

No computational model is fit here, so nothing separates a threshold-like account from an
inferential one the way the Hauser or Rapp entries attempt to. The key between-group
comparison is on distress, not draws-to-decision; the draws-to-decision/IU link is a
within-anxious-group correlation, which cannot rule out that it is a severity marker inside
anxiety rather than a population-wide driver. The clinical sample mixes anxiety diagnoses
rather than isolating GAD, so it cannot adjudicate anxiety-vs-compulsivity specificity.

## Confidence reasoning

0.45. Included as corroborating context for the stronger Rapp 2024 entry -- it shows the
IU-anxiety-sampling link is not a single-study artifact, but on its own it is too
behaviourally thin (distress, not draws-to-decision, as the group-level result) to carry
much independent weight on Q2.
