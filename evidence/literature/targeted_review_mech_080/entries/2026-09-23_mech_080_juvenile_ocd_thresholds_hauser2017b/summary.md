# Raised thresholds in juvenile OCD (Hauser et al., 2017, PLoS Comput Biol)

## What the paper did

This is the clinical companion to the population study in the sibling entry. Sixteen
juvenile OCD patients and sixteen matched controls performed a sequential information-
gathering task with manipulated sampling costs. The OCD group took more draws before
committing. They were not less accurate. They scored significantly *more points* than
controls. Modelling put the difference in a subjective cost function that escalated
non-linearly -- an urgency signal -- and the OCD group's urgency emerged later, raising the
effective decision threshold.

## The finding that helps MECH-080

MECH-080 carries an unusually well-specified falsifier: matched manipulations that fail to
dissociate the three signatures falsify the claim, *unless* all the effects reduce to lost
competence, in which case the result is uninformative rather than negative. That clause is
there because REE's conversion ceiling makes competence loss the default explanation for
almost any behavioural change, and it is the reason the experimental half of this claim is
currently blocked.

This paper is a clinical existence proof that the clause is describing something real and
separable. Here is a commitment-timing shift of exactly the kind MECH-080 is about, in a
diagnosed group, and it is not a competence deficit -- it is a competence *advantage*. The
patients gathered more evidence, and gathering more evidence paid. That tells a future REE
experiment what a valid positive looks like: shifted draws-to-decision with accuracy held or
improved. It also tells it what an invalid one looks like, which is the more useful half,
because the invalid pattern is the one V3 is currently likely to produce.

## The finding that hurts MECH-080

The arm assignment is wrong. MECH-080 routes OCD to attractor lock-in -- an abnormally deep
basin, MECH-076, normal signals failing to trigger basin exit -- and routes elevated
uncertainty with suppressed commitment to anxiety. What this paper observes in diagnosed OCD
is elevated uncertainty with suppressed commitment. Taken together with the compulsivity-
spectrum result in the sibling entry, the literature is saying the same thing twice in two
different designs: the phenotype the claim gives to anxiety is the one OCD actually shows.

I do not think this sinks the claim, and I want to say why rather than let the entry imply
more than I mean. Over-sampling before committing and perseverating after commitment are not
mutually exclusive, and OCD plausibly does both -- checking is over-sampling, and the ritual
that follows is perseveration. What it does sink is the clean three-way *assignment* of one
phenotype to each disorder, which is what MECH-080 as written asserts and what its
three-arm experiment would test. A dissociation design that expects OCD to show lock-in and
only lock-in is designed against the evidence.

## Confidence

0.55, `mixed`. Sixteen per group is the dominant limitation and I have let it set the ceiling;
a case-control difference at that n deserves the discount even in a good venue with a good
model. The two directions are genuinely opposed here -- competence-neutrality supports the
claim's methodology, the phenotype supports a revision of its content -- and `mixed` is the
honest encoding rather than a hedge.
