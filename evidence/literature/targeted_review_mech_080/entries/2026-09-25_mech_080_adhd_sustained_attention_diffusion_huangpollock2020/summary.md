# A diffusion model analysis of sustained attention in ADHD (Huang-Pollock et al., 2020)

## What the paper did

The same research group behind the already-banked 2017 go/no-go study ran a larger
follow-up: three independent samples of school-age children, 128 with ADHD and 59 controls
combined, performing a continuous-performance sustained-attention task at varying event
rates and discrimination difficulty. Performance was decomposed with both signal detection
theory and diffusion modelling.

## What is relevant to MECH-080

Q7 asks for literature that helps separate ADHD's drift-rate (competence) deficit from its
starting-point-bias (set-point) deficit. This paper does not directly extend that separation
-- what it reports is a replication, across a different task and a much larger combined
sample, of the same evidence-quality/sensitivity deficit at the heart of the 2017 finding:
children with ADHD showed reduced target-nontarget discriminability at every time point, plus
a secondary, time-on-task effect (rising no-go drift rate, read as a growing bias against
engagement as vigilance decays across the session).

That strengthens the competence side of the picture -- the drift/sensitivity deficit is now
robust across two tasks (go/no-go and sustained attention) and three independent samples --
without independently testing the starting-point side. It should be read as continuing to
weaken a pure-threshold account of arm 1 (the same direction as the 2017 entry), but it
neither confirms nor contests the starting-point-bias finding specifically; it is simply
silent on that parameter in the material available here.

## Limitations

The abstract text obtained did not surface a reported comparison on boundary separation or
starting point as independent parameters, so this entry cannot be used to argue the
starting-point finding failed to replicate -- only that this paper does not test it. Like the
2017 study, a continuous-performance task has no forward rollout, so rollout DEPTH remains
untested. The vigilance-decrement effect (drift rate rising with time on task) is itself a
within-session dynamic, not a standing set-point, which is a further disanalogy with a fixed
rollout_horizon parameter.

## Confidence reasoning

0.60. High source quality (same authorship group, large combined N, peer-reviewed
replication) discounted because it only partially adjudicates Q7 -- it reinforces the
competence/drift side without touching the starting-point side the question specifically asks
about.
