# Selective enhancement of cost sensitivity by methylphenidate in adult ADHD (Pedersen et al., 2026)

## What the paper did

Sixty-one adults with ADHD (tested on and off methylphenidate in a randomized
placebo-controlled design) and sixty-three healthy controls performed a cost-benefit task:
deciding whether to accept or reject an option based on learned cost and benefit value
associations. The authors fit a three-parameter drift diffusion model to separate reduced
sensitivity to the cost/benefit information itself, the decision threshold (how much
evidence is required before committing), and starting-point bias (a prior lean toward one
response before evidence arrives).

## What is relevant to MECH-080

Q7 asks whether ADHD's premature-commitment phenotype (MECH-080 arm 1) is a drift
(competence) account or a starting-point-bias (set-point) account, following Huang-Pollock
2017's finding that it decomposed onto reduced drift plus increased starting point but not a
lowered boundary. This paper, in an adult sample with a different task, reports differences
on all three parameters: reduced cost/benefit sensitivity (a drift-like competence measure),
a NARROWER decision threshold, and premature responding attributed to starting-point bias.

That is a genuinely new and complicating data point rather than a clean answer. It gives the
starting-point-bias reading independent support in a second, well-powered adult sample --
strengthening the set-point half of Huang-Pollock's finding. But it also reports a threshold
effect that the 2017 go/no-go study explicitly did not find, so the two studies disagree on
whether ADHD narrows the decision boundary. Read together, ADHD's prematurity does not look
parameter-pure in either study: some competence (drift) signature is present in both, and the
threshold/starting-point picture differs by task. The trial's own pharmacological probe adds
a further data point: methylphenidate selectively improved cost sensitivity (the drift-like
parameter) and, per the abstract, did not resolve the threshold or starting-point
differences -- suggesting the closest REE analog of stimulant treatment would act on the
competence component, not a truncation set-point.

## Limitations

This entry was built from an automated abstract summarisation rather than a full-text read;
the specific parameter labels ("narrower decision thresholds", "premature responding") should
be re-verified against the published methods before leaning on them for a precise REE mapping.
The task is a single-step economic accept/reject decision, not a forward rollout, so as with
Huang-Pollock 2017 it cannot speak to rollout DEPTH directly -- only to the commit-threshold
analog.
