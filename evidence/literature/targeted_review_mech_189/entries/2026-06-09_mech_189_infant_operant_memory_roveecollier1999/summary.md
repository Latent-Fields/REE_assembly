# Infant operant memory: the developmental existence-proof, and its honest limit

Rovee-Collier's mobile-conjugate-reinforcement paradigm is the closest thing developmental psychology has to a controlled demonstration of MECH-189's core event. A ribbon ties a 2-3-month-old infant's ankle to an overhead mobile; the infant discovers, by accident, that kicking moves the mobile; and within two short sessions the infant has formed an operant action-outcome memory that persists for days to weeks. This is — in the substrate's own terms — a high-salience, accidentally-contacted benefit being written into a durable bias on future behaviour. It is the human-developmental analogue that MECH-189 needs, and it is the second of the two anchors already named in the claim.

Two features of the data map onto the substrate with real precision. First, retrieval is sharply *context-dependent*: whether the infant kicks on a later occasion depends on the proximal and distal cues (the specific mobile, the crib liner, the room) being the same as at encoding. Change the context and the response collapses. That is the behavioural signature of a *cue-indexed* store — the infant's memory is keyed on context, exactly as `SuperOrdinalGoalMemory` keys its anchors on `z_world` context. Second, the reactivation finding — a brief reminder exposure 24 h before test fully reinstates an otherwise-forgotten memory — maps onto the substrate's reinforce-on-match write, where re-encountering a context within `merge_similarity` of an existing anchor EMA-blends and strengthens it rather than allocating a new slot. So the *mechanism class* — accidental salient contact → cue-indexed durable bias, kept alive by re-encounter — has a clean developmental instantiation here.

But this same body of work is the strongest single source of *tension* for the claim, and intellectual honesty requires me to lead with it rather than bury it. Infant operant memories are not durable by default. Forgetting is typically *complete* within about 8 days unless the memory is reactivated, and even within the retention window, retrieval is so context-bound that a change of setting abolishes it. MECH-189 asserts that childhood anchors "bias adult z_goal seeding across *novel* episodes." The Rovee-Collier data pushes back on both halves of that: persistence requires reactivation (not a one-shot write), and retrieval is the opposite of context-general — it is context-*specific*. The substrate as implemented models neither decay nor a reactivation schedule, and its novelty-based complexity gate plus a single READ match threshold have to thread a needle the biology says is narrow: anchors specific enough to be meaningful, but not so specific they never fire in a genuinely new context.

I have therefore scored this `mixed` at 0.6. It supplies the best available developmental grounding for the WRITE-and-cue-index half of the mechanism — that is a genuine "supports" — while its own findings on decay, reactivation-dependence, and context-specificity actively weaken the unqualified "persists across novel adult episodes" reading. The constructive reading is that MECH-189's persistent store is defensible as a model of the *consolidated end-state* of a reactivation-maintained childhood memory, not as a model of the labile infant trace itself; and that the validation EXQ should either build in repeated child-phase re-encounters (so anchors are reinforced, matching the reactivation biology) or be interpreted against that consolidated end-state. Which is precisely why the next entry — schema-accelerated consolidation — is load-bearing.
