# Incentive salience: the dopaminergic "wanting" signal that should gate a super-ordinal write

Berridge & Robinson's 1998 review is the canonical statement of incentive-salience theory, and it is already one of the two anchors named in MECH-189. Its central move is a triple dissociation: using 6-OHDA mesolimbic dopamine lesions and taste-reactivity assays in rats, they separate *liking* (the hedonic impact of a reward, carried by small opioid/GABA "hedonic hotspot" systems), *wanting* (incentive salience — the motivational-magnet quality that dopamine attributes to reward-related cues), and *learning* (associative prediction). Dopamine, on their account, is necessary for wanting but not for liking or for learning new likes. The slogan that does the work for us is that the brain can *want* something it does not (yet) *like*.

Why this matters for MECH-189: the substrate's WRITE gate (a) is a "high salience" conjunct, operationalised in `goal.py` as `benefit_exposure * (1 + drive_weight * effective_drive)`. The question the biology-before-mechanism pass has to answer is whether the *kind* of quantity that should mark a benefit contact for durable storage is a salience signal or a hedonic valuation. Berridge & Robinson answer it cleanly: it should be a drive-modulated salience ("wanting") signal, dissociable from and not dependent on calibrated liking. This is also the deepest grounding for MECH-329 (the registered child mechanism): if wanting is constitutively separable from liking, then the earliest goal anchors can be seeded by approach-drive salience before any hedonic-evaluation system is online — which is exactly MECH-329's "wanting before liking" ordering.

The honest limits are two, and they are both about what the paper is *not*. First, this is adult rat pharmacology. It establishes that wanting and liking are separable systems; it does not show that one *develops* before the other. So it supports the *premise* of MECH-329's ordering claim (separability) without supplying the *developmental ordering* itself — that ordering is grounded more weakly, and the MECH-329 anchor "Keren-Portnoy & Tomasello 2021" could not be independently verified in this pass (see the synthesis). Second, and more important for MECH-189 specifically: nothing here speaks to whether a salience event is ever *consolidated into a persistent, context-addressed store*. The paper is about an online motivational signal, not about a memory write. MECH-189's architectural heart — childhood salience events written to a cross-episode cue-indexed bank — has to be evidenced by the consolidation literature (Tse 2007), not here.

I have set confidence at 0.72: weighted up for source quality (a foundational, heavily replicated dissociation) and for the directness with which it grounds the salience-gating primitive, and weighted down for mapping fidelity, because the paper evidences only one of the substrate's several moving parts and says nothing about persistence, cue-indexing, or development. It supports the mechanism *class* at the level of "the right thing is being used as the write trigger," and that is the contribution I am crediting it with.
