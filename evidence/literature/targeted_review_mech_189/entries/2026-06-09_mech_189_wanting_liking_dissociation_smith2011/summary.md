# Neural separability of wanting, liking, and learning — the premise MECH-329 needs

MECH-329 — the registered child mechanism of MECH-189 — makes a temporal claim: the wanting system (mesolimbic dopamine / approach motivation) seeds the first super-ordinal goal anchors before the liking system (hedonic evaluation) is calibrated, so the earliest layer of the goal hierarchy is structured by approach drive rather than by mature preference. For that claim to be coherent at all, wanting and liking must be genuinely distinct, independently controllable signals — otherwise "wanting before liking" is not even a well-formed possibility. Smith, Berridge & Aldridge 2011 is the cleanest neural confirmation of that premise.

Recording single units in the ventral pallidum while manipulating the nucleus accumbens, they dissociate three reward signals within one circuit: hedonic impact (liking), incentive salience (wanting), and learned predictive value. The decisive manipulation is an intra-accumbens μ-opioid microinjection (DAMGO): it simultaneously increased behavioural liking reactions to sucrose *and* increased firing to a reward-*proximal* incentive cue (wanting), while leaving firing to a reward-*distal* learned-prediction cue unchanged. Three signals, separately tracked, separately modulable — and wanting moved without the learned-prediction signal moving. That is exactly the architecture MECH-329 presupposes: a salience/wanting signal that can be driven independently of both calibrated hedonic value and learned prediction. It also carries a design implication for the substrate — the WRITE gate should key on a salience/drive quantity (as it does) rather than on a learned value estimate, because tying the write to a learned value would reintroduce precisely the coupling this paper shows the brain keeps separate.

I have deliberately tagged this entry to MECH-329 only, not to MECH-189, and held confidence at 0.55 — and the reason is the same honest limit that runs through this whole pull. This is adult-rat electrophysiology, and it demonstrates *separability*, not *developmental ordering*. It establishes that wanting and liking are distinct systems that *can* operate independently; it does not show that wanting comes *online first* in development, which is MECH-329's actual assertion. The separability premise is well-grounded here; the wanting-*before*-liking developmental conclusion is not — it rests on the broader incentive-salience framework plus infant-intentionality work, and the specific anchor MECH-329 cites for the developmental ordering ("Keren-Portnoy & Tomasello 2021") could not be independently verified in this pass. So this paper does real work — it makes MECH-329's ordering claim *possible* and licenses the substrate's salience-keyed write — while leaving the developmental-timing half of MECH-329 as the registry's thinnest link, which the synthesis flags as the priority follow-up.
