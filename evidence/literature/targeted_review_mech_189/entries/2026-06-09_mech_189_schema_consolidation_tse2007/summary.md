# Schema-accelerated consolidation: how a labile early trace becomes a stable anchor

The previous entry left MECH-189 with a durability gap: Rovee-Collier's infant operant memories are labile and decay within days unless reactivated, yet the substrate posits anchors that persist into adulthood. Tse et al. 2007 is the entry that closes that gap mechanistically, which is why it is load-bearing for the persistence half of the claim. Using a flavour-place paired-associate task in an event arena, the authors show that once a rat has acquired a stable associative *schema* for the task, a brand-new flavour-place pair learned in a *single trial* becomes hippocampus-independent within 48 hours — consolidation that normally takes weeks now takes days. The medial prefrontal cortex is necessary for this schema-based assimilation, and a sibling 2011 study confirmed schema-consistent learning drives immediate-early-gene expression in mPFC.

This is exactly the shape of mechanism MECH-189 needs. The substrate posits a cue-indexed store (`key = z_world context`, `value = z_goal anchor`) that is written rapidly when a salient contact occurs and then held persistently across episodes. Tse et al. supply the biological analogue: a cue-indexed associative schema, hippocampally encoded and *cortically* (mPFC) consolidated, into which new cue-associated items are rapidly and durably integrated. The design doc names "hippocampal-vmPFC goal-anchor formation" as the target; the mPFC schema in Tse et al. is the natural seat of that cross-episode goal hierarchy. So the path Rovee-Collier → Tse is: an accidental salient infant contact creates a labile hippocampal trace (Rovee-Collier), and a schema-assisted consolidation step migrates it into a stable cortical anchor (Tse) — which is the durable super-ordinal goal the substrate stores.

The caveats are real and two of them bite on substrate design specifically. First, schema-assisted *speed requires a pre-existing schema*. The first associations into a *novel* schema consolidate slowly and stay hippocampus-dependent. But MECH-189's bootstrap does the opposite: an empty store yields complexity = 1.0, so the very first child-phase contacts write anchors at full strength immediately. The biology says those earliest, pre-schema anchors should be the *slowest* and least stable to consolidate — so the substrate's immediate full-strength bootstrap write is, if anything, optimistic about early-anchor durability. Second, the broader schema literature (including bioRxiv follow-ups) finds that schemas accelerate integration *at the cost of item-level memory specificity over time*: consolidated traces generalise toward the schema centroid and lose detail. If super-ordinal anchor keys drift the same way, READ retrieval in a novel context could pull `z_goal` toward an over-general anchor rather than a context-appropriate one — the flip side of the over-specificity risk the Rovee-Collier entry raised.

The transfer risk is also honest: this is adult-rat spatial flavour-place association, not developmental affect-tagged goal anchors. I am crediting the paper with grounding the *consolidation mechanism class* — rapid, cue-indexed, hippocampal-to-mPFC, schema-gated — not with demonstrating a goal-anchor result. At 0.62 `supports`, it is the strongest single piece of evidence that the persistence-and-cue-indexing half of MECH-189 corresponds to a real and well-characterised biological process, while the spatial-vs-value and bootstrap-vs-pre-existing-schema mismatches keep it short of high confidence.
