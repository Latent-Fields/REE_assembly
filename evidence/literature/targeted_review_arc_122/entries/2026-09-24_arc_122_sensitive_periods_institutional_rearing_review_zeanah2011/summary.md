# Zeanah et al. 2011 -- "Sensitive Periods" (institutional rearing review)

**What they did.** Investigators from the main institutional-rearing studies (BEIP, ERA and US adoptee cohorts) reviewed what those studies say about sensitive periods in human development. They distinguish sensitive periods at the level of neural circuits from the complex behaviours (IQ, attachment, externalising problems) that studies actually measure, and argue that many circuit-level periods probably sit under each behaviour.

**Why it matters for ARC-122.** This is the closest thing to a direct answer to the claim's open question, "what specifically consolidates, on what timescale". Their reading: across most but not all domains, institutional care limited to the first 4-6 months carries no added long-term risk. Past that, the evidence for sharp sensitive periods is weaker, and the defensible rule is "the earlier the better". That describes graded, staggered windows, not a clean handoff from one phase to the next.

**Where it doesn't reach.** The review is about the cost of deprivation at different ages, not about whether development has to happen in a fixed order. So it weakens the "strict" and "sequential" parts of ARC-122's framing but is silent on whether play depends on prior secure coupling. "Less compelling" beyond 6 months partly reflects small samples and confounded timing, not proof that no window exists.

**Confidence.** 0.55, weakens (the strict-sequence reading specifically). The claim would sit better with this literature if it described overlapping, graded windows with an ordering tendency rather than four discrete sequential phases.
