# Gee et al. 2013 -- deprivation accelerates amygdala-mPFC maturation (Tottenham lab)

**What they did.** Under typical conditions, functional coupling between medial prefrontal cortex and amygdala is immature (positive) in childhood and becomes adult-like (negative) in adolescence. Rodent work shows maternal deprivation speeds this up. Gee, Tottenham and colleagues scanned children and adolescents who had been institutionalised early in life. They showed adult-like negative coupling at ages when comparison children still showed the immature pattern, and cortisol mediated the difference.

**Why it matters for ARC-122.** Phase (2) of the claim is "coupling consolidation with plasticity reduction". This paper suggests that in humans the timing of that consolidation is not a fixed calendar event. It is set by the caregiving signal. Without a caregiver buffering stress, the regulatory circuit matures early. This fits Tottenham's broader argument that caregiver presence scaffolds amygdala-PFC development during a period of heightened plasticity.

**Where it doesn't reach.** The design is cross-sectional, so "acceleration" is inferred from a connectivity sign rather than tracked in the same children. The early-mature pattern gave some anxiety reduction, so it is partly adaptive, not simply damage. And it addresses one regulatory circuit, not play or language.

**Confidence.** 0.5, mixed. It supports the claim's premise that caregiving shapes consolidation, but implies a curriculum should gate the consolidation step on coupling quality (a dependency condition) rather than on a fixed schedule. Premature consolidation under poor coupling is a failure mode ARC-122 does not currently name.
