## What consolidates, and when (answer to the claim's open question)

Several things on staggered, partly overlapping timetables: in humans, long-term risk appears after ~4-6 months of deprivation for social/neurodevelopmental outcomes (ERA); attachment security and HPA/parasympathetic reactivity are still recoverable with placement up to ~18-24 months, with graded decline (BEIP); cognitive deficits can remit even after long deprivation; amygdala-mPFC regulatory maturation is pulled earlier by caregiver absence. In rats, the approach-only attachment regime closes around PN10 but is reversibly re-opened by caregiver presence. The consistent picture is graded, domain-specific, overlapping, caregiver-gated windows, not a strict four-phase sequence.

## Suggested amendments to ARC-122 text (not applied)

1. Replace "four sequential developmental phases" with "four developmental phases with an ordering tendency and overlapping, graded windows"; the "skipping or reordering" falsifier should be restated as degradation that scales with delay, not a hard fail. As written, the strict-sequence reading is weakened by BEIP/ERA/Zeanah 2011.
2. Phase (2) "plasticity reduction" should be gated on coupling quality, not elapsed time: Gee 2013 shows poor coupling can trigger premature consolidation, a failure mode the claim does not currently name (consolidating too early under a bad caregiver).
3. Phase (1) "innate coupling with a competent environment": the Sullivan work shows the innate coupling mechanism binds regardless of care quality; competence of the environment is a condition for a good outcome, not for coupling to happen. Worth making explicit.
4. Consider citing disinhibited social engagement (ERA, BEIP) as the concrete human analogue of "compromised social capabilities" -- a failure of selective trust, which is what phase (4)'s "trusted channel" needs.
5. The claim's second open lit-pull (play depends on secure attachment; Bowlby/Ainsworth secure base) was not covered here and remains open.
