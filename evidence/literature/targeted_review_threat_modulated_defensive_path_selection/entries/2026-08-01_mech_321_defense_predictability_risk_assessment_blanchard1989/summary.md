# Blanchard & Blanchard 1989 -- Attack and defense in rodents as ethoexperimental models for the study of emotion

**Source**: Blanchard RJ, Blanchard DC (1989). *Progress in Neuro-Psychopharmacology & Biological Psychiatry* 13(Suppl):S3-S14. [DOI 10.1016/0278-5846(89)90105-x](https://doi.org/10.1016/0278-5846(89)90105-x). PMID 2694228.

## What the paper did

This is a foundational, classical (1989) ethoexperimental review from the Blanchard lab, laying out a taxonomy of rodent offense and defense behavior — largely independent of, and predating, the Fanselow PIC vocabulary used in most of the rest of this pull, though later work in that lineage builds on and is consonant with it. It is included as an older, independent anchor rather than as a load-bearing primary source, precisely because convergence from a separate theoretical tradition strengthens confidence in the underlying pattern.

## Key findings relevant to the claim

Defensive behavior is shaped jointly by the threat stimulus itself and the surrounding situation. Discrete, approaching, PRESENT threat (an actual predator or conspecific closing in) and nondiscrete, POTENTIAL threat (ambiguous, not-yet-present) share several behaviors in common, but potential threat additionally recruits an important RISK-ASSESSMENT behavioral complex — apprehensive expectation, vigilance, scanning — that is specifically absent for discrete, present threat. This risk-assessment complex is behaviorally and functionally similar to generalized-anxiety symptomatology. The paper also notes that neuroanatomical and pharmacological manipulations frequently have different, sometimes opposite, effects on offense versus defense sub-components, underscoring that these are not a single undifferentiated system.

## How this translates to REE

This paper independently reinforces the design question's threat-features sub-question along a dimension distinct from raw magnitude: whether a threat is DISCRETE/present or NONDISCRETE/potential recruits a qualitatively different behavioral complex, not merely a difference in response intensity. For SD-hazard-aware-policy-decomposition, this is a second, independent line of support (alongside the Fanselow 2022 BST/predictability entry in this pull) for treating discreteness/certainty of the harm source as a candidate SECOND input dimension to the selection step, separate from raw `z_harm_a` magnitude — a mature implementation should plausibly be able to represent and respond differently to a diffuse, ambiguous harm signal (favoring broader, more exploratory candidate consideration, closer to REE's existing structural decompose/keep test) versus a discrete, confirmed harm signal (favoring narrower, more committed selection toward a specific low-harm tile).

## Limitations and caveats

This is a classical whole-organism behavioral taxonomy paper, not a study of any internal computational or circuit mechanism, and it predates the specific PIC terminology used elsewhere in this pull. Its relevance to REE's redecomposition-selection junction is conceptual — "discreteness/predictability of the threat source is a behaviorally consequential feature, distinct from magnitude" — rather than a mechanism or functional form that could be implemented directly.
