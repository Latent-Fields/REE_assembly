# Konrad, Firk & Uhlhaas (2013) -- the maturational imbalance behind adolescent risk-taking

According to PubMed ([DOI 10.3238/arztebl.2013.0425](https://doi.org/10.3238/arztebl.2013.0425), PMID 23840287), Konrad, Firk and Uhlhaas review adolescent brain development and land on a now-familiar picture with a precise ordering inside it. Gray-matter density peaks first in the primary sensorimotor cortex and last in the prefrontal cortex. Subcortical structures -- the limbic and reward systems in particular -- mature earlier still. The result during adolescence is a structural imbalance: mature subcortical drive sitting under an executive cortex that has not finished integrating. That imbalance, the authors argue, is what produces the signature adolescent profile of novelty-seeking, strong emotion, and risk-taking, at an age when sensory and motor competence is essentially adult.

## Why it earns a place beside the Sydnor review

INV-064 does not just claim an ordering; it claims the ordering unifies a specific set of otherwise-loose developmental facts, and the first on its list is "why adolescent risk-taking persists well past sensory-motor competence." This paper is that fact, stated in almost the same terms: E1/E2 (sensory, sensorimotor) are done, E3 (executive/PFC) is not, and the behavioural consequence follows from the gap. Crucially it comes from a different lab and tradition than the Sydnor/Satterthwaite S-A-axis work, so it gives the ordering independent corroboration rather than a second reading of the same dataset. It also restates INV-064's environmental-leverage prediction -- that the high plasticity of the adolescent brain lets experience bite unusually hard.

## Where I capped it

Two reasons this is a 0.66 support and not higher. It is a narrative review written for a general medical readership, not primary data, so its evidential weight is synthetic. And the mature-subcortical / immature-PFC imbalance model, while dominant, is not the only story for adolescent risk-taking -- incentive-processing and social-context accounts compete, and if risk-taking is really driven by incentive salience largely independent of the maturational lag, then INV-064's specific "because E3 is still integrating" reading loses this particular prop. As with the Sydnor entry, what is documented is ordering plus a behavioural correlate, not the necessity claim. Good, independent support for the sequence; not a test of why the sequence is the right one.
