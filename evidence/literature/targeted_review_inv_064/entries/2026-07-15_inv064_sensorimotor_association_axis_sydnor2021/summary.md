# Sydnor et al. (2021) -- the sensorimotor-to-association axis is the developmental curriculum INV-064 names

According to PubMed ([DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2021.06.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2021.06.016), PMID 34270921), Sydnor and colleagues synthesise a large body of human neuroimaging into a single organising idea: cortical development runs, over decades, along a sensorimotor-to-association (S-A) axis. Lower-order primary and unimodal sensory and motor cortices mature first; higher-order transmodal association cortices -- the ones doing executive, socioemotional and mentalizing work -- mature last, and carry a protracted plasticity window. That extended malleability is presented as a double-edged inheritance: it is what gives association cortex its distinctive functional richness, and it is also where developmental psychopathology gets its purchase.

## Why it is the anchor entry for INV-064

INV-064 claims that the E1 (sensory) / E2 (sensorimotor, world-model) to E3 (executive) maturational sequence is not an incidental constraint but the correct curriculum order for building a competent E3. The S-A axis is, as close as external neuroscience gets, a direct instantiation of that ordering. The lower-order tier settles first and becomes the scaffold; the executive/association tier integrates on top of it, last and longest.

The review also does independent work on two of INV-064's specific unifying predictions. First, that early-life disruption has outsized and specific effects on executive function rather than just emotional regulation -- here explained by the association tier owning the longest, most environmentally-sensitive plasticity window. Second, that intensive novel experience in childhood has disproportionate long-term cognitive returns -- consistent with protracted association plasticity being an extended sensitivity window rather than a fixed one. INV-064 predicted these should fall out of the ordering; this review shows they do.

## The honest boundary

What the review gives is ordering plus correlates, observationally. What INV-064 actually asserts is necessity -- that you could not build a competent E3 without E1/E2 maturing first. That is a counterfactual, and no amount of observing the human program in its natural order tests it. The "correct curriculum order" reading rests on the program's evolutionary conservation and on how costly its disruption is, not on a curriculum-ablation experiment. And the tier-to-E-stage mapping (sensorimotor -> E1/E2, association -> E3) is a functional analogy, not an architectural identity. I have scored it a strong support at 0.78 -- the ordering match is about as good as cross-domain evidence gets, but the load-bearing word "necessity" remains an inference the literature is consistent with rather than one it demonstrates. That gap is exactly what INV-064's experimental backlog (evidence_needed = experimental) should target: an in-silico curriculum-order ablation is the missing test.
