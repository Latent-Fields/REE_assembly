# Schmidt, Gagliardi & Redish 2026 -- "Just how goal-directed are hippocampal theta sweeps, anyway?"

This entry exists for a specific, deliberate reason: this repo's lit-pull discipline calls for a
skeptical non-degeneracy check alongside supporting evidence, not just a stack of papers that all
point the same way (SD-097's own lit-pull, cited as the model to follow, included Kinny &
Georgeff 1991 explicitly for this purpose -- a "bold" agent with no stored relational structure at
all that performs near-perfectly whenever the environment isn't highly dynamic). I went looking
for the equivalent counterweight to Yu et al. and Tang et al. (the other two supports entries in
this directory) and found this commentary, published in the same Nature Neuroscience issue.

Two independent WebSearch queries returned consistent paraphrase: the commentary frames hippocampal
theta-sweep interpretation as an live, unresolved controversy, not settled science -- planning is
"thought to be grounded in" these sequences, but "recent studies have suggested a simpler mechanism,
fundamentally incompatible with those planning hypotheses, underlies these sequences," and the two
empirical papers in the same issue are characterised as "first steps toward resolving that
controversy," not a resolution. That framing, on its own, is a real and useful corrective to reading
the Yu/Tang results as more settled than the field itself considers them.

I have to be honest about the limits of what I actually verified here, though: I could not reach the
full text. `nature.com` redirects every request to an authentication wall
(`idp.nature.com/authorize`), and unlike the two empirical papers, a commentary/News & Views piece
typically has no bioRxiv preprint or PMC mirror -- I found none. So everything in this entry rests on
search-engine paraphrase, corroborated across two separately-worded queries returning the same
substance, but never the primary text itself. I don't know what the specific "simpler mechanism" is
that the authors are pointing to, and I can't tell whether their skepticism targets the whole
goal-directed-planning framing or one specific analytical step within it. That is a materially
weaker evidentiary basis than the other three entries in this directory, and the confidence score
reflects that honestly rather than borrowing credibility from the venue or the author's reputation.

For REE, the practical use of this entry is as a flag, not a refutation: it says the biological
grounding this pull otherwise strengthens for MECH-236/MECH-289 should be held with the same caveat
the field itself is holding it with -- interesting, directionally supportive, but actively contested
at the mechanistic level. It does not touch REE's own architectural rationale for having *some*
destination-privileging and generative-traversal mechanism, which was motivated by design
requirements (SD-004, ARC-007) independent of how this specific neuroscience debate resolves.

**Confidence: 0.35.** Deliberately low. The venue and author credentials (A.D. Redish has a long
track record in hippocampal decision-making research) keep this from being negligible, but the
inability to read the primary text, combined with not knowing the specific competing mechanism
being proposed, means this entry cannot do more than flag that a real controversy exists --
following up by actually reading the full commentary (via institutional access, if available) is a
legitimate next step if a future session wants to sharpen this further.
