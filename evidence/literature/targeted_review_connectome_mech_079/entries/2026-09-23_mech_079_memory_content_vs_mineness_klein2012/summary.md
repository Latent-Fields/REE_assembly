# Content without mineness -- Klein and Nichols 2012

The registered audit disposition on MECH-079 is R7: phenomenological continuity
is conflated with choice persistence. That disposition was reached from inside
REE, by reading the claim against what V3 can measure. What makes this entry
worth the directory is that the clinical literature reaches the same verdict from
the outside, and does so with a case rather than an argument.

Klein and Nichols report patient R.B. During a defined period, R.B. could
retrieve highly specific autobiographical episodes -- the sort of one-off,
richly particular memory that is the paradigm of episodic recollection, being on
a beach in New London. The content was accurate. He knew the episodes were about
him and not about someone else; the self-indexing was intact. What was missing
was the first-personal sense that *I had these experiences* -- what the
authors call mineness, and what Tulving's tradition calls autonoetic awareness.
The content arrived correctly addressed and phenomenally unowned.

Put that against MECH-079's chain: same topology -> similar rollouts -> similar
commitments -> subjective identity persistence. Read the chain carefully and
everything before the last arrow is content and indexing. Stable geometry gives
you reproducible rollouts; reproducible rollouts give you persistent
context-conditioned commitments; persistent commitments give you a retrieved
autobiography that is correctly labelled as yours. R.B. had all of that and the
last arrow still failed. Whatever phenomenal ownership is, it is not what falls
out of content plus correct self-indexing -- and content plus correct
self-indexing is all that stable map geometry could ever deliver. The claim is
not thereby refuted; it is shown to be missing a term.

I want to be careful about how much this licenses, because the temptation is to
read it as showing that selfhood floats free of hippocampal geometry, and it
does not. It shows insufficiency, not irrelevance. Geometry may well be
necessary for ownership and simply not sufficient, and this case cannot
distinguish those. Nor do Klein and Nichols say anything about hippocampal maps:
they are arguing against memory-based theories of personal identity in the
philosophical sense, and they report no imaging or lesion localisation for R.B.
The bridge from their dissociation to MECH-079's substrate is REE's, and it
should be cited as REE's.

There is also an irony in the measurement problem that I think is the most
useful thing here. MECH-079's substrate note says there is no V3 recorder for
phenomenological selfhood, and that residue and anchor geometry are only
computational proxies. Klein and Nichols have the same problem from the other
direction: their only instrument for the presence or absence of mineness is
R.B.'s own report, and a sceptic can reasonably say he lost a metacognitive
judgement about his memories rather than their phenomenal ownership. Nothing in
the case settles that. So the honest summary is that the one construct MECH-079
needs to measure is one that even the clinical literature can only reach by
asking the patient -- which is a reason to doubt that a V3 null or positive on
map geometry could decide the claim as worded, and a reason to think the claim
should be split rather than tested. Confidence 0.71: high mapping fidelity
because the dissociation lands on the claim's own joint, held down by a source
base of exactly one self-reported case.
