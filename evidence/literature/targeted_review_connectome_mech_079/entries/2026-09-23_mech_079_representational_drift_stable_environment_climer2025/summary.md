# Representational drift in a held-constant world -- Climer et al. 2025

MECH-079 carries its weight in one adjective. The claim is not that hippocampal
map geometry is involved in selfhood; it is that phenomenological continuous
selfhood is an artefact of *stable* map geometry, by way of the chain same
topology -> similar rollouts -> similar commitments -> subjective identity
persistence. So the interesting question to put to the literature is not
whether the hippocampus builds maps -- it plainly does -- but whether the map
is the stable thing in the system. Climer and colleagues say it is not.

They imaged CA1 place cells in head-fixed mice navigating a virtual linear
environment across days, and they did the thing the earlier drift literature was
fairly criticised for not doing: they held the world still. The environment was
richly multisensory and highly reproducible, and the behaviour was matched, so
that a changing code could not be excused as a map correctly tracking a changing
world. The representations drifted anyway. Then they went looking for what
predicted drift, and the answer was not anything about the environment: it was
the intrinsic excitability of the individual cell, with more excitable cells
drifting less. Drift, in other words, is generated inside the substrate.

The consequence for MECH-079 is not that the claim is wrong but that its first
premise is doing work it cannot support. If the biological map does not hold
still under constant input, then a mechanism that explains self-continuity by
geometric persistence is explaining a stable output by a substrate that is not
stable. The honest REE reading is that continuity, if it lives in map geometry at
all, must live in something that survives drift -- a re-registration process, a
realignment against slower anchors, a coarse topology preserved while the
population vector rotates underneath it. Each of those is a *different*
mechanism from the one the claim names, and each would need its own falsifier.

There is a retreat available to the claim, and I want to state it rather than
pretend the paper closes the question. MECH-079 says *topology*; Climer et al.
measure population-code similarity. A code can rotate substantially while the
coarse topology -- the arrangement of basins, the neighbourhood structure -- is
preserved. Nothing here rules that out. But the retreat is a narrowing, not a
free move: it commits the claim to specifying which geometric invariant is
supposed to be the carrier of continuity, and to measuring that invariant rather
than the code. That is a considerably more demanding claim than the one
currently registered, and the second-order point is that the registered wording
does not distinguish the two.

I have set confidence at 0.74. The source is strong and the control is the right
one, so I have little doubt about the finding. What holds the number down is the
distance between what was measured and what is claimed: mouse CA1 navigation
across days, versus human phenomenal self-continuity. The paper is silent on
selfhood and offers no phenomenological readout, which is precisely the gap
MECH-079's own audit disposition (R7) already flags. This entry weakens the
claim's substrate premise; it does not touch the claim's phenomenal terminus,
and it should not be cited as though it did.
