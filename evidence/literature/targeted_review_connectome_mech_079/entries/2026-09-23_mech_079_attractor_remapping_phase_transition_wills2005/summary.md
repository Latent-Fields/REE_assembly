# Discrete transitions are real -- Wills et al. 2005

MECH-079 is easier to assess if it is read as two claims rather than one. The
phenomenal claim -- that subjective identity persistence is an artefact of map
stability -- is the one the audit flagged R7 and the one the other entries in this
directory press on. But the notes also contain a structural sub-claim that stands
on its own: that identity-level change is a *phase transition* in map geometry, a
discontinuous restructuring of the attractor-basin profile rather than a gradual
slide. That sub-claim is about dynamics, it is substrate-checkable, and this is
the paper that checks it.

Wills, Lever, Cacucci, Burgess and O'Keefe familiarised rats with a square and a
circular enclosure, then recorded CA1 place cells across a morph sequence of
intermediate shapes. If the hippocampal representation of environmental geometry
were an interpolating code, the intermediate shapes should have produced
intermediate representations. They did not. The population switched abruptly and
coherently -- the whole ensemble moving together, at a critical point in the
sequence, between the square-like and the circle-like configuration. That is the
signature behaviour of a system with discrete attractors and pattern completion
toward stored states, and it is about as clean a demonstration of it as this
literature has.

So the claim's dynamical vocabulary earns its keep. When MECH-079 speaks of
basins, of discontinuous restructuring, of a profile that reorganises rather than
drifts, it is not borrowing a metaphor from physics; it is naming a property the
substrate demonstrably has. For REE this matters practically, because it says the
right shape for an identity-change mechanism is a bistable or multistable one with
hysteresis, not a continuously-parameterised one -- and that is a design
constraint a builder can use.

Two honest qualifications, and the second is the more interesting. First, what
is switching here is a representation of enclosure shape, in rats, over minutes.
Nothing self-referential, autobiographical or identity-related was measured. The
support extends to the *form* of the transition and not one step further toward
the proposition that such a transition constitutes a change of self. Second --
and this is a tension I would rather record than smooth over -- this entry sits
awkwardly beside the Climer 2025 entry in the same directory. A system with strong
pattern completion toward stored states is a system that *corrects* endogenous
noise; Climer et al. show noise accumulating steadily instead, in a held-constant
environment, across days. Both results are well-supported. The resolution is
presumably one of timescale: fast within-session settling into a basin, slow
across-day reorganisation of where the basins are. But MECH-079 as worded does not
say which timescale is supposed to carry identity, and so it cannot claim support
from both. Being specific about that is, I think, the most useful thing this
directory can hand back to governance.

Confidence 0.63. The source is excellent and the finding is foundational; what
holds the number down is mapping fidelity at 0.45, because the paper speaks to one
adjective in the claim's notes and is silent on everything else in it.
