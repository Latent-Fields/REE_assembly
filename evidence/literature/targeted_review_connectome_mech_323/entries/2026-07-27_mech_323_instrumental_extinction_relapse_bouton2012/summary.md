# Bouton, Winterbauer & Todd 2012 -- Relapse processes after the extinction of instrumental learning: renewal, resurgence, and reacquisition

Barnes et al. 2005 gives the striatal-substrate half of the dissolution-asymmetry case. This gives the behavioural half, and specifically the INSTRUMENTAL half, which is the reason to prefer it over Bouton's better-known Pavlovian reviews. MECH-323 chunks actions, not cues; if the asymmetry only held for stimulus-outcome associations it would be much weaker evidence than it looks.

The review pulls together three relapse effects following extinction of an operant response. In renewal, responding returns when the animal is tested in a context different from the one extinction happened in. In resurgence, responding recovers when an alternative response -- reinforced during extinction of the first -- is itself extinguished. In rapid reacquisition, extinguished responding returns far faster than it was originally acquired when reinforcement resumes. The three converge on one conclusion: extinction "weakens behavior without erasing the original learning", and what it actually installs is new, context-dependent learning stored alongside the old rather than replacing it.

For MECH-323 and MECH-324 this does two things. It establishes that the F_low < F_high asymmetry is a real property of ACTION learning at the level REE operates on, which together with Barnes takes the inequality from asserted to reasonably grounded. And it sharpens what the dissolution operator should do: install a competing, context-gated suppression alongside the chunk rather than deleting it. That is the same structural correction Barnes implies from the neural side, arrived at independently from the behavioural side, which makes me more confident in it than in either source alone.

The payoff is three falsifiers MECH-323/324 do not currently have, and rapid reacquisition is the cheapest to implement and the sharpest. If dissolution is suppression-with-retention, then re-forming a previously dissolved chunk should require far fewer than `R_min` repetitions -- a single-parameter prediction with an obvious experiment behind it. Renewal predicts that a chunk dissolved in one context should still fire in another, which interacts directly with the `initiation_set` field MECH-323 already carries: if dissolution is context-blind while formation is context-conditioned, REE cannot show renewal at all. Resurgence is the awkward one, because it requires a chunk to return not because its own evidence improved but because a competitor was extinguished -- that needs dissolution state shared across candidates in the library, which nothing in the current registration produces.

Marked `supports` at confidence 0.68. Mapping fidelity sits above Barnes (0.65 against 0.55) because the object of learning is instrumental and the asymmetry is measured behaviourally at the level MECH-323 operates on; source quality sits below Barnes (0.78 against 0.92) because this is a review of the authors' own laboratory programme rather than a primary report, though renewal and resurgence are widely replicated outside it. Two limits are worth stating plainly. These are single-response operant contingencies -- a rat pressing a lever for food -- so the extinguished object is much simpler than a composed chunked primitive, and whether a multi-element SEQUENCE extinguishes with the same asymmetry is extrapolation rather than evidence. And as with Barnes, nothing here quantifies a variance threshold. F_high = 0.45 gains no numeric support from this entry either. What both entries support is the inequality and the retention-not-erasure character of dissolution; the number remains an engineering default awaiting calibration.
