# Bo, Borza & Seidler 2009 -- Age-related declines in visuospatial working memory correlate with deficits in explicit motor sequence learning

If Sakai 2003 tells us that chunks are typically two to four items and occasionally five, the obvious next question is why that number and not some other. Bo et al. give the most satisfying available answer: chunk length tracks visuospatial working-memory capacity. Their earlier work established the correlation in young adults; this paper extends it to older adults, who show the same capacity-to-chunk-length relationship and a joint reduction in both quantities relative to the young.

That converts the chunk-size budget from a primitive into a derived quantity, which is a better position for MECH-323 to be in even though it complicates the claim. The 2-5 range is not a fact about the chunking machinery; it is the shadow that a capacity bottleneck casts on the chunking machinery. This explains why the number lands near the classic short-term capacity range rather than anywhere else, and it gives MECH-323 a principled place to read the budget from -- whatever plays the capacity role in REE, meaning the number of policy primitives the agent can hold jointly while evaluating a candidate sub-sequence -- rather than configuring it independently.

The cost of that reframing is the same one Ramkumar et al. impose from the other direction: a budget hard-coded at 2-5 for every agent asserts a uniformity the human data specifically contradicts. Individuals differ, and the same individual's chunk length declines with age as capacity declines. Taken together the two papers say the budget should be neither fixed across agents nor fixed within an agent's lifetime, which is a fairly strong constraint on how MECH-323 should expose the parameter.

One dissociation in the results is worth carrying forward because it bears on a different parameter. The capacity-to-learning-RATE correlation held in young adults but not in older ones, while the capacity-to-chunk-LENGTH correlation held in both. Chunk size and formation rate are therefore separable, which means MECH-323's `R_min` (repetition count, default 20) and its size budget should not be calibrated as though one underlying quantity governed both. That is a small point but it is exactly the kind of coupling assumption that gets baked into a calibration sweep without anyone deciding to make it.

Marked `mixed` at confidence 0.62 -- supporting the magnitude by explaining its origin, weakening its status as an architectural constant. Two caveats hold the confidence down. The design is correlational, so capacity and chunk length covary without the paper showing that capacity SETS chunk length, and general processing speed declines with age too and is not excluded as a third factor. More importantly for REE, the task is EXPLICIT motor sequence learning, which recruits declarative and working-memory resources that MECH-323's DLS-analog procedural accumulator is specifically not supposed to depend on -- the whole point of the Graybiel repetition-and-consistency framing is that it is the habit path, not the deliberate one. So there is a live possibility that this capacity constraint belongs to a different system than the one being modelled, and that importing it imports the wrong system's limit. I have treated the paper as an explanation of where the range comes from rather than as independent confirmation of the numbers two and five.
