# Wessel & Aron 2017 -- global motor suppression and the shape of the interrupt threshold

**Claim tested:** Q-090 -- does the admission criterion for retaining a predicted-but-not-acted-on alternative (MECH-485 leg 3) share the interrupt's cut-scale at a lower cut-point, or does it have an independent relevance criterion such as goal-match?

**Direction:** supports the same-cut-scale horn. **Confidence: 0.5.**

## What the paper does

This is a synthesis, not an experiment. Wessel and Aron take three literatures that had grown up separately -- action errors, unexpected action outcomes, and unexpected perceptual events -- and argue they are all doing the same thing to behaviour. Each produces motor slowing and cognitive distraction, and each, on their account, does so by recruiting a common fronto-basal-ganglia network for stopping: prefrontal nodes projecting to the subthalamic nucleus, with a global suppressive effect on basal-ganglia output. The strong word in the title is *globality*. The suppression is not aimed at the offending effector or the offending representation; it is broadcast, which is why an unexpected sound can cost you the item you were holding in working memory.

## Why it bears on Q-090

Q-090 is a question about what kind of quantity the triage in MECH-485 is cutting. The same-scale reading says there is one predicted-harm magnitude with two thresholds on it -- interrupt high, retain low. The independent-criterion reading says retention is picked out by something else entirely, goal-match in the MECH-292/293 sense, which could admit a small-magnitude alternative that happens to matter and reject a large one that does not.

What this paper contributes is a fact about the *upper* cut. If the interrupt is recruited by a mechanism that is common across event types and global in its effect, then the interrupt is not consulting content. It is reading a scalar and acting on it. That is exactly the architecture the same-scale horn presupposes, and it is worth knowing that the biology appears to have built it that way rather than building a content-addressed interrupt. A single scale with two cut-points is a coherent thing to ask for, partly because the top cut-point demonstrably works like that.

That is also the honest limit of the contribution. The paper says nothing whatsoever about what happens to the events that *fail* to reach the interrupt threshold. It does not follow them into memory, does not ask whether they are retained, and offers no reason to think the retention machinery reads the same scalar. To take this as evidence for the same-scale horn is to assume the disputed premise. So I have recorded it as support with a deliberately suppressed mapping fidelity: it shows the same-scale horn is buildable and biologically plausible on its interrupt side, not that it is the correct reading of leg 3.

## Caveats and where the mapping strains

Three things trouble the translation, and the third is the one I would watch.

First, the global character of the suppression cuts slightly against the very reading it supports. If one and the same signal both interrupted and admitted-to-memory, and the signal is content-indifferent, then retention would be indiscriminate -- everything mildly surprising gets kept. That is the failure mode a goal-match criterion exists to prevent, and it is a reason to suspect the retention leg has *some* additional selectivity even if it does read the same magnitude.

Second, this is a review. The convergent-evidence style is a genuine strength for establishing that a common mechanism exists, and a genuine weakness for establishing any quantitative claim about thresholds. Q-090 is ultimately quantitative -- it asks about cut-points on a scale -- and a review cannot settle that.

Third, and most important: the paper's driving variable is *unexpectedness*, and MECH-485's is *predicted-harm magnitude*. In stop-signal and oddball paradigms these travel together, because the harmful thing is the surprising thing. They come apart in the case REE actually cares about, where a fully anticipated large harm should still interrupt a committed trajectory. If REE's magnitude term is genuinely about harm rather than about surprise, then the mechanism this paper describes may be an analogue of something adjacent to leg 1 rather than of leg 1 itself, and the support weakens accordingly.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality is high (0.85) and does not drive the number. Mapping fidelity is what does: at 0.4 it records that the paper addresses the interrupt leg while Q-090 asks about the retention leg, so every inference to the claim passes through an unverified bridge. Transfer risk sits at 0.45 for the human-laboratory-to-agent leap compounded by the surprise/harm substitution. Weighting mapping fidelity heavily -- appropriate here, since Q-090 is an architectural question and the empirical facts are not in dispute -- gives 0.5. That is deliberately a "this horn is coherent" number rather than a "this horn is right" number.
