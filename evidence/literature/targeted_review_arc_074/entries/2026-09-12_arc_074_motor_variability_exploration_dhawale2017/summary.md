# Dhawale, Smith & Olveczky 2017 -- the variability literature supports less than it looks like it does

This is the canonical review of motor variability's functional role, and its thesis is the one
ARC-074 leans on: trial-to-trial variability is not the unwanted consequence of a noisy nervous
system but a feature of how sensorimotor systems operate and learn. Rooted in reinforcement learning
theory, the view equates motor variability with purposeful exploration of motor space that, *when
coupled with reinforcement*, drives motor learning. The review surveys the circuit mechanisms that
generate and regulate it across humans and animal models.

I have marked this `mixed` and given it the second-lowest confidence in the pull, and I want to be
explicit that this is a judgement about ARC-074 rather than about the paper, which is excellent.
ARC-074 makes two claims that are easy to run together and should not be. The first is that
structured exploratory variability is functional machinery, actively generated by dedicated circuitry
rather than noise to be minimised. That claim this review establishes about as firmly as a literature
can. The second is that exploration must be temporally *prior to* and *decoupled from* any
reward-weighted scoring -- that a reward-free epoch has to complete before E3 comes on. That claim
this review does not address, and the clause "when coupled with reinforcement" sits in mild tension
with it.

The tension deserves stating rather than leaving implicit, because it points somewhere uncomfortable.
The review documents variability being actively *regulated* by reinforcement signals -- the system
tunes how much it explores based on how learning is going. If that regulation is part of how
exploration does its work, then a Phase 0 that suppresses reward-weighted scoring entirely has
removed the very signal biology appears to use to shape exploration. Read at its least favourable,
this body of work is a mild argument *against* a fully reward-free prologue and in favour of a
reward-*attenuated* one. I do not think that reading is forced, but ARC-074's description cites the
pre-reward ordering as though the developmental and variability literatures straightforwardly
supply it, and on this review's evidence they do not.

This matters for how the claim gets adjudicated. ARC-074's `what_would_answer` already contains an
unusually careful cross-reference warning that the claim must not be read as a rediscovery of the
MECH-439 / INV-088 / MECH-457 conversion-ceiling findings. The complementary risk is the one this
entry records: that the supporting literature gets read as establishing the ordering prediction when
it establishes only the functional-exploration premise underneath it. The ordering is the novel part
of ARC-074 and it remains, on the literature, unevidenced.

Confidence 0.58. Source quality is high at 0.88 -- Annual Review of Neuroscience, authoritative
authors, and I would cite it without hesitation for what it actually says. Mapping fidelity 0.55 is
deliberately low and is the whole reason the entry is here: it records that the most obvious
supporting literature for ARC-074 supports a weaker proposition than the claim makes. Transfer risk
0.35; secondary source, primary data surveyed rather than reported.
