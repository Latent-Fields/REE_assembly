# Ndousse, Eck, Levine & Jaques (2021), *Emergent Social Learning via Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning* -- ARC-047

## What the paper does

The question is whether an RL agent, dropped into an environment that happens to contain competent others, will learn to exploit them as a source of information. Not imitation learning -- the novice never sees the expert's trajectory or observations, only its behaviour from a third-person view in a shared partially observed gridworld, and the expert has no incentive to teach. The environments are built on gym-minigrid: Goal Cycle, 13x13, where a set of goals must be visited in an unknown cyclic order and guessing wrong is penalised, and Four Rooms, 17x17, used for zero-shot transfer. The authors deliberately stack the deck in favour of social learning: individual exploration is made expensive, and each agent's colour encodes a decaying prestige value derived from its accumulated reward, so a novice can see at a glance which other agent is worth watching.

Vanilla model-free RL agents did not take the hint. The paper's phrase is that they "do not use social learning" in most circumstances, and it holds even in the environment purpose-built to make social learning pay. The rescue is architectural: a model-based auxiliary loss (SociAPL) that forces the agent to model transition dynamics in the presence of others. With it, agents exploit expert cues, outperform the experts they trained alongside, and transfer zero-shot to new environments with unfamiliar experts -- while agents that never learned to rely on social cues generalise poorly.

## Why this is the paper ARC-047 most needs to have read

ARC-047 proposes SocialGridWorld as the minimal substrate for empirically testing ARC-010 mirror modelling, MECH-041 affective expression and MECH-031 other-selflike inference, on the reasoning that these claims currently have no test substrate and this is the smallest environment that could generate evidence. That reasoning is sound as far as it goes. What this paper supplies is the part ARC-047 has not accounted for: someone has already built approximately this substrate, including approximately this design idea, and found that the substrate alone does not produce the phenomenon.

The correspondence is closer than a family resemblance. ARC-047's central design move is that affective state leaks involuntarily into the world as scent channels -- a scalar derived from the agent's internal state, rendered into other agents' observations as a perceptual field. The prestige cue here is exactly that object: an internally-derived scalar, involuntarily broadcast, rendered as agent colour, put there specifically so that observers could identify whose behaviour carried information. It was not enough. That is not an argument against ARC-047's design; it is an argument that ARC-047's design does not, on its own, constitute a test.

The consequence is concrete and it is about experimental validity rather than architecture. If REE builds SocialGridWorld and runs model-free agents in it, the most likely outcome is that no social reading emerges, and that null result will be indistinguishable from this paper's null result. It will not be evidence against ARC-010. It will be a re-measurement of a known property of the training regime. An experiment whose null outcome is uninformative is not yet a test, and ARC-047 as currently written does not carry the positive control -- a condition known to produce social reading -- that would make the null interpretable.

## The defence available to REE, and why it is not yet usable

There is a real reply here, which is that REE agents are not the agents that failed. What SociAPL had to bolt on was a model-based objective requiring the agent to predict transition dynamics in the presence of others -- and a REE instance already carries predictive machinery of that general shape. It is entirely possible that REE agents sit on the right side of this result by construction, and that the failure reported here is a property of generic model-free policies that simply does not apply. I think that is the most likely resolution. But it is a hypothesis about REE's architecture, not an observation, and it is exactly the kind of assumption that should be tested before a substrate is built on top of it rather than after. If ARC-047 is going to rely on it, it should be stated as a dependency, and ideally probed cheaply in a single-channel version before the seven-channel environment is built.

## What it does not settle

The prestige cue is one channel carrying competence; ARC-047 specifies seven carrying wanting, seeking, alarm, harm stress, direction, celebration and defence. Nothing here says whether seven simultaneous Gaussian gradient fields are learnable or simply raise the observation's noise floor, and that is a real risk that scales with channel count. The authors also limit their claim explicitly to exploratory navigation, where the effective social strategy is following -- and ARC-047's target behaviours are not following. They are harm avoidance under a predator, counterfactual other-cost, and voluntary defence, which are structurally different problems where the informative signal is another agent's distress rather than another agent's competence.

## Confidence

0.78, direction mixed. It supports ARC-047's premise that a gridworld with broadcast internal state is a buildable and reasonable substrate -- these authors built one and it worked as an environment. It weakens the word doing the work in the claim, which is *minimal*: minimal for producing the behaviour, this substrate is not. The confidence is high for a mapping this specific and is held under 0.85 only by the navigation-task scope limit and by the open question of whether REE's own architecture is exempt.
