# Leibo et al. (2021), *Scalable Evaluation of Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning with Melting Pot* -- ARC-047

## What the paper does

Melting Pot is a benchmark, and its motivating complaint is about evaluation rather than about agents. Existing multi-agent RL suites, the authors argue, do not make generalisation to novel *social* situations their primary target -- they measure how well a set of agents does in the configuration it was trained in. The suite's answer is a collection of 2D gridworld substrates built on DeepMind Lab2D, with over eighty held-out test scenarios covering social dilemmas, reciprocity, resource sharing and task partitioning, in which a trained agent is dropped in among background populations it has never met. The design exploits a fact that is obvious once stated and consequential once taken seriously: one agent's behaviour constitutes part of another agent's environment, so novel co-players are novel environments, and they can be generated cheaply. The headline empirical observation is that standard MARL algorithms show weaknesses under this evaluation that their training performance does not reveal.

## What it gives ARC-047

Chiefly, it retires a question. ARC-047 asserts that a multi-agent gridworld is an adequate substrate for empirically studying cooperation, other-modelling and affective expression, and someone might reasonably ask whether that is wishful -- whether social phenomena of the kind REE cares about can appear at all in an environment that small and that discrete. Melting Pot is a strong answer that they can. A well-resourced group converged on precisely this environment class for precisely this class of question, and the suite has been widely adopted since. The feasibility premise underneath ARC-047 is not speculative, and it is worth having that on the record from a benchmark that people actually use rather than from an assertion in a claim registry.

What it takes back is aimed at one word. ARC-047 says SocialGridWorld is the *minimal* substrate. The same group that validated the environment class also judged that characterising social behaviour required many substrates and eighty-odd held-out scenarios, and their reason is directly relevant: behaviour that looks social in the configuration it was trained in frequently is not, and the only way to tell is to change the co-players. That is a methodological constraint on any ARC-047 experiment, not a refutation of the claim. But it bites specifically on what ARC-047 wants the substrate for. ARC-010 is a claim about a *capability* -- that the agent models others' internal states -- and a capability claim cannot be settled by an outcome measured against one fixed set of partners, because a policy fitted to those particular partners produces the same outcome. If SocialGridWorld is built, held-out co-player populations should be in it from the start, not added later when someone asks.

## What it does not give

Nothing about mechanism, and I want to be careful not to let the entry drift into implying otherwise. Melting Pot's scenarios score behavioural outcomes -- did they cooperate, did they share, did they reciprocate -- and are deliberately agnostic about what produced them. That agnosticism is exactly the thing ARC-010 and MECH-031 are about: whether the agent tagged the other as another agent and modelled its state, or merely learned a policy that happens to be conditioned on the other's pixels. There is no affective-leakage channel here, no involuntary broadcast of internal state, no predator, and the agents are generic deep-RL policies. The seven-scent design that is ARC-047's actual content is untouched by this paper.

## Confidence

0.6, direction mixed, with mapping fidelity deliberately set low at 0.5. The temptation with a paper like this is to count a well-known benchmark in the right environment class as support for a claim that proposes an environment in that class, and that would be crediting ARC-047 for work that never addresses it. What it honestly supports is the feasibility premise; what it honestly warns about is single-configuration evaluation; and on the substance of the claim -- that affective leakage as gradient scent fields is what makes social cognition testable -- it is silent.
