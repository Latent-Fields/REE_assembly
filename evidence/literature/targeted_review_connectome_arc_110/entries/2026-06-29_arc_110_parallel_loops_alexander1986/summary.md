# Alexander, DeLong & Strick (1986) — Parallel organization of functionally segregated circuits linking basal ganglia and cortex

**Claim tested:** ARC-110 (parallel segregated cortico-BG-thalamic loops; motor / associative / limbic)
**Direction:** supports · **Confidence:** 0.82
**Source:** *Annual Review of Neuroscience* 9:357–381. According to PubMed (PMID 3085570), [DOI 10.1146/annurev.ne.09.030186.002041](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.ne.09.030186.002041).

## What the paper did

This is the foundational synthesis — the one ARC-110's own title names as its translation target. Alexander, DeLong and Strick pulled together primate tract-tracing and single-unit recording to argue that the basal ganglia and cortex are wired not as one convergent funnel but as a family of parallel, re-entrant loops. Each loop originates in a distinct cortical field, passes through its own territory of striatum, then pallidum/substantia nigra, then a specific thalamic relay, and returns to (near) its cortical origin. They identify a motor circuit, an oculomotor circuit, a dorsolateral-prefrontal ("associative"/cognitive-set) circuit, and a limbic/anterior-cingulate circuit. The load-bearing assertion is *segregation*: the loops keep their information largely separate as they traverse the subcortex, rather than collapsing into a shared arena.

## Why it bears on ARC-110

ARC-110 proposes that committed-action selection in REE should run through separate striatal loop channels — motor (F), associative (dACC+lPFC), limbic (OFC+liking) — each with its own eligibility-and-gate, arbitrated only *after* within-loop competition. The hypothesis is that the F-dominance conversion ceiling (MECH-439, F monopolising 88–89% of committed-selection variance) is partly an artefact of collapsing every channel into one F-dominated arena. Alexander/DeLong/Strick is precisely the biological warrant for that decomposition: if the brain's own committed-selection substrate is organised as parallel segregated channels rather than one shared competition, then REE's single-arena E3 selector is the architectural outlier, and the segregated-loop refactor is the brain-faithful move. This is a clean instance of the ARC-106 grounding principle — follow biology by function where it disambiguates an underdetermined design fork.

## What it does *not* establish (the honest boundary)

The paper grounds the *organisational* claim — the loops exist and are segregated — but not the *functional* claim ARC-110 actually rests on, namely that selecting through separate loops *relieves* the F-dominance ceiling. Alexander et al. never run a behaving-agent selection experiment in which collapsing-vs-segregating channels changes which action wins; that is REE's hypothesis, to be settled by the validation experiment V3-EXQ-707, not by 1986 anatomy. So the mapping is faithful at the level of "build N parallel loops" and silent at the level of "and therefore F stops drowning the limbic computation." I have kept the confidence at 0.82 rather than higher for exactly this reason: source quality is near-ceiling, but the mapping covers the architecture, not the predicted benefit.

There is also a subtler caveat worth recording. A strict, no-cross-talk reading of "segregated" is stronger than even this paper licenses — Alexander et al. describe macro-scale segregation while leaving the degree of inter-loop integration open. That open question is the subject of the companion entry in this directory (Haber 2003), which documents directional limbic→cognitive→motor information flow through the striatonigrostriatal spiral. Read together, the two papers say: the channels are real and separable (supports ARC-110's decomposition), but pure isolation is an idealisation (a caveat on any implementation that hard-partitions the loops with zero cross-channel routing). For REE that argues for segregated loops *with* a constrained, directional integration stage rather than fully independent arenas.
