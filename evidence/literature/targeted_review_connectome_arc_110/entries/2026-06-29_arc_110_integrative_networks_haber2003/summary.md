# Haber (2003) — The primate basal ganglia: parallel and integrative networks

**Claim tested:** ARC-110 (parallel segregated cortico-BG-thalamic loops; motor / associative / limbic)
**Direction:** mixed · **Confidence:** 0.78
**Source:** *Journal of Chemical Neuroanatomy* 26(4):317–330. According to PubMed (PMID 14729134), [DOI 10.1016/j.jchemneu.2003.10.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jchemneu.2003.10.003).

## Why this paper is in the file as a deliberate counterweight

It would be too easy to assemble a literature record for ARC-110 out of nothing but the Alexander/DeLong/Strick parallel-loops picture and call the claim "supported." Haber (2003) is here precisely to test whether the strong reading of ARC-110 — separate channels arbitrated only after within-loop competition — is what the primate substrate actually does. The answer is *yes and no*, and the "no" is the more interesting half.

## What the paper argues

Haber starts from the same parallel organisation: limbic, cognitive (associative), and motor cortico-basal-ganglia pathways. But her contribution is to ask the question the parallel model deliberately brackets — *how does information get from one circuit to another so that new learned behaviours can be assembled from emotional, cognitive, and motor inputs?* Her answer is two anatomical networks that move information across circuits: the striato-nigro-striatal (SNS) network and the thalamo-cortico-thalamic network. Within each, reciprocal connections bind regions of like function, but *non-reciprocal, feedforward* connections link regions belonging to *different* circuits. Crucially the cascade has a direction: information is channelled limbic → cognitive → motor. The upshot, in her words, is that action decision-making is influenced by motivation and cognition rather than executed by an isolated motor channel.

## How that maps to ARC-110 — and where it cuts against the naive design

On the supporting side: Haber accepts the limbic/cognitive/motor decomposition as the correct starting architecture, which is exactly what ARC-110 wants to build. So the decomposition itself is well-grounded.

On the qualifying side — and this is the load-bearing caveat — the brain does *not* run these as fully isolated arenas. There is a directional integration stage by which the limbic "is this worth committing to" signal propagates forward into the motor channel. For REE this has a concrete design consequence. ARC-110's hypothesis is that the F-dominance ceiling is an artefact of the single-arena collapse, and that segregation lets F dominate only the motor loop while the limbic loop computes its own value. Haber's anatomy says: yes, separate the loops — but couple them with a *constrained, directional* limbic→cognitive→motor routing, not zero cross-talk. A pure-partition implementation (N independent competitions, no inter-loop routing) would diverge from the primate substrate. And it raises a sharper possibility worth flagging to whoever runs V3-EXQ-707: the predicted F-dominance relief may depend as much on getting the *integration* stage right (so limbic value actually reaches the committed motor action) as on the separation itself. Separation without a working limbic→motor conduit could just re-isolate the very computation ARC-110 is trying to let influence commitment.

## Limitations and why the direction is "mixed"

This is anatomy: Haber infers directional information flow from tract-tracing topography, not from a causal experiment showing the SNS spiral changes which action is committed. Mapping "information can flow limbic→motor" onto "limbic value reaches the committed action" is a functional reading of structure. The transfer from a primate spiral to an RL selection head is structural analogy, scoped — as ARC-106 requires — to function rather than homology. I have logged the direction as *mixed* rather than *supports* because the paper simultaneously endorses the decomposition and contradicts the strongest (no-cross-talk) version of it; the net effect on ARC-110 is to refine the build, not simply to confirm it. Confidence 0.78 reflects strong source quality, good fidelity for the specific caveat, and a moderate-to-high transfer risk on the anatomy→function step.
