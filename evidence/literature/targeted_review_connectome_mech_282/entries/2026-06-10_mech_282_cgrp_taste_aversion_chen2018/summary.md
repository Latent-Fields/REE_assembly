# Parabrachial CGRP neurons and aversive taste memory (Chen et al., 2018)

According to PubMed, Chen, Campos, Jarvie and Palmiter (Neuron, 2018; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2018.09.032)) provide what is probably the cleanest causal account of how a body-origin distress signal becomes a learned aversion. When a novel taste is paired with sickness, an animal learns to avoid it -- the conditioned taste aversion (CTA), the textbook interoceptive-malaise paradigm. The authors show that CGRP neurons in the parabrachial nucleus are both necessary and sufficient for establishing this aversion. Photoactivating their projections to either the central amygdala or the bed nucleus of the stria terminalis is enough to induce a robust CTA on its own; the neurons undergo plasticity after conditioning; and reactivation by the conditioned food later drives expression of the memory, which can be attenuated by inhibiting the neurons or disrupting consolidation genes (Arc, Grin1).

This matters for MECH-282 because the REE claim explicitly names "visceral malaise" and "taste-aversion analogs" as the interoceptive distress signals the LPB analog routes into harm-arbitration. Chen et al. is the direct demonstration that exactly those signals travel a PBN-CGRP -> CeA path and write an aversive valuation downstream. Where Kang et al. (2022) anchor the *broadcast* function of the arm with multi-sensory innate threat, this paper anchors the specifically *interoceptive* instance -- the body-state side of the threat-side architecture that distinguishes MECH-282 from a purely exteroceptive predator-threat account.

But the paper also surfaces the sharpest tension in MECH-282, and I think the governance record should state it plainly. The CGRP -> CeA projection that carries this interoceptive malaise signal is, on the evidence of the companion literature, the *same* conduit that carries exteroceptive innate threat. MECH-282's stronger commitment is not merely that interoception is routed into arbitration -- it is that interoceptive harm has a *separable* input channel, dissociable from external-threat z_harm. A single shared CGRP -> CeA arm argues against that separability being located in the arm itself. The honest reading is that if separability exists, it more likely lives *upstream* -- in input-specific parabrachial subpopulations feeding the CGRP relay -- than in the CGRP projection MECH-282 currently foregrounds. That is a refinement the claim should absorb rather than a refutation.

A second caveat: this study establishes the route for taste-aversion/visceral malaise specifically. The other interoceptive insults MECH-282 lists -- hypoxia and hypoglycemia -- are not tested here, though they are plausible PBN inputs on independent grounds.

I score this 0.8. Source quality is very high (a landmark Palmiter-lab study with sufficiency, necessity, and memory-expression all demonstrated). I hold mapping fidelity in the mid-0.7s and the aggregate at 0.8 because, while the interoceptive-routing claim is strongly supported, the shared-conduit finding actively complicates the separability claim that is the more distinctive and more vulnerable part of MECH-282.
