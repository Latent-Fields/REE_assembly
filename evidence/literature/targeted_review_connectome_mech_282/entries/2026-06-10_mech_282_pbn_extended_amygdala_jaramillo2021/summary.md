# Parabrachial-extended amygdala circuits for danger and distress (Jaramillo et al., 2021)

According to PubMed, Jaramillo, Brown and Winder (Neuropharmacology, 2021; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropharm.2021.108757)) offer a synthesis that reads almost as a prose version of MECH-282's architectural intent. Their organising move is to treat the parabrachial nucleus not as a single alarm but as a relay whose downstream effect depends on which extended-amygdala target it addresses. PBN -> central amygdala (CeA) projections, they argue, modulate innate responses to physical stimuli and fear conditioning; PBN -> bed-nucleus-of-the-stria-terminalis (BNST) projections modulate distress in unpredictable contexts. The framing sentence is the one MECH-282 effectively borrows: the PBN provides a pathway for alarming interoceptive *and* exteroceptive stimuli to be processed and relayed to the extended amygdala to induce stress-relevant affect.

For MECH-282 this is the most direct conceptual support among the three entries, precisely because it is the one that explicitly deconstructs the signal into interoceptive and exteroceptive components. MECH-282's distinctive commitment -- that interoceptive harm has a dissociable input arm alongside the external-threat arm -- is exactly the dissociation this review builds its argument around. Where the two primary papers (Kang 2022, Chen 2018) supply the broadcast function and the interoceptive instance respectively, Jaramillo et al. supply the claim that interoceptive and exteroceptive distress are handled as separable streams through the parabrachial relay. That is the architectural backbone of the claim.

The caveat is one of axis. The review locates the dissociation principally along a *target* axis -- CeA for innate/physical, BNST for unpredictable distress -- and is careful to add that the two circuits are partly redundant, intertwined, and reciprocal. MECH-282, by contrast, asserts a separable interoceptive *input* channel feeding what is fundamentally a CeA-centred arbitration (PBN-CGRP -> CeA -> PAG). So the biological dissociation the review describes is real but (a) leakier than a clean separable channel, and (b) drawn along a somewhat different topographic line than the claim states. The right inference is that MECH-282's separability is biologically plausible but should be held as graded and partly target-defined rather than as a crisp orthogonal input.

The other limitation is simply genre: this is a review, not a new causal test. It inherits a rodent-dominant primary literature and offers no fresh experiment isolating an interoceptive z_harm channel from an exteroceptive one. Its value is as the synthesising scaffold that shows the field already thinks in the interoceptive/exteroceptive terms MECH-282 needs.

I score this 0.75. It is a strong, well-cited review whose conceptual mapping onto MECH-282 is good, but it is secondary evidence, and its CeA-vs-BNST framing of the dissociation diverges enough from the claim's within-CeA separable-input statement that I keep mapping fidelity in the mid-0.7s and the aggregate just below.
