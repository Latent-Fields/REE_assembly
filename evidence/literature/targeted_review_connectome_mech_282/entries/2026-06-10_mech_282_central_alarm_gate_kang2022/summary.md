# Central alarm system gating threat cues to the amygdala (Kang et al., 2022)

According to PubMed, Kang and colleagues (Cell Reports, 2022; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2022.111222)) asked a deceptively simple question: how do innate threat signals arriving through different senses converge before they reach the amygdala? Their answer is a CGRP-defined relay. Calcitonin-gene-related-peptide neurons in the external lateral parabrachial nucleus (PBel) -- and a parallel population in the parvocellular subparafascicular thalamus -- respond to aversive cues across modalities and carry negative valence forward to the central and lateral amygdala. Crucially, the manipulation is causal in both directions: silencing these CGRP populations or their amygdala projections impairs multi-sensory threat perception and blocks aversive memory formation.

For MECH-282 this is close to a load-bearing citation. The REE claim posits a lateral-parabrachial-nucleus analog (LPB) acting as the *upstream broadcast arm* of the threat-side architecture, pushing distress into harm-arbitration along a PBN-CGRP -> CeA path. Kang et al. give that arm its empirical body: a CGRP "central alarm system" that gates threat cues into the amygdala and is necessary for the downstream affective consequence. The framing the authors themselves use -- a unified alarm that integrates and conveys threat across modalities -- is almost a one-to-one restatement of the broadcast-into-arbitration function REE attributes to the LPB analog, sitting parallel to the predator-side VMHdm arm.

Where I want to stay honest is the interoceptive specificity. MECH-282's distinctive architectural commitment is not merely that the parabrachial nucleus broadcasts threat -- it is that *interoceptive* harm (visceral malaise, taste-aversion analogs, hypoxia, hypoglycemia) has a *separable* input channel, dissociable from external-threat z_harm. This paper treats the CGRP relay as multi-sensory and innate; it does not carve off a purely interoceptive sub-channel, nor does it test dissociation between visceral and exteroceptive threat at the level MECH-282 needs. So it supports the existence and necessity of the broadcast arm strongly, but it speaks only indirectly to the separability claim that is the more contestable part of the architecture.

Two further caveats temper the transfer. First, everything causal here is in mice; REE's harm-arbitration substrate is an architectural abstraction, and the leap from a rodent innate-threat circuit to a reweighted z_harm channel is a modelling decision, not a measured fact. Second, the paper stops at the amygdala -- it says nothing about a downstream CeA -> PAG arbiter or about any override/reweighting signal of the kind SD-037 supplies in REE. Those remain our commitments to defend separately.

I score this 0.82. The source quality is high (cell-type-specific, bidirectional causal control, strong venue), and the mapping onto the broadcast-arm architecture is tight. I hold it below 0.9 because the interoceptive-channel separability -- the part of MECH-282 most in need of evidence -- is only partially addressed, and because the rodent-to-REE transfer for the arbitration framing is unverified.
