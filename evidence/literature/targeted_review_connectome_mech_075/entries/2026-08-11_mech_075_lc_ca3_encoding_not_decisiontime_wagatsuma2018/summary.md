# Summary: Wagatsuma et al. 2018 -- LC input to CA3 drives single-trial learning, not decision-time retrieval

**Entry:** 2026-08-11_mech_075_lc_ca3_encoding_not_decisiontime_wagatsuma2018
**DOI:** [10.1073/pnas.1714082115](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1714082115)
**Claim tested:** MECH-075

---

This is the field's closest thing to a direct causal test of "LC input shapes CA3." Wagatsuma and colleagues, working in Tonegawa's lab, used optogenetic inhibition of LC axon terminals specifically within CA3 while mice explored a genuinely novel open-field context for four minutes. The design is elegant precisely because it separates two moments that are easy to conflate: the moment of encoding (day 1, first exposure to the novel context) and the moment of retrieval (day 2, re-exposure to what is now a familiar context). Silencing LC-to-CA3 signaling during the day-1 encoding exposure produced a clear deficit: mice failed to show the normal reduction in exploratory behavior on day 2, and in parallel, calcium imaging of individual CA3 pyramidal cells showed degraded place-field similarity between the two sessions -- more cells had switched to representing a different spatial location. Critically, the authors also ran the converse manipulation: silencing the same LC-to-CA3 pathway during the day-2 recall session itself. That had no effect at all. The pathway is not needed to retrieve or use an existing CA3 representation -- only to lay one down in the first place.

That asymmetry is the whole finding, and it is a strikingly clean illustration of the construct-validity question this review was assembled to answer. The paper's authors describe the mechanism explicitly as driving "plastic changes in the CA3 microcircuit" -- this is encoding-time, plasticity-mediated causal architecture. The behavioral and representational readout is not available until a full day later, after presumed synaptic consolidation has had time to act. There is no moment in this experiment where an experimenter asks "does the attractor look different right now, within this exploration bout, while LC is active" -- the entire logic of the design routes around that question by comparing two temporally separated exposures.

The relevance to V3-EXQ-905a should be direct. MECH-075's dorsal leg, as operationalized in the failed run, computes basin_width synchronously with the LC_GAIN manipulation -- within a single decision window, no delay, no separate retention test. Structurally, that is closer to Wagatsuma et al.'s day-2 recall-silencing condition (LC input active or silenced at the moment of the readout itself) than to their day-1 encoding condition (LC input active during encoding, readout deferred to a later session). And the day-2-style manipulation is exactly the one this paper found to be null. If the biological mechanism genuinely works the way this paper suggests -- LC drives plasticity that must be consolidated before it shows up in CA3 geometry -- then a same-decision basin_width readout is not a weak test of MECH-075's dorsal leg; it is testing something the mechanism was never proposed to do.

The honest caveat: this paper never measured attractor basin width in any formal sense, so the inference that its "no effect at retrieval" finding predicts V3's basin_width null is REE's own extrapolation, not something the paper claims. It is also entirely possible that a decision-time attractor effect exists in parallel with the encoding-time plasticity effect this paper characterized -- the study was not designed to detect one, so its absence here is not proof of absence. But as the single most directly on-target LC-CA3 causal study available, it is strong circumstantial support for reading V3-EXQ-905a's null basin_width result as a timescale mismatch between the manipulation and the metric, rather than as evidence against the dorsal leg's underlying biology.
