# Summary: McKenzie et al. 2025 -- NE tracks hippocampal attractor deviation, but on a minutes-not-seconds clock

**Entry:** 2026-08-11_mech_075_ne_attractor_minutes_timescale_mckenzie2025
**DOI:** [10.7554/eLife.105183.1](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.105183.1) (reviewed preprint)
**Claim tested:** MECH-075

---

Of everything surfaced in this review, this is the paper that speaks most directly in MECH-075's own language. McKenzie and colleagues used a genetically encoded NE sensor (GRAB-NE) to record norepinephrine release in dorsal CA1 in real time while mice explored a series of environments, and paired that with a separate electrophysiological dataset of CA1 population activity across the same kind of context transitions. The headline finding is exactly the shape of claim MECH-075's dorsal leg wants: NE release spikes sharply at the moment of an event boundary -- entering a new or changed environment -- and the hippocampal population representation simultaneously becomes markedly less similar to its eventual steady-state pattern. As both signals settle, they settle together: the authors report that NE decays exponentially back toward baseline over the following several minutes, and the population representation's rate of convergence back to a stable "modal" pattern tracks that same decay curve. Most tellingly, they frame this explicitly in attractor language, writing that NE "perturbed neural activity away from the stored attractor" and that the magnitude of NE release scales with "the deviance between the current neural representation and the steady-state attractor." This is, almost verbatim, the mechanism MECH-075's dorsal leg proposes: an LC/NE arousal signal widening or displacing the hippocampal attractor state.

The catch, and it is the catch this entire literature pull was built to find, is the clock. The representational settling this paper documents happens over roughly ten minutes -- their own comparison contrasts population correlation "in the first minute" against correlation "10 minutes into the session." That is not a decision-time process in any sense V3's CEM decision loop would recognize; a single CEM candidate-selection step operates on a timescale many orders of magnitude shorter than a ten-minute exponential decay. If this paper is a correct description of how NE actually perturbs and re-settles the dorsal hippocampal attractor, then the biological process it describes is real, is genuinely attractor-shaped, and is genuinely NE-driven -- but it unfolds on the timescale of an extended behavioral episode, not a single decision within that episode. V3-EXQ-905a's basin_width metric, computed synchronously with the LC_GAIN manipulation within one decision step, is asking this mechanism to show up roughly two orders of magnitude faster than the one paper that actually measured it found that it does.

Two honest caveats belong alongside this reading. First, this is a reviewed preprint under eLife's model, not yet a finalized version of record -- the specific quantitative claims could shift on further revision, though the qualitative attractor-perturbation-and-decay picture is unlikely to reverse outright. Second, the NE fiber-photometry cohort and the electrophysiology cohort were different animals entirely; the tight coupling between NE decay and representational drift is an across-study inference rather than a single-session causal demonstration, and the authors themselves note they did NOT find the replay signature they expected, meaning their own account of the underlying mechanism is not fully settled.

**What this adds to the construct-validity picture:** where Wagatsuma (2018) and Grella (2019) show LC/NE hippocampal effects measured across sessions or days, this paper closes some of that gap by showing an effect measurable within a single ongoing session -- but "within a session" here still means minutes, not the sub-second-to-second span of a single decision. Taken together with the other four entries, the pattern across every paper this review could find that actually measures LC/NE effects on hippocampal representational geometry -- rather than assuming them -- is the same: the field's fastest documented timescale for this class of effect is minutes, roughly two orders of magnitude slower than V3-EXQ-905a's decision-time basin_width readout.
