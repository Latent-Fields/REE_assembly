# Summary: Prince et al. 2021 -- in current CA3 models, noradrenaline gates plasticity, it does not shape the attractor online

**Entry:** 2026-08-11_mech_075_noradrenaline_plasticity_gate_not_online_prince2021
**DOI:** [10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009435](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009435)
**Claim tested:** MECH-075

---

Where the previous four entries in this batch ask what has been directly measured, this one asks a different but equally diagnostic question: when computational modelers who specialize in CA3 ensemble dynamics build a model of how noradrenaline participates in that circuit, what do they give it to do? Prince and colleagues built a biophysically grounded model of CA3 ensemble formation under theta-burst input, and gave acetylcholine and noradrenaline deliberately separable roles based on their reading of the electrophysiology literature. Acetylcholine gets an online channel: in the model it immediately depolarizes CA3 pyramidal cells by about 5mV and instantaneously suppresses recurrent CA3-CA3 synaptic transmission, reshaping the network's dynamics moment-to-moment. Noradrenaline gets no such channel. The paper states this in almost exactly these words: noradrenaline "was assumed to enable synaptic plasticity between CA3 pyramidal cells and therefore ensemble formation but did not otherwise affect CA3 network properties." In the model, NE's entire causal footprint is a permission slip for spike-timing-dependent plasticity to proceed -- it does not touch cellular excitability, does not touch recurrent weight, and beyond a modest ~30% reduction in feedforward inhibition (versus acetylcholine's >70%), does nothing to the network's instantaneous computational properties.

The behavioral signature of this asymmetry in the model is worth noting on its own terms: under noradrenaline alone, stable ensembles only form when theta-burst input arrives at 30Hz, and are "almost completely abolished" at 20Hz, whereas acetylcholine can rescue ensemble formation even at the lower frequency. That is a plasticity-permissiveness effect -- NE narrows the window in which learning can successfully carve out an ensemble -- not a basin-widening or gain-type effect on an already-existing attractor. Nowhere in the paper's architecture is there a parameter that lets noradrenaline change the geometry of an attractor within a single burst or a single decision; the authors simply never modeled a fast NE channel, and did not discuss NE receptor kinetics or a phasic/tonic distinction at all.

Read alongside the rest of this batch, this paper is not independent proof that no fast NE-attractor mechanism exists -- it is evidence about what the field currently believes is worth modeling, and that belief was itself built on the same sparse, largely plasticity-oriented empirical base (Kempadoo 2016, Lemon & Manahan-Vaughan 2006, and papers of that shape) that this whole review has been auditing. If a future modeler incorporated a paper like McKenzie et al. 2025 (also in this batch), which does document a real-time, NE-linked attractor perturbation, the model's architecture would likely change to include a fast channel. As things stand today, though, this paper is a direct signal that MECH-075's dorsal-leg operationalization -- treating LC-arousal as a within-decision gain/basin-width modulator -- diverges from how the closest existing computational literature currently frames noradrenaline's role in CA3. A null basin_width result under a correctly-calibrated manipulation is exactly what this model's architecture would predict, not an anomaly to be explained away.
