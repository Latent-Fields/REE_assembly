# Summary: Grella et al. 2019 -- phasic LC triggers "global remapping," but only measurable across separated exposures

**Entry:** 2026-08-11_mech_075_global_remapping_crossexposure_grella2019
**DOI:** [10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1956-18.2018](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1956-18.2018)
**Claim tested:** MECH-075

---

The title of this paper is, on first read, exactly what a decision-time attractor claim would want: "global remapping" is the classic hippocampal term for a hard switch between two distinct population codes for the same physical space -- as close to an attractor-state transition as the field's vocabulary gets. Grella and colleagues, extending Bouret and Sara's network-reset theory, show that pharmacologically activating locus coeruleus in a phasic-like mode (glutamate microinfusion) just before a rat's second exposure to an already-familiar open field causes the hippocampal population -- across dentate gyrus, CA1, and CA3 -- to shift toward the population code it would normally use for a genuinely novel environment. Tonic-mode LC activators (bethanechol, orexin A, CRF) do not produce this effect; silencing LC before a truly novel exposure blocks the remapping that would normally occur there. This is a clean dissociation, and it is real evidence that phasic (but not tonic) LC activity is causally linked to a discrete hippocampal population-code switch.

But look closely at how "remapping" is actually measured, and the timescale question reopens. The technique is catFISH -- cellular compartmental analysis of temporal fluorescence in situ hybridization -- which exploits the fact that an immediate-early gene transcript (Arc, Zif268) appears first in the nucleus and only migrates to the cytoplasm roughly 25-30 minutes later. By comparing which hippocampal neurons show nuclear-only, cytoplasm-only, or both labeling patterns after two separated 5-minute exposures 20 minutes apart, the authors can infer whether largely the same or largely different neurons were active across the two sessions. This is a genuinely elegant way to ask "did the population code change between visit one and visit two" -- but it cannot, even in principle, ask "did the population code change within visit one, while the rat was actively exploring and LC was phasically firing." The authors say so themselves, plainly, in their limitations: their method "only index[es] global remapping" across exposures, and "rate remapping, place field rotation, or place field splitting occurring with novelty would not be detected as a loss of network overlap." Within-session dynamics are simply outside what catFISH can see.

There is a further layer worth naming: the pharmacological manipulation itself -- a 30-second glutamate microinfusion followed by a 60-second dwell before the animal is placed in the environment -- is itself far slower than a single decision. Nothing about this experimental design could have detected an effect unfolding over seconds even if the biology supported one.

For the construct-validity question this review was built to answer, this paper is genuinely double-edged, and both edges matter. On one hand, it is the strongest available evidence that phasic (not tonic) LC activity specifically -- the mode most analogous to a within-decision arousal signal -- is causally linked to a population-code reorganization the field is comfortable calling "remapping," lending real credibility to the idea that MECH-075's dorsal leg is asking a biologically sensible question. On the other hand, it is also direct, explicit evidence that the literature has not yet demonstrated this reorganization happening on a decision-time (seconds, within-session) timescale -- only across temporally separated exposures 20 minutes apart. V3-EXQ-905a's basin_width metric asks the within-session version of this question, and this paper -- the closest thing to a "yes, LC remaps hippocampal attractors" result in the literature -- explicitly cannot speak to it either way.
