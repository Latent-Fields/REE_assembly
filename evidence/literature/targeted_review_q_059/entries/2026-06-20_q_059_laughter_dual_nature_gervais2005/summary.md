# Laughter is two-faced by design: Duchenne vs non-Duchenne (Gervais & Wilson 2005)

According to PubMed, Gervais & Wilson (2005), *The evolution and functions of laughter and humor: a synthetic approach*, *The Quarterly Review of Biology* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/498281)), is the synthesis that pulled the scattered laughter literature into one evolutionary frame, and its central organising move is exactly the one Q-059 needs adjudicated. They insist that any serious account of laughter has to honour the empirical existence of **two distinct types**. Duchenne laughter is stimulus-driven, emotionally genuine, and affiliative -- they reconstruct it as a ritualised medium for playful emotional contagion that, in early hominids between roughly 4 and 2 million years ago, coupled the emotions of small groups and promoted resource-building social play during the brief windows of safety and satiation that punctuated bipedal life. Non-Duchenne laughter is the later, volitional, co-opted form. Once the spontaneous signal existed, it could be produced deliberately and bent to strategic ends, and it is from this co-option that what they call the "dark side" of laughter emerged -- laughter used to exclude, to dominate, to deride.

That bifurcation is precisely the premise Q-059 is built on. The question asks whether REE needs a crying / distress-vocalisation guardrail analogue to its laughter mechanism (MECH-110 / MECH-364), and -- the part this paper speaks to -- how social laughter should be adjudicated as social-field *repair* versus *damage or exclusion*, "given that laughter is not monotone-affiliative." Gervais & Wilson are the biological warrant for that "given." They establish, with breadth across ethology, psychology, and phylogeny, that laughter genuinely is not a single bond-positive signal; it carries both a sincere affiliative channel and a co-optable strategic one that can do social harm. A REE social-affect system that scored all laughter-analogue events as bond-positive would therefore mishandle exactly the cases the claim flags, and the review's evidence is that those cases are central to laughter's natural history, not rare artefacts.

There is a sharp design implication buried in the Duchenne/non-Duchenne distinction that is worth surfacing for the REE side. The two types share much of their surface form but diverge in provenance and sincerity. That means an adjudicator keyed only on the *surface* of a laughter-analogue event -- its acoustic or behavioural signature -- is structurally spoofable, because non-Duchenne laughter is built to mimic the affiliative form while serving a different end. If REE ever instantiates this guardrail, the lesson from the biology is that adjudication has to condition on provenance and context (was this a spontaneous, genuine response, or a volitionally produced one in a competitive context?), not on the signal's appearance alone.

I want to be clear about the limits, because they bound the confidence. This is a review of human and hominid-evolutionary laughter; it motivates the *need* for adjudication but offers no adjudication mechanism, and it says essentially nothing about the crying / distress-vocalisation half of Q-059 (that half is covered by the Zeifman entry in this same pull). It also does not touch the open `implementation_phase: v4` / `substrate_conditional` status of the question -- whether REE should actually build this remains a separate, later decision. So the paper strengthens the claim that the *question is well-posed and non-trivial*, not that REE must answer it now.

I rate this **supports** at confidence 0.7. Source quality is high (0.85) for a canonical Q Rev Biol synthesis; mapping fidelity is good (0.7) because the Duchenne/non-Duchenne split maps almost one-to-one onto Q-059's repair-vs-damage axis; and transfer risk is modest (0.35) because Q-059 is itself a design question, so a robust, well-documented biological bifurcation is exactly the right kind of grounding for "does this distinction motivate a REE guardrail?"
