# Laughter as relationship regulation, contagious by mirror mechanism (Palagi, Caruana & de Waal 2022)

According to PubMed, Palagi, Caruana & de Waal (2022), *The naturalistic approach to laughter in humans and other animals: towards a unified theory*, *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2021.0175)), takes a deliberate run at two assumptions that, if left standing, would mislead a REE laughter analogue. The first is the reflexive coupling of laughter to humour; the second is the idea that laughter is uniquely human. Against both, they marshal evidence that laughter is, before anything else, a **social behaviour aimed at regulating social relationships** -- easing social tensions, establishing and maintaining bonds -- and that homologous or homoplasic behaviours exist in primates and rodents, running on shared neural circuitry. They further argue that the everyday contagiousness of laughter, its tendency to spread through a group, is mediated by a specific mirror mechanism.

For Q-059 this is the entry that fills in the **social-field repair** pole. Where Gervais & Wilson establish that laughter bifurcates into affiliative and exclusionary uses, Palagi and colleagues clarify what the affiliative use actually *is* at the functional level: not a display of positive affect for its own sake, but a regulator of relationships. That reframing matters for how a REE laughter analogue (MECH-110 / MECH-364) ought to be conceived. If the primitive function is relationship regulation, then the analogue belongs in the social field as a *regulatory* signal -- something that adjusts the state of a relationship -- rather than as a hedonic emission tagged onto an agent. Q-059's own language ("social-field repair vs damage") already presupposes this regulatory reading, and this paper supplies its naturalistic grounding.

The contagion point is the one I would flag hardest for the REE design, because it is a substrate-level fact with adjudication consequences. If a laughter analogue spreads sub-deliberately through a mirror-like mechanism, then an *exclusionary* instance of laughter could propagate across agents in the social field before any repair-vs-damage adjudication stage has a chance to run. That is a concrete architectural risk: a guardrail that only evaluates laughter after the fact, while contagion is already doing its work, would be too late. The biology suggests adjudication has to be able to *gate contagion*, not merely label completed events.

The honest limitation, and the reason I weight this entry a notch below the Gervais & Wilson one, is that it leans hard on the benign side of the ledger. Its emphasis is tension-easing and bonding -- the repair pole -- and it is comparatively quiet about laughter as exclusion, dominance, or threat. Taken in isolation it could bias a REE designer toward treating the laughter analogue as essentially benevolent, which is exactly the error Q-059 is trying to guard against. That is why the two laughter entries in this pull are meant to be read together: Palagi et al. for what the affiliative/regulatory function is and how it spreads, Gervais & Wilson for why it cannot be assumed benign. Neither offers an adjudication *mechanism*; both are about why one is needed and what it must respect. And like the rest of this pull, the paper is silent on Q-059's open `v4` / `substrate_conditional` status -- the build decision stays downstream.
