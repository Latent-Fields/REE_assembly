# Perception in real-time: predicting the present, reconstructing the past.

Hinze Hogendoorn (2022). Trends in Cognitive Sciences. doi:10.1016/j.tics.2021.11.003

Hogendoorn argues that real-time perception cannot represent a single instant given neural delays. Instead it represents a timeline: predictive mechanisms extrapolate ahead to cover the delay, and reconstruction mechanisms go back and revise what was represented when a prediction turns out wrong.

For MECH-501 this matters mainly as independent convergence. The claim's parent (ARC-129) says 'now' is forward-displaced; MECH-501 says a violation forces a backward correction. Hogendoorn's framework says both of these, for perception, and ties them to existing evidence (flash-lag, extrapolation into the blind spot, EEG position decoding). It gives the claim a literature home that is stronger than the loose analogies named in its note.

It does not test anything, and it is about perceiving external events, not about the agent's own state during a committed action. It also frames the revision as rewriting the past part of the timeline; MECH-501 is about replacing the estimate treated as current. Those could come apart.

Direction supports, confidence 0.45. Cite as the framework MECH-501 instantiates in an action setting, not as evidence for it.
