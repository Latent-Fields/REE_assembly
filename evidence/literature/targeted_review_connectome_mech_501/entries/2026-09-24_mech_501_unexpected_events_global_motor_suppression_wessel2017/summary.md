# On the Globality of Motor Suppression: Unexpected Events and Their Influence on Behavior and Cognition.

Jan R Wessel, Adam R Aron (2017). Neuron. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2016.12.013

Wessel and Aron review evidence that action errors, unexpected action outcomes and surprising percepts all recruit the fronto-basal-ganglia stopping network (prefrontal nodes projecting to the subthalamic nucleus), producing broad motor suppression. They argue the same suppression disrupts ongoing cognition, such as working-memory maintenance.

This is solid grounding for the trigger in MECH-501: a consequential surprise does interrupt committed action through a fast, dedicated circuit. But that is the part of the claim already covered by MECH-090 and MECH-342. The review is evidence for interruption as a control event over action and cognition, not for a backward re-anchoring of the state estimate.

The cognitive side-effects it describes are closer to flushing or disrupting current contents than to replacing a forward-displaced estimate with a less-displaced one. So this literature does not support the reconstruction step, and it does not test whether that step is separate from decommitment. It does suggest a practical test: whether surprise-triggered stopping changes the state representation in a direction-specific way (toward the sensed present) or just degrades it.

Direction mixed, confidence 0.4.
