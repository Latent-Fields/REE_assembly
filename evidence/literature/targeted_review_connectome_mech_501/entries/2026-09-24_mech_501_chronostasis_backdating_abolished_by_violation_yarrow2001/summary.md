# Illusory perceptions of space and time preserve cross-saccadic perceptual continuity.

K Yarrow, P Haggard, R Heal, P Brown, J C Rothwell (2001). Nature. doi:10.1038/35104551

Yarrow and colleagues studied the stopped-clock illusion: after a saccade to a ticking clock, the first second seems too long. They showed the percept of the saccade target is extended back in time to just before the saccade started. When the target was moved unpredictably during the saccade, the illusion disappeared.

Chronostasis is one of the four phenomena MECH-501's note names as a loose analogy. This result cuts against that analogy. The backward referral here happens when the post-saccadic prediction is confirmed and is abolished by a violation. MECH-501 says a violation triggers backward correction. Same word, opposite dependency.

It does not falsify MECH-501. Chronostasis back-dates a percept to fill a gap in time during saccadic suppression; MECH-501 pulls a forward-displaced estimate back toward the sensed state. They are different operations. The honest reading is that chronostasis should be dropped as an analogue.

Direction weakens (the analogy), confidence 0.45.
