# The time-course of prediction formation and revision in human visual motion processing.

Tessel Blom, Stefan Bode, Hinze Hogendoorn (2021). Cortex. doi:10.1016/j.cortex.2021.02.008

Blom, Bode and Hogendoorn decoded the position of an apparent-motion stimulus from EEG over time. Position representations were available earlier when the stimulus was where it was expected to be, and there was an extra delay when the trajectory unexpectedly reversed: the predictive activation had to be overcome before the new position was represented.

This is the neural-level version of what MECH-501 needs: a forward prediction that is active in the representation, and a measurable revision event when a violation arrives. Because the cost is specific to violated expectation, it also leans toward the claim's corollary that unexpected interruptions should produce more reconstruction than expected corrections.

It stops well short of the claim. The measure is decoding latency, not a jump in an estimate back toward the sensed state. The paradigm is passive, with no action or consequences, and no matched expected-correction condition. Generic expectation accounts would also predict a latency penalty for surprises.

Direction supports, confidence 0.4.
