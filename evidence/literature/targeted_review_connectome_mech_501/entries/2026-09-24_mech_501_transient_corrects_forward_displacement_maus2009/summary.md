# Going, going, gone: localizing abrupt offsets of moving objects.

Gerrit W Maus, Romi Nijhawan (2009). Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance. doi:10.1037/a0012317

Maus and Nijhawan had observers localise moving objects at the moment one of them abruptly disappeared. A continuing object was seen ahead of where it was, while the disappearing one was localised accurately. When they weakened the offset transient (brief disappearance, partial contrast drop), perceived positions fell in between. They propose two representations: an extrapolated internal-model position that runs ahead, and a transient-driven signal that corrects the forward shift when the trajectory changes abruptly.

This is the best-fitting phenomenon for MECH-501 found in this pull, and it is not one of the four the claim's note names. It shows a forward-displaced estimate being pulled back toward the sensed position by an abrupt, unpredicted change, and the correction is graded by the relative strength of the two signals. That is the computational event the claim describes, minus the action.

It does not reach the parts of the claim that make it distinctive. There is no committed action, so nothing here separates state-estimate reconstruction from decommitment (MECH-342) or gate re-opening (MECH-090). The trigger is a low-level sensory transient, not a consequence-weighted prediction violation. And the 'reconstruction' is inferred from end-point reports; a postdictive account without extrapolation could produce similar reports.

Direction supports, confidence 0.55. Useful as the perceptual existence proof for 'forward estimate plus violation-triggered correction', and it suggests the claim should predict graded rather than all-or-none reconstruction.
