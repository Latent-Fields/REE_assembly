# Chen et al. (2020) — the prefrontal→STN hyperdirect pathway is real, monosynaptic, and genuinely fast in humans

*According to PubMed.* Source: Chen W, de Hemptinne C, Miller AM, Leibbrand M, Little SJ, Lim DA, Larson PS, Starr PA. "Prefrontal-Subthalamic Hyperdirect Pathway Modulates Movement Inhibition in Humans." *Neuron* 106(4):579-588.e3, 2020. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2020.02.012) · PMID 32155442 · PMC7274135.

## What the paper did

Where the DCM anchor (Zhang & Iwaki 2019) infers the fast pathway from model comparison, this paper measures it. The authors recorded high-resolution field potentials simultaneously from the inferior frontal gyrus (IFG) and the subthalamic nucleus (STN) in 21 human patients undergoing deep-brain-stimulation surgery, during a stop-signal task. They also stimulated the STN and recorded the cortical evoked responses to probe the physical connectivity between the two structures.

## Key findings relevant to MECH-141

Three results matter for the claim. First, cortical potentials evoked by subthalamic stimulation showed *short-latency* events consistent with **monosynaptic** connectivity between the IFG and the ventral STN — i.e. the hyperdirect pathway is a genuine direct projection, not a slow multi-synaptic detour. Second, during stopping, the stopping-related potentials in cortex **preceded** stopping-related activity in the STN — the expected temporal order if IFG drives STN to abort the action. Third, the synchronization between these task-evoked potentials **predicted stop-signal reaction time**: the tighter the IFG–STN coupling, the faster the stop. Together these establish that a prefrontal→subthalamic hyperdirect pathway is present in humans and mediates *rapid* stopping.

## How this maps to REE

This is the empirical backbone for the "fast reactive, milliseconds-scale" half of MECH-141. A monosynaptic cortico-subthalamic route that can interrupt an already-initiated action, whose coupling strength sets the speed of the abort, is exactly the fast post-commitment interrupt REE places at the cancel-window (MECH-138) at its shortest timescale. Crucially, the *latency* claim in MECH-141 — that this pathway operates on a fundamentally shorter timescale than the proactive one — is here backed by measured short-latency monosynaptic events and a cortex-precedes-subcortex ordering, rather than by inference from pathway length. That is what upgrades the dual-timescale claim from "two pathways, presumed different speeds" to "one pathway measured to be fast."

## Limitations and caveats

This paper carries only one of the two pathways MECH-141 needs. It says nothing about the slow proactive prefrontal→caudate route and nothing directly about whether the two arbitration timescales can be collapsed — it corroborates the fast half and leaves the dissociation to be carried by Zhang & Iwaki and Jahfari. The larger caveat is the population: these are Parkinson's-disease patients recorded intraoperatively, so the timing is measured in a basal ganglia with known dopaminergic pathology, and (as with all human intracranial work) the sample is restricted to surgical candidates. I therefore rate source quality very high (rare simultaneous human IFG+STN recordings with a causal stimulation probe) but mapping fidelity only moderate and transfer risk elevated, netting to 0.80. The disease confound is worth stating plainly rather than smoothing over: it is the reason the healthy-architecture reading of these latencies is a reasonable inference and not a certainty.
