# Aron & Poldrack (2006) — the canonical STN role in reactive stopping, and where the hyperdirect story began

*According to PubMed.* Source: Aron AR, Poldrack RA. "Cortical and subcortical contributions to Stop signal response inhibition: role of the subthalamic nucleus." *J Neurosci* 26(9):2424-33, 2006. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4682-05.2006) · PMID 16510720 · PMC6793670.

## What the paper did

This is the foundational paper the whole reactive-stopping literature — including the three others in this pull — builds on, so it belongs in the record as the independent anchor for MECH-141's fast route. Two fMRI experiments used a stop-signal task: subjects responded to Go signals and tried to cancel the initiated response on occasional Stop signals. Experiment 1 contrasted Going against Stopping; Experiment 2 used high-resolution functional and structural imaging to localise the stopping activation.

## Key findings relevant to MECH-141

Going activated the frontal–striatal–pallidal–motor chain expected of response *initiation* (the direct pathway). Stopping, by contrast, specifically activated the **right inferior frontal cortex (IFC)** and the **subthalamic nucleus (STN)** — and Experiment 2 confirmed the subcortical focus was in the vicinity of the STN. The individual-difference result is the one that matters most here: stopping-related activation was significantly greater for **fast inhibitors** than slow ones in *both* IFC and STN, and activity in the two regions was correlated across subjects. Aron & Poldrack proposed that the STN suppresses thalamocortical output to block Go execution, and — in the sentence that launched a decade of work — asked whether this could be implemented via a direct IFC→STN "hyperdirect" projection.

## How this maps to REE

For MECH-141 this is convergent, independent support for the fast reactive pathway: the STN is the node that halts an already-initiated action, and the strength of IFC/STN engagement scales with how fast a subject can stop — exactly what you would expect of a fast interrupt whose recruitment sets abort latency. It corroborates, from an independent lab and an earlier methodology, the reactive half that Chen (2020) later pinned down with intracranial timing and Jahfari (2011) placed within a two-pathway model.

## Limitations and caveats

I have deliberately weighted this entry lowest (0.62). It is a 2006 activation-and-correlation study: it shows IFC and STN *co-activate* during stopping and that their activation *correlates* with stopping speed, but it does not demonstrate a pathway and does not measure latencies — the hyperdirect projection is explicitly named as a *hypothesis for future work*, which the later papers in this pull then discharged. It also speaks only to reactive stopping; it contains nothing about a proactive pathway or about the two operating at segregated, non-collapsible timescales, so on its own it cannot distinguish MECH-141's dual-timescale architecture from a single-pathway account. Its value is as the canonical, heavily-replicated foundation stone for the fast route — the reason the claim's reactive half rests on convergent evidence from four labs rather than one, not as primary evidence for the dissociation itself.
