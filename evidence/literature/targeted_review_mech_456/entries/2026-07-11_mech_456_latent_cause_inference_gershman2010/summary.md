# Gershman, Blei & Niv (2010) -- Context, learning, and extinction

According to PubMed (PMID 20063968; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0017808)), this paper recasts context-dependent learning and extinction as *normative statistical inference*. The animal is treated as running online Bayesian inference within a model that allows an unbounded number of latent causes: each observation is assigned to a cause, and crucially a **new** latent cause is inferred whenever the existing ones stop explaining the data well. With that single idea the authors reproduce a diverse set of conditioning results -- renewal, latent inhibition -- that had resisted simpler state-classification accounts, and they predict, correctly, that context dependence collapses when the capacity to infer new causes is restricted (in young animals, or after hippocampal lesions made before training).

This is the entry that supplies MECH-456's *rationale* rather than its mechanism. The claim's grounding text names latent-cause inference explicitly -- 'switch causes when the current one stops explaining the data' -- and points at Gershman 2010/2017. What this paper adds beyond the trigger papers is normativity: re-binding to a competing world-configuration when that competitor better explains the agent's realised situation is not just a mechanistic reflex, it is the *optimal* thing for a rational agent to do under structure uncertainty. The object-file paper grounds that bindings are updatable; Weilnhammer and Cole ground that prediction error drives the switch; Gershman, Blei and Niv ground that *switching when the current explanation fails is the correct inference*. That is the missing 'why' in the dossier, and it is the same abstract move MECH-456 makes one layer up, over self<->world-config bindings rather than conditioning contexts -- which is also why the claim files it alongside, not identical to, the MECH-269(b) anchor-reset it sits above.

The gap I want to flag is timescale and framing. Latent-cause inference here operates across *trials* -- renewal and latent inhibition are slow, associative-learning phenomena unfolding over a session -- whereas MECH-456 rebinds *within* an episode, over a perceptual self-anchor binding. And there is a subtle framing difference: MECH-456's 'a competitor overtakes' is a comparison between concurrently-available configurations, while latent-cause inference is more naturally 'the current cause stops fitting, so infer one (possibly new)'. These are adjacent but not identical. There is also a piece of the paper the claim does *not* import -- the emphasis that new-cause inference is a *limited*, hippocampus-dependent, developmentally-gated capacity. That is interesting for a future substrate question but is not part of MECH-456 as registered, and I have not stretched the mapping to cover it.

I have set source quality high (0.85 -- this is the canonical latent-cause paper and the modelling is careful), mapping fidelity 0.66 (the normative switch-rule is nearly MECH-456's, at the level of principle), and transfer risk 0.4 (across-trial conditioning contexts vs within-episode perceptual binding, and a behavioural model rather than an architectural test), aggregating to 0.68. It rounds out the dossier by grounding the *optimality* of rebinding, the one leg the mechanism-and-updatability papers leave open.
