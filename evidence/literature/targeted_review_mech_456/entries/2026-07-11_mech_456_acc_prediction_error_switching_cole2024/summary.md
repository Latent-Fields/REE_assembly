# Cole, Harvey, Myers-Joseph, Gilra & Khan (2024) -- Prediction-error signals in ACC drive task-switching

According to PubMed (PMID 39154045; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-51368-9)), this is a rare *causal* demonstration of a prediction-error-driven switch. Mice were trained to respond to the same stimuli under two different rules and to switch between them, in a single trial, on the basis of a prediction error. Using optogenetic silencing and un-silencing together with widefield and two-photon calcium imaging, the authors show that anterior cingulate cortex is specifically required for this rapid switching -- but *only when* it carries neural prediction-error signals. Those PE signals are projection-target dependent, they are *larger* preceding successful transitions, and a disinhibitory interneuron circuit is needed to compute them.

This is the entry that turns the MECH-456 trigger from a correlation into a causal principle. Weilnhammer (also in this dossier) shows that prediction-error accrual *correlates* with endogenous perceptual switches; Cole and colleagues show that a prediction error is *causally necessary* for switching the operative model, and -- importantly for us -- that the effect is **graded**: the bigger the prediction error, the more reliably the switch succeeds. MECH-456 makes two demands of a genuine rebinding: that it be prediction-error / competitor-overtake driven (the trigger), and that it produce a graded, non-saturating behavioural consequence (DV2, deliberately specified as graded after the V3-EXQ-725a `frac_state_div` saturation lesson). Cole et al. give the animal-causal analogue of both at once -- a PE that has to be there for the switch, scaled to how decisively the current model has failed. That the switch is a *single-trial* event also matches the within-episode timescale MECH-456 works on, unlike the slower conditioning timescale in the Gershman entry.

The honest boundary is what is being switched. Here it is a *response rule* -- a cognitive set -- not a self-anchor <-> world-configuration binding. The abstract mechanism ('drop the current model when prediction error says it is wrong, in proportion to the error') transfers well; the bound object does not. And the causal substrate is ACC-specific mouse cortical circuitry, including a particular disinhibitory interneuron motif, for which REE has no homologue. So this grounds the *principle* -- PE causally and gradedly drives single-trial model switching -- rather than REE's binder implementation. It is worth noting that, unlike bistable perception, this paradigm *does* have a correct rule, so ground-truth tracking (MECH-456 DV1) is implicitly present; but the paper does not isolate 'tracks the true competitor' from 'switches on PE', so I would not lean on it for DV1.

I have set source quality high (0.85 -- causal opto plus all-optical imaging is strong, and single-trial causal PE results are uncommon), mapping fidelity 0.62 (the trigger and the graded-consequence map in spirit, but onto a task-rule rather than an entity binding), and transfer risk 0.42 (mouse ACC circuit to latent binder is a principle-level jump), for an aggregate 0.71. Together with Weilnhammer it forms the backbone of the trigger grounding: one correlational-computational, one causal-biological, both pointing at prediction-error-driven, graded switching.
