# Weilnhammer, Stuke, Hesselmann, Sterzer & Schmack (2017) -- A predictive-coding account of bistable perception

According to PubMed (PMID 28505152; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005536)), this model-based fMRI study asks what actually drives the spontaneous switches in bistable vision -- when a constant ambiguous stimulus flips between two interpretations. Their answer is a Bayesian predictive-coding model in which a switch is triggered by *prediction errors accumulating from residual evidence for the currently-suppressed percept*. The model reproduces the temporal statistics of bistability, a per-subject stabilisation parameter predicts how often that subject switches, it beats the classic adaptation / noise / adaptation-plus-noise models in formal comparison, and its prediction-error time-course tracks BOLD in inferior frontal gyri and anterior insula.

For MECH-456 this is the most mechanistically on-target entry in the dossier. The claim's trigger is stated in almost these exact terms: the competitor world-configuration overtakes 'when the current config accrues prediction-error', and the whole point of the 2026-07-10 re-registration was to ground the trigger on *prediction-error / competitor-overtake* dynamics rather than on the coherence-specific selection factor that V3-EXQ-725a settled as no-claim. Weilnhammer and colleagues give a biological-plus-computational instantiation of precisely that: residual evidence for the alternative builds prediction error until it forces the switch. Better still, their stabilisation parameter is the empirical cousin of the hysteresis threshold MECH-456 needs (from the serial-dependence side) -- one mechanism carries both the switch drive and the resistance to chatter. And because the account is predictive-coding / Bayesian, it maps cleanly onto REE's E(tau) prediction-error machinery, which is where the claim explicitly locates its grounding.

The caveat I care most about is the ground-truth one, and it is what keeps this at 0.72 rather than higher. Bistable perception has *no correct answer* -- the two interpretations are equally valid readings of the same stimulus, and switching is endogenous. MECH-456's functional test (V3-EXQ-733a) demands more than switching-on-prediction-error: it demands that rebinding tracks the **true** competitor, the world-configuration that genuinely becomes better-supported by the environment, above a shuffle control (DV1). Weilnhammer's paradigm cannot speak to that, because there is no true winner to track. So this paper is strong evidence for the *trigger* -- prediction error accrual drives the switch, gated by a stabilisation term -- and silent on *veridicality* of the switch, which is exactly the half REE has to demonstrate on its own substrate.

The remaining transfer notes are the usual ones: the neural localisation is correlational fMRI in a perceptual-report task rather than a latent binder, and bistable dynamics are famously fittable by several model families, so the predictive-coding win is evidential rather than decisive. I set source quality 0.82, mapping fidelity 0.7 (the trigger is nearly verbatim), transfer risk 0.35, aggregating to 0.72 -- the strongest single grounding here, and appropriately so, since it targets the mechanism the claim was rebuilt around.
