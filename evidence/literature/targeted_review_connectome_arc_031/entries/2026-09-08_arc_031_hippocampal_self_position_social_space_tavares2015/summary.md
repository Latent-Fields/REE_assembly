# The self's own position in a non-physical space -- as close as the literature gets

**Tavares, Mendelsohn, Grossman, Williams, Shapiro, Trope & Schiller (2015), _Neuron_ 87(1):231-43.**
Claim assessed: **ARC-031** -- HippocampalModule navigates z_self trajectory space in addition to
z_world action-object space O.

## What the paper did

Participants played the lead in a role-playing game, finding a home and a job by interacting with
virtual characters whose responses shifted the relationship. The authors modelled the resulting
social relationships as a two-dimensional space framed by power and affiliation, and asked whether
hippocampal activity tracked the participant's position and movement within it. It did. And the
covariance was stronger in participants who reported better social skills.

The detail worth dwelling on is *movement*. Most of the abstract-cognitive-map literature -- the
Constantinescu entry in this pull included -- demonstrates position coding. Here it is the vector of
travel through the space that predicts hippocampal response.

## Why this is the most valuable entry in the ARC-031 pull, and the most dangerous

Valuable, because it is the only published result I could find in which the thing being navigated is
the *agent's own state* rather than a map of external entities or an exteroceptive stimulus space.
ARC-031's whole architectural bet is that the hippocampal navigator can take the self as its domain.
Tavares et al. is the nearest existing instance of a hippocampus doing that.

Dangerous, because of an equivocation on "self" that I want to state plainly rather than leave for
someone to trip over later. Social space is the self's position *relative to other people*, along
power and affiliation. ARC-031's z_self is the self's own *epistemic* condition -- effective
dimensionality, hypothesis-tag integrity, self-coherence cost. Both are non-physical. Both are
legitimately "about the self." They are not the same coordinate system, and there is no argument in
this paper or elsewhere that a system which maps one will map the other.

## The second gap: lived is not planned

ARC-031's distinguishing content is not that the agent has a position in self-space. It is that the
agent can *plan a route through it* -- the claim's own gloss is "I will first resolve uncertainty in
domain X, then attend to Y, then commit." That is prospective, and it requires an evaluation
function; ARC-031 specifies E3 scoring trajectories by self-coherence, hypothesis-tag integrity and
self-maintenance cost rather than harm cost.

Tavares et al.'s trajectories are lived. Participants moved through social space by making choices
and receiving consequences. Nothing here shows a candidate future sequence being generated,
simulated, or scored. So the paper supplies the space and something trajectory-shaped within it, but
not the planning that is the claim's actual novelty.

## Confidence

0.45. Mapping fidelity 0.4, held there by the social-versus-epistemic conflation, which I regard as
the single most likely way this pull could mislead a future reader. And the standing ARC-031 caveat
applies as it does to every entry here: the claim is phase-locked v4, zero distinguishing substrate
exists in ree-v3, and HippocampalModule currently takes z_self only as a conditioning input
alongside z_world. This evidence bears on whether the v4 build is worth attempting. It cannot bear
on whether it works.
