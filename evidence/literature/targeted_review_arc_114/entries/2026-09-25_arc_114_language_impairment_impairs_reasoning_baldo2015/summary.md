# Baldo, Paulraj, Curran & Dronkers (2015) -- Impaired reasoning in language impairment

**What they did.** Baldo and colleagues tested a large group of people with aphasia after stroke. They compared two putatively non-verbal WAIS subtests that differ in how much reasoning they demand: Picture Completion (spot the missing part) and Picture Arrangement (order a story sequence). They also updated earlier work on the Wisconsin Card Sorting Test. Separately they tested "Chelsea", a neurologically intact woman who had no effective language exposure until hearing aids at 32 and acquired only limited language after that.

**What they found.** Picture Completion was relatively spared and Picture Arrangement was not. People with the most profound core language deficits (impaired comprehension plus disordered output) did especially badly on problem solving. Chelsea did well on visuospatial tasks and was disproportionately impaired on Raven's Matrices and Picture Arrangement. The authors conclude that language supports complex reasoning, probably through verbal working memory and inner speech.

**Why it is in this directory.** ARC-114 needs the counter-case on file. Read carelessly, this paper says language is a substrate for reasoning. Read more carefully, the pattern fits ARC-114's own interface list. What breaks is the sequential, multi-step, hold-it-in-mind kind of reasoning, which is where a scaffold and working-memory aid would matter. Reasoning that can be done in one look survives. Anyone who has watched a patient talk themselves through a task will recognise this. Language is the notepad, not the mind doing the sums. The awkward point is that Kean et al. 2026 find formal logic intact even in severe aphasia. Either task type separates the findings (set-shifting and sequencing lean on inner speech, deduction does not), or lesion extent confounds this one. Large left-hemisphere strokes that abolish language also tend to damage neighbouring frontoparietal cortex.

**What it does not settle.** Like the rest of this pull, it measures worse performance, not changed reliance. ARC-114's compensation leg says a weaker non-linguistic system should lean *more* on language. That needs a design where the non-linguistic stage is weakened and language use is measured, for example private speech increasing with task difficulty in children, not one where language is removed.

**Confidence.** 0.5, mixed: a genuine boundary on how strongly ARC-114 can state independence, and good support for the scaffold role specifically.
