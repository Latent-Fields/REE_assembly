# Fedorenko, Piantadosi & Gibson (2024) -- Language is primarily a tool for communication rather than thought

**What it is.** A Nature Perspective, not a primary study. The authors introduce the human language network, then gather the evidence for a double dissociation between language and thought. People with global aphasia still do arithmetic, logic, theory of mind, music and navigation. Healthy adults do not engage the language network for those tasks. They then argue, from the structure of languages themselves, that language looks optimised for communication (efficient transmission under noise, word-length and ambiguity trade-offs) and not for internal computation.

**Relevance to ARC-114.** This is the strongest available articulation of ARC-114's central split. Reasoning lives somewhere else, and language is how its products get compressed, shared and coordinated across minds. It also answers the LLM objection indirectly. If language mainly reflects cognition, a system trained only on language inherits the traces of reasoning, not its machinery. That is exactly the distinction ARC-114 wants to draw.

**Where it and ARC-114 part company.** The paper's word is "primarily", and it plays down language's contribution to thinking. ARC-114 is more generous. It keeps working-memory support, long-horizon scaffolding and compensation as genuine interface functions. So the paper supports ARC-114's negative claim (not the substrate) more firmly than its positive list. The compensation leg, a directional prediction about increased reliance under weakness, is not addressed. A clinician will also notice what the dissociation data under-sample: multi-step, self-directed problem solving, where inner speech seems most active. Baldo et al. 2015, in this same directory, is the counterweight.

**Confidence.** 0.72, a well-argued synthesis in a top venue that maps well onto half the claim, discounted for being a perspective and for the interface-list mismatch.
