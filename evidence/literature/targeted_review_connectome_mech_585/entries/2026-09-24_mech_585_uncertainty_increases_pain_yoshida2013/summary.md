# Uncertainty increases pain: evidence for a novel mechanism of pain modulation involving the periaqueductal gray.

Yoshida, Seymour, Koltzenburg and Dolan showed participants the ratings a group of people had given to a painful heat stimulus before delivering the same stimulus. The mean of those ratings pulled subjective pain toward it. Separately, the spread of the ratings made the stimulus hurt more, an effect that tracked activity in the periaqueductal gray.

This bears on MECH-585 in two ways. It supports the idea that the reliability of a harm prediction is represented apart from its magnitude and has its own downstream effect. But the direction is instructive: uncertainty amplified the harm signal instead of reducing the weight of the prediction. In a precision-weighting scheme, an unreliable prediction should matter less; here low reliability made harm more salient.

The uncertainty came from other people, not from the subject's own forward model, so this is not a test of predictor self-reliability, calibration, or world-versus-model attribution. The locus is the brainstem.

Confidence 0.4, mixed. The useful implication is a design caution: the claim should say what low harm-predictor reliability does to harm salience, because the biological default appears to be caution, not discounting.
