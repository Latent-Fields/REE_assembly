# The periaqueductal gray and Bayesian integration in placebo analgesia.

Grahl, Onat and Buchel treated placebo hypoalgesia as Bayesian integration: an expectation about pain relief (prior) is combined with nociceptive input (likelihood), each weighted by its precision. In 62 subjects receiving heat pain during fMRI, placebo effects were larger in people whose treatment expectations were more precise, and scaled with the relative precision of the prior. The precision term had neural correlates in the periaqueductal gray and rostral ventromedial medulla.

This is the most direct support found for the part of MECH-585 that says a predicted-harm quantity carries its own reliability, stored separately from its magnitude, and that the reliability is actually used downstream. It also fits the claim's point that the weight should come from the harm prediction's own precision: the prior here is weighted by its own spread, not by some unrelated signal.

It stops short of the claim in two ways. The precision is the subject's belief precision, estimated from rating spread, with no test of whether it is calibrated against realised error; MECH-585 depends on calibration (its P2 precondition). And the precision governs how a percept is formed, not how a prediction error is attributed to world versus model. The anatomy is brainstem, not insula or ACC.

Confidence 0.55, supports, with the calibration gap stated.
