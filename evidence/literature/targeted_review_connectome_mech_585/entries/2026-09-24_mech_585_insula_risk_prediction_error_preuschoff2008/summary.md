# Human insula activation reflects risk prediction errors as well as risk.

Preuschoff, Quartz and Bossaerts ran a gambling task in which the probability of winning, and so the variance (risk) of the outcome, changed from trial to trial. Beyond the familiar reward prediction error, they modelled a risk prediction error: the difference between the squared prediction error that actually occurred and the squared error that was expected. An early anterior insula response tracked this risk prediction error, and a later response tracked predicted risk.

For MECH-585 this is the clearest neural precedent that a first-order prediction can travel with a learned estimate of how large its error should be, updated by its own error signal. That is the shape of the claim's ingredient (a), a per-state expected error, and it sits in the insula, which is also the region that carries aversive and interoceptive predictions.

It does not reach the claim's central distinction. The risk in this task is the variance of a lottery whose structure subjects are told; there is no learned forward model that could be wrong, so "expected error" here is world noise, not predictor unreliability. The study cannot show the insula separating world surprise from model error, and it has no analogue of the competence floor against a trivial baseline. It is also a reward task.

Confidence 0.5, mixed: supports the existence of a separately represented expected-error channel, silent on whether that channel measures the predictor's own reliability.
