# Anxious individuals have difficulty learning the causal statistics of aversive environments.

Browning and colleagues had participants learn which of two actions led to an electric shock, in blocks where the contingency was either stable or volatile. People low in trait anxiety raised their learning rate in volatile blocks and lowered it in stable ones, close to a Bayesian learner. People high in trait anxiety adjusted much less, and their pupil responses were less sensitive to volatility.

For MECH-585 this matters because it is aversive. It shows that harm predictions in humans come with an estimate of how far current predictions can be trusted, and that the estimate changes how much a harm prediction error moves the model. The anxious group is a useful natural contrast: a harm stream whose error signal is not properly discounted by reliability, which is roughly what the claim says the current REE substrate does.

The reliability being tracked is environmental volatility, a property of the world. That is the "world surprise" side of the claim's split; the study has nothing on estimating the predictor's own error or on beating a trivial baseline. The circuit locus is not measured (pupil, taken as a noradrenergic proxy), so it does not speak to insula or ACC directly.

Confidence 0.5, mixed: supports reliability-gated use of aversive PEs, and in the same breath shows that use can fail.
