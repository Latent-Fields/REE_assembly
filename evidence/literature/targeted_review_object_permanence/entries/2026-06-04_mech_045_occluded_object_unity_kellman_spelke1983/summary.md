# Perception of partly occluded objects in infancy (Kellman & Spelke 1983)

## What the paper did

Kellman and Spelke habituated 4-month-old infants to a rod whose top and bottom were visible
but whose centre was hidden behind a nearer block, then tested them with two fully revealed
displays: a single connected rod, and two rod-pieces with a gap where the occluder had been.
The logic of habituation: infants look longer at whatever differs from the entity they had
encoded. The headline result is that when the rod's visible ends moved together — common
motion behind the occluder — infants treated the *broken* display as the novel one, implying
they had encoded the occluded rod as a single connected object. When the rod was static, with
aligned edges, a simple regular shape, and homogeneous colour, infants showed no such unity
inference. Motion of the visible surfaces, not static "good form," was the decisive cue.

## Why it grounds MECH-045 / ARC-006

This is the *spatial-gap* sibling of object permanence. Full permanence (Baillargeon) is about
an object persisting across a *temporal* gap, when it is wholly hidden. Perceptual completion
is about an object being represented as one connected token across a *spatial* gap, when it is
partly hidden right now. The two are continuous: both require the system to posit unseen
existence — the hidden middle of the rod, the hidden box behind the screen — to maintain a
coherent object. Kellman & Spelke show the binding operation that produces a single token
entity bridging an occluding gap, which is exactly the unit MECH-045's object-file must hold
and ARC-006 calls a "bounded, bindable structure."

The detail that earns this paper its place in the permanence pull is the *cue*. Unity came from
common motion — spatiotemporal coherence — and not from static feature similarity. That lines
up with Xu & Carey's finding that infants individuate by spatiotemporal information before they
use property/kind information, and it sends a pointed message to REE. REE's existing object
stores key on the wrong things for this: the SD-039/MECH-292/293 ghost-goal bank keys on a
*spatial anchor*, and SD-049 identity keys on a *static type tag*. Neither is the motion-based
amodal-completion cue the infant uses to decide "these two visible bits are one thing." If the
permanence pillar is built to mirror biology, the binding cue should be spatiotemporal
coherence, not anchor-location or category — and the live substrate currently has neither.

## Limitations and caveats

Two honest limits. First, this is completion of a *currently partly-visible* object, not a
*fully-hidden* one persisting over time — it stops one inferential step short of permanence
proper. It shows the unit gets bound across a gap; it does not show the bound token survives
the object becoming wholly invisible. MECH-045 has to add that temporal-persistence step.
Second, common-motion binding is a perceptual-input grouping operation. It establishes the
unit; it is not itself a memory buffer. So the paper grounds *what should persist* (a
motion-coherent token) more than *the persistence itself*. I have logged both as failure
signatures rather than letting the entry over-claim.
