# Beyond pinball loss: quantiles are the right family, but pinball alone isn't calibrated (Chung et al. 2021)

**Claim under test:** SD-063 — E2's world-forward should carry a *conditional* per-input predictive-uncertainty head, in *distribution-free quantile/pinball* form, feeding E3 commitment gating. The implemented head (`e2_world_uncertainty.py`) predicts 9 quantile levels (0.1..0.9), monotone-rearranges them, and derives a predictive variance from the [q0.1, q0.9] IQR.

## What the paper did

Chung, Neiswanger, Char and Schneider (ICML 2021 UDL Workshop; later NeurIPS 2021) examine quantile regression as a route to uncertainty quantification. They open by noting exactly why quantiles are attractive: they describe complex predictive distributions *without parametric assumptions*, are interpretable in the target's units, allow easy construction of prediction intervals, and — they explicitly cite model-based reinforcement learning (Chua et al. 2018; Malik et al. 2019) as a motivating downstream application. The pinball (tilted-absolute) loss `ρ_τ(y, ŷ) = (ŷ - y)(𝟙{y ≤ ŷ} - τ)` is the standard objective for learning a quantile. Their central critical finding: **optimising the pinball loss is not, by itself, an adequate objective to achieve calibration.** Because pinball loss is a proper scoring rule it *implicitly* balances calibration and sharpness, but that balance is arbitrary and depends on the expressivity of the model class — a highly expressive network can be skewed toward sharpness at the expense of calibration. They propose two remedies: a model-agnostic quantile-regression procedure (MAQR) that estimates conditional quantiles from local empirical CDFs then regresses onto them, and a tunable combined loss that lets the user set an explicit calibration/sharpness balance. Empirically MAQR gives the best average calibration (ECE) on 7 of 8 UCI datasets and the best adversarial-group calibration on 6 of 8.

## Why it bears on SD-063 — and why it is *mixed*

**Supporting side.** This paper endorses SD-063's *choice of family*. Where the Kendall & Gal and PETS entries use Gaussian-NLL heads, Chung et al. argue for quantiles precisely because they are distribution-free and make no parametric assumption about the predictive distribution — the exact property the V3-EXQ-712 diagnostic exploited when `quantile_pinball` beat `mse_point`, `hetero_gaussian`, and `mixture_gaussian` on CRPS. And they name model-based RL as a target use case, which is structurally what E2→E3 is.

**Cautioning side.** Their headline result is a caveat that lands squarely on SD-063's implemented head. SD-063 trains a *raw* multi-level pinball head and reads an IQR off it. Chung et al. say a raw pinball head is not guaranteed calibrated, and that expressivity can push it toward sharpness over calibration. SD-063's monotone rearrangement of the quantiles fixes *crossing* (ordering) but does nothing for *average or adversarial-group calibration* in Chung's sense. Since E3's commit gate will act on the *magnitude* of E2's predictive spread, whether that magnitude is calibrated is not obviously irrelevant.

## Where the mapping strains

The two works optimise and measure different things. Chung et al. score calibration by ECE over quantile levels on UCI regression; V3-EXQ-712 scored CRPS and `precision_error_corr` on held-out latent transitions. A CRPS / error-correlation win does *not* certify Chung-style calibration — but, symmetrically, Chung-style miscalibration may not hurt SD-063 if E3's gate only needs a *monotone, error-correlated ranking* of "where is this prediction uncertain" rather than absolute coverage. Which of those E3 actually requires is unresolved in the claim as written. So the paper flags a genuine risk to the absolute magnitude of E2's spread without establishing that that magnitude is load-bearing for the specific gating use.

## Confidence

**0.60, mixed.** Source quality moderate — a strong UQ group, but a workshop-length venue. Mapping fidelity is good on the *form* (quantile/pinball is exactly SD-063's choice) yet the calibration metric differs from what SD-063 optimised. Transfer risk is elevated: UCI supervised regression is far from a latent forward-model commit gate. Net: this entry ratifies SD-063's distribution-free quantile decision while adding a concrete, actionable caveat — if a downstream experiment finds E3's gate is sensitive to spread *magnitude* (not just ranking), a post-hoc calibration step (MAQR-style, or conformalised quantile regression) should be layered on top of the raw pinball head.
