# Two-timescale AgRP regulation -- Su, Alhadeff & Betley 2017 (Cell Reports)

According to PubMed. Source: Su Z, Alhadeff AL, Betley JN. "Nutritive, Post-ingestive Signals Are the Primary Regulators of AgRP Neuron Activity." *Cell Reports* 21(10):2724-2736, 2017. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2017.11.036)

**What the paper did.** Using in vivo fiber-photometry of arcuate AgRP neurons together with manipulations of gut-derived satiation signals, Su et al. dissected the dynamics of hunger-neuron activity into two separable components. The first is a *rapid but transient* drop driven by the sensory detection of food (sight/smell at the cue). The second is a *slower, sustained* reduction that tracks the caloric content of what is actually ingested -- nutrients in the gut, they show, are both necessary and sufficient for the lasting reductions, and the magnitude of the slow reduction is proportional to calories obtained. They also found the rapid sensory inhibition is itself trainable: a single trial of nutritive experience reshapes the anticipatory cue response.

**Why it matters for MECH-306.** MECH-306's core requirement is that the drive signal feeding effective_benefit be carried on a *slow* timescale, because the fast component collapses at contact and cannot, on its own, hold the seeding threshold open. Su et al. supply direct evidence that the hunger circuit genuinely runs on two distinct timescales -- a fast transient and a slow sustained one -- rather than a single instantaneous readout. This is the architectural licence for REE to separate an instantaneous drive_level from a slow-traced (EMA or floored) drive used specifically for seeding. The claim's "sustained drive at contact, not instantaneous drive alone" framing is, in effect, the two-component decomposition this paper measures.

**Where the mapping strains.** The slow component Su et al. characterise is *satiation* -- a sustained inhibition of the hunger neurons -- not sustained hunger. Reading it as evidence for a sustained *drive* trace requires assuming the drive axis carries an analogous slow component of opposite sign, which the paper does not test. There is also the standard inferential gap: the study measures neural-activity dynamics, not any downstream seeding or goal-selection variable, so the link to z_goal seeding is one step removed. And the finding that the fast component is itself plastic (single-trial trainable) is a quiet warning: a REE model that hard-codes a fixed fast-collapse term would mis-model the learned anticipatory inhibition the biology shows.

**Confidence.** 0.80. High source quality (rigorous photometry plus causal gut-signal work, strong venue). Mapping fidelity moderate-high for the two-timescale architecture, trimmed by the satiation-versus-drive sign issue and the inferential step to a seeding variable. Together with Chen 2016 this pair establishes both that the fast signal collapses and that a separable slow signal exists -- the two halves MECH-306 needs.
