# Incentive salience as drive-modulated wanting -- Berridge 2006 (Psychopharmacology)

According to PubMed. Source: Berridge KC. "The debate over dopamine's role in reward: the case for incentive salience." *Psychopharmacology (Berl)* 191(3):391-431, 2006. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-006-0578-x)

**What the paper does.** This is the canonical theoretical statement of the incentive-salience ("wanting") account of mesolimbic dopamine. Berridge reviews the evidence across three competing explanations of dopamine's role in reward -- hedonic "liking," reward learning, and "wanting" -- and argues that the wanting hypothesis fits more of the data than either rival: dopamine is neither necessary nor sufficient for hedonic impact, is not needed for new learning, yet is causally required for normal "wanting" and can be sufficient to enhance cue-triggered incentive salience. The construct that matters most for our purposes is his claim that incentive salience is computed by *integrating Pavlovian associative cue information with the organism's current physiological state* -- so the same cue commands more wanting when the relevant drive state is high.

**Why it matters for MECH-306.** MECH-306's effective_benefit = drive x benefit_exposure is, read carefully, a computational rendering of exactly Berridge's integration rule. The benefit_exposure term is the Pavlovian/associative cue information; the drive term is the physiological state; their product is the incentive salience attributed to the benefit at that moment. The claim's insistence that drive must be present (floored or sustained) at the instant of contact for seeding to fire mirrors the theory's insistence that wanting is generated by combining cue with state *at the encounter* -- if the state has collapsed, the wanting it would have multiplied is gone. So this review supplies the principled "why" beneath the AgRP papers' mechanistic "how": the brain multiplies cue by drive, and REE does too.

**Where the mapping strains.** Berridge's incentive salience is graded and continuously re-computed -- a smooth multiplicative gate -- whereas MECH-306 operationalises seeding around a *threshold crossing*. The theory supports the drive-modulated-wanting principle but says nothing about a specific threshold implementation; if REE's seeding is a hard threshold on a summed quantity it may mis-model the graded, state-multiplicative character the theory describes. The construct is also explicitly dissociated from liking and from learning, attributed specifically to dopamine -- a REE drive-multiplier that quietly conflated wanting with hedonic benefit or with reward learning would violate the very dissociation this review is built on. And, being a cross-study theoretical review in the dopamine/addiction domain, the mapping is to the *principle*, not to any single measured datum.

**Confidence.** 0.70 -- deliberately below the three empirical AgRP papers. Source quality is high for a review but it is not a single experimental result; mapping fidelity to the drive-modulated-wanting principle is high; transfer risk is elevated because it is synthesised cross-study theory that must be ported from the dopamine/addiction frame to REE's drive-multiplier site. Its role in the cluster is to name the computational principle (cue x state) that the AgRP circuit papers then instantiate.
