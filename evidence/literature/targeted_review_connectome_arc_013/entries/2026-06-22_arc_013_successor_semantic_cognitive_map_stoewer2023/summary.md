# Neural-network formation of cognitive maps of semantic spaces (Stoewer, Schilling, Maier & Krauss, 2023)

According to PubMed, published in *Scientific Reports*, [DOI 10.1038/s41598-023-30307-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-30307-6).

**What the paper did.** The authors built a neural network that learns a cognitive map of a *non-spatial* space — 32 animal species encoded as feature vectors — using the multi-scale successor representation (SR), the mathematical principle they take to underlie place- and grid-cell computation. The network recovers the similarity structure of "animal space," and the map's *geometry changes with scale*: fine-grained maps spread the animals roughly evenly across the space, while coarse-grained maps cluster them by biological class (amphibians, mammals, insects). Novel or incomplete inputs can then be represented by interpolating on the learned map, with reconstruction accuracy reported up to ~95%, while next-neighbour prediction sits near ~30% (close to the theoretical ceiling once you allow that each item has several equally-valid successors).

**Why it bears on ARC-013.** ARC-013's two prongs are (i) hippocampal paths form a cognitive map and (ii) the carried information is persistent latent-space curvature. The Baram and Kim entries supply the neural grounding; this paper supplies the *computational existence proof* that the same SR machinery generalises beyond physical space and produces a map whose content is its multi-scale geometry. The class-clustering-at-coarse-scale result is a clean illustration of "curvature carries meaning": the abstraction (mammal vs insect) is not stored as a label but emerges from how the latent space folds at a given scale. That is congenial to REE's view of residue/path structure as a reusable latent scaffold over arbitrary spaces, not just rooms.

**The mapping, honestly bounded.** This is the weakest of the three ARC-013 entries on directness, and I have rated it accordingly. It is an in-silico proof on 32 hand-built vectors, not neural data; it shows SR *can* yield a curved, multi-scale latent map, not that the hippocampus's residue *is* that map, and certainly not that residue is separable from salience and uncertainty (the V3-EXQ-697 question). I also keep the two reported accuracies distinct: the ~30% next-neighbour figure says the forward map is approximate, while the ~95% figure is for interpolating held-out inputs — a different operation from the persistence ARC-013 cares about. So I treat this as corroborating *mechanism* (SR builds curved latent maps), not as evidence about residue persistence.

**Confidence.** I set this at 0.50 — deliberately at the boundary where mapping becomes indirect. Source quality is mid (peer-reviewed but a small toy model), mapping fidelity is moderate, and transfer risk is real because the inferential chain from a 32-item SR network to REE's residue substrate is long. It earns inclusion because it closes the loop with the two neural entries: human structure-transfer (Baram), mouse single-neuron geometry (Kim), and a computational account of why SR over any space yields the curved, scale-dependent map both describe (Stoewer). Together they support the geometry face of ARC-013 and leave the irreducibility face for experiment.
