# Hamid, Frank & Moore 2021 -- wave-like dopamine dynamics and credit assignment

**What they did.** Imaging dopamine axons and dopamine release across the whole dorsal striatum in mice, Hamid and colleagues found that dopamine isn't a uniform wash. Transients organise into travelling waves and cluster within functionally related subregions. The wave's direction depended on the task: from dorsomedial toward dorsolateral when reward depended on the animal's actions, and the other way when it didn't. They proposed a model in which these waves are shaped by inference about agency and steer credit to the specialised subregion that earned it. Dorsomedial dopamine during reward pursuit tracked how much instrumental control the animal had, and predicted its later behavioural adjustments.

**Why it cuts both ways for MECH-452.** The claim's *goal* is that credit lands on the loop that actually voted for the action, and this paper supports that goal strongly. The brain clearly cares about routing credit to the right subregion. But the claim's *mechanism* is "one shared delta_t, with all the locality in the eligibility trace," and that's exactly the premise this paper complicates. Here part of the locality lives in the signal itself: different regions get differently timed and differently shaped dopamine. The DMS-to-DLS gradient maps, loosely, onto REE's associative-to-motor loops.

**What I take from it.** I don't think it falsifies MECH-452. Trace locality (Yagishita, Shindou) and signal structure (Hamid) are more likely complementary than rival. But it means the claim, as written, may be modelling half the mechanism. And there's a live alternative REE hasn't registered: loop-specific or wave-modulated delta_t. If the independent-arm experiment finds that trace locality alone gives little credit-attribution advantage, this paper names the next hypothesis, and that result shouldn't be read as the brain not caring about locality.

**Confidence.** 0.58, recorded as `mixed`. A strong source, but the step from waves to credit assignment is a model supported by correlation, not a causal test.
