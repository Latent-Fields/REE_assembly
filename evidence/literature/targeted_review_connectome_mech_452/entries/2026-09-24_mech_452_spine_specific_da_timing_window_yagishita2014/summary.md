# Yagishita et al. 2014 -- a critical time window for dopamine at single spines

**What they did.** In mouse striatal slices, Yagishita and colleagues stimulated glutamatergic input to single dendritic spines (two-photon uncaging) and dopaminergic axons (optogenetics) separately, so they could control the timing between them. Dopamine enlarged a spine only if it arrived 0.3 to 2 seconds *after* that spine's glutamate input. Earlier or later did nothing. They traced the timing detector to a fast, spatially confined cAMP/PKA signal in thin distal dendrites, where high phosphodiesterase activity closes the window quickly.

**Why it matters for MECH-452.** MECH-452 says that even if the dopamine teaching signal is one shared broadcast, credit must be decided locally: update = shared delta_t x local eligibility trace x active synapse. This paper is about the cleanest cellular demonstration of that factorisation. The dopamine is everywhere, but only the spine that was just active, within a two-second window, takes the update. The claim's warning, that a too-broad or temporally smeared trace would deliver the right signal to the wrong synapses, reads almost like a description of what the phosphodiesterase is there to prevent. The brain spends biochemistry keeping the trace narrow.

**Where the mapping strains.** The locality here is at the level of single spines, far finer than REE's per-loop, per-channel traces. The paper supports "eligibility must be local and short." It doesn't specifically support "eligibility must be organised by loop," which is REE's grain choice in ARC-110. Loop locality is plausibly an emergent consequence of spine-level locality (different loops, different inputs, different active spines), but that step is our inference.

**Confidence.** 0.76. A superb source with a near-literal fit to the factorisation, docked for the grain mismatch. It also sharpens the claim's falsifier: the independent-arm test should sweep trace *width* in time as well as locality, since the biology says the window's narrowness is part of the mechanism.
