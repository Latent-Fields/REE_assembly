# Shindou, Shindou, Watanabe & Wickens 2018 -- a silent eligibility trace in striatum

**What they did.** Using whole-cell recordings in adult mouse striatal slices, the Wickens group tested the old hypothesis that synaptic activity leaves a silent tag which dopamine arriving later can convert into lasting change. In D1 neurons, pre-before-post pairing caused LTD that was masking a concurrent LTP. With the LTD blocked, the potentiation depended on Ca-permeable AMPA receptors, which showed a brief 2 to 4 second signature after pairing. Timed dopamine uncaging then showed potentiation *only* at synapses active about two seconds before dopamine arrived, not earlier and not later.

**Why it matters for MECH-452.** It is an independent, electrophysiological replication of the core factorisation Yagishita showed with imaging. The teaching signal is global, the tag is local and short, and only tagged synapses learn. That convergence across labs and methods is what lets me treat "credit locality lives in the trace" as a solid biological premise for MECH-452 rather than one lab's result.

**Where the mapping strains.** Two things. The trace was demonstrated in D1 neurons; the D2 population didn't even show the pre-post LTD. So the paper can't tell us whether indirect-pathway channels carry the same kind of local trace. That matters because REE's loop-local traces cover all gating channels, including the ones doing No-Go duty. And the grain is again the synapse, not the loop.

**Confidence.** 0.70. It corroborates rather than extends. Together with Yagishita 2014 it supports MECH-452's premise well. Neither paper touches the claim's specifically loop-level architecture or its behavioural consequence, which only the independent-arm REE experiment can settle.
