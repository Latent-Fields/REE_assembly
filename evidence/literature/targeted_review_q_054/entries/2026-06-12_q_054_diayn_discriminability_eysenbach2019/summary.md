# DIAYN (Eysenbach et al. 2019) — the diversity↔discriminability coupling Q-054 is about

Eysenbach B, Gupta A, Ibarz J, Levine S. "Diversity is All You Need: Learning Skills without a Reward Function." *ICLR 2019* ([arXiv:1802.06070](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06070)).

Q-054 asks a sharp, quantitative question: what is the minimum trajectory-class diversity floor — the Rung 1 `first_action_entropy` threshold — needed for the ARC-062 context discriminator to learn a reliable cut between reef and foraging contexts, and is the observed SP-CEM entropy lift of 0.497 nats above or below it? Before asking whether 0.497 is enough, it is worth grounding *why* there is a floor at all, and DIAYN is the cleanest statement of the underlying structure.

DIAYN learns a skill-conditioned policy by maximising the mutual information between visited states and a latent skill index, `I(S;Z) = H(Z) − H(Z|S)`, using a learned discriminator `q(z|s)` plus a maximum-entropy policy. The `−H(Z|S)` term says the skill must be *easy to infer from the states it visits*; the max-entropy term pushes each skill to spread out. The policy and discriminator play a cooperative game — the policy seeks states that make the discriminator's job easy, the discriminator rewards the policy for being identifiable. Read in REE's vocabulary, ARC-062's reef/forage discriminator *is* `q(z|s)`, the upstream `first_action_entropy` that ARC-065 must generate *is* the policy-entropy term, and the lower bound Q-054 cites (the mutual information between context and action) *is* the DIAYN objective. So DIAYN supports the existence and the form of the diversity→discriminability dependence Q-054 is built on.

What DIAYN does *not* give is the number Q-054 actually wants. It never reports a critical entropy below which discrimination fails; it optimises toward a mutual-information optimum and the interesting regime is "as much identifiable diversity as possible," not "the least diversity that still works." So this is framework-level support, not a threshold measurement — the 0.497-nat sufficiency question stays empirical, which is what V3-EXQ-543b is for.

There is also a subtler caution that I think is the most useful thing this paper contributes to Q-054. In DIAYN the discriminator and policy are trained *jointly*; the discriminator's reward shapes the behavioural stream rather than passively reading a frozen one. If ARC-062 is allowed to do the same — to shape ARC-065's stream toward discriminability rather than scoring a fixed upstream entropy — then Q-054's "static floor" framing may be partly ill-posed, because the floor would be a moving target co-determined by the discriminator. Whether REE wants a fixed-stream-then-discriminate pipeline or a jointly-trained one is a design fork worth making explicit before the sweep. Confidence 0.62: foundational and well-cited, but it answers the qualitative coupling, not the quantitative floor, and the joint-vs-fixed-stream gap is a genuine transfer risk.
