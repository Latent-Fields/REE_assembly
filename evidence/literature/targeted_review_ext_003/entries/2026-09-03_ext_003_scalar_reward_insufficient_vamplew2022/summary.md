# Scalar reward is not enough (Vamplew et al., 2022) — EXT-003

**Source:** Vamplew P, Smith BJ, Källström J, de Oliveira Ramos G, Rădulescu R, Roijers DM, Hayes CF, Heintz F, Mannion P, Libin PJK, Dazeley R, Foale C. *Scalar reward is not enough: a response to Silver, Singh, Precup and Sutton (2021)*. Autonomous Agents and Multi-Agent Systems 36:41 (2022). DOI 10.1007/s10458-022-09575-5. Preprint: arXiv:2112.15422.

## What the paper does

This is a formal reply to Silver et al.'s *Reward is Enough* (also in this pull, as the counterweight). The authors concede more than one might expect: they do not dispute that intelligence might be understood as reward maximisation. What they contest is the quiet auxiliary assumption — that the reward being maximised can be a scalar. Their case runs on three tracks. A computational track argues that scalarisation forecloses behaviours a multi-objective agent can reach, most sharply through the observation that solutions lying in *concave regions of the Pareto front* are optimal trade-offs for which no linear weighting exists that would select them; they are not merely hard to find under scalarisation, they are unreachable by it. A biological track surveys evidence that organisms have no single primary objective — even satisfying a single nutrient requirement (their salt example) recruits taste receptors, interoceptive ingestion signals and blood osmolality detectors as separate proxy objectives — and that humans hold multiple "irreducible and irreconcilable" moral and life objectives. A safety track argues that scalar reward design exposes AGI to unacceptable risk of unsafe or unethical behaviour, citing reward misspecification and reward hacking as already-observed failures in trivial systems that only worsen as agents generalise.

## The finding that matters for EXT-003

Section 4.3 is the part I would keep if I could keep only one paragraph. It anticipates the strongest empirical objection to MECH-069 and disarms it. The objection is simple: mammals appear to have a single dominant reward-prediction-error carrier, so why should REE need three separate learning channels? The authors' answer is that finding one neurotransmitter performing the RPE function "is not the same as saying mammalian RL is single-objective" — that signal "is properly thought of as hard-coded to achieve multiple objectives." This is exactly the distinction REE needs to be able to draw, and it is worth stating in REE's own vocabulary because the two are easy to conflate: *the number of error-carrying signals is not the same quantity as the number of credit-assignment channels*. MECH-069 is a claim about the latter. E1's sensory prediction error, E2's motor-sensory error on `z_gamma`, and E3's harm/goal error are incommensurable not because they must travel on distinct wires but because each indexes a different kind of fidelity, and no one of them can be recovered from another.

The concave-Pareto-front point does something else useful: it gives "incommensurable" a formal reading. It is tempting to hear MECH-069 as saying only that scalarisation is *lossy* — that you can collapse three channels into one but you throw information away. The stronger and more interesting statement is that there exist optimal trade-offs between the channels that no scalarisation can select at all, at any weighting. If MECH-069 is to be more than an efficiency preference — and ARC-021 explicitly claims the separation is "not for efficiency; it is for correct credit assignment" — this is the shape the argument has to take.

## Limitations and the honest caveats

The paper proves nothing and measures nothing. It is argument, and citing it establishes that a well-credentialed position exists and has been defended in a peer-reviewed venue, not that the position is correct. That alone caps what this entry can carry.

The mapping caveat is sharper and I want it on the record rather than buried. Vamplew et al. partition objectives one way — homeostatic drives, moral values, life goals — and MECH-069 partitions them a different way, by the *epistemic type* of the error: world-model fidelity, motor-model fidelity, ethical and purposive fidelity. Those partitions are compatible; they are not the same claim. So this paper supports "objectives are irreducibly plural, and scalarising them is a mistake" and it does not, by itself, support "the plurality is exactly three and cuts along E1/E2/E3." The specific three-way cut is REE's own commitment and needs its own evidence. Anyone using this entry to argue that the literature endorses ARC-021's particular architecture is overreading it.

There is also a scope mismatch worth naming. EXT-003's `subject` is `llm.reward_hacking`, and this paper is about reinforcement learning and AGI in general — transformers and RLHF do not appear. The LLM-specific instantiation of the failure mode is carried by Gao et al. (2023) in this same pull; this entry supplies the principle, not the instance.
