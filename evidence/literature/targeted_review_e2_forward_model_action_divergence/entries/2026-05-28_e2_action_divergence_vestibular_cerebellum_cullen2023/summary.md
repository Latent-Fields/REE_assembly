# Vestibular cerebellum and corollary-discharge gating (Cullen 2023)

According to PubMed, [Cullen (2023), *Internal models of self-motion: neural computations by the vestibular cerebellum*, Trends in Neurosciences](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tins.2023.08.009).

**What the paper does.** Cullen synthesises two decades of single-unit recording from the vestibular cerebellum -- floccular lobe, anterior vermis, nodulus and ventral uvula -- across passive and voluntary self-motion. The central finding for our purposes is that the cerebellum's prediction of vestibular consequences is *gated by which specific action is being executed*. When the head is moved passively, the vestibular cerebellum responds one way. When the head is moved by a voluntary motor command (which delivers a corollary discharge to the cerebellum), the same vestibular afferent input is processed differently -- the predicted sensory consequence has been built into the cerebellar response, and the residual mismatch alone propagates. The review explicitly highlights recent work establishing that the cerebellum builds predictive models of self-generated movement at the action-specific level.

**Why it matters for REE.** This is the strongest circuit-level biological evidence in the lit-pull. The cerebellum does not maintain an action-averaged prediction that downstream regions then disambiguate; it maintains action-specific predictions gated by the corollary-discharge signal *at the prediction step itself*. That is precisely the architectural property V3-EXQ-571 documents as broken in REE-v3. The cerebellar substrate goes to substantial structural lengths -- a dedicated corollary-discharge pathway, separate mossy-fibre projections to cortex and nuclei, a granule-cell layer combinatorial expansion -- to preserve action-specificity through the forward step. None of that machinery would be necessary if action identity could be cleanly read off later from a generic prediction; the cerebellum acts as if it cannot.

The corollary-discharge mechanism specifically is what transfers to REE. The right architectural property for E2 is not just "actions are inputs to the forward model" but "the action signal *gates* how the prediction is computed, such that the predicted next-state carries the action's signature." Option (i)'s first-action one-hot concatenation at the consumer is a downstream proxy for this; option (ii)'s E2 fix is the biological homologue. The two converge on the same outcome at the consumer but commit to different architectures.

**Mapping caveat.** Cullen's review is specifically about the vestibular cerebellum and self-motion -- eye, head, and body movement in primates. REE's E2 operates on a much more abstract latent space, and there is no vestibular-style sensory channel involved. The transferable lesson is the *form* of action-specific gating, not the specific implementation (vestibular wiring does not translate). The cerebro-cerebellar review (Tanaka 2020) is the closer architectural analogue. I include Cullen because the corollary-discharge mechanism is the load-bearing piece of the biological forward-model story that most directly enforces action-specificity at the prediction step.

**Confidence.** 0.71. Source quality is very high (Cullen lab, Trends in Neurosciences). Mapping fidelity is the limiting factor: 0.62 because the substrate gap (vestibular self-motion vs abstract latent rollout) is real. Transfer risk at 0.38 reflects that we are transferring a mechanism (corollary-discharge gating) rather than a wiring diagram.

**Lit-only verdict, not governance.** Supports option (ii) on architectural-mechanism grounds. The biological forward model preserves action-specificity through the prediction step itself, via corollary-discharge gating; it does not rely on downstream consumers extracting action identity from a generic prediction. If REE wants to claim cerebellar substrate fidelity, E2 should do likewise.
