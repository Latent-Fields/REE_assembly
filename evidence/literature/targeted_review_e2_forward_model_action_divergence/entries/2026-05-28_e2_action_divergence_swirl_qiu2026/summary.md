# SWIRL (Qiu et al. 2026)

**Source.** Qiu, Zhao, Li, Ziser, Korhonen, Cohen & Ponti, *Self-Improving World Modelling with Latent Actions*, arXiv preprint (Feb 2026). [arXiv:2602.06130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06130).

**What the paper does.** SWIRL trains a forward world model whose objective is to maximise the conditional mutual information between predicted next states and latent action variables, given the prior state. The authors treat actions as hidden variables that must be jointly inferred and the next-state prediction as the channel through which action identity has to be conserved. The result is a self-improving loop: better latent-action inference produces sharper next-state predictions, which produces better latent actions. They report substantial benchmark gains (16-28% across AURORABench, ByteMorph, WorldPredictionBench, StableToolBench).

**Why it matters for REE.** SWIRL gives us a third independent ML lever for the same family of fix (option ii). PLSM enforces action-effect *factorisation* via an MI minimisation; contrastive RSSM enforces action-conditional *distinguishability* via a discriminative loss; SWIRL enforces action-identifiability via MI *maximisation* between (state, action) and predicted next-state. All three address the same underlying failure: a learned forward model that lets action identity leak away during the prediction step. The fact that the literature has converged on three structurally distinct training objectives that all attack this problem is itself informative -- it tells us the failure mode is recognised, named, and treated as a real architectural concern in the field, not as an artefact of any particular implementation.

For REE-v3 specifically, a SWIRL-style auxiliary objective on E2 would penalise the model for the V3-EXQ-571 failure directly: if K candidate first-actions produce identical predicted z_world, the mutual information between (z_t, a_t) and z_{t+1} is at its floor across the K-batch, and the objective fires. The framing is the contrapositive of PLSM's, but the diagnostic signal is the same.

**Mapping caveat.** Two reservations. First, this is a February 2026 preprint and has not been peer-reviewed; the strong benchmark numbers should be treated as preliminary. Second, SWIRL frames actions as *inferred latent variables*, which is essentially the inverse-dynamics problem. REE's actions are observable inputs -- we know perfectly well which action the CEM candidate proposed; what we don't know is whether E2's predicted next-state preserves that. The MI-maximisation objective transfers cleanly to REE's setting (you maximise MI between known action and predicted next-state, rather than inferring action); the latent-action machinery does not.

**Confidence.** 0.60 -- the lowest of the three ML entries here. Source quality is reduced by the preprint status. Mapping fidelity is solid for the objective itself but requires translation from the latent-action framing. Transfer risk is moderate. I include it because the three ML papers together represent a triangulation: when reconstruction, MI-factorisation, and MI-maximisation all flag the same issue and offer different fixes for it, the existence of the problem is no longer the open question.

**Lit-only verdict, not governance.** Third independent ML lever for option (ii). When choosing the actual training objective for an E2 fix, the choice between PLSM (factorise out action effect), contrastive RSSM (discriminative next-state), and SWIRL-style MI maximisation is a design question -- all three are tractable starting points. PLSM has the highest empirical maturity; contrastive RSSM has the simplest implementation; SWIRL has the most recent benchmark evidence but the loosest peer review.
