# FPC and alPFC in counterfactual rollout (Miyamoto, Rushworth, Shea 2023)

According to PubMed, [Miyamoto, Rushworth & Shea (2023), *Imagining the future self through thought experiments*, Trends in Cognitive Sciences](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2023.01.005).

**What the paper does.** The authors propose a dissociation between two anterior prefrontal regions in the cognitive architecture of counterfactual thought. Frontopolar cortex (FPC) keeps track of and evaluates *alternative choices not taken* -- what might have happened had events unfolded differently. Anterior lateral prefrontal cortex (alPFC) compares *simulations of possible future scenarios* -- what might happen under different courses of action -- and evaluates their reward values. Together these two regions support the construction of suppositional or *Gedankenexperimente* scenarios. The review synthesises primate single-unit, lesion, and human imaging evidence.

**Why it matters for REE.** This is the closest biological reference for what REE's E2-driven counterfactual rollout plus E3 evaluation stack is supposed to do at the cognitive level. ARC-065 commits to a diversity-generation pathway whose downstream consumer is exactly this comparison function: E3 has to evaluate distinct predicted futures and prefer one. ARC-062 commits to rule apprehension whose substrate likewise depends on comparing simulated rollouts. SD-033a (lateral PFC top-down bias) and SD-033b (orbitofrontal value) are REE's substrate analogues of the alPFC and ventral-PFC comparison machinery.

The constraint the paper imposes is functional: the cognitive function of comparing alternative future scenarios is only computationally well-defined if those scenarios are *distinguishable* in the neural representation. If alPFC's simulation comparator received K candidate future representations that were all identical at one-step horizons, there would be nothing to compare. The V3-EXQ-571 collapse therefore describes a situation in which REE's substrate analogue of alPFC's comparator function is operating on a degenerate input -- which is exactly what the V3-EXQ-569 / 571 / 573 behavioural arms have shown empirically (all six arms producing identical entropy).

This is the second strong biological prior in favour of option (ii). The Tanaka et al. entry covers the motor / cerebellar side of the forward-model literature; Miyamoto et al. covers the prefrontal counterfactual-rollout side. They converge: action identity must be preserved across the prediction step both for motor control and for cognitive comparison. If REE wants either function, the prediction step has to preserve it.

**Mapping caveat.** This is a conceptual / cognitive-architecture review, not a circuit-level measurement of per-action divergence in FPC or alPFC populations. The biological constraint is functional ("the cognitive function requires distinguishable simulations") rather than quantitative. There is a parallel literature in primate prefrontal single-unit recording (Mante & Sussillo, Schuck, Stalnaker) that has measured action-conditional population geometry directly, but I am citing the higher-level review here because it ties the function to the REE-side claims most directly. A circuit-level entry could be added as a follow-up if needed.

**Confidence.** 0.70. Source quality is high (Trends in Cognitive Sciences, Rushworth lineage). Mapping fidelity is strong because REE's design explicitly commits to the alPFC-style comparison architecture. Transfer risk is the residual gap between qualitative ("distinguishable scenarios required") and quantitative ("by how much, at what horizon") -- the paper does not give us a threshold.

**Lit-only verdict, not governance.** Supports option (ii) on cognitive-function-faithfulness grounds. Combined with Tanaka et al.'s motor-side argument, the biology side of this lit-pull is consistently aligned: both the cerebellar reference and the prefrontal counterfactual reference treat per-action distinguishability as a constitutive property of forward modelling, not as a property to be emergent. A workaround at the consumer level (option i) functions, but it commits REE to an architecture the biology does not require.
