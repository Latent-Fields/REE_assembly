# Contrastive RSSM (Srivastava et al. 2021)

**Source.** Srivastava, Talbott, Bertran Lopez, Zhai & Susskind, *Robust Robotic Control from Pixels using Contrastive Recurrent State-Space Models*, NeurIPS 2021 Deep RL Workshop. [arXiv:2112.01163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01163).

**What the paper does.** The authors swap the reconstruction loss in a standard Recurrent State-Space Model for a contrastive next-state prediction loss: given (s_t, a_t), the model is asked to identify the true next-state representation against a batch of distractors. They show this yields more robust control under visual perturbations (camera shake, background changes) and outperforms reward-divergence-based bisimulation methods.

**Why it matters for REE.** Reconstruction-based latent dynamics losses do not, by construction, penalise action-conditional indistinguishability. If E2's training objective is "predict next z_world from (z_t, a_t)" and the cheapest way to do that in expectation is to learn a state-dominated predictor where actions contribute marginally, then gradient descent will find that solution -- especially when state dynamics are slow and action effect is small. A contrastive objective directly forbids this: you literally cannot identify the correct (s_t, a_t) -> s_{t+1} pair against distractors if your model collapses different actions to the same next-state representation. Whatever the model has to do to win the contrastive task, preserving per-action distinguishability is necessary.

This entry supports option (ii) (fix E2) along the same lines as PLSM but via a different mechanism. Where PLSM forces action-effect factorisation through an MI regulariser, contrastive RSSM forces it through the training objective itself. For REE-v3, the relevant translation is: even without re-architecting E2, swapping a portion of its training loss for an action-conditional contrastive term would directly target the V3-EXQ-571 failure.

**Mapping caveat.** Two concerns. First, the paper is a workshop submission and does not report a metric like "pairwise distance between predicted latents across actions" -- the claim that contrastive losses prevent K-action collapse is structural rather than empirically demonstrated for our specific failure mode. Second, contrastive RSSM in the paper uses pixel-derived negative samples; REE's E2 lives in a learned z_gamma space, so the negative-sample distribution has to be redesigned. Naively sampling other candidate trajectories from the same CEM batch would be a reasonable starting point, but the choice would need experimentation.

**Confidence.** 0.68. Source quality is moderate (workshop). Mapping fidelity is decent -- the structural argument is clean -- but the absence of a direct per-action distinguishability result for our setting is the main reservation. Transfer risk is bounded but real: contrastive learning's effectiveness depends heavily on the negative-sample distribution, and a poorly chosen one can produce no signal.

**Lit-only verdict, not governance.** Supports option (ii) by showing a second concrete training-objective mechanism (contrastive next-state) that would address the K-action collapse. The choice between PLSM-style MI regularisation and contrastive next-state prediction is downstream of the architectural decision; both are evidence that the ML field treats this as a tractable problem with known solutions.

**Cross-referenced for MECH-520 (2026-08-26, not re-pulled).** MECH-520 claims a value-carved representation degenerates without a cross-horizon predictive obligation to hold it open. This paper's finding -- reconstruction-shaped losses are agnostic to action-conditional collapse, while a contrastive next-state alternative is not -- is a narrow, single-axis (action identity only) instance of that same general pattern: an objective shaped around one thing (pixel reconstruction here, value elsewhere) admits collapse along axes it doesn't explicitly police, and a differently-shaped predictive/contrastive term can close that gap.
