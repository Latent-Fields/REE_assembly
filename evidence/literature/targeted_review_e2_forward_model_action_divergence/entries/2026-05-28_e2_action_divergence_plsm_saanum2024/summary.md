# PLSM (Saanum, Dayan, Schulz 2024)

**Source.** Saanum, Dayan & Schulz, *Simplifying Latent Dynamics with Softly State-Invariant World Models*, NeurIPS 2024. [arXiv:2401.17835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17835).

**What the paper does.** The authors observe that current world models lack a systematic representation of action effects: actions enter the recurrent latent transition, but nothing in the loss function compels the learned dynamics to make the action's contribution legible, separable, or consistent across states. They introduce the *Parsimonious Latent Space Model* (PLSM), which adds a regulariser minimising the mutual information between the latent state and the change an action produces in the latent state, conditioned on the action itself. The result is a soft factorisation: a state-dependent prior plus an action-conditioned delta that is largely independent of where in state space the agent is. The paper demonstrates that this regularisation can be combined with three different downstream uses (future latent state prediction, pixel prediction, planning), with measurable improvements in all three.

**Why it matters for REE.** The failure mode that surfaced in [v3_exq_571_root_cause_2026-05-25.md](../../../planning/v3_exq_571_root_cause_2026-05-25.md) is, mechanically, the symptom that PLSM is designed to prevent. CEM proposes K candidates that differ only in their first action one-hot. E2's world-forward predictor compresses them to identical first-step z_world (`cand_world_pairwise_dist = 0.0000`) -- the action's contribution to the next latent has been folded into the state-update path and lost. PLSM's regulariser is exactly the architectural lever option (ii) of the 2026-05-25 disposition would need: rather than relying on emergent action-conditioning in E2's learned dynamics, factorise z_{t+1} explicitly into a state-prior term and an action-effect term, and penalise the model for letting them entangle. The literature names the problem and offers a concrete training-objective fix.

**Mapping caveat.** PLSM was evaluated in vision-based RL where the latent is being learned end-to-end from pixels. REE-v3's substrate is different in a way that may either help or hurt the transfer. E1 has already produced a slow z_world before E2 sees it; E2 acts on `z_gamma` (the unified conceptual sensorium per the architecture docs), not on raw observations. If the per-action compression in E2 is happening in the action-fan-in layer specifically (small action embedding bandwidth, or batch-averaging across K candidates during training), PLSM's regulariser is the right lever. If the compression is downstream -- for example, an MLP that mixes action embedding and latent and squashes the result through a small bottleneck -- PLSM alone may not be sufficient and would need architectural restructuring to make the factorisation expressible. Honest read: PLSM points at the right *kind* of fix without pre-committing us to the right *instance*.

**Confidence.** I'm at 0.78. Source quality is high (NeurIPS, Dayan/Schulz lineage). Mapping fidelity is the limiting factor: PLSM addresses the same failure family but in a different substrate, and the regulariser would have to be re-derived for REE's latent-stack-after-E1 setting before we'd know it works. Transfer risk is real but bounded -- the mathematics generalises; what doesn't generalise automatically is the architectural placement.

**Lit-only verdict, not governance.** This entry supports option (ii) (fix E2 to preserve per-action divergence) by showing the ML literature has both diagnosed the failure family and proposed a concrete training-objective remedy. It is silent on whether REE's specific compression is best addressed by PLSM-style MI regularisation, by an architectural change to E2's action fan-in, or by sourcing per-candidate signal upstream (option iii). That choice is governance-side.
