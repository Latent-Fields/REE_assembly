# Sequences acquire brackets, and the brackets do not know about value (Jin & Costa 2010)

Mice ran a self-paced operant task in which a particular sequence of lever presses earned an
outcome. Jin and Costa recorded from nigrostriatal circuits while the animals learned it, and found
neurons whose activity specifically marked the *initiation* or the *termination* of each sequence.
The signals emerged as the sequence was learned rather than being present from the start, were
specific to particular actions, and -- the clause that matters most here -- were not explained by
interval timing, movement speed, or action value. Disrupting striatal circuit function genetically
prevented the start/stop activity from developing and selectively impaired sequence learning, which
makes the link causal rather than correlational.

For Q-085 this supplies the structural half of the picture. The question asks whether committed-macro
grain silently sets an interval over which the planner cannot intervene. That framing presupposes
that a committed sequence is a bracketed object with addressable edges -- and here is a circuit
that literally constructs those edges as the sequence consolidates. Boundaries are where the
representation offers a handle; between them, the sequence runs.

The value-blindness result is the part I find genuinely striking, because it echoes the substrate
finding that motivated Q-085 in the first place. Verified at source, V3 can release a committed
trajectory mid-execution through exactly five mechanisms: acute z_harm threat (MECH-091), degraded
execution readiness (MECH-342), the rung-6 natural-commit duration lever under SD-033e frontopolar
pressure, V_s prediction failure on the remaining macro (MECH-321/ARC-070), and SD-034 closure
de-commit. Not one of them reads whether the committed outcome is still worth having. Jin and
Costa's bracket signal is value-blind too. Two systems, built by completely different processes,
arriving at commitment machinery that is orthogonal to outcome value -- that is worth noting even if
neither observation causes the other.

The caveat is important and I want to state it in the direction that resists my own conclusion. That
the start/stop signal does not encode action value is *not* evidence that nothing can interrupt a
sequence on value grounds. Jin and Costa ran no devaluation test. Ostlund's shams, in the other
entry in this directory, demonstrably withheld a whole chunk when its outcome was devalued. So the
honest reading is: sequence boundaries exist, are learned, and are themselves value-blind. The
stronger claim -- that mid-sequence value intervention is impossible -- is a V3 substrate fact,
established by reading the release set, not something this paper licenses.

Two further limits. There is no dose-response: sequences were short and length was never
manipulated, so nothing here bears on whether the uncorrectable interval scales with grain, which is
the monotonicity Q-085's RESOLVED-YES criterion turns on. And the emergence-with-learning result is
worth carrying into the experimental design as a readiness point -- boundaries are a property of a
*consolidated* sequence. That is the same failure mode V3-EXQ-810 hit when chunks crystallised on
seed 101 and not on 202 or 303, and it is why Q-085's gate (a) treats zero committed
`arc071_chunk` trajectories as a readiness failure rather than a null.

Direction supports, confidence 0.64.
