# Warden et al. 2012 -- a projection-specific channel selects active engagement (ARC-079)

**What they did.** Warden and colleagues built a continuous readout of active responding in the forced swim test and recorded mPFC single units while rats swam. They then used optogenetics in two ways: activating mPFC principal cells in bulk, and activating only the cells defined by where they project, either the dorsal raphe or the lateral habenula.

**What they found.** Many mPFC neurons tracked the moment-to-moment decision to act, not movement as such. Bulk activation of mPFC did nothing to behaviour. Activating the DRN-projecting cells produced a fast, reversible shift into the active state, and it did not change open-field locomotion, so this was not general motor drive. Activating the LHb-projecting cells did the opposite and reduced active responding.

**Mapping to ARC-079.** The strongest point of contact is separability. ARC-079 insists, with MECH-340, that failure-to-disengage and over-eager disengagement are about a *missing or miswired separable gate*, not a mistuned global weight. Here global prefrontal drive is causally inert, and a specific wiring-defined channel does the selecting. That is the right kind of evidence for the architectural claim.

**The complication.** The LHb projection matters. If the passive state can also be actively *driven*, then disengagement is not only "the gate not firing." ARC-079's text makes abandonment the absence of persistence. The biology suggests at least two channels, an engage-gate and a disengage-driver, and possibly an arbitration between them. That does not falsify ARC-079, but it marks a place where the claim may be simpler than its substrate. The forced swim test also carries interpretive baggage: is immobility despair, energy-saving, or a learned strategy?

**Confidence.** 0.55: excellent causal methodology, a meaningful separability result, a distant behavioural proxy, and one qualifying finding recorded as a failure signature.
