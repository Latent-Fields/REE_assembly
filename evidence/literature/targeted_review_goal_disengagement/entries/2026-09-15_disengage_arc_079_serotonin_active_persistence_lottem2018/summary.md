# Lottem et al. 2018 -- serotonin extends active persistence at a depleting site (ARC-079, mixed)

**What they did.** Mice foraged in a task where reward at a site was probabilistic and ran out, so they had to infer when to give up and move on. Exploiting a site needed active nose-pokes, which separated *active* persistence from mere inaction. DRN serotonin neurons were activated optogenetically.

**What they found.** Serotonin activation made mice keep trying longer before they left. The authors use this to argue against the old "5-HT = behavioural inhibition" account: here serotonin favours effortful persistence, not passivity.

**How it bears on ARC-079.** Functionally it fits. Leaving a depleted site is the baseline, and continuing to try is an operation a modulatory signal can turn up. That is persistence-as-gated rather than disengagement-as-gated. The task also makes the gate input an *attainability* inference (is there anything left here?), which lines up with the unattainability appraisal ARC-079 borrows from Klinger and Wrosch.

**Where it doesn't fit.** ARC-079's circuit story comes from the stressor-controllability work, where DRN activation drives the passive default and mPFC control inhibits it. Here DRN activation drives *persistence*. Both can be true, since DRN is heterogeneous and context changes everything between shock and foraging. But it means REE should not implement the persistence gate as a single serotonergic dial. It also shows attainability inference feeding persistence, which is somewhat broader than MECH-340's deliberately control-only appraisal. Is control detection really the only admissible input, or is "can this still pay off?" a legitimate gate variable too? That sits squarely in Q-053.

**Confidence.** 0.48, mixed. Good causal evidence for the functional shape, with a real conflict at the level of substrate.
