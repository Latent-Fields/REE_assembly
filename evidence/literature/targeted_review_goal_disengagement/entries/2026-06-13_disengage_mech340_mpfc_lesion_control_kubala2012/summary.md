# Remove the detector and the controllability benefit vanishes (Kubala et al. 2012)

**Claim under test:** MECH-340 — the persistence/efficacy gate. Persistence is a gated override of a disengagement default; the gate reads a control/efficacy-detection signal, not accumulated failure, effort-cost, or staleness. Where Baratta et al. 2018 supplies the *separability and gain-of-function* side of the argument (the gate is a distinct circuit, present-but-silent, pharmacologically restorable), this paper supplies the **necessity / loss-of-function** side.

## What the paper did

Kubala and colleagues ran the stressor-controllability design in adolescent rats: escapable versus yoked-inescapable tailshock, with social exploration and dorsal-raphe activation as readouts. They first replicate the standard "stressor controllability effect" — escapable shock, unlike inescapable shock, neither reduces juvenile social exploration nor activates the serotonergic dorsal raphe; behavioural control mitigates the stressor's consequences. They then show two things that matter for MECH-340. First, **excitotoxic lesions of the medial prefrontal cortex prevent the effect**: with mPFC removed, escapable and inescapable stress become equivalent — control no longer protects. Second, a **controllable adolescent stressor inoculates** against an inescapable stressor in adulthood, so a prior experience of control shifts the later response.

## Why this is the necessity evidence MECH-340 needs

MECH-340 places the burden of persistence on an explicit control/efficacy detector. The strong form of that claim is that persistence depends on *detection* of control, not on control being objectively available. The lesion result is the direct test: control *is* available to the lesioned escapable animals — they can terminate the shock — yet without an intact mPFC detector the benefit disappears and the dorsal-raphe-mediated default proceeds. That is exactly the dissociation the claim predicts: knock out the gate and the system reverts to disengagement regardless of the objective facts. Read alongside Baratta 2018, the bracket is clean — present-but-unengaged gives no benefit and switching the circuit on restores it (gain-of-function); removing the circuit abolishes the benefit (loss-of-function). Both point at the same architecture: a separable, necessary control-detection gate sitting over a disengagement default.

The inoculation result speaks to a subtler part of the claim. MECH-340's framing (via Wrosch 2003 and Klinger 1975) is that the gate is held by an *appraisal* that the goal is still under some control, and that the appraisal is reengagement-coupled — history matters by reshaping the appraisal, not by accumulating a failure or effort tally. A single controllable experience protecting against later uncontrollable stress is consistent with history acting through a durable control-expectation appraisal rather than through a running cost counter, which is the side of the hard-negative set MECH-340 cares about.

## Caveats

This is rodent aversive neuroscience in adolescents, with social-exploration and serotonergic readouts — not an agent re-probing a stored goal. The lesion establishes *that* an mPFC control detector is necessary for the buffering (persistence is gate-dependent); it does not tell us *which* appraisal variables feed the gate. MECH-340 deliberately leaves that to Q-053, so this entry should not be read as evidence for any particular control signal — only for the existence and necessity of a detector-gated override. The adolescent developmental specifics (and the developmental inoculation window) have no current REE analogue and are not imported.
