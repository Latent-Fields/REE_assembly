# The cell that has to be non-zero before any of the others mean anything

MECH-024 is written with an unusual amount of methodological conscience, and the sentence that does most
of the work is the first one: *each of the three targeted lesions must move its own readout — a non-zero
diagonal — before any off-diagonal is interpretable.* An inert lesion makes the whole matrix vacuous. You
can run nine expensive cells, find a beautifully clean pattern of nulls off the diagonal, and have
learned nothing at all, because what you actually demonstrated is that your lesions did not do anything.

This paper is the human demonstration for the ethics diagonal. Koenigs and colleagues took six patients
with focal bilateral ventromedial prefrontal damage and put them through moral dilemmas. On high-conflict
personal dilemmas — the ones where aggregate welfare is bought with an emotionally aversive act — the
patients gave an abnormally utilitarian pattern. On other classes of dilemma they were normal.

Two properties matter here, and the second is the one usually skipped over. The first is that the lesion
moved the readout at all: valuation structures are not epiphenomenal to moral output, and knocking one
out changes what the system decides. The second is that it moved it *selectively*. The patients did not
become globally worse at moral reasoning, or globally impaired at everything. One class of judgement
shifted, in a specific direction, and the rest held. That is what a targeted lesion looks like when it
works, and it is the standard MECH-024 sets for itself when it insists that the diagonal must remain the
largest effect on its own row and that a matrix explained by proportional global degradation is not
confirmation.

I want to be honest about what this entry does *not* license. It is silent on every off-diagonal. Nobody
measured whether these six patients' self-representation or their trait-level control sensitivity moved
under the same lesion. There is a separate and much older clinical literature on acquired sociopathy that
gestures at exactly that, but it is a different literature with different measurements, and importing it
under this citation would be the kind of quiet slippage that makes a governance record useless later. So:
the ethics row's diagonal is certified non-inert by this paper. The ethics row's off-diagonals are not
touched by it.

The other limitation is arithmetic. Six patients. Focal bilateral vmPFC damage is rare enough that this
cohort recurs across the literature, so a coupling argument assembled from several Iowa-cohort papers is
much less independent than the citation count suggests. I would not want MECH-024's design justified by
stacking three papers that turn out to be the same patients.

What I take forward into the REE design is narrower and more useful than a general endorsement. The
selectivity finding says the ethics readout should be expected to be *conflict-graded* — the lesion
showed up only where the dilemma pitted two live considerations against each other, and was invisible
where it did not. MECH-024's ethics readout is the harm-veto and residue-cost rate over a fixed hazard
probe set. If that probe set is built from unambiguous hazards, where the veto is the obvious answer, the
analogous prediction is that freezing the residue field will look *inert* — not because the field does
nothing, but because the probe has no conflict for it to resolve. That would trip the non-degeneracy
precondition and void the matrix for a reason that is entirely about probe construction. Pre-registering a
conflict-graded hazard probe set, rather than a uniformly severe one, looks like the cheapest thing this
literature can buy the experiment.
