# The one study that measures both axes at once, and it splits

Every other entry in this directory measures one axis and infers the other. Arroyo-Anlló and colleagues
gave 21 bvFTD patients and 21 matched controls a single self-consciousness questionnaire whose facets
include both *moral judgments* and *awareness of personal identity*. Same patients, same lesion, same
instrument, both readouts. Structurally that is one row of MECH-024's coupling matrix, and it is the
reason this small and statistically modest paper is here at all.

The result does not go one way. The most disturbed facets were anosognosia, affective state and moral
judgments. The least disturbed were awareness of identity and body representation.

Read as support: the ethics facet degrades under a frontal lesion alongside other self-consciousness
facets, and the authors locate the common deficiency in orbito-frontal function. Ethics is not sitting in
a sealed module while the rest of self-consciousness carries on.

Read as a problem — and I think this is the more valuable reading — the *selfhood axis fractionates*.
The facet that most resembles what REE means by z_self coherence, the sense of being a continuing
particular person, is among the best preserved. The facets that go are the evaluative, conduct-facing,
insight-dependent ones. If that pattern is real, then MECH-024's decision to treat SELFHOOD as one
readout is a decision with consequences: a scalar z_self coherence measure would average a moving facet
against a stationary one and could return a null off-diagonal for reasons entirely about aggregation.
A null in the ethics→selfhood cell would then be uninterpretable in the direction the claim cares about —
not evidence of separable modules, just evidence of a coarse readout. That is worth pre-registering,
because it is cheap to hedge in advance (report the components of the z_self coherence measure, not only
its aggregate) and impossible to fix afterwards.

There is an awkwardness I should put on the record rather than smooth over: this entry and the
Strohminger and Nichols entry in the same directory disagree. There, damage to the moral faculty was *the*
driver of identity discontinuity. Here, moral judgment is disturbed while identity awareness is largely
spared. The two are not straightforwardly contradictory, because they measure identity from opposite
sides — Strohminger's is a caregiver's judgement of whether this is still the same person, Arroyo-Anlló's
is the patient's own reported sense of identity. It is entirely possible, and rather interesting, that
both are true: moral conduct is what *observers* use to decide whether you are still you, while your own
felt identity survives its loss. If that is the structure, it tells us something REE should care about,
namely that "self coherence" is at least two different measurements and the coupling to ethics may only
exist for one of them. I would rather leave that tension standing in the governance record than pick the
result that flatters the claim.

Two further limits. Anosognosia is among the most disturbed facets, which means the patients whose
self-consciousness we are measuring by self-report are, by the same measurement, impaired at
self-monitoring — a shared impaired-introspection factor could generate the correlations without any
coupling between the constructs. And the orbito-frontal attribution is a common-cause account: one region
damaged, several facets affected. MECH-024's falsifier is explicit that shared substrate is not the same
as structured coupling. A matrix confirming through common cause rather than through influence would pass
the letter of the criterion and miss its point.
