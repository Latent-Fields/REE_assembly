# Valence is legible in CA1 — but shaped by amygdala/mPFC (McMakin et al. 2022)

According to PubMed, McMakin, Kimbler, Tustison, Pettit & Mattfeld (*Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience*, 2022; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsab089)) studied negative overgeneralization in 34 peripubertal youth (9–14 y) using an emotional mnemonic similarity task under fMRI. Negative overgeneralization tracked greater and more-similar CA1 activation for negative-versus-neutral stimuli, and — critically for how we read it — the amygdala showed greater functional coupling with CA1 and dorsal mPFC during the negative items that were later overgeneralized. Their summary: negative overgeneralization is "rooted in amygdala and mPFC modulation at encoding and pattern completion at retrieval."

This is a genuinely two-faced datum for MECH-073, which is why I score it **mixed** rather than forcing it onto one side. On the intrinsic-valence side: valence is *legible in the hippocampal representation itself* — negative and neutral experiences produce distinguishable CA1 pattern-completion signatures, so affective value is carried in the hippocampal activity geometry, not only elsewhere. That leans toward MECH-073. On the applied-downstream side: that CA1 valence signature is *driven by* amygdala and mPFC coupling at encoding — extrinsic affective circuitry shaping the hippocampal signal. That leans against MECH-073, toward the view that valence is imposed on the hippocampus from outside. The study cannot separate the two, and I have not pretended it can.

The transfer caveat is real and I have weighted it heavily. This is an emotional *memory-generalization* task in a developmental/clinical youth sample, not a spatial-map-valence recording; "CA1 pattern completion" is a memory-similarity index, not map geometry in the place-cell sense that MECH-073 (and the Michon/Duvelle entries) are really about. So this belongs in the file as an *affective-memory analogue* that shows valence and hippocampal representation are entangled in humans, while being explicit that it does not directly test the map-geometry formulation. It adds a third axis to the conflict — human, affective, developmental — rather than tipping the dorsal-spatial electrophysiology either way.

Scored **mixed** at **0.48**: relevant human evidence on the affective side of the claim, but a loose task/construct mapping (memory generalization, not map geometry) and an unavoidable intrinsic-vs-extrinsic ambiguity (CA1 valence present, but amygdala/mPFC-modulated) keep both fidelity and confidence low.
