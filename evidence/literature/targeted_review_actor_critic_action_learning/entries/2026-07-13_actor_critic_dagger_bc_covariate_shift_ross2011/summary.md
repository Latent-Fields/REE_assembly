# A Reduction of Imitation Learning to No-Regret Online Learning (Ross, Gordon & Bagnell, 2011) — MECH-457

## What the paper did

This is the canonical statement of why behavior cloning is not as safe as it looks. Ross, Gordon & Bagnell observed that imitation learning breaks the i.i.d. assumption of supervised learning: because the learner's own actions determine which states it later encounters, small per-step errors carry it into regions the expert never demonstrated, where it errs again. They proved this makes the total cost of a cloned policy grow *quadratically* in the task horizon T — O(εT²) for a policy with supervised error ε on the expert's own state distribution. Their fix, DAgger, iteratively runs the current learner, queries the expert on the states the learner *actually visits*, and aggregates that corrective data; this reduces the problem to no-regret online learning with error *linear* in T. It was validated on a driving simulator and handwriting prediction.

## Why it is the load-bearing caveat here

The combined 747/748/749 autopsy reached a clean and correct conclusion — potential-based reward shaping rescues neither representation, and only behavior-cloning of an expert clears the 1.0 floor, so the wall is the RL exploration/credit-assignment bootstrap, not representation and not reward density. This paper does not dispute that. What it does is discipline how we read the *positive* half of the result before it turns into a build target. The 748/749 arms report high held-out action-match accuracy (0.802 and 0.872) — and per-step action-match accuracy is *precisely* the quantity Ross et al. show compounds over an episode. A policy that matches the expert 87% of the time per step can still drift off the expert's manifold within a foraging episode and land in states no demonstration covered. Worse, REE's BC arms cloned a *fixed* expert dataset; DAgger's linear-error guarantee requires an *interactive* expert queryable at the learner's own visited states, which a fixed set does not provide. So the offline-BC ceiling applies in full.

The upshot for the build decision is directional. This entry `weakens` the reading "the BC PASS shows cloning is a sufficient fix" while `supporting` MECH-457's deeper insistence that a dedicated RL / interactive-correction loop is load-bearing — cloning alone inherits a quadratic failure mode that only closed-loop correction (DAgger) or continued RL (the DQfD entry) removes. That is why I labeled it `mixed`: it cuts against a lazy interpretation of the empirical PASS and in favour of the claim's actual thesis.

## The mapping and its honest limits

Two boundaries keep this from being decisive. First, the O(T²) bound is worst-case and horizon-dependent; REE forage episode lengths and how benign drift is in that specific environment are not characterised, so the *practical* severity here is unquantified — the bound warns of a failure mode, it does not prove REE will suffer it badly. Second, there is a measurement subtlety: the theorem concerns deployment-time closed-loop cost, whereas 748/749 report evaluated return. If that evaluation was already closed-loop (the agent acting under its own induced distribution), then some compounding is *already priced into* the PASS margins, and this caveat is really about robustness and generalisation beyond the measured setting rather than about the PASS being illusory. Either way, the governance-relevant conclusion holds: do not read the BC PASS as licensing "clone and stop".

## Confidence reasoning

0.74, `mixed`. Source quality is very high — a formal, task-agnostic guarantee with a decade of downstream validation — which is also why transfer risk is low: a theorem about compounding error does not depend on the domain being Atari or REE. Mapping fidelity is high because the result speaks directly to interpreting the 748/749 action-match numbers. The entry's role in the dossier is to make sure the H-optim leg is framed correctly: even if an expert teacher proves *necessary*, the build target is a scaffold that keeps a correcting RL loop, not a static clone.
