# Developmental emergence of fear learning corresponds with changes in amygdala synaptic plasticity (Thompson, Sullivan & Wilson, 2008)

**Claim tested:** SD-035 (amygdala analogue as avoidance-learning substrate)
**Direction:** supports | **Confidence:** 0.70

## What the paper did

Where Debiec & Sullivan (2017) describe the developmental timetable of avoidance learning at the behavioural level, this earlier primary paper from the same group asks the mechanistic question: *what changes in the substrate when avoidance learning becomes possible?* The authors combined behavioural odor-aversion conditioning across the sensitive-period boundary with in vitro slice electrophysiology in the basolateral amygdala, measuring whether tetanic stimulation could induce long-term synaptic plasticity at different ages.

## Key findings relevant to the claim

The behavioural and physiological onsets line up. Pups younger than PN10 (tested at PN8) could not learn odor aversions; pups older than PN10 (PN12) could. The electrophysiology gives the substrate correlate: "tetanic stimulation induce significant long-term synaptic plasticity in post-sensitive period pups, but no significant plasticity in sensitive period pups incapable of learning odor aversions." The plasticity, in other words, is inducible only once the animal is old enough to learn the aversion. The authors then localise the gate to inhibitory maturation: blocking GABA-A receptors with picrotoxin in mature slices reverted the plasticity to the immature pattern, leading them to conclude that "sensitive period deficits in fear conditioning may be related to impaired amygdala synaptic plasticity and the immature state of GABAergic inhibition."

## How this translates to REE

This is the substrate-level anchor for the cluster autopsy's "substrate not ready" verdict. The 603g probe found that the survival/hazard-avoidance leg does not train even when isolated as a dedicated Stage-H. The Thompson result says, in biological terms, exactly why that shape occurs when a substrate is immature: the learning signal can be delivered, but the substrate cannot yet *encode* it, so no amount of conditioning produces the aversion. That distinguishes a substrate-readiness failure from a budget failure, and it points the REE fix toward making the avoidance signal learnable at the substrate/architecture level (the SD-035/MECH-279 wiring direction) rather than toward more episodes on the existing substrate. It also offers a concrete, if loose, design hint -- that an inhibitory gating element may be part of what licenses the relevant plasticity -- though I treat that as a prompt, not a prescription.

## Limitations and confidence reasoning

Confidence is 0.70. The study is a clean primary result, but it is slice electrophysiology and behaviour in developing rat amygdala, and REE's substrate has no literal LTP or GABAergic synapse, so only the *principle* (a maturation gate must be in place before the avoidance signal is learnable) transfers, not the molecular mechanism. The paradigm is Pavlovian odor-aversion, whereas REE's failing leg is closer to instrumental survival-under-hazard -- a gap I flag explicitly because the next paper in this review (Moscarello & LeDoux 2013) shows that the Pavlovian-to-instrumental step has its own, additional substrate requirement. The GABAergic account is well-supported but is one of several maturational changes over this window, so I do not treat it as the sole gate.
