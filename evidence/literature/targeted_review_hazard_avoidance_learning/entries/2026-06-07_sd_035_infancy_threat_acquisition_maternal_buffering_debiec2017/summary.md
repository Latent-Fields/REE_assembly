# The neurobiology of safety and threat learning in infancy (Debiec & Sullivan, 2017)

**Claim tested:** SD-035 (amygdala analogue as the BLA/CeA salience and -- on the reading this lit-pull is testing -- avoidance-learning stage)
**Direction:** supports | **Confidence:** 0.74

## What the paper did

This is a review from the Sullivan group synthesising rodent (and some human) work on how threat and safety learning develop across early postnatal life. Its organising claim is that the infant brain is not a small adult brain: in the newborn rat pup attachment learning is dominant and threat learning is quiescent, and the machinery for adult-like, amygdala-dependent fear and avoidance learning comes online only gradually. The review ties three things together: the developmental timetable of amygdala plasticity, the behavioural switch between approach/attachment and avoidance, and the role of the caregiver in buffering the pup's stress-hormone (HPA-axis / corticosterone) response during acquisition.

## Key findings relevant to the claim

Three facts matter for us. First, **avoidance learning has a developmental onset**: around postnatal day 10 (PN10) adult-like fear learning emerges, and the review is explicit that "the amygdala fails to show learning induced plasticity until pups reach 10 days of age." Before that, no amount of odor-shock pairing yields an amygdala-dependent aversion. Second, there is a **transitional sensitive period (PN10-PN15)** during which the pup can be switched between threat learning and attachment learning depending on whether the mother is present. Third, the switch is implemented by **maternal buffering of the stress axis**: maternal presence "suppresses threat-induced HPA axis activation by blocking NE action in the paraventricular nucleus of the hypothalamus (PVN) and prevents threat/aversion learning enabling attachment/preference learning." Raising corticosterone artificially can prematurely enable amygdala-dependent fear learning; lowering it reinstates the preference-learning regime. From PN16 onward maternal presence can attenuate but no longer fully abolish fear learning.

## How this translates to REE

This is direct biological support for the V3-EXQ-603g/624c/651a cluster autopsy's central adjudication: the survival/hazard-avoidance **learning leg is a developmentally-acquired competency, not a property the agent possesses at substrate-init**. The 603g probe inserted an isolated hazard-avoidance Stage-H and expected it to train within budget; it did not (G_H 0/3). The biology says that is the expected shape if the avoidance-learning substrate is treated as a flat, always-available capacity. In mammals, avoidance learning is gated both by maturation of the amygdala's plasticity and by a scaffolding/buffering signal supplied during acquisition. That maps onto two design moves: (i) treat `scaffolded_sd054_onboarding` as a genuine developmental curriculum where avoidance learning is staged after, and protected during, an earlier regime, rather than as a budget knob; and (ii) wire the SD-035 amygdala analogue as the avoidance-**learning** driver, not merely a salience-weighting stage. ## Limitations and confidence reasoning

I have held confidence at 0.74 rather than higher because the mapping is an analogy, not an isomorphism. REE has no homologue of the corticosterone/attachment switch or the maternal-buffering mechanism, so the *specific* developmental switch does not transfer -- only the higher-level principle that avoidance learning is developmentally gated and scaffold-dependent. The data are rodent infant odor-aversion (Pavlovian), whereas REE's failing leg is closer to instrumental survival-under-hazard, and the review's own human material is thinner. The principle is what we should take forward; the mechanism is a prompt for design, not a template to copy. The honest residual risk is that we over-read a maturational story onto what is, in part, a curriculum-engineering problem -- which is exactly why this lit-pull pairs the developmental biology with the active-avoidance circuit work (Moscarello & LeDoux 2013) and the safe-RL curriculum work (Turchetta et al. 2020).
