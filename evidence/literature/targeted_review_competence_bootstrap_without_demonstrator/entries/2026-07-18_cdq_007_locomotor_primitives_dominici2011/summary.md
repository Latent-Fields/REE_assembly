# Dominici et al. 2011 -- Locomotor primitives are retained, not replaced

According to PubMed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1210617), *Science* 2011), Dominici and colleagues recorded multi-muscle EMG during stepping in human neonates, toddlers, preschoolers and adults, and factorised those recordings into basic patterns of lumbosacral motoneuron activation. The prevailing assumption they set out to test was the developmental-replacement story: that the crude neonatal stepping pattern is *suppressed* during maturation and a wholly new adult control scheme is *learned* in its place. What they found instead is that the two basic patterns present in the stepping neonate persist unchanged into adult walking, with two additional patterns appearing first in toddlers. They then showed markedly similar primitives in rat, cat, macaque and guineafowl -- species separated by enormous phylogenetic distance and gross morphological difference.

The reason I pulled this paper for CDQ-007 is that it speaks to the *shape* of the bootstrap question rather than to either hypothesis's content. The REE converter is currently being asked to extract a competent policy from a provably sufficient observation with no prior at all -- to *construct* the action repertoire, in effect. Dominici et al. are evidence that biology does not solve the equivalent problem that way anywhere it has been looked at. Development augments a pre-specified basis; it does not synthesise one. If the biological case is the reference, then "learner with zero action prior fails to reach competence" is not an anomaly requiring a novel explanation -- it is the expected outcome of removing something biology never removes.

The honest limits are important and I do not want to launder them. First, the level is wrong: these are motoneuron activation patterns for rhythmic stepping, which is the action *alphabet*, not the *policy* that sequences actions toward food. A REE agent handed innate primitives could still forage at floor if nothing points those primitives at a goal. Second, the design isolates development from learning by age-staging in intact, caregiver-reared infants -- it is not a demonstrator-deprivation experiment, so it cannot on its own tell us whether a demonstrator is dispensable. Third, the innateness inference for humans rests on the neonatal recordings being early enough to precede meaningful practice, which is a reasonable but not airtight assumption (Zeng et al. 2021, also in this review, shows embryonic self-generated movement can already be instructive).

Confidence 0.82, direction supports. It is a high-quality, cross-species, mechanistically explicit result that bears squarely on whether "refine an innate basis" or "construct from experience" is the biological default. It supports the H-approach-primitive leg only at the level of the action basis, not at the level of the drive -- and I have scored mapping_fidelity at 0.68 to hold that distinction visible rather than let the paper carry more of the discrimination than it can.
