# Szczypka et al. 2001 -- Without the drive substrate, a competent animal starves

According to PubMed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0896-6273(01)00319-1), *Neuron* 2001), Szczypka and colleagues used dopamine-deficient mice -- animals in which tyrosine hydroxylase is selectively inactivated in dopaminergic neurons -- to ask where in the brain dopamine must be restored for normal behaviour to return. Untreated, these mice become hypoactive and hypophagic and die of starvation by four weeks of age. Viral restoration of dopamine synthesis in the caudate putamen restored feeding on regular chow and nest-building; restoration in nucleus accumbens restored exploratory behaviour; restoration in either region restored preference for sucrose and palatable diet, without fully rescuing movement initiation or coordination.

This is the entry in the pull that speaks most directly to the *necessity* question CDQ-007 raised: is there evidence that reward-contingent learning alone cannot bootstrap competent goal-directed action? The DD mouse is a striking answer, because what is *not* broken is as telling as what is. These animals perceive food. They possess the motor apparatus. They retain hedonic reactivity -- once dopamine is restored anywhere in the relevant striatal territory, preference for palatable food comes straight back. What they lack is the substrate that converts all of that into approach. And lacking it, they starve in front of available food.

The mapping to the REE failure is uncomfortably close and I think that closeness is the paper's real value here. The converter's arms show readiness met on every leg -- local_view_greedy at 48-55 and oracle at 57-61 against a floor of 1.0 -- and yet forage at approximately zero. That is the signature of an agent that can see, can move, and can (in principle) value, but does not go. Szczypka et al. describe an animal with exactly that profile and identify what is missing in it.

The limits matter and cut against over-reading. Most importantly, this is loss-of-function in an animal that *already acquired* the behavioural repertoire; it evidences that the drive is necessary to *express* competence, not that it is necessary or sufficient to *acquire* it. The REE question is an acquisition question. Second, the authors themselves report that coordination and movement initiation are not fully rescued by either regional restoration, so aphagia is partly confounded with motoric failure -- one cannot cleanly say "motivation" alone. Third, feeding-for-sustenance and preference-for-palatable dissociate anatomically here, meaning "the approach drive" is already more than one thing in biology; a single scalar drive parameter in REE would be a coarse analog of a circuit that nature has split.

Confidence 0.75, direction supports. It supplies the necessity evidence the question explicitly asked for, in the right direction, with the caveat that it is necessity-for-expression rather than necessity-for-acquisition -- a gap the biology in this pull does not close.
