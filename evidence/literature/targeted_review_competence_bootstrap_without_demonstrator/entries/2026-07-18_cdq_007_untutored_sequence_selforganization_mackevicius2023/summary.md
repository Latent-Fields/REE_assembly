# Mackevicius et al. 2023 -- Both legs, in order, with a clock

According to PubMed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.77262), *eLife* 2023), Mackevicius, Gu, Denisenko and Fee set out to disambiguate two contributions to the emergence of premotor neural sequences in the zebra finch song system: how much is tutor *experience* and how much is *development*. Their design was to delay tutor exposure and image HVC with calcium imaging throughout. Three findings, and each speaks to a different part of CDQ-007. First, neural sequences emerge in the complete absence of tutoring -- tutor experience is not necessary for sequence *formation*. Second, once a tutor is introduced, those pre-existing sequences can become tightly associated with newly learned syllables. Third, and to my mind the most interesting: only half the delayed birds learned new syllables at all, and the ones that failed were precisely those whose pre-tutoring sequences were already most crystallized around their own untutored song.

I pulled this paper because it is the only source I found that runs both legs of the CDQ-007 discrimination inside one experiment, and its answer is that they are not alternatives. The innate/self-organizing process supplies the *scaffold* -- the temporal sequence structure -- demonstrator-free. The demonstrator supplies the *content* that gets bound onto that scaffold. Untutored birds are not incompetent in the sense of producing nothing; they produce isolate song, which is structured but abnormal. So "sequences form without a tutor" and "competent behaviour requires a tutor" are both true here, at different levels of description, and conflating them is the main way this literature gets misused.

The third finding adds something neither REE hypothesis currently states: the demonstrator-derived prior is only *usable* while the self-organized scaffold is still plastic. A prior that hardens becomes a barrier. If one is asking whether an early behavioural-cloning seed would be *outgrown* -- the transience question CDQ-007 raised explicitly -- the biology here suggests the more pressing risk runs the other way: it is the *self-generated*, untutored structure that hardens and locks the learner out of later improvement. That is a genuinely different worry from "the BC prior will become a permanent crutch", and I think it is worth carrying forward as a framing correction rather than as an answer.

Caveats. Vocal learning is a specialised system with a dedicated nucleus and a genetically scheduled critical period; a REE converter has no maturational clock, so the timing result in particular is suggestive rather than predictive. The species-typical endpoint here is a song, not a foraging policy, and the credit-assignment problem in song learning (match a stored template) is structurally unlike foraging (discover which actions pay). Confidence 0.8, direction mixed. Mixed is the right label: it supplies real evidence *for* each leg and for neither *over* the other, which is exactly what an informing review should produce.
