# Kean et al. (2025) -- an abstract formal reasoning network distinct from Multiple Demand

This is the paper behind the MIT "Separating logic and language" coverage that ARC-113's source list
already cites, so it is worth being careful about what it does and does not establish for the claim.
Using deep-data precision fMRI -- 24 participants, one to three sessions of two to three hours each,
ten experiments -- Kean and colleagues identify a frontal network supporting abstract formal
reasoning and show it dissociates sharply from the domain-general Multiple Demand network, the
current leading candidate substrate of fluid intelligence, as well as from the language, intuitive
physical reasoning, and social reasoning networks. The overlap statistic is striking: a Dice
coefficient of 0.02 across the brain and 0.04 within frontal lobes (p<0.001). MD responded robustly
to spatial working memory (Hard>Easy, p<0.001) but showed no significant response to logical
reasoning at network level (p>0.3); the reasoning network showed the converse profile.

The first-order finding supports ARC-113 only at second order, and I want to be explicit about that
rather than let the entry be over-counted. What is dissociated here is reasoning from *other
systems* -- language, executive control, physics, theory of mind. ARC-113 asserts non-collapse among
stages *inside* the rule pipeline. Those are different propositions. What this paper contributes to
the claim is the prior step: the rule pipeline is a real functional object with its own substrate
rather than a label for whatever domain-general control happens to be doing, and so the question of
its internal organisation is a well-posed one.

The detail that does bear directly on ARC-113 is a secondary one. MD was engaged by inductive
reasoning and by matrix problems but *not* by deductive syllogisms, while the reasoning network
responded to both (deductive Hard>Easy p<0.001; inductive Pre-Solution>Post-Solution p<0.001). That
is a dissociation between reasoning operations, not merely between reasoning and non-reasoning, and
it is the sort of thing ARC-113 predicts: apprehension-type stages that must search a hypothesis
space draw on resources that derivation-type stages do not.

Against that, the paper contains a result which, read plainly, is the collapsed-stage reading. The
abstract reasoning network responded robustly and *similarly* during deduction, induction, matrix
reasoning, multiplication and division. At network resolution, one region responding to everything
abstract is exactly what a single undifferentiated reasoning module would produce. Precision fMRI
resolves networks, not pipeline stages, so this design could not detect stage collapse within the
reasoning network even if it were total -- the evidence simply does not discriminate the hypotheses
at that grain. I have recorded this as a failure signature.

Confidence 0.62, and the decomposition explains the shape: source quality is the highest here at
0.8 (precision fMRI, large per-subject data, explicit overlap statistics), but mapping fidelity is
the lowest in this pull at 0.45, and for an architectural claim mapping fidelity is what should
dominate. It remains a preprint, not yet peer reviewed, and the authors note they sampled only
neurologically healthy educated adults from an industrialised culture and question generalisation
across populations and reasoning types. Good science; oblique evidence.
