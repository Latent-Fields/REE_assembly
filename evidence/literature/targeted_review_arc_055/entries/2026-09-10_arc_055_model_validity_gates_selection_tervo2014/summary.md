# A model-validity signal that decides whether the model gets to choose

Tervo and colleagues put rats against virtual competitors and found that the animals do what primates do: they counter-predict, using history and a model of the opponent rather than choosing randomly. The interesting case is the opponent that cannot be beaten this way. Faced with a competitor immune to counter-prediction, the rats switched into what the authors call a stochastic mode, in which outcomes associated with their own actions stopped influencing subsequent choices and normal engagement of anterior cingulate cortex was suppressed. Using circuit perturbations in transgenic animals, they showed that the switch between the strategic and stochastic modes is controlled by locus coeruleus input into ACC, and framed the function plainly: under uncertainty about environmental rules, noradrenergic input alters ACC output and prevents erroneous beliefs from guiding decisions.

That last phrase is why this paper belongs in an ARC-055 directory, and why I think it is the closest mapping the claim has yet acquired. The other four entries here are about confidence in a decision -- how likely is this particular choice to be right. ARC-055 is not, on its own terms, a claim about decision confidence. Verisimilitude is a signal about how well the internal model corresponds to the world, which is a different quantity, and it is precisely the quantity at stake in this experiment. The rat's model of its opponent is either tracking or it is not. When it is not, something causally disconnects the model from action selection. Translated into REE's vocabulary: when V(t) is low, E3 stops taking the generative model's trajectory rankings at face value. That is the E3 leg of ARC-055, stated as a causal circuit result rather than as a design argument.

It also supplies something the rest of the directory does not, which is a reason why an architecture would want the signal on the selection path in the first place. Reading the existing entries, the obvious sceptical position is the one Lak et al. licenses: if choice runs fine without the explicit confidence signal, the signal is for reporting and for learning, and E3 can be left alone. Tervo et al. suggests the function is not to sharpen a good model's choices but to prevent a bad model from committing the agent to trajectories it has no business trusting. That is a disengagement function, and it maps onto ARC-055's kappa_commit -- a threshold below which commitment should not occur -- more naturally than onto anything in the claim's learning leg. An architecture that only gave V(t) to the learning update would have no mechanism for this at all; it would keep acting on a model it had already begun to discount.

The caveats are real and I do not want to smuggle them past. The first is granularity. ARC-055 says V(t) influences E3 trajectory selection, which reads as graded re-ranking -- high-verisimilitude trajectories weighted up, low ones weighted down. What is demonstrated here is a switch between two regimes, one model-guided and one that ignores action-outcome history entirely. A wholesale gate and a graded weight are genuinely different architectures, and if the biology only implements the gate then ARC-055's continuous formulation is over-specified. The second caveat is sharper, because it cuts at the claim's load-bearing distinction. Nobody measured a validity signal here. The gating quantity is inferred from which behavioural regime the animal occupies. A reading in which noradrenergic drive simply raises processing noise until the model's influence is swamped fits these data and requires no explicit signal represented anywhere -- and ARC-055 turns entirely on the difference between an explicit signal and an emergent epiphenomenon.

That alternative is not hypothetical. Kane et al. (2017, Cognitive, Affective & Behavioral Neuroscience) stimulated locus coeruleus tonic activity with DREADDs in rats performing a patch-foraging task and found that animals left patches earlier, but that the effect was best explained by an increase in decision noise rather than by a systematic shift in the leaving threshold. Same neuromodulator, same broad disengagement phenotype, and the mechanism that best fit was noise injection rather than a represented quantity entering the selection computation. I have filed this entry as supporting because the causal, circuit-specific result lands on selection and the quantity gated is the right one for ARC-055 -- but the explicitness conjunct is left open here, exactly as Lak et al. left it open, and a governance reader should not treat the E3 leg as settled by it.
