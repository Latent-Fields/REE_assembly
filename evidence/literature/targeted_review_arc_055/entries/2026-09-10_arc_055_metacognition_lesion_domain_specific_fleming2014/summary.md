# A second dissociation, and a sharper problem underneath it

Fleming and colleagues tested seven patients with anterior prefrontal lesions on matched perceptual and memory tasks with trial-by-trial confidence ratings, against a comparison group of eleven temporal-lobe lesion patients and nineteen healthy controls. Metacognitive accuracy was quantified as meta-d'/d', which asks how well confidence ratings track actual performance while factoring out first-order sensitivity. The anterior prefrontal group showed a selective deficit in perceptual metacognitive accuracy (95% CI 0.28-0.64) while performing the first-order perceptual task equivalently to both control groups. Their metacognitive accuracy on the memory task was unimpaired (95% CI 0.78-1.29). The temporal-lobe group was intact in both domains.

I went looking for this kind of paper deliberately. The ARC-055 directory as it stood held one causal study bearing on the selection leg, Lak et al. 2014, and it pointed the wrong way for the claim: inactivate the region carrying the explicit confidence signal and confidence-guided waiting collapses while decision accuracy survives. The defence written into that entry was that a two-alternative perceptual choice has a degenerate trajectory space, making it a weak test rather than a disconfirmation. That defence is an argument, not evidence, and it was the only thing holding the leg up. A second clean dissociation was therefore the most consequential thing this pull could produce, and this is one: the same shape of result, in humans rather than rats, from structural lesion rather than pharmacological inactivation, with a properly bias-corrected metacognitive measure rather than a wagering proxy. One dissociation can be a quirk of a preparation. Two, converging across species and method, raise the burden on ARC-055's assertion that V(t) must be available to E3 selection.

But the finding I did not anticipate is the more interesting one for this claim, and it is the domain-specificity. ARC-055 does not merely assert that a confidence-like signal exists and is explicit. It asserts that V(t) and D_V are available to E3 trajectory selection, *and* influence E1 state representation, *and* are predicted by the E2 transition model, *and* prioritise learning updates. That is one quantity on a shared bus with four consumers -- an architecture-level signal, published once and read in several places. What these patients show is metacognitive accuracy fractionating by domain within the same individuals: perception impaired, memory spared, from the same lesion. That is what you would expect if confidence-like signals are computed locally, per-domain, and consumed by whatever needs them nearby, rather than being a single global quantity on a bus. If that is how it works, ARC-055 is the wrong shape independently of whether the E3 leg survives, because the claim's whole structure presumes one V(t) rather than several.

That also opens a tension inside this directory that governance should see rather than have averaged away. Masset et al. 2020, already here and filed as supporting at 0.76, reported an abstract, modality-general confidence signal in orbitofrontal cortex feeding more than one consumer -- which is close to the shared-bus picture ARC-055 needs. Fleming et al. point the other way, toward fractionation. Those two are not straightforwardly reconcilable, and I am not going to pretend to resolve them here; different species, different regions, different measures, and it is entirely possible that OFC carries something general while anterior prefrontal cortex carries something perceptual-specific. The honest statement is that the directory now contains an unresolved question about whether the confidence-like signal is global or local, and ARC-055 as written requires it to be global.

The caveats run in the claim's favour and I should give them their weight. The scope limit recorded in the Lak entry applies here with full force: a 2AFC perceptual discrimination has no trajectory structure, so preserved first-order performance under a degraded confidence signal says nothing about whether selection among *extended* trajectories would be preserved. The directory now holds two instances of the same weak test rather than one strong one, and confidence should not accumulate from repetition of an instrument that is wrong for the question. Beyond that: first-order performance equivalence is partly a design requirement, since difficulty was matched across groups, so the sparing reads as less of a discovered dissociation than it first appears. Seven patients with naturally occurring, heterogeneous, non-experimentally-placed lesions is weak causal evidence next to the transgenic circuit perturbation in Tervo et al. or the within-subject cTBS in Zajkowski et al. And meta-d'/d' is a property of the correspondence between confidence *reports* and performance, so strictly what was degraded is a reporting pathway, not demonstrably a selection-available signal.

Filed as weakening at 0.64, with mapping fidelity 0.62 -- the lowest of the four entries added in this pull. Both of its findings run against ARC-055 as written, which is why the direction is what it is, but the number is deliberately restrained: the dissociation reaches the selection leg only through the same indirect route as Lak et al., and it is the domain-specificity, not the dissociation, that lands the cleaner blow.
