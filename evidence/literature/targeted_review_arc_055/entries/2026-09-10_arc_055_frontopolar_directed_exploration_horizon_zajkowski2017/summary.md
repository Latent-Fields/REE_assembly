# Causal necessity of an uncertainty term for directed exploration -- and only when the horizon is long

Zajkowski, Kossut & Wilson (2017) applied continuous theta-burst TMS to right frontopolar cortex in 25 healthy adults performing the Horizon Task, and compared it within-subject against vertex stimulation. The task gives participants four forced observations of two slot machines and then either one free choice (horizon 1) or six (horizon 6). Because the forced trials can be arranged unequally -- three observations of one machine, one of the other -- the design separates two reasons a participant might pick the less-attractive option: because they want to learn about it (directed exploration, modelled as an information bonus added to its value), or because their choice rule is noisy (random exploration, modelled as decision temperature). Inhibiting right frontopolar cortex reduced the information bonus by roughly 4.8 points at horizon 6, with 99% of posterior samples showing a reduction, and left decision noise untouched (F(1,24)=0.88, p=0.36 for the main effect). The stimulation-by-horizon interaction on directed exploration was significant, F(1,24)=4.96, p=0.036, with the simple effect carried entirely by horizon 6, t(24)=2.62, p=0.008.

What makes this the entry the ARC-055 selection leg was missing is where the dependent variable sits. The three papers already in this directory -- Meyniel & Dehaene, Masset et al., Lak et al. -- all measure either a confidence report or a learning update. Lak et al. is the uncomfortable one: inactivate the region carrying the explicit confidence signal and the confidence-guided waiting collapses while decision accuracy survives, which is precisely the pattern of a signal that selection does not need. Here the inference runs the other way. Remove the region carrying an explicit, model-identified uncertainty term and the *choice itself* changes: participants stop preferentially sampling the machine they know less about. That is selection, not report, and it is what ARC-055 asserts when it says V(t) must be available to and influence E3 trajectory selection.

Two features of the result matter more for this claim than the headline. The first is the selectivity. Random exploration survived intact, so this is not a blunt degradation of decision-making under stimulation; one identifiable term was subtracted from the selection computation and the rest of it carried on. ARC-055's central architectural commitment is that V(t) and D_V are explicit signals rather than emergent epiphenomena, and a term that can be selectively removed with a specific and bounded behavioural consequence is about as close as a TMS study can get to demonstrating that a quantity is separately addressable. The second is the horizon interaction, and it is the more interesting one, because ARC-055 has a depth conjunct that the existing three entries leave entirely unevidenced. At horizon 1 the same stimulation to the same site did nothing. The causal necessity of the uncertainty term was itself conditional on there being a future over which the information could pay off. ARC-055 names H, the horizon, as the control-plane parameter setting the temporal window for D_V evaluation; this is the first datum in the directory that speaks at all to why an architecture would evaluate a precision-like signal over a window rather than instantaneously.

I want to be careful not to let that become more than it is. The Horizon Task manipulates how many choices remain, which changes how much an information-seeking bonus is worth. D_V, as ARC-055 uses it, is a depth property of the verisimilitude estimate -- something about how far the model's fidelity extends, not about how many trials are left to exploit it. These are cousins, not the same thing. The honest statement is that the horizon-conditionality of the causal effect is suggestive for ARC-055's H parameter and is materially more than the directory had before, but nobody here measured a depth-like verisimilitude quantity, and D_V remains unevidenced. Similarly, the term manipulated is an information bonus over option value -- uncertainty about which machine pays better -- and ARC-055's V(t) is a model-fit signal. They are formally similar, both being precision-like terms entering a selection computation, but they are not the same quantity. This entry therefore supports the architectural *shape* of the E3 leg considerably more strongly than it supports REE's specific choice of signal.

The interpretive limit that constrains Lak et al. constrains this too, in mirror image: cTBS inhibits a region, not a signal, and the authors themselves note the stimulation may reach vmPFC and OFC. An architecture in which frontopolar cortex computes the exploration bonus and hands it to a downstream selector is not distinguishable here from one in which frontopolar cortex is part of the selector. ARC-055's picture -- an explicit signal on a bus, read by E3 -- is compatible with these data without being uniquely picked out by them. Against that, the horizon-selectivity is an unusually good internal control, because a nonspecific stimulation-induced performance deficit could not switch itself off at horizon 1.
