# Is one mean direction enough? The operation is right; the estimator may be incomplete

**Aquino, Fulcher, Parkes, Sabaroedin & Fornito (2020), NeuroImage 212:116614.** Retrieved via PubMed. [DOI 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.116614](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.116614)

## What the paper did

The sibling entry in this directory (Murphy et al. 2009) asks what common-mode removal does to the *distribution* of what remains. This paper asks a different and, for SD-079, more architectural question: is a single global mean the right thing to remove in the first place?

The authors' method is a good piece of scientific craft. Global signal fluctuations are conventionally spotted by eye in a "carpet plot" — a matrix with voxels as rows, time as columns, intensity as colour — where large coherent bands appear before global signal regression and mostly vanish after it, which is the visual argument that the step worked. Aquino et al. reordered the rows to emphasise cluster structure in the data rather than anatomical convention, and the reordering revealed a considerably greater diversity of widespread signal deflections than the conventional view showed. Their precise form varies across time and across participants, and global signal regression turns out to be effective against only *specific kinds* of them. They then built an alternative — Diffuse Cluster Estimation and Regression (DiCER), which iteratively identifies representative signals associated with large clusters of coherent voxels rather than assuming a single global mean — and evaluated it across three independent resting-state datasets plus 47 task contrasts from 7 Human Connectome Project tasks. DiCER removed diverse widespread deflections more effectively, reduced the correlation between connectivity estimates and head motion, reduced inter-individual variability in global correlation structure, recovered canonical networks comparably or better, and better preserved the expected spatial structure of task-related activation.

## Why this matters for SD-079

The two entries in this directory divide the question cleanly. Murphy et al. establish what centering does to the values. This paper establishes something about the *estimator*: common-mode removal is the correct operation, and a single mean is an incomplete way to perform it.

SD-079's baseline is a single EMA-estimated mean vector, updated at one fixed rate (`goal_cue_baseline_alpha`, default 0.05), owned by `AnchorSet` and shared with `GhostGoalBank` through its existing reference, subtracted from `z_goal` before every `goal_match`. That is exactly the class of estimator this paper finds insufficient. The prediction it licenses is that centered `z_goal` retains residual shared structure the EMA baseline does not reach, and — the sharper part — that the residual is non-stationary in *form*, not merely in magnitude.

That distinction is worth dwelling on, because SD-079 has already met and solved the magnitude version of this problem. The alpha default of 0.05 is measured, not inherited: `z_goal` drifts, and a baseline tuned for a stationary cue lags it, so over seeds 101/202/303 the `goal_match` spread is 0.0942 / 0.1508 / 0.3319 at alpha 0.02 versus 0.9995+ at 0.05. That is the team discovering, empirically and independently, that the common mode moves and the estimator has to track it. It is genuinely reassuring — it puts SD-079 on the right side of the estimator question the fMRI field spent a decade arguing about. But no choice of alpha addresses the paper's actual point, which is about the estimator's *shape*: a single mean direction can only ever remove structure that is common in one direction, however fast it adapts.

## Why I have not marked this as a defect finding

I want to be careful here, because the temptation with a paper like this is to over-read it into a criticism of the landed fix, and the evidence does not support that.

The mapping is the weakest of the four entries in this morning's pull, and it is weak in a specific way. The paper's evidence for estimator insufficiency is *spatial* — reordered carpet plots exposing clusters of coherent voxels, structure that is widespread across anatomy. `z_goal` has no spatial organisation and nothing corresponding to a "cluster of coherent voxels", so the diagnostic that motivated DiCER simply cannot be run on it. The claim that an analogous residual exists in REE is structural inference, not measurement. DiCER's demonstrated advantages are likewise measured against criteria with no REE counterpart: head-motion correlation, inter-individual variability, canonical network recovery. "Better" here has not been shown to be better for anything REE cares about. And DiCER is an offline estimator with access to the whole session, where SD-079's baseline must be causal and online — the superiority of an offline method says nothing about whether a richer online one is affordable.

Set against that, SD-079's own measurements are direct and they are good: V3-EXQ-807 recovered a 0.999 spread from 0.009, with the floor excluding 3/24 and the gate live on ~3.3/24, and V3-EXQ-823 then passed the downstream consumer test cleanly and non-degenerately with all preconditions met. Whatever residual structure a single mean fails to remove, it is not currently preventing the mechanism from working.

So the honest use of this entry is as a **named open question** rather than a finding: *is one mean direction enough for `z_goal`?* It is cheap to ask. After centering, the residual's own covariance structure is computable from what the SD-079 contract fixtures already collect, and a second dominant direction — if there is one — would show up as a large leading eigenvalue in that residual. If it is absent, this entry is closed and SD-079's estimator is vindicated on a point the literature says is not automatic. If it is present, we would learn it before it becomes a puzzle in some future run where the gate quietly stops discriminating.

One further caution belongs on the record because it touches how we run experiments rather than how the substrate works. The paper notes that removing a shared component can distort case-control comparisons. The REE analogue is arm-versus-arm: an ON arm and an OFF arm differing in whether centering is applied do not differ in one term only, because centering changes the distribution of every downstream match. A between-arm delta can therefore partly reflect the estimator rather than the mechanism under test. That is a design consideration for the next consumer test in this family, and it points the same way as the Murphy caveat — toward a test where the centered match has to recover a *known* ordering, rather than one where it only has to become non-degenerate.

## Confidence

0.55, with mapping fidelity binding rather than source quality. The study itself is strong — three independent replication datasets, a large public task corpus, a proposed method judged against several orthogonal criteria instead of one flattering metric, open code. I have kept the aggregate close to the mapping component rather than the quality component on the principle that for an architectural `design_decision`, a well-executed study of a differently-shaped system is worth roughly what its mapping is worth, and no more.
