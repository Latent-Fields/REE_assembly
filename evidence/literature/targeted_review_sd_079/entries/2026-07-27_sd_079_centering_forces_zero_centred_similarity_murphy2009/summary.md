# Centering necessarily re-centres the similarity distribution on zero — which is why "the gate is live" is not yet "the gate is informative"

**Murphy, Birn, Handwerker, Jones & Bandettini (2009), NeuroImage 44(3):893–905.** Retrieved via PubMed. [DOI 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2008.09.036](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2008.09.036)

## What the paper did

Resting-state fMRI has a preprocessing step, global signal regression, that is structurally the same operation SD-079 performs: a shared component — here the whole-brain average BOLD time series — is estimated and removed from every channel before any pairwise similarity is computed. The motivation is the same too. The global signal inflates correlations between all region pairs, so functional-connectivity maps look diffuse and unspecific; removing it sharpens them.

The authors asked what that removal does *by construction*, and the answer is the reason this entry exists. They show analytically that after global signal regression, the correlation values between a seed voxel and all others **must sum to a negative value**. They then confirm this experimentally — using a combined breath-holding and visual task to manipulate the global signal deliberately — finding that global signal regression produces bell-shaped correlation distributions centred on zero. In simulation, they show that small phase differences between two regions are sufficient on their own to produce negative correlations after regression. Their conclusion is that the widely-reported anti-correlated resting-state networks, which had been interpreted as evidence that the brain is intrinsically organised into opposing systems, are most likely an artefact of the preprocessing step used to find them.

## How this maps to SD-079 — the supporting half

The diagnosis transfers almost without modification. SD-079 measured `z_goal` at pairwise cosine min 0.9878, mean 0.9969, with ‖mean(z_goal)‖ / mean‖z_goal‖ = 0.9987 — a set of channels sharing a common component so large that the residual contrast is invisible. `goal_match` spans 0.0111 across a 24-anchor pool. That is precisely the situation global signal regression exists to address, and this literature is unambiguous that the shared component is what destroys the contrast and that subtracting it is what restores it. So SD-079's core move has a two-decade precedent in a field that has argued about it exhaustively, and the argument has never been about whether the operation works — it is about what to make of the result.

There is also a disanalogy that runs in REE's favour, and I want it on the record so this entry is not misread as an argument against centering. The whole fMRI controversy turns on the global signal being of *uncertain status* — plausibly neural, plausibly vigilance-related, plausibly artefact, and therefore possibly real signal being discarded. REE's common mode has no such ambiguity: it is a known and separately-documented consequence of SD-008 `z_world` under-differentiation, a defect that SD-079 is explicitly routing around rather than a signal whose nature is in dispute. The case for removal is stronger here than it is there.

## The half that should change what we conclude

Here is what I think this paper costs us, and it is not small.

SD-079's evidence that the fix works downstream is that after centering, the MECH-292 `goal_match_floor` excludes 9 of 24 anchors where it previously excluded 0, and the MECH-339 outshining gate is nonzero on 9 of 24 where it was previously identically zero. Both are absolute thresholds applied to the centered quantity. Murphy et al.'s result says that centering *any* such pool forces the resulting similarity distribution to sit approximately on zero — so a fraction of the pool falling below a small threshold afterwards is the arithmetic signature of having centered, not evidence about the cue. Nine of twenty-four is 37.5%, comfortably inside what a near-zero-centred distribution would produce against a floor near the middle of it.

This does not weaken SD-079 as stated. The claim is that the gates are unconditionally dead before the fix and live after it, and that is exactly right and is what the exclusion counts demonstrate. What the paper does is drive a wedge between two readings that the current evidence note runs together: *the gate now varies across the pool* (established, and by itself a real repair — an identically-zero term contributes nothing and would make MECH-339 read as falsified by a mechanism that never ran) versus *the gate now ranks anchors by motivational relevance* (not established, and not establishable from an exclusion count, because the count is what centering produces regardless of content). SD-079 is currently `provisional` on three consecutive PASSes (806/807/823), and I do not think this paper should disturb that. I do think the next consumer test for this family should be designed so that the centered `goal_match` has to track something independently identifiable — an anchor pool with a known relevance ordering, where the question is whether centering recovers *that* ordering — rather than only showing that the spread became nonzero.

The second warning is smaller but concrete. Centering will push some `goal_match` values negative, and MECH-339's gate is `clip((outshine_pivot − goal_match) / outshine_pivot)`. A negative match drives that expression above 1 before clipping. Murphy et al.'s simulation result — that trivial phase differences suffice to produce spurious negatives after regression — is the caution that those below-zero values may encode nothing, while the gate treats them as maximal outshining. Worth a look at whether the contract fixtures for SD-079 currently exercise the negative-match branch at all.

## Limitations of the transfer

The analytic result carries; the empirical apparatus does not. The sum-to-negative derivation is a property of removing a shared component from a set of signals and correlating what remains, and REE's centered cosine is that construction, so I expect the distributional consequence to hold. But everything establishing that the negatives are *spurious* is BOLD-specific — haemodynamic phase relationships, breath-holding-driven global fluctuations, seed-voxel connectivity across a scan session. `z_goal` is not a time series correlated against another time series; it is one latent vector compared by cosine to stored snapshots. The phase argument has no direct counterpart, so the spuriousness finding transfers as a reason to check rather than as a prediction of what we will find.

## Confidence

0.62. The source is strong — analytic derivation, simulation, and a designed experiment, from a group with no stake in either side of the anti-correlation debate, and the paper substantially reset how a whole field interpreted its own results. The discount is about transfer, and specifically about the asymmetry within the paper: the part I am leaning on hardest (the distributional consequence) is essentially algebra and transfers well, while the part that gives it teeth (that the resulting negatives are artefactual) is physiology and does not. I have deliberately not weighted this as high as its rhetorical force might suggest, because an entry whose contribution is a caveat should not borrow authority from empirical work that does not reach REE.
