# Intracortical modulation, and not spinal inhibition, mediates placebo analgesia (Martini et al., Eur J Neurosci 2015)

## What the paper did

Two groups of healthy volunteers -- one conditioned for placebo analgesia, one unconditioned control
-- received laser stimuli at three suprathreshold energies on the right hand while laser-evoked
potentials (LEPs) were recorded. LEPs buy millisecond resolution, which is the point: they let you
ask *when* in the processing sequence the placebo effect appears. The answer was that placebo
significantly reduced the amplitude of the **late P2** component while leaving the **early N1**
component -- which reflects the arrival of nociceptive input at primary somatosensory cortex --
unaffected, N1 varying only with stimulus energy. The authors conclude that the selective
suppression of late LEPs indicates intracortical modulation rather than inhibition of nociceptive
input at the spinal level.

## Why this bears on ARC-104

If this result stands, it is ARC-104's architecture rendered at millisecond resolution. The harm
signal **arrives intact**. What changes is what happens to it afterwards, at the evaluative stage.
The veto, on this picture, is not an active blocking mechanism at all -- it is realised as the
*integrity of the early afferent signal*, which the expectation manipulation simply cannot reach.
Language (or expectation more broadly) gets to re-weight; it does not get to edit the registration.

That suggests an implementable form for the guard in REE, and it is a more tractable one than "no
path" might imply: protect the harm channel's **primary registration**, and permit symbolic
influence only strictly downstream of it. One does not need to prove a universal negative about
paths; one needs a registration stage that the language pathway has no write access to.

## Limitations -- and there are three, all serious

I want to be blunt about this entry, because its conclusion is convenient for the claim and that is
exactly when one should press hardest.

First, **the supportive part of the result is a null**. "N1 unaffected" in a modest-N two-group
design is weak evidence of preservation, not proof of it. Second, **the placebo was induced by
conditioning, not verbal suggestion** -- so strictly this speaks to the experiential route, not the
symbolic route ARC-104 actually guards, and Spisak et al. (2026) in this same pull give us positive
reason to think those routes differ. Third, and most important: it is **directly contradicted by
Eippert et al. (2009)**, also in this pull, which reports strongly reduced pain-related spinal cord
activity under placebo. The locus of placebo action is contested, not settled.

So this entry should not be cited as resolving anything. It is one side of a live dispute, and the
governance record should carry it as such.

## Confidence

0.65, and the aggregate is **deliberately set below the mean of its components**. Source quality
0.72 (mid-tier journal, modest N, a null on the critical component). Mapping fidelity 0.75 -- the
component-level dissociation really is the shape ARC-104 asserts, and that is worth something even
under dispute. Transfer risk 0.40.

The reason for pushing the aggregate down: the contradiction with Eippert is a property of the
*evidence base*, not of either paper's internal quality, and it would be dishonest to let it sit
only in the caveat text while the number reported upward looked like a clean 0.7+. Both papers are
in this pull, pointing opposite ways, and the confidences should show that.
