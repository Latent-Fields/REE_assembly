# Distinct neural correlates of conditioned versus verbally induced placebo analgesia (Spisak et al., Nature Communications 2026)

## What the paper did

This is an individual-participant-data meta-analysis of 16 within-participant placebo neuroimaging
studies, n = 409, using both univariate and multivariate approaches, from the groups that largely
define this field (Wager, Bingel). Its design does something no single study in the literature does:
it **separates the purely symbolic route from the experiential one** and asks how deep each reaches
into the nociceptive hierarchy.

The findings, in the order that matters for us. Both verbal suggestion alone and suggestion combined
with conditioning activated dorsolateral prefrontal and inferior parietal cortices, and both
decreased insula, putamen and primary sensory activity at the conjunction level. But the arms came
apart in two ways. Behaviourally, instruction alone produced 9.2 VAS points of analgesia (Hedges
g = 0.367, small-to-moderate) against 12.2 points for conditioning-plus-instruction (g = 0.744,
moderate-to-large), a significant difference at p = 0.006. Anatomically -- and this is the sentence
the whole entry turns on -- **significant decreases in S1 and mid-posterior insula appeared only in
the conditioning-plus-instruction arm**, not in the instruction-only arm. The authors conclude the
two strategies "rely on partially distinct mechanisms in the brain", and gloss instruction in
predictive-coding terms as acting as a reference point against which sensory input is evaluated.

## Why this bears on ARC-104

I did not expect to find a biological result this close to the claim's exact wording. ARC-104 has
two halves: symbolic input may *condition priors*, and the harm substrate *retains veto*. This
meta-analysis maps onto both. Symbolic input reaches evaluative and expectancy machinery -- dlPFC,
inferior parietal -- and shifts the prior against which harm is judged. What it does not do, by
itself, is significantly suppress the primary sensory-nociceptive representation. That requires
first-hand experience.

And the authors' own predictive-coding gloss -- instruction as a reference point against which
sensory input is *evaluated* -- is very nearly a restatement of the sentence from
`trust_and_deception.md` that the whole LTI-1 node was built from: *symbolic input is informative
but not authoritative*. Biology appears to have implemented something with that shape.

## The part that cuts the other way, which I do not want to bury

Verbal instruction alone was **not inert**. It produced real analgesia (9.2 VAS points is not
nothing) and it contributed to joint deactivations in putamen, insula and cingulate isthmus. So
symbolic input does modulate the harm experience; what it spares is the primary sensory
representation specifically. Against the strictest reading of ARC-104 -- *no* path by which symbolic
content suppresses a harm signal -- this is a counterexample. Against a graded reading -- language
conditions the evaluative layer and cannot reach the primary registration -- it is support. That is
why the direction is recorded as `mixed` rather than `supports`, and it is the finding I have routed
to governance, because it bears on the claim's wording rather than merely on its confidence.

## Limitations

Two, and the second is the one governance should weigh. First, this is pain *perception* modulated
by expectation in healthy humans, not an agent's harm veto being overridden by an interlocutor's
assertion; the layering is analogous, the substrate is not, and REE's harm channel is not a
nociceptive pathway. Second, and more carefully: **the supportive finding is a null**. "No
significant S1 decrease under instruction alone" rests on 5 studies and n = 147, against 11 studies
and n = 268 for the combined arm. A null on the smaller subset is partly a statement about power. It
must not be read as demonstrated absence of a path, and I have said so in the record's
`mapping_caveat`. What biology licenses here is a *graded* guard; it does not license the absolute
prohibition ARC-104 currently asserts.

## Confidence

0.72. Source quality 0.90 -- the strongest methodology in this pull by a distance: IPD rather than
coordinate-based, within-participant designs, large pooled n, authoritative groups. Mapping fidelity
0.70 and transfer risk 0.40, both reflecting that expectation-modulated nociception is an analogue
of, not an instance of, a linguistic override of a harm veto. The aggregate sits below what the
source quality alone would suggest because the direction is genuinely two-sided.
