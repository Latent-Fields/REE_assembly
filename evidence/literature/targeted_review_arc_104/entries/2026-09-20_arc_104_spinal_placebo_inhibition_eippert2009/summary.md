# Direct evidence for spinal cord involvement in placebo analgesia (Eippert et al., Science 2009)

## What the paper did

Using functional MRI of the human cervical spinal cord -- technically difficult, and the reason this
was a *Science* paper -- Eippert and colleagues tested whether placebo analgesia reduces nociceptive
processing at the spinal level. Subjects received a placebo analgesic cream and painful heat on the
arm. They reported less pain, and showed reduced activity in the ipsilateral dorsal horn. The
authors present this as direct evidence for spinal inhibition as one mechanism of placebo analgesia,
and draw the broader conclusion that psychological factors can act on the earliest stages of pain
processing in the central nervous system.

## Why this is in the pull, and why it is recorded as `weakens`

This is the entry that argues against ARC-104 as currently worded, and it belongs here for exactly
that reason. ARC-104 asserts there must be **no path** by which symbolic content overwrites or
suppresses a harm signal. Biology, on this result, has built such a path, and routes it all the way
down to the first synapse of the nociceptive system via descending modulation. The dorsal horn is
not a late evaluative stage; it is the primary relay. If a top-down expectancy signal can attenuate
activity there, then the absolute form of ARC-104 describes an architecture that natural design
declined to adopt.

I do not think the right inference is that the guard is wrong. I think it is that **the absolutism
is biologically unprecedented**, and that biology's actual solution is instructive: it permits a
top-down write to the harm channel, and constrains it by making that write a *specific, identifiable,
bounded circuit* -- descending opioidergic modulation with known anatomy -- rather than an arbitrary
overwrite available to any input. That is a materially different design commitment from "no path",
and arguably a more implementable one: a channel you can audit beats a prohibition you must prove
universally.

## The unresolved question, which limits how much weight this can carry

Was the induction **symbolic or experiential**? This matters enormously and I could not settle it.
Placebo-cream paradigms in this literature standardly combine verbal instruction with a conditioning
procedure (surreptitiously reducing stimulus intensity during a training phase). Spisak et al.
(2026), in this same pull, show those routes have partially distinct neural correlates, with S1
effects appearing only under conditioning. If conditioning was present here -- and I would expect it
was, though I could not confirm it -- then this result may not bear on the purely *linguistic* path
ARC-104 guards at all, and its force as a counterexample drops sharply.

**Provenance note, stated plainly:** the full text was not retrievable by this session --
science.org returned HTTP 403 -- and the PubMed record carries only the short *Science* brevia
abstract. Sample size, the exact induction protocol, and the effect statistics are therefore
**unverified**, and I have asserted none of them anywhere in this entry or in the record. Everything
above is at the level the abstract and secondary descriptions support. A future session with
institutional access should retrieve the methods and, if conditioning is confirmed, revisit this
entry's direction -- it may deserve downgrading to `mixed` or reclassifying as evidence about the
experiential route only.

It is also contradicted by Martini et al. (2015), in this pull, which finds the early nociceptive
component unaffected and locates the placebo effect intracortically.

## Confidence

0.62, carrying two independent discounts. Source quality is high at 0.85 -- a landmark result from a
leading group in a top journal, and the conceptual finding has been influential and broadly
sustained. But mapping fidelity is only 0.60 because of the symbolic-versus-experiential ambiguity
above, and transfer risk is 0.45, the highest in the pull, because a descending-modulation finding
in the nociceptive system generalises poorly to a designed harm veto in an artificial agent. The
unverified methods are a further reason not to score this higher than a disconfirming entry of this
importance might otherwise warrant.
