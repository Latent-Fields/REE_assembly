# Milstein et al. 2022 -- Reward-biased replay emerges from CA3 network constraints

According to PubMed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1113/JP283216), J Physiol 2022), Milstein, Tran, Ng and Soltesz built a recurrent spiking model of hippocampal CA3 and asked which biological constraints are sufficient to produce flexible offline replay. With population sparsity, stimulus selectivity, rhythmicity, spike-rate adaptation and associative connectivity in place, the network spontaneously generated forward, reverse and mixed-direction replay during a noise-driven offline state. Notably for this review, it also recapitulated the experimental observation that **offline replay over-represents salient locations such as reward sites** -- the reward bias emerged from the population code rather than being imposed by a dedicated mechanism.

I treat this the way the MECH-319 dossier treats Joo & Frank 2018: as a **substrate-availability anchor** rather than a functional test. Its value for MECH-443 is to answer a specific worry -- that adding a graded priority signal to REE's replay write channel would require a bespoke "priority module" with no biological license. Milstein et al. show the opposite: reward/salience-weighted replay falls out for free once a recurrent network has realistic structure, because the online code over-represents salient positions and the offline dynamics inherit that bias. Prioritized replay is, in this sense, biologically cheap.

The caveat that keeps this at moderate-low confidence is the same one that applies to any availability anchor: it establishes that the bias *can emerge*, not that the bias is *read out* as a write-strength onto a downstream rule or value layer. The model is about the genesis of biased replay content within CA3, not about how that content gates updates elsewhere. For the downstream write-gating step I lean on Haga & Fukai and Mattar & Daw; this paper underwrites the upstream premise that a priority-carrying replay signal is available to be gated in the first place.

Confidence 0.58 (supports), deliberately below the function-level papers. It is the correct kind of evidence to cite for "the substrate supports a graded priority," and it would be a mistake to cite it for "the priority gates the write" -- a distinction MECH-443 has to keep clean if it is to be non-vacuous.
