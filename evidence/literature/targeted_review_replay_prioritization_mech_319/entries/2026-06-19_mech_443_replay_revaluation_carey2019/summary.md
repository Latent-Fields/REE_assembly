# Carey, Tanaka & van der Meer 2019 -- Revaluation biases replay AWAY from the preferred outcome

According to PubMed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-019-0464-6), Nat Neurosci 2019), Carey, Tanaka and van der Meer recorded awake hippocampal replay while rats ran a T-maze offering a free choice between food and water, under alternating hunger and thirst restriction. Behaviour did what motivational accounts predict: animals shifted their choices *toward* the currently restricted (more valuable) outcome. Replay content did the opposite -- it shifted *away* from the restricted outcome and toward the non-restricted arm. The effect preceded each day's experience and did not reverse with task exposure.

This is the paper I deliberately went looking for, because a literature pull that only assembled confirmations would be doing the project a disservice. It is a clean dissociation between behavioural preference and replay content, and it falsifies the most tempting -- and wrong -- way to implement MECH-443: equating replay priority with reward magnitude, or with the action-value of the currently committed policy. If REE were to read a priority signal off "how valuable is this outcome right now," this result says it would point the system the wrong way.

But notice what it does *not* falsify, and why I scored it `mixed` rather than `weakens`. It strongly confirms the deeper premise of MECH-443: that replay is **prioritized by internal motivational state and is not limited to recent experience or to trajectories toward the preferred goal**. Replay is selecting content for a reason -- it is just that the reason is closer to Mattar & Daw's *need* term (cover states that are under-represented or about to matter) than to reward level. Read together with Mattar & Daw, the picture is coherent: prioritize by the value of the *update* (gain x need), which can and does push replay toward currently non-preferred but informationally-important content. That is precisely the refinement MECH-443 and MECH-444 must encode -- the priority is update-utility, not reward, and a MECH-444 target "refreshed against current value" would mis-rank here unless the refresh is about model-staleness/coverage rather than current preferred-outcome value.

Confidence 0.62, direction mixed. The source quality is high (Nat Neurosci, direct recordings, a revaluation design built exactly to probe model-based control), and it is the single most decision-relevant paper in this set for *how to shape* the REE priority signal. The transfer caveat is that the away-from-preferred direction may partly reflect the coverage demands of this particular T-maze, so I would not over-read the specific direction; what survives is the hard constraint that reward magnitude is not the priority variable.
