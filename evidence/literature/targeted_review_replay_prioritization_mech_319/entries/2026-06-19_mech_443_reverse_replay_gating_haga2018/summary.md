# Haga & Fukai 2018 -- Reverse replay gates which pathway is strengthened

According to PubMed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.34171), eLife 2018), Haga and Fukai built a biophysically-constrained recurrent model of hippocampal area CA3 to ask how reverse replay -- which occurs frequently at rewarded locations -- could selectively support goal-directed learning. The puzzle they address is sharp: symmetric spike-timing-dependent plasticity in CA3 would, naively, strengthen both forward (start-to-goal) and reverse (goal-to-start) pathways equally during a replayed sequence. They show that in the presence of short-term synaptic depression or afterdepolarization, a firing sequence instead **biases potentiation toward the pathway opposite to the direction of propagation**, so that *reverse* replay selectively strengthens the *forward*, goal-directed route. The reward-anchoring of reverse replay thereby determines which synaptic pathway gets written.

I included this paper because it speaks to the part of MECH-443 the recording studies leave inferred: not just *whether* replay writes, and not just *which content* is replayed, but *which downstream pathway the write strengthens*. Mattar & Daw give the priority ordering; Olafsdottir gives the content bias; Haga & Fukai give an explicit mechanism by which reward-anchored replay **gates the target of the update**. That is the conceptual core of moving from MECH-319's binary mode-gate to MECH-443's graded, content-selective write: replay carries information about *what* to update, not merely permission *to* update.

The caveats are substantial and I do not want to oversell the mapping. First, this is a model, not a recording -- it demonstrates that the gating is mechanistically *possible* under realistic constraints, not that the brain demonstrably does it this way. Second, and more important for transfer, the "write" here is potentiation of a place-cell synaptic pathway, which is an analog of, not an instance of, a rule-arbitration weight update. The cellular mechanism that produces the selectivity -- short-term depression interacting with symmetric STDP -- has no counterpart in REE's parametric `rule_state` substrate. So the *what* (reward-anchored replay selects its update target) transfers; the *why* (the specific plasticity interaction) does not.

Confidence 0.6 (supports). It earns its place as the one source that directly addresses the write-gating step rather than the content-selection step, but its model status and the synaptic-vs-arbitration substrate gap keep it from rising higher. Read alongside the recording papers it closes a gap in the argument; on its own it would be too indirect.
