# Mattar & Daw 2018 -- Prioritized memory access explains planning and hippocampal replay

According to PubMed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-018-0232-z), Nat Neurosci 2018), Mattar and Daw asked a question that is almost exactly the one CDQ-005 poses to REE: of all the experiences an agent could replay, which ones *should* it replay to most improve future decisions? Their answer is a normative theory in which each potential memory access is scored by its **expected value of a backup (EVB)**, factorized into two terms -- **gain** (how much performing this backup would change the agent's policy) and **need** (how often the agent expects to occupy the backed-up state). Replay, in their account, is the brain executing the highest-EVB backups first. Crucially, this single prioritization rule reproduces a long list of otherwise-disparate replay phenomena: reward-biased replay, replay of novel or surprising trajectories, forward replay before goal-directed departure, reverse replay after reward receipt, and the consolidation literature, all fall out of one ordering principle.

For MECH-443 (priority-weighted replay write selection) this is the cleanest possible support. It establishes that biological replay is **not uniform** -- the very thing that distinguishes MECH-443 from MECH-319's binary gate. MECH-319 decides *whether* a simulation-mode pass may write; Mattar & Daw establish that, even among admissible replays, biology imposes a graded ordering by update-utility. The refinement the paper forces is important and I want to state it plainly: the priority is **not raw reward magnitude** but **gain x need** -- the value of the *update*, which can be high for a low-reward state whose estimate is badly wrong, and low for a high-reward state already well-learned. That is the form MECH-443's priority signal should take.

For MECH-444 (staleness-gated target refresh) the support is real but one step more indirect. The **gain** term is, operationally, a measure of how *stale or wrong* the current estimate is: a backup has high gain exactly when propagating new information would move the policy, i.e. when the stored target no longer reflects what the current model would compute. This is the biological shadow of MuZero/EfficientZero reanalyze -- refreshing a stored target against the current model and weighting the update by how much it has drifted. The paper does not, however, demonstrate a literal "recompute-the-target-then-write" cellular operation; it shows that an idealized agent *ought* to prioritize by something the reanalyze trick approximates.

My confidence (0.79, supports) is weighted toward mapping fidelity rather than away from it, because the gain x need decomposition is an unusually faithful analog of the two quantities REE wants to register. The honest caveat is the substrate gap: Mattar & Daw prioritize accesses to a tabular spatial value function, whereas REE's rule-arbitration layer is a parametric EMA over `rule_state`. "Gain" cannot be read off the REE substrate directly; it has to be operationalized as a surprise or value-prediction-error proxy. So the paper licenses the *existence and form* of MECH-443's priority signal, and the *interpretation* of MECH-444's staleness term, without specifying the REE implementation -- which is exactly what a candidate claim should rest on.
