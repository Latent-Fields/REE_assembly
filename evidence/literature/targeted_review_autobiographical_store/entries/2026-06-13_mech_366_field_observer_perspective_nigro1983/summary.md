# Field vs observer perspective as the construct behind MECH-366's viewpoint tag

**Nigro & Neisser (1983), *Cognitive Psychology* 15:467-482. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(83)90016-6)**
Claim grounded: **MECH-366**. Direction: **mixed**.

## What the paper does

Nigro and Neisser established, across four studies of naturalistic personal memories, that autobiographical recollection comes in two structurally distinct points of view. In a **field** memory the rememberer sees the scene from their original first-person vantage — the field of view they actually had. In an **observer** memory they see *themselves* in the scene, from an external vantage, as a spectator would. The distinction is robust and reportable. They also found it is not random: which perspective dominates varies systematically with the **emotionality** of the event, the degree of **self-awareness** at encoding, and the **age** of the memory, with older and more self-focused memories tending toward the observer view.

## Why it grounds MECH-366

MECH-366 asserts that REE's autobiographical event tokens carry a **viewpoint label** — participant/first-person vs observer/third-person — as a *represented, switchable property of the episode* rather than a fixed artefact of how it was encoded. Nigro and Neisser are the canonical source for the first half of that claim. They show perspective is a genuine, dissociable structural property of an episode, and the field/observer pair they name is exactly the participant/observer pair REE adopts. Without this anchor, MECH-366 would be inventing a memory dimension; with it, the dimension is one the autobiographical-memory literature has studied for four decades.

## Where the mapping strains (why this is `mixed`, not `supports`)

MECH-366's load-bearing move is not merely *that* viewpoint is represented — it is that viewpoint is **freely switchable at retrieval, independent of the encoding viewpoint**. Nigro and Neisser do not show this. Their method is self-report on naturalistic memories; no manipulation demonstrates that a single memory can be voluntarily re-experienced from the *other* viewpoint on demand. Worse for the strong reading: the very regularities they report — perspective tracking emotion, self-awareness, and memory age — suggest viewpoint is *partly determined* by encoding conditions and affect, not arbitrarily re-set whenever the system pleases. That is a mild contra-signal to encoding-independent switchability.

So this entry does two honest things at once. It grounds the **construct** MECH-366 builds on (strong), and it flags the **switchability** half as not-yet-evidenced and even slightly contraindicated by the encoding-dependence findings. The cleaner evidence for voluntary perspective-shifting lives in later experimental work (e.g. instructed perspective-shift paradigms); a follow-up pull should target that to close the gap. Until then MECH-366's switchability assumption is a design commitment, not a grounded one — appropriate for a V4 candidate, and exactly the kind of thing a forward roadmap should know it still owes evidence on.

## Confidence

0.60, direction `mixed`. Source quality is solid for a seminal self-report construct. Mapping fidelity is moderate because the paper carries the construct but not the switchability. Transfer risk is non-trivial: human introspective report on real-life memories maps only loosely onto a synthetic token's viewpoint field. Raises literature_confidence modestly; promotes nothing.
