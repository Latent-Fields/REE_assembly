# I can see it both ways: First- and third-person visual perspectives at retrieval (Rice & Rubin, 2009)

**Claim grounded:** MECH-366 (switchable episodic perspective tag) — the *separability* of viewpoint from content, and its dual-valued, queryable character.

## What the paper did

Rice and Rubin set out to correct a methodological habit: treating field (first-person) and observer (third-person) perspective as the two ends of one bipolar scale, so that "more first-person" automatically means "less third-person." Across rating studies of autobiographical and laboratory events, they had people rate, separately, the degree to which they experienced each memory from each perspective, and looked at how those two ratings related to one another and to other phenomenological properties such as vividness.

## Key findings relevant to the claim

The headline is in the title: people can see it both ways. A single remembered event is frequently experienced from first- *and* third-person perspectives, and the two ratings do not simply trade off — they correlate differentially with vividness. The implication is that the two perspectives are separable represented attributes of the episode rather than two values of one forced-choice dimension. The paper also reports individual and gender differences (third-person experience is more common in women), which says the default perspective is partly a stable property of the rememberer.

## How it translates to REE

For REE this entry does a job distinct from St. Jacques (switchability) and Nigro & Neisser (construct existence): it grounds the claim that viewpoint is a *separable, dual-valued property* that can be queried independently of the episode's content. That is precisely the distinction the task asks me to secure — MECH-366's viewpoint label has to be shown to be a property *distinct from SD-005's self-vs-world content split*. Rice & Rubin's result that both perspectives can coexist on one memory, and load differently onto vividness, is the cleanest behavioural evidence that perspective is not a re-description of content but its own attribute on the token. A viewpoint *label* (settable to either value, or readable from either) fits their data better than a single baked-in camera angle.
