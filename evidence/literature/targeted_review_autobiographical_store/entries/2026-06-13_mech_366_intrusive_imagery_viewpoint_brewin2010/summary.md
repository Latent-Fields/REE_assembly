# Intrusive images in psychological disorders (Brewin, Gregory, Lipton & Burgess, 2010)

**Claim grounded:** MECH-366 (switchable episodic perspective tag) — viewpoint as a represented, dissociable, *modifiable* property that also holds for imagined/re-experienced material — and, in the same breath, the boundary condition on free switchability.

## What the paper did

Brewin and colleagues review intrusive mental imagery across PTSD, depression, anxiety, eating disorders and psychosis, and offer a dual-representation account of it. The core distinction is between a contextually bound representation that can be deliberately retrieved and situated in time, and a sensory-bound representation that is triggered involuntarily and re-experienced with a vivid first-person "nowness." On this view the *viewpoint* of an image is not incidental — it is a clinically meaningful property, and one of the levers therapy pulls is to change it (for instance, helping a patient re-experience a flashback from an observer perspective rather than a first-person reliving, which reduces its distress).

## Key findings relevant to the claim

Two things matter for MECH-366, and they pull in opposite directions, which is why I marked this entry MIXED. On the supporting side: viewpoint is a represented, dissociable property that applies to *imagined and re-experienced* content, not only to deliberate recall, and it can be shifted — the treatment literature on perspective change is, in effect, applied evidence for switchability. That extends the field/observer construct beyond the recall paradigms of St. Jacques and Rice & Rubin into the imagery domain MECH-366 cares about (replay *and* prospective simulation). On the bounding side: traumatic intrusions are characteristically *involuntary* and locked to a first-person reliving perspective that the patient cannot re-set at will. The viewpoint is precisely what is stuck. And the therapeutic shift, when it works, is effortful and structured — not a costless retrieval-time toggle.

## How it translates to REE

This is the clinical/neural anchor that keeps MECH-366 honest. It supports the claim's strongest ambition — that the same event/image can in principle be re-experienced from either viewpoint — and grounds it for imagined material, which the laboratory recall studies do not directly cover. But it also supplies the most concrete real-world counterexample to a naive "freely switchable at will" reading: under high affective load, the viewpoint can be involuntarily fixed, and switching has a cost. For a V4 implementation that is a useful constraint rather than a defeater. It suggests the ARC-085 viewpoint label should be modelled with an *eligibility/cost* on switching that is modulated by the episode's affective and provenance status — which dovetails with the affect-as-write-weight strand (MECH-361/MECH-368) and the provenance gate (MECH-365). The switch is available, but not unconditionally.
