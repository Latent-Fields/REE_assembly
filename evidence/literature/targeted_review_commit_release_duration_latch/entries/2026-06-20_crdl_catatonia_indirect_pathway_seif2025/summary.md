# Catatonia and indirect-pathway dysregulation (Seif 2025)

According to PubMed, Seif (2025), *Does Dysregulation Of The Indirect Pathway Contribute To The Pathophysiology Of Catatonia Through Neurotransmitter Imbalance?*, Clinical Neuropsychiatry ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.36131/cnfioritieditore20250306)), is a narrative review that synthesises clinical, neuroimaging and preclinical work (1980-2025) around one hypothesis: that catatonia -- a syndrome of stupor, rigidity and stereotypies affecting a substantial minority of psychiatric inpatients -- arises from dysfunction of the basal ganglia's **indirect (No-Go) pathway**. The review marshals reduced GABAergic tone, dopamine D2 receptor dysfunction, glutamatergic hyperactivity, and altered serotonergic/cholinergic modulation as converging on **impaired inhibitory control** within that pathway, and notes that benzodiazepines -- which enhance GABA-A receptor activity -- remain the first-line treatment, which is at least consistent with an inhibitory/indirect-pathway locus.

I pulled this paper for one specific job: to give the **over-maintenance pole** of the BG-3 psychiatric column a concrete clinical anchor. The node's column says "refractory-too-long maps to rigidity/perseveration"; catatonia is what that pole looks like at the bedside -- a held motor/behavioural state that the system cannot release. In REE terms, it is the predicted clinical face of a commit/de-commit latch whose release fails: the SD-034 closure operator does not fire its done-token, or MECH-446's de-commit magnitude is too weak to overcome the maintained occupancy, so the committed (or held-still) state persists past where it should. What makes this entry more than a loose metaphor is *where the review locates the lesion*: the **indirect pathway**, i.e. the same No-Go machinery REE already models in MECH-260 and is generalising in MECH-449. So the over-maintenance pole and REE's No-Go constitution are anchored to the same biological structure -- catatonia is No-Go over-pressure, exactly the failure mode the selection-face grounding (the parallel `targeted_review_connectome_mech_439` review) predicts when the indirect pathway is too strong. The two faces of the F-dominance front meet here: the selection face asks who is *allowed* to compete; the duration face asks how long the winner is *held*; and an over-strong No-Go brake breaks both in the same clinical direction.

The caveats are real and I have priced them into a deliberately modest confidence. Catatonia is **multi-determined** -- the review itself lists GABA, D2, glutamate, serotonin and acetylcholine -- and it is benzodiazepine-responsive in a way that a pure "latch failed to release" story does not explain. Mapping the whole syndrome onto a single failed latch-release is a functional simplification appropriate for the psychiatric-failure-mode *column* (which exists to say "if this component breaks, here is the disorder it would resemble"), but it is emphatically **not** a claim that REE's latch *is* the catatonia mechanism. The ARC-106 guardrail here is the honest one: a speculative disorder mapping that would mislead a clinician is worse than none, so this entry grounds the *direction and plausibility* of the over-maintenance pole, not a quantitative duration law and not a treatment claim.

There is also a venue caveat I will not hide. This is a **single-author narrative review in a lower-tier journal**, synthesising rather than demonstrating; it proposes a unifying indirect-pathway framework. For grounding the *direction* of a psychiatric-failure-mode mapping that is acceptable -- it is the right kind of source for "what disorder does over-maintenance resemble, and is that disorder plausibly an indirect-pathway/No-Go problem" -- but it carries little weight as primary evidence, and I would not let it drive any quantitative claim.

I rate this **supports** at confidence 0.50. Source quality is modest (0.50: single-author narrative review, recent, synthesising). Mapping fidelity is moderate (0.60): catatonia-as-over-maintenance / No-Go-over-pressure is a clean directional anchor for the BG-3 over-maintenance pole and usefully ties it to REE's existing No-Go machinery, but the syndrome is multi-determined. Transfer risk is moderate (0.50): a human clinical syndrome onto an ANN latch parameter is analogy-level by construction. Paired with Loh/Rolls/Deco 2007 (which anchors the under-maintenance/distractibility pole and the single-stability-parameter structure), this completes the two-poled psychiatric column the node requires.
