# Basal ganglia subcircuits encode the parsing and concatenation of action sequences (Jin, Tecuapetla & Costa 2014)

According to PubMed, Jin, Tecuapetla & Costa (2014), *Basal ganglia subcircuits distinctively encode the parsing and concatenation of action sequences*, Nature Neuroscience ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3632)), recorded basal-ganglia neurons in mice that had learned to produce rapid lever-press sequences. They found two functionally distinct signals. First, **start/stop neurons** whose activity marked the initiation and termination of an entire sequence -- the brackets around a chunk of committed behaviour. Second, a separate population with **sustained (or inhibited) activity that persisted throughout the execution of the whole sequence**, and whose level *covaried with the rate of execution* of the individual elements, consistent with the elements being concatenated into one motor unit. Direct and indirect pathways were concomitantly active at sequence initiation but behaved differently during performance. This paper is the maturation of the same lab's earlier finding (Jin & Costa 2010, *Start/stop signals emerge in nigrostriatal circuits during sequence learning*, Nature, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09263)), which first isolated the start/stop boundary signals and showed they emerge with learning and are specific to particular actions.

For the commit/de-commit latch this is almost a one-to-one functional template. REE's MECH-090 posits a commitment-gated routing variable that decouples E3's internal updating from action-selection output, with the gate opening at completion events or urgent interrupts. The biological **start signal is the gate-close / commit-entry event** and the **stop signal is the gate-open / commit-release event** -- the SD-034 "done" token that, on satisfaction of a committed rule_state's completion condition, releases the latch. The biological **sustained-throughout-the-sequence population is the maintenance signal itself**: the held routing state that keeps a committed run insulated from moment-to-moment re-selection. MECH-342's commit-maintenance-release sits naturally on top of this -- it is the readiness-degradation pathway that can release the sustained state mid-sequence rather than waiting for the stop boundary. So the cluster's three load-bearing pieces (entry, maintenance, release) each have a recorded biological correlate here.

The honest caveats are two, and both are exactly what the BG-3 node wants surfaced. First, **these signals are learned, not pre-wired**: the start/stop activity *emerges* during sequence learning and is action-specific. REE's latch is governed by a refractory parameter set at configuration time; it does not reproduce the learned, behaviour-shaped emergence of these boundaries. Second -- and this is the divergence that the whole lit-pull is anchoring -- the biological sustained signal's **duration is set by behaviour**: it covaries with the rate at which sequence elements are produced. The hold lasts as long as the chunk takes to execute, because it is the chunk's execution. REE instead sets the hold's duration with a committed-run-scaled refractory, a tuned timer that runs alongside the behaviour rather than being identical to it. That is the precise sense in which REE's "beta-gate refractory dynamics are tuned, not bio-sourced": biology does not appear to keep a separate latch-duration clock; the maintenance lasts as long as the concatenated action does.

There is also a structural simplification worth flagging for the divergence ledger. The paper shows direct and indirect pathways doing *different* things during sequence performance (concomitant at initiation, divergent during execution). REE collapses this two-pathway organisation into a single commitment-gated routing variable. That collapse is the same one the selection-face grounding (the parallel `targeted_review_connectome_mech_439` review and MECH-449's Go/No-Go constitution) is wrestling with; here it shows up specifically as the loss of a separate indirect-pathway contribution to *terminating* a maintained sequence. It is a reasonable engineering economy, but it means REE's single latch cannot express a dissociation between "hold this action" and "actively brake the competing one" that the biology has structurally available.

I rate this **supports** at confidence 0.63. Source quality is high (Nature Neuroscience, the Costa lab, a clean in vivo dissociation with a replicated lineage). Mapping fidelity is moderate (0.60): the entry/maintenance/release *structure* maps cleanly onto MECH-090/SD-034/MECH-342, but REE collapses two pathways into one variable and -- decisively for this node -- the biological maintenance duration is behaviour-set, not timer-set. Transfer risk is moderate (0.50): rodent learned operant sequences onto an ANN commit latch is a functional analogy. This is a good L2 anchor for the *maintenance and boundary structure* of the latch, with the duration-setting mechanism explicitly marked as the place REE diverges.
