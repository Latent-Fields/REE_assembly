# Changes of mind in decision-making (Resulaj, Kiani, Wolpert & Shadlen 2009)

According to PubMed, Resulaj, Kiani, Wolpert & Shadlen (2009), *Changes of mind in decision-making*, Nature ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature08275)), asked a question that most decision models of the time could not answer: once a decision has been committed -- the subject has begun the movement that reports it -- why and how does the decision-maker sometimes reverse it without any new external evidence? Subjects discriminated the direction of noisy random-dot motion and reported by reaching to a target. On some trials the hand trajectory started toward one target and then curved to the other: a change of mind, betrayed by the movement itself. The authors extended a bounded-accumulation model so that evidence already *in the processing pipeline* at the moment of commitment continues to be evaluated against a second criterion, and can either reaffirm or reverse the initial choice. The model reproduced both how often minds changed and the telling asymmetry that changes of mind were more likely to correct an error than to spoil a correct initial choice.

Why this matters for the BG-3 commit/de-commit latch: it is the cleanest biological statement that **commitment is not an irreversible argmin**. A committed plan is held, but the hold admits a bounded reversal window. That is precisely the dynamic REE's latch cluster has to support. MECH-446 (de-commit-authority magnitude) asserts that a closure-coupled signal can lower post-commit latch occupancy with enough authority to overcome the natural-commit occupancy it competes against; SD-034's closure operator is the thing that *turns evaluation off* and releases the MECH-090 bistable latch. Resulaj et al. give the function a name in the brain: the commitment state is maintained but releasable, and release/reversal is a normal part of the decision architecture, not a failure of it.

Where I want to stay honest is the **trigger**, and this is the load-bearing caveat. In the paper, the reversal is driven by *continued evidence accumulation* -- information that was already travelling up the sensorimotor pipeline when the bound was first crossed. REE's de-commit is not evidence-driven in that sense: it is triggered by a closure/completion token (the rule_state's completion condition firing) and shaped by a committed-run-scaled refractory. So the two systems share the FUNCTION -- a committed action admits a bounded release -- but not the mechanism that opens the window. If a future REE write-up reads this paper as evidence that REE's *specific* release timing matches the biological reversal window, it would over-claim. The paper anchors the existence and the boundedness of the release window; it does not anchor REE's duration law.

That distinction is exactly the divergence the BG-3 node is built to surface. The biological reversal window is set by how much evidence remains in the pipeline and by a processing deadline -- it is *content-determined*. REE's release is set by a tuned refractory parameter -- it is *parameter-determined*, and that parameter is the thing the node flags as "tuned, not bio-sourced." Resulaj et al. tell us the window should exist and should be bounded; they explicitly do *not* tell us that a fixed refractory is the right way to bound it. A REE latch whose refractory is set too long would suppress the kind of error-correcting reversal this paper documents (the perseveration pole); one set too short would reverse commitments that biology would have reaffirmed (the distractibility pole). The clinical mapping in the BG-3 column falls straight out of that.

I rate this **supports** at confidence 0.66. Source quality is near-ceiling (Nature, the Shadlen lab, a paradigm that has been replicated and extended many times). Mapping fidelity is moderate (0.62): the existence of a bounded post-commit release window maps cleanly onto MECH-446/SD-034, but the reversal trigger differs (pipelined evidence vs closure token). Transfer risk is real but bounded (0.45): human reaching psychophysics onto an ANN committed-action latch is a functional analogy, and REE's release is an engineered refractory rather than an evidence-accumulation bound. For a grounding (L1->L2) anchor on the de-commit face that is what I want -- a canonical existence proof of the function, with the divergence stated plainly rather than papered over.
