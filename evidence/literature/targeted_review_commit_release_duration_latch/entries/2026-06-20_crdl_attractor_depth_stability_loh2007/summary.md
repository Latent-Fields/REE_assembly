# A dynamical systems hypothesis of schizophrenia (Loh, Rolls & Deco 2007)

According to PubMed, Loh, Rolls & Deco (2007), *A dynamical systems hypothesis of schizophrenia*, PLoS Computational Biology ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.0030228)), built integrate-and-fire spiking networks of cortical attractor states and asked what happens when you vary the **depth of the attractor basins**. Their central result is mechanically simple and, for this node, exactly on target: a *reduced* depth of the basins -- produced by lowering NMDA receptor conductance -- destabilises the network against the constant stochastic fluctuations that arise from the random spiking of neurons. The held state can no longer resist noise, so short-term-memory states decay early and the network becomes **distractible**. The authors map this onto the cognitive symptoms of schizophrenia (poor attention, working-memory deficits, distractibility), tie lowered firing rates in orbitofrontal/anterior-cingulate networks to negative symptoms, and argue that combined NMDA+GABA reductions make the basins so shallow that thoughts "move too freely round the attractor energy landscape," which they relate to positive symptoms.

The reason this paper belongs on the commit/de-commit latch shelf is not its psychiatric taxonomy but its **control structure**. It demonstrates, in a working spiking model, that a *single stability parameter* -- attractor depth -- sets **how long a network holds a state against noise**, and that this one knob trades stability against flexibility. That is the formal cousin of REE's premise for the BG-3 latch: there is one duration-setting parameter (the committed-run-scaled refractory / maintenance gain behind MECH-090 and MECH-342), and the system's health depends on where it sits. Turn the knob one way and the held state collapses too early; turn it the other and it cannot be displaced. The BG-3 node's psychiatric column -- refractory-too-short maps to distractibility/disorganisation, refractory-too-long maps to rigidity/perseveration -- is precisely the two-poled failure structure this model exhibits. Loh, Rolls & Deco give REE the cleanest existing argument that **both clinical poles can be consequences of mis-setting one stability parameter**, which is the strongest possible grounding for treating the refractory as a single load-bearing dial rather than an incidental constant.

The honesty I want on the record is about *which* pole this specific paper anchors and *what* the persistence mechanism is. The headline simulations foreground the **shallow** end -- reduced depth, instability, distractibility, disorganised "free movement" round the landscape. That is the under-maintenance pole, and it is anchored empirically (within the model). The **deep** end -- over-stable, perseverative, unable-to-switch -- is the symmetric complement that the broader Rolls/Deco programme develops (e.g. deepened attractors as a model for obsessive/perseverative states); it is implied by the same energy-landscape logic but is not this paper's primary demonstration. So when I lean on this entry for the over-maintenance pole, I am leaning on the *symmetry of the framework*, not on a simulation in this paper. The dedicated over-maintenance clinical anchor in this review is the catatonia/indirect-pathway entry (Seif 2025), which is why I pulled it alongside this one.

Two further caveats for the divergence ledger. First, this is a **computational model**, not data -- its evidential weight is "a mechanism that works in simulation and reproduces known symptom clusters," not "a measured fact about commitment." Second, and more important for fidelity: the model's persistence mechanism is **recurrent, NMDA-stabilised firing in a cortical working-memory/semantic network**, not a basal-ganglia motor commitment latch and not a refractory *timer*. REE holds a commitment with a gated routing variable on a refractory clock; Loh et al. hold a memory with a self-sustaining attractor. Both are persistence mechanisms and both have a single stability parameter, but equating attractor depth with REE's refractory is an analogy at the level of *control structure* ("one knob, two failure poles"), not a mechanistic identity. That is the right altitude for an L1->L2 grounding anchor: it justifies the *shape* of REE's parameterisation and its psychiatric column, while leaving the timer-vs-recurrence question as an explicit open divergence.

I rate this **supports** at confidence 0.62. Source quality is solid (PLoS Computational Biology, the Rolls/Deco group, heavily cited) but capped because it is a model rather than data. Mapping fidelity is the highest in this set (0.70) for the *specific* claim being grounded -- that one stability parameter sets duration and that mis-setting it produces opposite clinical poles -- because that trade-off is literally what the model is about. Transfer risk is moderate (0.50): a cortical working-memory attractor mapped onto a motor commitment latch shares the persistence-against-noise function but differs in substrate and in whether duration is set by a timer or by recurrence.
