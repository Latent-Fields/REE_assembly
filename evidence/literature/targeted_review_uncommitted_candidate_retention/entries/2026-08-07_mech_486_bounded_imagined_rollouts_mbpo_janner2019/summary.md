# When to trust imagined rollouts: MBPO (Janner et al. 2019)

Janner, Fu, Zhang and Levine's "When to Trust Your Model: Model-Based Policy Optimization" (MBPO; NeurIPS 2019, [arXiv:1906.08253](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08253)) is the machine-learning half of this lit-pull, and it answers the part of the chip's question the neuroscience cannot: *given* that you retain and use imagined/non-executed content, under what discipline is doing so legitimate rather than self-poisoning? MBPO's answer is a bounded, uncertainty-gated regime. It generates *short* model rollouts branched from real environment states -- rather than long rollouts from the start state -- to keep compounding model error small, and it decouples the model (imagination) horizon from the task horizon. The theoretical contribution is a monotonic-improvement analysis: real off-policy data is, in general, preferable to model-generated data, *unless* an empirical estimate of the model's generalization justifies limited model usage, in which case bounded model use yields faster and higher-performing learning. The failure mode it exists to prevent is model exploitation -- the policy learning to exploit regions where the learned model deviates from reality.

The mapping to REE is nearly one-to-one with the template the MECH-485 and INV-012 Leg-3 notes *require*: any retention mechanism must follow the MECH-322 shape -- bounded, audited, provisional-until-corroborated -- and must not be a general relaxation of the MECH-094 imagination/reality write gate. MBPO is that shape rendered as a concrete algorithm. Retained rejected candidates (imagined, non-executed trajectory content) may feed evaluation and learning only within a horizon whose length is gated by measured model uncertainty; content that is not corroborated by real execution loses trust. MECH-322's own fail-closed conditions and its accelerated dissolution of any chunk not corroborated by real waking execution are the REE instantiation of MBPO's short-horizon trust bound. The analogy even yields a knob: the trust bound should scale inversely with model uncertainty, which in REE is the epistemic_deficit term (MECH-482) -- so leg-3 retention would be gated by the same confidence signal that, in MECH-485's pipeline, routes low-confidence predictions to the orient/survey leg. That is a satisfying convergence: the same uncertainty estimate both defers action *and* bounds how far retained content may be trusted.

The honest boundary. MBPO bounds *value-estimation error* for the purpose of policy optimization. It says nothing about provenance confusion -- the risk that imagined content is mistaken for real experience, which is MECH-486's central hazard and the one the confabulation literature (Schnider 2013; Bouzerda-Wahlen 2015) addresses. So this entry supplies the *bounding discipline* (how much imagined content to trust and for how long) while the Schnider entries supply the *typing discipline* (keep it tagged not-real). Neither alone is sufficient; MECH-486 needs both. Because the transfer here is structural rather than a validated import of the specific guard, I set confidence at 0.6 with an elevated transfer-risk component, and pair it deliberately with the clinical entries rather than leaning on it alone.
