# Factor graphs unify directed and undirected models (Frey 2003) — Q-079

**Source:** Brendan J. Frey, "Extending Factor Graphs so as to Unify Directed and Undirected Graphical Models," UAI 2003 (arXiv:1212.2486).
**Direction:** weakens (the distinct-novel-object reading of Q-079).

## What the paper does

Frey shows that the factor graph — a bipartite graph of variable nodes and factor nodes — subsumes both Bayesian networks (directed, conditional-probability factors) and Markov random fields (undirected, compatibility-function factors) as special cases of one representation, and that a single sum-product / message-passing algorithm runs inference over the unified object. The contribution is representational: directed and undirected dependency structure are not rival formalisms requiring a higher synthesis; they are two parameterisations of the same factor graph.

## Limits and caveats

Frey's factor graph is **static**: structure is specified, not learned. It delivers nothing on **(c)** latent-node birth/death/merge/split, **(d)** granularity/scale shift, action coupling, or residue. So this entry weakens the narrow claim that (a)+(b) jointly is novel; it does not on its own collapse the full four-capacity DLIF combination. That collapse is earned only when (c) and (d) are also shown to live in one framework — which the active-inference entries in this review supply.
