# Scale-free active inference / Renormalising Generative Models (Friston et al 2024) — Q-079

**Source:** Karl Friston, Conor Heins, Tim Verbelen, Lancelot Da Costa, Tommaso Salvatori, Dimitrije Markovic, Alexander Tschantz, Magnus Koudahl, Christopher Buckley & Thomas Parr, "From pixels to planning: scale-free active inference," arXiv:2407.20292 (2024).
**Direction:** weakens (the distinct-novel-object reading of Q-079).

## What the paper does

The Renormalising Generative Model (RGM) is a single discrete state-space model equipped with a renormalisation-group (RG) operator. The operator takes a description of the system and returns a coarse-grained version that retains the properties of interest while discarding irrelevant detail; because each successive level is renormalisable, the model can generate — and infer the causes of — data at any scale. RGMs are described as discrete homologues of deep convolutional networks or continuous state-space models in generalised coordinates, they learn compositionality over space and time, and they are embedded in the active-inference loop (so they perceive, learn, and act). The paper demonstrates image classification, video and music compression/generation, and learning Atari-like games.

## Why it bears on Q-079 — the decisive combination entry

This is the entry that closes the four-capacity combination inside one object. The DLIF research map's provisional research claim is that "no single neighbouring formalism appears to fully capture the target object" and "the gap is the combination" of directed update, cyclic coherence, latent structure, scale transformation, action coupling, precision, and consolidation. The RGM is a counterexample to that claim for the load-bearing four:

- **(d) granularity/scale shift** — the RG operator *is* granularity zoom: coarse-grain to abstract, refine to detail, by construction. This is the capacity the research map flagged as served only by the information-bottleneck literature, now native to one generative model.
- **(c) latent structure** — the model learns its own compositional structure, complementing Smith et al's expansion + Bayesian model reduction.
- **(a) directed inference + action** and **(b) cyclic coherence** — inherited from the active-inference / factor-graph substrate (de Vries & Friston 2017; Frey 2003).

So a *single existing framework* — hierarchical active inference in its scale-free RGM form — already jointly delivers the directed-update + cyclic-coherence + latent-birth/death + scale-shift combination that Q-079 asks whether a new object is needed for. The named prime candidate in the falsifier ("hierarchical active inference") is confirmed to cover the combination. This is the core of the ANSWERED-NEGATIVE verdict and the direct vindication of the pilot's HA1 (re-notation risk) and HA6 (combination-of-known-pieces is a weaker novelty claim).

## The residue exception — and the HA4 warning

The RG operator works by *discarding* irrelevant detail at each coarse-graining. Like Bayesian model reduction, it is structurally anti-residue: nothing in it preserves an unresolved moral/epistemic trace against compression. So once again the one capacity not covered is residue/non-erasure — and the Q-079 falsifier requires that to be shown *separable* from salience + uncertainty (ARC-013's open V3-EXQ-587), not assumed. There is also a live mapping risk (pilot HA4): the RG operator models *perceptual* coarsening, but REE's scale axis runs up to *ethical-precedence* selection. Perceptual coarse-graining and ethical-constraint selection may not be one operator — so even the scale capacity transfers to REE with a caveat.
