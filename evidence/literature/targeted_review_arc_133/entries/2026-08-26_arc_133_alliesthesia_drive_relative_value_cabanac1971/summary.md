# Alliesthesia: the same stimulus, opposite value, by internal state (Cabanac 1971)

**Source.** Cabanac, M., *Physiological Role of Pleasure*, Science 173(4002):1103-1107, 1971. [DOI:10.1126/science.173.4002.1103](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.173.4002.1103), PMID 5098954.

**What the paper does.** Cabanac coins and demonstrates "alliesthesia": the finding that the hedonic sign of a sensation -- pleasant or unpleasant -- depends not on the fixed physical properties of the stimulus but on the subject's current internal physiological state. In the thermal case, the same tepid water applied to the skin is rated pleasant by a subject whose core temperature is elevated (the water helps cool them, correcting the error) and unpleasant by a subject whose core temperature is normal or low. In the gustatory case, a glucose solution is rated pleasant when blood glucose is low (hungry/hypoglycemic) and unpleasant or neutral once satiety is reached. The stimulus does not change; only the sign and magnitude of its motivational value does, tracking whether it currently serves to correct a homeostatic error.

**Why it matters for ARC-133.** ARC-133 proposes objects can be individuated by usefulness "relative to goals, harm, and interoceptive/metabolic state" -- and its own registration flags drive-relative re-individuation (the same scene carving differently when hungry, thirsty, or injured) as its sharpest divergence from MECH-278's causal-coherence criterion. For that claim to be non-vacuous, drive-relative value computation has to be a real thing a perceptual/representational system can do at all. Alliesthesia is the classic, foundational demonstration that it is: the SAME external stimulus is assigned opposite motivational sign purely as a function of internal state. It is also a close conceptual match to REE's own existing mechanism -- SD-057's `wanting[k] = base_value[k] * (1 + kappa * drive_axis[k])` in `ree_core/goal.py` is architecturally the identical move (a base term scaled by a drive-relative modulation), which the claim's registration itself points to as evidence the criterion is "already half-instantiated." Cabanac's paper is the physiological-level precedent for exactly that formula's shape.

**What it does not give ARC-133.** This is the paper's central limitation for this claim, and it needs to be stated plainly: alliesthesia is drive-relative VALUATION of a stimulus whose identity is already fixed and given to the subject -- nobody in these experiments is asked whether the tepid water is "one thing or two," only whether it feels pleasant. ARC-133's sharpest, most distinctive prediction is drive-relative INDIVIDUATION -- that the boundaries or count of objects in a scene change with drive state, not just their valence. Cabanac's finding is a necessary precondition for that claim (value has to be drive-relative for individuation-by-value to be coherent at all) but is not itself evidence for the individuation step. Anyone citing this entry as direct support for ARC-133's carving claim would be overreading it.

**Confidence.** 0.6. Source quality is solid for a foundational, extensively-replicated finding, though the report itself is from 1971 and lighter on methodological detail than a modern paper would be -- this is offset by decades of subsequent replication and extension (thermal and gustatory alliesthesia are now textbook phenomena, not a fragile one-off result). Mapping fidelity is capped at the value-vs-individuation distinction described above; transfer risk is moderate (human psychophysics to an artificial agent's representational architecture, but the underlying claim -- value as base x drive-modulation -- is architecture-agnostic).

**Lit-only verdict, not governance.** Supports ARC-133's precondition that motivational value is drive-relative, and gives a concrete physiological/formal precedent for REE's own SD-057 formula. Does not evidence the individuation step itself -- that remains the open, unaddressed part of ARC-133's sharpest test even after this pull. See the Connolly et al. (2016) entry in this same directory for the closest available (but likewise indirect) evidence on the individuation-independence side of the claim.
