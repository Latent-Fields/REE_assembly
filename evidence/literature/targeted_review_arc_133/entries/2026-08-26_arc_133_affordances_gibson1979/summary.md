# Affordance theory: strong general precedent, weaker on the radical reading (Gibson 1979)

**Source.** Gibson, J.J., *The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception*, Houghton Mifflin, 1979 (Chapter 8, "The Theory of Affordances").

**What the source says.** Gibson's ecological approach argues that a large part of perception is direct perception of affordances: what an environment offers, provides, or furnishes an organism for action, given the fit between the organism's body/capabilities and the environment's structure. A rigid, horizontal, knee-high surface affords sitting for an adult human; the same surface may not afford sitting for a much smaller or larger animal. The crucial architectural move is that affordances are, for Gibson, properties of the organism-environment SYSTEM -- not a "neutral" physical description of the environment to which usefulness is subsequently added by a separate cognitive step, and not a purely subjective construal either. Perception of "what this is for" is proposed as direct and primary, not derived from first perceiving causal/physical structure and then computing utility on top of it.

**Why it matters for ARC-133.** This is the primary theoretical anchor named at ARC-133's own registration, and it is the deepest available precedent for the claim's core architectural move: that usefulness/relevance-to-organism can be a PRIMARY category of perceptual organization, not one parasitic on a prior causal-coherence representation (MECH-278's criterion). Read this way, it supports ARC-133's CONSERVATIVE reading well -- a fourth, use-relative individuation facet, alongside type/anchor/token, that is directly perceived rather than computed post hoc.

**The tension worth naming plainly.** ARC-133's own registration flags drive-relative re-individuation -- the same scene carving differently when the organism is hungry, thirsty, or injured -- as its SHARPEST divergence from MECH-278. Gibson's affordances, as he defines them, are pitched at the level of relatively stable organism-environment fit: an organism's size, strength, and locomotor repertoire relative to environmental structure. They are explicitly treated as invariants of that relationship, directly perceivable whether or not they are currently perceived, rather than quantities that fluctuate moment-to-moment with internal drive state. A chair affords sitting whether the perceiver is hungry or full. This sits in real tension with -- rather than straightforwardly supporting -- the RADICAL, moment-to-moment drive-relative reading of ARC-133 that the claim's own registration identifies as its most distinctive prediction. This is not a fatal problem for ARC-133 (Gibson's theory does not directly rule out a drive-modulated affordance either, and later ecological-psychology work has explored state-dependent affordance perception more than the 1979 text itself does), but it is a genuine gap that a future governance pass on ARC-133's conservative/radical fork should not paper over.

**Confidence.** 0.55, direction marked mixed rather than supports. Source quality is high (foundational, canonical, still the reference point for affordance theory across cognitive science and robotics) but this is theory, not an empirical result, and offers no test that could falsify either reading of ARC-133. Mapping fidelity is capped by the stable-vs-fluctuating tension above; transfer risk is low (the theory is domain-general and was written with exactly this kind of organism-relative individuation question in mind).

**Lit-only verdict, not governance.** Strong general precedent for ARC-133's architectural legitimacy and for its conservative reading specifically; weaker, and arguably mildly discouraging, evidence for the radical drive-relative reading that the claim's registration treats as its sharpest test. This asymmetry -- support for the conservative fork, tension with the radical one -- is itself useful information for whichever governance pass eventually puts the fork to the user, and should be carried forward rather than flattened into a single "supports" verdict.
