# Threat as an independent organizing dimension (Connolly et al. 2016)

**Source.** Connolly, Sha, Guntupalli, Oosterhof, Halchenko, Nastase, Visconti di Oleggio Castello, Abdi, Jobst, Gobbini & Haxby, *How the Human Brain Represents Perceived Dangerousness or "Predacity" of Animals*, Journal of Neuroscience 36(19):5373-5384, 2016. [DOI:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3395-15.2016](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3395-15.2016).

**What the paper does.** Twelve participants viewed images from a deliberately factorial stimulus set -- 12 animals crossing taxonomic class (mammal, reptile/amphibian, insect) with threat level (high, low), so that within each taxonomic class there are both dangerous and harmless exemplars, and within each threat level there are taxonomically disjoint exemplars (a cobra and a scorpion are both "high threat" but belong to entirely different phyla and share little low-level visual structure). Using representational similarity analysis across ventral temporal and superior temporal sulcus (STS) cortex, the authors ask which dimension -- low-level visual similarity, taxonomic class, or threat -- best explains the geometry of neural response patterns at each cortical location, and how that changes moving posterior to anterior.

**Key finding.** Visual similarity dominates in early visual cortex, taxonomic class dominates in lateral occipital and ventral temporal cortex, but both dimensions "fall away progressively from posterior to anterior temporal cortices, leaving threat as the dominant explanatory variable" along a network centered on right STS. Threat organizes the representation independently of -- not merely by inheriting from -- causal or taxonomic similarity.

**Why it matters for ARC-133.** ARC-133's registration explicitly names "the animal-cognition predator-category literature that unifies causally-dissimilar things by their relation to the organism" as an anchor it has not yet verified. This paper is close to the sharpest available instantiation: a cobra (reptile, low visual similarity to mammals or arachnids) and a scorpion (arachnid) are causally and taxonomically about as dissimilar as two animals can be while both being "high threat," and the brain represents them as more similar along a dimension that is neurally dissociable from causal/taxonomic structure. That is direct evidence that relation-to-organism (here: predation risk) is a real, independent representational axis rather than a re-description of causal coherence -- exactly the minimal claim ARC-133 needs to be a live architectural rival to MECH-278's causal-coherence criterion, rather than a notational variant of it.

**What it does not give ARC-133.** Two gaps, both material to how this entry should be weighted in any future governance pass. First, the task is passive categorical viewing (same/different oddball detection), not active object individuation -- it shows the dimension exists in representational geometry, not that an agent uses it online to decide "one object or two." Second, and more important given the claim's own framing: the study never manipulates the observer's internal state. ARC-133's registration flags drive-relative re-individuation -- the same scene carving differently when hungry, thirsty, or injured -- as its sharpest divergence from MECH-278, and this paper says nothing about that; threat level here is a fixed property of the stimulus set, not a function of the viewer's momentary interoceptive state. So this entry supports the general architectural legitimacy of a relation-to-organism individuation axis, but the drive-relative claim specifically remains untested by it.

**Confidence.** 0.68. Source quality is good (peer-reviewed, well-controlled factorial design, though modest n=12 as is standard for fMRI representational analyses). Mapping fidelity is moderate: strong on "relevance is an independent organizing dimension," weak on "this dimension is drive-relative" and on "this generalizes from passive categorization to active individuation." Transfer risk is real but bounded -- human ventral/temporal representational geometry is one plausible mechanistic step removed from an artificial agent's object-individuation machinery, but the representational-independence logic itself is domain-general.

**Lit-only verdict, not governance.** This paper supports ARC-133's core architectural commitment (a non-causal, organism-relative dimension can independently organize object/category representation) without resolving the conservative-vs-radical fork the claim's own registration leaves open, and without touching the drive-relative test that the registration itself identifies as the sharpest discriminator against MECH-278. A future targeted pull specifically on interoceptive-state manipulation of active perceptual/categorical judgments (not just hedonic valuation, which entry `alliesthesia_drive_relative_value_cabanac1971` in this same directory partially covers) would close that remaining gap.
