# The teleological stance as a non-causal cognitive primitive (Gergely & Csibra 2003)

**Source.** Gergely, G. & Csibra, G., *Teleological reasoning in infancy: the naive theory of rational action*, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 7(7):287-292, 2003. [DOI:10.1016/S1364-6613(03)00128-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1364-6613(03)00128-1).

**What the paper does.** This is the authors' own synthesis of a body of violation-of-expectation habituation studies with pre-verbal (around one-year-old) infants. The core experimental logic: infants are habituated to a goal-directed action performed under some situational constraint (e.g. an agent jumping over an obstacle to reach a target), then shown either the same action-path in a new context where it is no longer the most efficient means, or a new, more efficient action-path to the same goal. Infants dishabituate (look longer, indicating surprise) at the former but not the latter -- they expect actions to be rational relative to a goal and the constraints of the situation. The authors argue this "teleological stance" is a distinct interpretive system: it requires representing action, goal-state, and situational constraint, and evaluating efficiency among them, but crucially does NOT require representing the actor's mental states (beliefs, desires) or the causal-mechanical process by which the action produces its effect. Teleological interpretation is proposed as developmentally prior to, and a precursor of, the later mentalistic "intentional stance."

**Why it matters for ARC-133.** ARC-133's own registration names "functional/teleological categories in infant cognition (Gergely & Csibra)" as an anchor it has not yet verified, and this is the canonical citation. What it offers ARC-133 is not a direct test of object individuation but a structural precedent: it demonstrates that a function/goal-relative representational scheme can be a genuine cognitive primitive rather than a late-arriving gloss on an underlying causal representation. That is exactly the architectural move ARC-133 needs to make for objects -- "individuated by usefulness relative to goals/harm/state" as primary rather than derivative of causal-coherence tracking (MECH-278) -- and this paper shows REE's broader theoretical neighborhood already accepts that move in an adjacent domain (interpreting other agents' actions).

**What it does not give ARC-133.** Two honest limits. First, the domain is agency interpretation (third-person goal attribution to others' actions), not object or entity individuation -- the transfer to ARC-133 is analogical, not a direct empirical test of "is this one object or two." Second, and more pointed given how ARC-133's registration frames its sharpest test: the teleological stance as described concerns the relation between an action, a goal-state, and situational constraints -- it says nothing about the OBSERVER's own interoceptive or motivational state changing how a scene is carved. It does not bear on the drive-relative re-individuation prediction at all.

**Confidence.** 0.52. Source quality is high for a canonical, widely-replicated review, discounted slightly because it synthesizes primarily the authors' own experimental program (standard practice for a targeted theoretical anchor, not a defect, but worth naming). Mapping fidelity is the binding limitation -- this is precedent for the legitimacy of function-relative representation as a cognitive primitive, not a direct test of object individuation, and it is silent on drive-relativity specifically.

**Lit-only verdict, not governance.** Supports the general architectural legitimacy of ARC-133's move (functional/teleological representation as primary, not causal-coherence-derivative) by analogy from an adjacent, well-established domain. Does not adjudicate the conservative-vs-radical fork the claim's own registration leaves open, and does not touch the drive-relative test at all -- see the Cabanac (1971) and Connolly et al. (2016) entries in this same directory for what partial coverage exists on those two fronts respectively.
