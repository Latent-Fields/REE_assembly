# Poulet & Hedwig 2006 -- The cellular basis of a corollary discharge

This is the cellular capstone of Poulet & Hedwig's cricket corollary-discharge series (Nature 2002, J Neurosci 2003, J Neurophysiol 2003), identifying a single, multisegmental interneuron that provides both pre- and post-synaptic inhibition of the ascending auditory pathway, timed to the animal's own stridulation (song) motor command. Crickets sing very loudly relative to the environmental sounds they need to keep hearing -- rival males, approaching predators, a responding mate -- so the corollary discharge circuit's job is to protect the auditory pathway from self-generated saturation while NOT simply deafening the animal during song. The paper demonstrates this at single-cell resolution: one identified interneuron, activity locked to the motor command, delivering graded inhibition to the auditory afferents.

This entry earns its place in this pull because it is the sharpest available precedent for a mechanism GFLAG-0432's corrected framing calls for and MECH-098/SD-007 do not currently implement: gain/precision modulation as distinct from subtractive cancellation. REE's ReafferencePredictor subtracts a predicted self-caused delta from z_world -- a point-estimate correction. The cricket circuit does something different in kind: it modulates the GAIN of the downstream pathway, scaled to and timed by the motor command, rather than subtracting a predicted waveform from the auditory signal. The functional target is the same (preserve sensitivity to externally-caused input against a self-generated background), but the implementation strategy is the other branch of Crapse & Sommer's taxonomy (this directory's framework entry): gating/gain modulation rather than subtraction.

Read against REE's actual regime, this paper is instructive by CONTRAST rather than direct analogy. The cricket's problem is a large self-generated signal threatening to swamp a small external one; REE's measured problem (V3-EXQ-1075a: action-driven z_world displacement ~0.013-0.016 RMS/dim) is closer to the opposite -- a SMALL self-generated (action-conditioned) signal that needs to remain legible against the encoder's other input drive, not a large one that needs suppressing. But the underlying computational move generalizes across both directions: what keeps a signal discriminable against a differently-scaled background is not primarily subtraction accuracy, it is GAIN CALIBRATED to the actual scale of the signal that needs to be read. A REE mechanism that gave the world-forward comparator a precision/gain term set by the predicted action-consequence magnitude -- larger gain when the predicted consequence is small, so that a genuine small action effect is not lost in the encoder's ambient noise floor -- would be the REE-appropriate mirror image of what this circuit does for the cricket's loud self-signal.

Caveat: this is a single-neuron gating mechanism in an insect auditory pathway with a well-defined, temporally precise, high-amplitude self-generated reference signal (the cricket's own song). REE's z_world has no equivalent single-channel "auditory nerve" analogue, and the mapping here is at the level of computational strategy (gain modulation vs. subtraction) rather than circuit-for-circuit correspondence. The PubMed abstract available via efetch did not surface the interneuron's specific name or exact synaptic timing figures (the full paper and its 2002/2003 companion papers would carry those); the entry is graded accordingly on the well-replicated phenomenon rather than on independently re-verified numeric parameters.

Confidence 0.76: high source quality (Science, single-cell intracellular resolution, part of an established multi-paper series), moderate mapping fidelity given the substrate distance, elevated transfer risk because this insect system sits at the opposite end of the small/large self-signal ratio from REE's own measured regime.
