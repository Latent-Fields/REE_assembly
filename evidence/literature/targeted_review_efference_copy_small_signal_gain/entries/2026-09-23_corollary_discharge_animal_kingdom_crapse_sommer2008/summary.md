# Crapse & Sommer 2008 -- Corollary discharge across the animal kingdom

This is the field's standard synthesis review of corollary discharge (CD): the general finding, replicated from insects to primates, that the nervous system routes a copy of the outgoing motor command to sensory-processing areas *before* the movement's sensory consequences arrive, so that the predicted, self-caused portion of the incoming sensory stream can be discounted. The review's organizing question is exactly the one CDQ-012 (as corrected by GFLAG-0432) is asking: not "does a copy of the action reach the sensory system" -- that is answered in every system the review surveys -- but "what does the nervous system do with that copy once it has it," and in particular how the copy is transformed from a motor-coordinate command into a sensory-coordinate prediction that can be combined, quantitatively, with the actual sensory input.

The paper is useful here less for new data (it reports none) than for making explicit a distinction REE's current SD-007/MECH-098 pairing collapses: CD is not a single mechanism but a family, and subtractive cancellation (which is what SD-007's `ReafferencePredictor` implements -- predict the self-caused delta, subtract it) is only one of several strategies the review catalogues. The others -- gain modulation of the sensory response as a function of the predicted movement, and outright gating of specific sensory channels during specific movement phases -- are the mechanisms that the remaining three entries in this directory (Enikolopov 2018, Keller 2012, Poulet & Hedwig 2006) instantiate concretely. Read together, the four entries in this directory sketch the answer to GFLAG-0432's actual question: subtraction alone is not what keeps a small action-driven signal discriminable against a large ongoing sensory background; subtraction has to be paired with a scaling/gain step precisely because the residual after an imperfect subtraction is what the downstream comparator has to work with, and that residual's discriminability depends on how well it is scaled relative to noise -- not merely on whether it was subtracted at all.

Mapping to REE: MECH-098's `z_world` reafference subtraction is architecturally the review's canonical strategy #1 (subtraction). MECH-095's TPJ-analogue comparator is the review's canonical downstream consumer -- the structure that decides, from whatever signal survives the CD-based correction, whether an event was self-caused or externally caused. Nothing in REE's current substrate implements the review's strategy #2 (gain modulation): the ReafferencePredictor subtracts a single point estimate with no dependence on how large or small the predicted consequence is relative to the encoder's other input drive, so the small-per-step-displacement case (measured 0.013-0.016 RMS/dim, V3-EXQ-1075a) is exactly the regime where subtraction-only degrades to near-zero residual with no accompanying precision boost to keep that residual legible to the comparator.

Caveat: this is a review, so its evidentiary weight for governance purposes rests on the primary literature it and the other REE lit-pulls already cite (Sommer & Wurtz 2002 in `targeted_review_mech_098`, von Holst 1950 and Cullen 2019 in `targeted_review_connectome_mech_101`, Blakemore et al. in `targeted_review_sd_007`). Its contribution here is the explicit cross-species framing and the strategy taxonomy (subtraction / gain / gating), which none of the existing entries state as a taxonomy.

Confidence 0.78: high source quality (Nature Reviews Neuroscience, the standard citation for this topic), good mapping fidelity at the framing level, appropriately discounted because it supplies no new primary measurement.
