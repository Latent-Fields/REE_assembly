# Enikolopov, Abbott & Sawtell 2018 -- Internally generated predictions enhance detection of small sensory stimuli

This paper is the direct functional follow-up to Sawtell 2010 (already cited in `targeted_review_sd_047`), from the same lab and the same mormyrid electric fish electrosensory lobe circuit. Where the 2010 paper established that granule cells learn "negative images" -- corollary-discharge predictions of the sensory consequences of the fish's own electric organ discharge, shaped by anti-Hebbian plasticity -- this 2018 paper closes the functional loop: it asks whether subtracting that negative image from the actual sensory input *improves* the fish's ability to detect small, prey-like external stimuli that arrive superimposed on the much larger self-generated reafferent signal, and reports that it does, at both the single-neuron coding level and the behavioral level.

This is, as directly as the literature gets, the paper GFLAG-0432 asks this pull to find. CDQ-012's original framing asked whether the action-conditioned prediction is *large enough to read* -- but the corrected framing asks the more biologically apt question: does removing the predictable self-caused component *help the downstream comparator* discriminate the small external signal that remains? This paper's answer, in a system that has been measured rather than merely architected, is yes, and it locates the benefit specifically in the regime where the self-caused signal dominates the external one -- which is REE's actual regime (V3-EXQ-1075a measured the world-forward head's own action-driven displacement at ~0.013-0.016 RMS/dim per step, small relative to the encoder's broader input drive).

Mapping to REE: SD-007's `ReafferencePredictor` implements the subtraction half of this mechanism (predict, then subtract) but REE's evidence base for it (Blakemore et al., Sawtell 2010, already cited) documents only that the subtraction is accurate, not that it functionally pays off downstream. This paper supplies the missing claim -- that the payoff is measurable as an improvement in external-event detectability -- and, importantly, supplies a testable REE-side falsifier: if the ReafferencePredictor mechanism is doing its biological job, z_world's post-correction residual should discriminate external events better than the uncorrected z_world_raw, and the effect should be *most* pronounced, not least, when the self-caused component is large relative to the external one. That inverts the implicit assumption in CDQ-012's original completion criterion, which read a small action-conditioned prediction as evidence of a broken mechanism rather than as the very regime the subtraction mechanism exists to handle.

Caveat: PubMed's abstract for this entry (retrieved via NCBI efetch, per CLAUDE.md's PubMed routing rule) does not carry the quantitative detection-improvement figures -- no SNR gain, d-prime, or percent-correct numbers were recoverable from the abstract text alone, so this entry is graded on the design and mechanistic claim rather than on independently-verified magnitudes. A full-text pull (PMC, if open-access) would let the confidence be raised further. The implementation substrate is also a cerebellum-like circuit with a single, precisely-knowable reafferent ground truth (the fish's own EOD timing); REE's z_world is a learned high-dimensional latent with no equivalent ground-truth decomposition, so any REE-side test of the same claim needs its own operationalization (e.g. an external-event classifier's AUC on corrected vs. uncorrected z_world) rather than a literal transplant of the fish psychophysics paradigm.

Confidence 0.80: high source quality (Neuron, same rigorous electrophysiology tradition as the already-cited Sawtell 2010), good mapping fidelity at the functional-logic level, moderate transfer risk given the substrate difference and the missing quantitative detail from the abstract alone.
