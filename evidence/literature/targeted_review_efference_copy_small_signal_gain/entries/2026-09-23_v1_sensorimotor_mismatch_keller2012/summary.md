# Keller, Bonhoeffer & Hubener 2012 -- Sensorimotor mismatch signals in primary visual cortex of the behaving mouse

This paper established, in a now widely-replicated closed-loop virtual-reality paradigm, that mouse V1 layer 2/3 neurons are not simple visual filters -- they combine a locomotion-derived (motor/efference) signal with the actual visual flow and respond most strongly to a MISMATCH between the two. When the experimenters decouple visual gain from running speed (so the visual flow no longer matches what the animal's own movement predicts), the resulting prediction-error responses are larger than the response to either the motor signal or the visual signal alone. This is a comparator computation, built directly into the earliest stage of mammalian cortical visual processing, and it is causally demonstrated (the mismatch is experimentally induced on demand via closed-loop gain manipulation), not merely observed in passing correlational data.

This paper answers the second half of GFLAG-0432's question -- "how does the comparator use" the action-conditioned prediction -- more directly than anything else surveyed in this pull. MECH-095 proposes a TPJ-analogue comparator that distinguishes self-caused from other-caused change; this paper shows a concrete, causally-verified biological instance of exactly that computation, sited where the action-derived prediction and the raw sensory stream both already arrive rather than in some downstream integration hub. For MECH-101 ("z_world encoding requires reafference context to correctly attribute external vs self-caused change"), this paper is close to direct support: V1 responses in this preparation are demonstrably uninterpretable as "was that change self-caused or not" without the motor-derived expectation term -- which is MECH-101's claim almost verbatim, just in a different anatomical system than REE's architecture diagram implies.

The small-signal reframing this paper motivates is worth stating plainly, because it changes how CDQ-012's original completion criterion should be read. A mismatch signal is a DIFFERENCE between predicted and actual sensory consequence. Its informativeness therefore depends on how ACCURATE the prediction is relative to the TRUE scale of the sensory consequence -- not on whether the true consequence is large in absolute terms. A small true displacement with an accurate small prediction produces just as clean a mismatch signal (near zero, correctly) as a large displacement with an accurate large prediction; what destroys the comparator's usefulness is prediction INACCURACY at whatever scale the true signal sits, not smallness per se. This reframes the original CDQ-012 worry (the world-forward head's action-driven displacement is small, ~0.013-0.016 RMS/dim, V3-EXQ-1075a) away from "is the signal big enough to matter" and toward the better-posed question "is the prediction accurate enough, AT that small scale, for the comparator's difference computation to stay near the noise floor when nothing external happened, and to rise cleanly above it when something did."

Caveat: the locomotion-visual-flow relationship in this paradigm is close to linear and highly stereotyped (visual flow speed tracks running speed by a near-fixed multiplier), which is a much more benign prediction problem than REE's discrete, non-linear grid-world action-to-z_world mapping. The specific gain-modulation mathematics here would need substrate-appropriate re-derivation, not direct transplant, and the mechanism is a distributed cortical-population phenomenon rather than a single identified comparator neuron (contrast with the cricket entry in this same directory, where a single interneuron is identified).

Confidence 0.82: high source quality (Neuron, closed-loop causal manipulation, extensively replicated including by the same lab's later Alzheimer's-model work), good mapping fidelity to MECH-095/MECH-101's comparator function, moderate transfer risk from the linear-coupling paradigm to REE's discrete action space.
