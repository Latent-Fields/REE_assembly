# Moral Disengagement at Work: A Meta-Analysis (Ogunfowora, Nguyen, Steel & Hwang, 2022)

Every other entry in this directory studies individuals or dyads in a laboratory. MECH-012 is about
institutions. This meta-analysis is the only source I found operating at the right scale, and it
earns its place for that reason even though its evidential form -- self-report scales, largely
cross-sectional -- is weaker than the experimental work elsewhere in this folder.

Moral disengagement is Bandura's name for the cognitive tactics by which people sidestep the moral
self-regulatory processes that would otherwise prevent wrongdoing, and the tactics map onto MECH-012's
institutional repertoire almost item for item: euphemistic labelling and moral justification are
denial, displacement of responsibility is absorption, diffusion of responsibility is diffusion, and
dehumanization is the removal of the target's standing to generate residue at all. The meta-analysis
finds that this repertoire predicts workplace misconduct, turnover intentions, reduced citizenship
behaviour and reduced task performance; that abusive supervision and perceived organizational
politics are strong contextual enablers of it; and that it retains incremental validity over
narcissism, Machiavellianism, psychopathy and entitlement. That last point matters more than it
looks. It closes off the easiest alternative story about institutional harm -- that bad institutions
simply attract bad people -- and leaves MECH-012's structural account standing.

The finding I did not expect, and the one I think should change how REE writes this claim, concerns
where in the moral pipeline the damage occurs. The route from disengagement to misconduct was
mediated by lower moral awareness, lower moral judgment and lower *anticipated* guilt. But
disengagement was *positively* related to guilt and shame *after* the misconduct. The residue, in
other words, is not gone. What has been disabled is its prospective, gating use -- its participation
in the decision that would have prevented the act -- while the marker itself is still written, and
re-presents afterwards with nowhere to go and no institutional acknowledgement to meet it. Anyone who
has sat with a clinician or a soldier describing what their organisation asked of them will recognise
that shape immediately; it is moral injury, and it is not well described by saying the institution
absorbed the residue. The institution severed the residue's control pathway and left the residue.

For REE this is an architectural instruction rather than a nuance. If institutional displacement
operated by discharging residue, then residue write and residue read could share a path. The evidence
says they cannot: whatever MECH-012's substrate eventually looks like, the residue *write* and the
residue-to-policy *gate* have to be separable components, because the phenomenon to be modelled is
precisely one attacking the second while leaving the first intact. It also predicts an asymmetry
worth testing. Enablers here were strong and deterrents relatively weak -- ethical leadership and
organizational justice moved the needle less than abusive supervision and politics did. If that
asymmetry is real, then a REE mitigation built on adding virtuous institutional language will
underperform one built on removing the enabling conditions, and note that this reverses the symmetry
Bandura et al. found in the laboratory, where a humanizing label was as effective in the protective
direction as a dehumanizing one was in the harmful direction. Laboratory symmetry, organizational
asymmetry: that is a discrepancy worth an experiment rather than a shrug.

The limits are real and I want them on the record. These are mostly cross-sectional self-report
designs; causal direction between disengagement and misconduct is not established. Disengagement is
measured as a propensity on a questionnaire, not as an institutional utterance under experimental
control, so MECH-012's language clause is inherited from Bandura's taxonomy rather than tested here.
The outcome is misconduct as rated or reported, not embodied harm. And I could not obtain the full
text -- paywalled at both the publisher and the aggregator -- so I have deliberately quoted no effect
sizes, no number of samples and no total N; everything above comes from the abstract, which I read
verbatim through PubMed. Confidence 0.60, with mapping fidelity rather than source quality as the
binding constraint.
