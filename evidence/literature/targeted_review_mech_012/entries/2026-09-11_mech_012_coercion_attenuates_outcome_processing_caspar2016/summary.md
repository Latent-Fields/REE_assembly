# Coercion Changes the Sense of Agency in the Human Brain (Caspar et al., 2016)

The "Nuremberg defence" is usually treated as a lie told afterwards. Caspar and colleagues asked
whether it might sometimes be a report -- inaccurate, self-serving, but nonetheless a report of
something that really happened inside the person at the time. Their answer matters to MECH-012
because the claim rests on an implicit assumption: that when an institution says "you were only
following the protocol," it is doing something to the agent, not merely offering them a story. In
two experiments, volunteers pressed a key that delivered either a financial penalty or a genuinely
painful electric shock to a co-participant, in exchange for their own gain. The critical contrast
was between a coercive condition, in which an experimenter present in the room ordered the press,
and a free-choice condition delivering the same harm. Agency was measured implicitly, through the
perceived compression of the interval between action and outcome, and electrophysiologically,
through the neural response evoked by the outcome.

Both measures moved. Coercion lengthened the perceived action-outcome interval, the signature of a
reduced sense of agency, and it reduced the neural processing of the outcome of one's own action.
The methodological point is the one to hold onto: neither measure is one a participant can
strategically manage. Milgram's studies and most of what followed relied on explicit report, which
is exactly the channel a person motivated to disown a harm would distort. By going underneath that
channel, this study removes the easiest deflationary reading -- that obedience merely licenses a
convenient account -- and leaves a harder finding in its place.

The translation into REE is where this becomes interesting, and also where it sharpens the claim
rather than simply confirming it. MECH-012 says institutions may absorb, diffuse, or deny residue,
with language as the medium. One might read "absorb" as an accounting operation: the harm is
registered and then the ledger is re-attributed. What this study suggests instead is that the
symbolic act operates upstream of the ledger. The order attenuates the outcome signal itself, so
the residue that would have been written is smaller before any attribution question is asked. If
that is right, then a REE implementation that computes residue from the harm outcome along a path
insulated from agency attribution will systematically under-model institutional displacement -- it
will predict that an order changes only the narrative, when the evidence says the order changes the
measurement. Equally, an audit layer that verifies who caused what against a causal log will find
nothing amiss, because the causal facts were never in dispute; the participants knew perfectly well
they had pressed the key.

The caveats are substantial and I do not want to understate them. There is no institution here.
There is one experimenter, in the room, issuing an explicit order to one person -- a dyad, not a
collective, with no persistence, no membership, and no diffusion across members, which is the
substrate MECH-012 actually concerns. The attenuated quantity is an ERP amplitude to an outcome
tone; calling that residue is a substitution REE makes for its own convenience, not something this
paper licenses. And the claim's headline prediction -- that ethical failure arises when symbolic
language *fully decouples* from embodied harm signals -- is not tested, because coupling was
reduced here and never abolished. What we have is evidence that the dial exists and that language
turns it, not evidence about what happens at the end of its travel.

There is also a live interpretive dispute that governance should not read past. Haslam and Reicher's
engaged-followership account, entered separately in this directory, reads the same family of
obedience findings as identification with a collective goal rather than as loss of agency. The two
accounts are not straightforwardly reconcilable, and they imply different REE mechanisms: agency-loss
suggests residue is muted, identification suggests residue is re-signed. I have set confidence at
0.72 -- high enough to treat the displacement mechanism as real and architecturally consequential,
low enough to record that its interpretation is contested by a paper sitting in the same folder.
