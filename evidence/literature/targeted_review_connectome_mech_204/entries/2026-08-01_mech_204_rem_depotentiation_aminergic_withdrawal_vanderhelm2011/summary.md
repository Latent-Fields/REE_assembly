# van der Helm, Yao, Dutt, Rao, Saletin & Walker (2011) -- REM sleep depotentiates amygdala activity to previous emotional experiences

**Why this paper.** MECH-204's own biological-reference triage (in the failure autopsy that
commissioned this pull) flags it as an "is_formal_import: true" claim with only partial
biological grounding. This is the canonical human neuroimaging paper for the general architecture
MECH-204 asserts -- that REM sleep physiology, via suppression of an aminergic neuromodulator,
actively RESETS a waking-accumulated bias, rather than merely allowing it to passively decay -- and
is the best available first anchor for that architecture, even though (as detailed below) it does
not validate MECH-204's specific neurotransmitter or specific target variable.

**What the paper did.** Healthy adult participants viewed emotionally evocative images twice: once
at initial encoding, and again roughly 12 hours later, either after a full night of sleep or after
an equivalent period of daytime wakefulness (a within-subject, counterbalanced design). fMRI
measured amygdala reactivity to the repeated images at both time points, alongside polysomnography
to characterize sleep architecture including REM-specific EEG measures (notably suppression of
high-frequency, >30Hz activity linked to noradrenergic/adrenergic tone).

**Key finding.** Only the sleep condition -- not the equivalent-duration wake condition --
produced a measurable dissipation of amygdala reactivity to the previously-viewed emotional
images, along with altered amygdala-prefrontal connectivity and reduced next-day subjective
emotional intensity. This overnight change correlated with EEG markers of REM sleep physiology
specifically. The authors interpret this as evidence that REM sleep, through suppression of
central adrenergic neurotransmitters, actively depotentiates the neural and subjective response to
recent emotionally salient experience -- a targeted recalibration, not a generic effect of time
passing or of sleep in general.

**How this translates to REE.** MECH-204 (title: `serotonergic_rem_gate_zero_point`) claims that
serotonergic withdrawal during REM actively defines a precision-recalibration zero-point, by
removing a benefit gradient rather than merely permitting recalibration passively. This paper is
the closest available human precedent for that general SHAPE of claim: REM sleep, through
withdrawal of a specific aminergic neuromodulator, is shown to be the necessary condition (sleep
but not equivalent wakefulness) for an active reset of a waking-accumulated state. It licenses
MECH-204's basic causal architecture -- REM-specific aminergic withdrawal as an active corrective
process -- as a real biological phenomenon, at least for one neuromodulator-variable pairing.

**Limitations and honesty about the mapping -- read this before citing this paper as support for
MECH-204 specifically.** Two substitutions are made in applying this evidence to MECH-204, and
neither is validated by this paper. First, the neuromodulator: this paper implicates NORADRENERGIC
(adrenergic) suppression, not serotonergic withdrawal -- MECH-204 names serotonin specifically.
Second, the target variable: this paper measures amygdala reactivity to emotionally salient
images, not a metacognitive precision or confidence estimate. The paper establishes that REM's
active-recalibration architecture is real for the noradrenaline-amygdala pairing; it says nothing,
one way or the other, about whether the same architecture holds for a serotonin-precision pairing.
Treating this as direct evidence for MECH-204 would overstate what it shows -- it supports the
general causal SHAPE of the claim, not its specific biological content.

**Confidence reasoning.** Source quality (0.85) is high: Current Biology, a well-cited,
methodologically careful within-subject design with converging fMRI and EEG evidence. Mapping
fidelity (0.45) is low-moderate and is the binding constraint, given the two substitutions
described above. Transfer risk (0.55) is correspondingly elevated. Aggregate confidence 0.6,
filed as "supports" for the general REM-active-recalibration architecture MECH-204 asserts, with
the mapping caveat carrying the weight of what remains untested.
