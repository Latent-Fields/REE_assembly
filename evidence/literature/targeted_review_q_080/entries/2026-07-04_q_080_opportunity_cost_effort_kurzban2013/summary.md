# Kurzban, Duckworth, Kable & Myers 2013 — effort as opportunity cost, not depletion

According to PubMed. Source: Kurzban R, Duckworth A, Kable JW, Myers J. "An opportunity cost model of subjective effort and task performance." *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 36(6):661-79, 2013. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X12003196) · PMID 24304775.

## What the paper argues

This is the agenda-setting target article for the opportunity-cost theory of mental effort — and it is the source theory behind REE's own MECH-320/ARC-068 opportunity-cost-of-time machinery. The puzzle it takes on: why does sustained task performance *feel* aversive, and why does performance decay over time? The dominant answer had been a physical resource that depletes (ego-depletion, glucose, etc.). Kurzban et al. reject that. Their alternative: executive/computational mechanisms can serve only a limited number of simultaneous tasks, so deploying them on the current task carries an **opportunity cost** — the value of the next-best foregone use of those systems. The *phenomenology* of effort is the felt read-out of that cost/benefit computation, and it functions to motivate disengagement (reduced deployment) so the systems can be redirected. Much of the article is a systematic case *against* resource-depletion accounts: fatigue for a task can dissipate when you switch to a novel task, effort is sensitive to incentives, and so on — patterns that a genuine depleted resource should not show.

## Why it speaks to Q-080

Q-080 explicitly names MECH-320/ARC-068 opportunity-cost-of-time as the existing value-subtraction machinery it is weighing against a harm-coupling proposal. This paper *is* that machinery's provenance, so it is close to load-bearing for the "value-subtraction suffices" pole. It frames wasted or excess effort as **foregone value** — an opportunity cost — not as a harm, a residue, or an allostatic injury. If effort is opportunity cost, then REE's existing ARC-068/MECH-320 substrate already represents it, and there is no obvious gap for a harm channel to fill.

The sharper contribution is the second front. Q-080's harm-coupling proposal is built on a specific mechanistic story: *over-exertion → depletion → a harm-adjacent state*. Depletion is the load-bearing premise. Kurzban et al. spend the article arguing depletion is the *wrong* model of effort. So they don't merely offer a competing account — they attack the very premise harm-coupling would need. That is a double-weakening for the harm proposal: a complete non-harm account of effort, plus a direct critique of the depletion backbone.

The discriminating test falls straight out. If REE's over-exertion state behaves like *opportunity cost* — incentive-sensitive, relieved by a better alternative, dissipating on task-switch — then value-subtraction wins and harm-coupling is surplus. If instead it behaves like *damage* — persistent, incentive-insensitive, flagged with irreversibility the way REE's harm stream flags injury — then value-subtraction is incomplete and harm-coupling earns its place.

## The mapping and its limits

Two honest caveats keep this from being a strong weakens. First, a regime gap: Kurzban et al. are talking about **subjective mental effort** and executive time-sharing, whereas Q-080's harm-coupling is most naturally about **metabolic/physical over-exertion** and allostatic depletion. Their anti-depletion argument targets *mental* fatigue; it does not directly exclude genuine metabolic depletion from sustained physical exertion, which could still warrant harm-adjacent treatment. So this weakens harm-coupling for the cognitive-effort regime while leaving the physical-exertion regime genuinely open. Second, this is a BBS target article — deliberately provocative and extensively contested in its own issue, where several commentaries defend depletion or limited-resource views (one of which, Christie & Schrater's later energy-budget model, is the third entry in this review). The opportunity-cost account is influential but not settled.

## Confidence

0.66, `weakens` (relative to the harm-coupling proposal). High source quality — a landmark article and the origin of REE's opportunity-cost framing. Good mapping fidelity for the cognitive-effort regime and for the anti-depletion argument specifically. The discount is the mental-vs-metabolic regime gap and the fact that the depletion critique is itself contested.
