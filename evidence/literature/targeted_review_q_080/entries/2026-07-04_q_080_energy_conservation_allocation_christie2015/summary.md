# Christie & Schrater 2015 — energy conservation as optimal allocation of a *reversible* budget

According to PubMed. Source: Christie ST, Schrater P. "Cognitive cost as dynamic allocation of energetic resources." *Frontiers in Neuroscience* 9:289, 2015. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2015.00289) · PMID 26379482.

## What the paper argues

Of the three papers in this review, this is the one that sits closest to Q-080's actual proposal — and it is useful precisely because it splits the proposal's two halves apart. Christie & Schrater model cognitive cost as the brain **purposively conserving activity** in order to plan the longer-term allocation of a limited metabolic budget. A control-theoretic (optimal-control) model with an explicit energy budget reproduces effort avoidance as the *optimal* allocation of limited energetic reserves, and also captures reward-responsiveness and the cognitive decrements of hypoglycemia. The proposed biological substrate for the "limited reserve" is **astrocytic glycogen**: a buffer that can transiently support neural activity beyond the rate blood glucose alone sustains, whose published depletion-and-repletion dynamics line up with a broad range of cognitive-cost phenomena. The authors pitch the model as *subsuming* both the cost/benefit (opportunity-cost) view and the limited-resource view — a reconciliation of the two camps that the Kurzban target article had set against each other.

## Why it speaks to Q-080

Q-080's harm-coupling horn actually bundles two distinct requests: (A) that **energy conservation be weighted as an evolutionarily-primary drive** — a least-effort prior — and (B) that **over-exertion couple to REE's harm/residue/allostatic stream** with harm's irreversibility-aware special status. Christie & Schrater let us score these separately, which is why this entry is `mixed`.

On (A), the paper is *supporting*. Energy conservation is not a derived afterthought in their model; it is a first-class control objective. The brain is modelled as actively budgeting and conserving metabolic output over time. That is exactly a least-effort prior treated as primary — which is the part of Q-080's proposal I think the literature genuinely endorses.

On (B), the paper *weakens* the case. It achieves energy-primacy with a **reversible** buffer: glycogen depletes *and* repletes with rest. There is no damage, no residue, no irreversibility — and correspondingly no reach for a harm channel. The natural REE reading, then, is: adopt the least-effort prior and treat energy conservation as primary, but represent the resulting depletion as **reversible allostatic load** — which REE already carries in pACC — rather than as harm. You get the energy-primacy Q-080 wants without the harm-coupling it also asks for. The harm stream, with its irreversibility flag, is the wrong tool for a resource that refills overnight.

## The mapping and its limits

Two limits keep this honest. First, the glycogen-as-buffer mechanism is something the authors themselves flag as *provisional* in its biological basis, and the model is control-theoretic without direct experimental validation of that specific buffer — so the mechanistic story is a proposal, not an established fact. Second, and more important for governance: the model lives in the *reversible-buffer* regime. It says nothing about genuinely **excessive, damaging** over-exertion — the rhabdomyolysis-scale, actually-irreversible extreme — and that pathological tail is exactly where harm-coupling could still be the right representation. So this entry discriminates the *ordinary* energy-conservation regime (reversible → not harm, use allostatic load) while staying silent on the pathological extreme. It narrows Q-080 rather than closing it.

## Confidence

0.6, `mixed`. Source quality is good — a well-cited synthesis that explicitly unifies the opportunity-cost and limited-resource accounts, which is the terrain Q-080 occupies. Mapping fidelity is moderate-good because it is the closest paper to Q-080's own framing and cleanly separates energy-primacy (supported) from harm/irreversibility (not needed). The discount is transfer risk: the glycogen mechanism is provisional and unvalidated for the specific buffer, and the model is silent on the pathological over-exertion tail where harm-coupling could still hold.
