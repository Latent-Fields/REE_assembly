# Shenhav, Botvinick & Cohen 2013 — effort as a subtracted cost, not a harm

According to PubMed. Source: Shenhav A, Botvinick MM, Cohen JD. "The expected value of control: an integrative theory of anterior cingulate cortex function." *Neuron* 79(2):217-40, 2013. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.07.007) · PMID 23889930.

## What the paper argues

EVC is the field-defining normative theory of dorsal anterior cingulate function, and it is also the direct provenance of REE's own dACC `effort_cost` term. The claim is that dACC's bewildering diversity of reported roles — reward processing, conflict monitoring, performance monitoring, action selection — collapses to a single computation: the **Expected Value of Control**. dACC decides whether, where, and how much control to allocate by integrating three quantities: the expected payoff from a controlled process, the amount of control needed to obtain it, and the **cost of that control in terms of cognitive effort**. Effort enters the ledger as a *cost term subtracted from payoff*. Nothing more exotic is required; the whole account is value arithmetic.

## Why it speaks to Q-080

Q-080 poses a clean fork. Either effort/energy expenditure should **couple to REE's harm/residue/allostatic stream** — over-exertion → depletion → a harm-adjacent state, handled with harm's irreversibility-aware special status, with energy conservation weighted as an evolutionarily-primary least-effort prior — *or* the existing **value-subtraction** machinery (dACC EVC `effort_cost` plus MECH-320/ARC-068 opportunity-cost-of-time) and pACC allostatic load already suffice, and wasted effort is merely *foregone value*, not a harm.

Shenhav 2013 is the source theory sitting behind the value-subtraction pole, and it is entirely self-contained: effort is a cost, payoff minus cost drives allocation, and this reproduces the observed phenomenology of effort avoidance and effort-reward trade-offs without ever invoking a harm, a residue, or an allostatic-damage channel. Relative to Q-080's harm-coupling proposal, that is *weakening* evidence. The mainstream computational model of how the brain deals with effort does all of its explanatory work as value-subtraction. So the parsimony burden shifts onto the harm-coupling proposal: it must predict something value-subtraction cannot.

Where can it earn that keep? Precisely in the regime EVC is silent about. EVC treats effort cost as fully *reversible* and *linearly tradeable* — spend effort now, and once the trade is done the ledger is clean. REE's harm stream, by contrast, is irreversibility-aware: harm leaves a residue with special status. So the discriminating test Q-080 needs is whether *over-exertion leaves a persistent residue* that ordinary opportunity-cost cannot represent. If REE's effort behaviour is empirically fully reversible and tradeable, harm-coupling is surplus; if excess effort leaves a lasting depletion state that biases future valuation the way an injury would, value-subtraction is incomplete and harm-coupling is doing real work.

## The mapping and its limits

The honest boundary: EVC concerns *normal*, moment-to-moment control allocation. It says nothing about the over-exertion → depletion → harm-adjacent-state regime that is the actual content of Q-080's harm proposal. So this entry weakens the harm-coupling claim *for the ordinary regime* — it shows value-subtraction is sufficient where EVC applies — but it does not refute harm-coupling for the depletion regime, where EVC simply does not speak. It is also a theoretical integration, not a decisive experiment. This is why I did not tag it a strong weakens.

## Confidence

0.68, `weakens` (relative to the harm-coupling proposal). Source quality is very high — the canonical EVC theory and the literal origin of REE's dACC effort_cost term, so the mapping is unusually tight. The discount is the regime mismatch captured above: it establishes the sufficiency and parsimony of value-subtraction for normal effort allocation, not for the depletion edge case that is Q-080's real question.
