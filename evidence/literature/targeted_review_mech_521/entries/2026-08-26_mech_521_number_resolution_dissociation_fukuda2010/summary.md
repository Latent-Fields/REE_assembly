# Two orthogonal working-memory factors, but not MECH-521's specific pair (Fukuda et al. 2010)

**Source.** Fukuda, K., Vogel, E.K., Mayr, U. & Awh, E., *Quantity, not quality: the relationship between fluid intelligence and working memory capacity*, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 17(5):673-679, 2010. [DOI:10.3758/17.5.673](https://doi.org/10.3758/17.5.673), PMID 21037165, PMC3050565.

**What the paper does.** Seventy-nine undergraduates completed a battery of visual working memory tasks designed so that some were number-limited (how many items can be held) and others resolution-limited (how precisely each held item is represented), alongside a standard fluid-intelligence measure. An exploratory factor analysis asks whether performance across these tasks is best explained by a single underlying working-memory factor, or by two separable factors corresponding to number and resolution.

**Key finding.** The data support a two-factor model with fully orthogonal (statistically independent) number and resolution factors, and -- per the secondary reporting available for this pull -- the relationship to fluid intelligence is carried entirely by the number factor, not the resolution factor. This is a clean empirical demonstration that two working-memory-relevant quantities which could, in principle, have collapsed to a single underlying resource (a system that trades off between holding more items or holding each more precisely) instead behave as genuinely separable regulators with different external correlates.

**Why it matters for MECH-521.** MECH-521's central assertion is that capacity and occupancy are two distinct regulators rather than one -- against the possibility that "occupancy" could simply be a byproduct of a single underlying resource, which would make the two-regulator framing either unfalsifiable by definition or empirically false once actually measured. This paper is the clearest available demonstration, in an adjacent and well-established literature, that a working-memory system CAN maintain genuinely orthogonal capacity-like parameters rather than collapsing them under measurement. That is the general SHAPE of evidence MECH-521 needs.

**Where the mapping is imperfect.** The dissociation demonstrated here -- item count vs. per-item precision -- is not MECH-521's specific pair. MECH-521 is about an independently-set upper-bound CAPACITY versus a dynamically emergent, settling-derived OCCUPANCY (how many domains a scene is actually carved into on a given trial, which can sit anywhere at or below capacity). Fukuda et al.'s "number" and "resolution" factors are both properties of a FIXED number of maintained items, not a claim about what determines how many domains a scene gets carved into in the first place. So this entry supports the general architectural possibility of orthogonal working-memory regulators, without being a direct test of MECH-521's particular capacity/occupancy split.

**A verification gap worth flagging.** The specific correlation figures often quoted for this paper (a strong number-intelligence correlation, near-zero resolution-intelligence correlation) could not be independently confirmed against the primary full text in this pull -- both the PMC open-access copy and the original author-lab PDF returned unreadable or blocked content to automated fetch. Those figures are reported here as secondary-source-derived and should be spot-checked against the primary PDF before being cited with precision in any governance-facing document.

**Confidence.** 0.58. Source quality is good (well-cited, methodologically standard factor-analytic design from an established working-memory research group). Mapping fidelity is the binding limitation, given the mismatch between this paper's dissociated pair and MECH-521's specific pair; the unverified correlation figures further temper confidence slightly.

**Lit-only verdict, not governance.** Moderate, indirect support for the general plausibility of MECH-521's two-regulator structure, drawn from a different but structurally analogous dissociation in the same broad domain (visual working memory). Should be read alongside the Alvarez & Franconeri (2007) entry in this same directory, which targets a dissociation closer in spirit to capacity-vs-realized-count but is itself contested within its own literature -- together, the two entries triangulate on "dissociable working-memory regulators are a real, seriously-entertained phenomenon" without either one being a clean, uncontested direct test of MECH-521 as written.
