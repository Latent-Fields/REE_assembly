# Internal working model of self, attachment, and competence (Verschueren, Marcoen & Schoefs 1996)

**Claims grounded:** INV-082 (loveability as safe-base structural prerequisite), INV-043 (caregiver requirement for ethical development)
**Direction:** supports · **Confidence:** 0.66

## What the paper did

Two linked studies of Belgian kindergartners (~5 years old). Study 1 (n=95) related each child's *internal working model of the self* -- assessed via an adapted Puppet Interview, scored for affective quality (positiveness) and openness to admitting imperfections -- to teacher-rated competence, social acceptance, behavioural adjustment, and observed self-esteem. Study 2 (n=50) tested Bowlby's specific assumption that the working model of self is intertwined with the working model of attachment to the mother, using an Attachment Story Completion Task alongside the Puppet Interview. The headline results: the positiveness of the self-model related positively to competence, social acceptance, school adjustment and self-esteem; and the security of the child-mother attachment representation was *positively and strongly* connected to the positiveness of the self.

## Why it matters for the claim

INV-082 is the load-bearing new V5 claim this lit-pull exists to ground: loveability is not self-valence decoration but a *structural prerequisite* -- correction, social learning and post-error repair can be integrated without self-erasure only if care has been internalised as personally-applicable and stable. The first half of that claim -- "care internalised AS self-valence" -- is exactly what this paper operationalises in human development. A secure attachment representation (the trace of received caregiving) co-varies strongly with a positive working model of the self, and that positive self-model is what underwrites the child's subsequent competent, socially-engaged functioning. That is the developmental-psychology shape of the safe base: the loveability term (positive self-model) sits upstream of, and supports, capable social behaviour. It maps directly onto LOVE-2 and the DEV-NEED-017 loveability readouts, and onto the proposed coupling of received care into a scorable self-valence (self_model_v4:SELF-3).

It also extends INV-043. The existing INV-043 entries in this directory establish the caregiver *requirement* (early-deprivation neuroscience, synchronous caregiving, care ethics). This paper adds the missing intermediate link: the mechanism by which received care becomes ethically-relevant is via a positive working model of the *self*, not only a model of the other. That is the bridge from "caregiving matters" to "caregiving matters because it builds a loveable self."

## Limitations and caveats

The design is correlational and single-aged. It shows that a secure attachment representation and a positive self-model go together at age five; it cannot show that received caregiving *causes* the positive self-valence, nor rule out a third factor (temperament, for instance) driving both. More importantly for INV-082, the study says nothing about *stability under correction* -- the part of the claim that actually does the work. INV-082's safe base is one that survives being corrected without collapsing into "I am unloveable"; a cross-sectional snapshot of 5-year-olds' self-affect cannot speak to that. And the construct gap is real: a child's puppet-interview self-representation is not REE's z_self valence channel, and the inference from "positive self-model" to "a self-valence term that biases E3 viability scoring" is an architectural bet this paper does not test.

## Confidence reasoning

I put this at 0.66 -- a solid but not strong support. The source is a high-quality developmental journal and the test is a direct one of the Bowlby self-model/attachment link, which is precisely the conceptual core of INV-082. What holds it back from the 0.8 band is the small correlational samples, the single developmental window, and the moderate transfer risk from a human self-representation construct to a computational self-valence term. It grounds the *existence and origin* of internalised loveability well; it does not ground the *bound* or the *correction-survivability* that the rest of this lit-pull (Salles, Tangney, Tronick) has to carry.
