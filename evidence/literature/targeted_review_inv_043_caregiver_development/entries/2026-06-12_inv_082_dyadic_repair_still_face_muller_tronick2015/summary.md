# Dyadic reparation and infant stress regulation — the still-face repair anchor (Müller, Zietlow, Tronick & Reck 2015)

**Claims grounded:** INV-082 (loveability as safe-base structural prerequisite), INV-043 (caregiver requirement)
**Direction:** supports · **Confidence:** 0.63

## What the paper did

Forty-six mother-infant dyads (19 mothers with an anxiety disorder, 27 controls) were observed at 3–4 months postpartum in the Face-to-Face Still-Face paradigm — the canonical Tronick procedure in which normal interaction is deliberately interrupted (the mother goes "still-faced") and then resumed. The study coded the *latency to interactive repair* of dyadic mismatches and related it to infant salivary cortisol reactivity. The key finding: interactive repair during the reunion episode was associated with infant cortisol reactivity (p < 0.01), whereas maternal anxiety-disorder status was *not*. The authors frame this as the first demonstration that interactive repair relates to infant psychobiological stress reactivity, and draw the pointed conclusion that "infants respond to what they experience and not to the maternal diagnostic category."

## Why it matters for the claim

The whole loveability-and-ethical-agency arc rests on a rupture-and-repair model of relationships: ruptures (mismatches, corrections, errors) are normal and recurrent, and what makes them survivable — what makes a *safe base* — is a reliable repair loop after the rupture, not the absence of rupture. INV-082 asserts that correction and post-error repair can be integrated without self-erasure when care is internalised; LOVE-5 (love-mediated repair) names repair as relationship-restoration rather than punishment-avoidance. This paper is the empirical anchor for the load-bearing premise that *repair is the regulating event*. The title says it directly — "what dyadic reparation is meant to do" — and the answer is: it down-regulates the infant's stress. That grounds the constructive counterpart to the failure modes the plan catalogues (repair-as-appeasement, repair-as-self-erasure, repair-absent): functional interactive repair is what a healthy dyad does, and it has a measurable psychobiological payoff. The "infants respond to what they experience" result also echoes a standing project discipline — the agent must learn from the actual correction/repair dynamics it undergoes, not from a label attached to its caregiver.

It extends INV-043 as well: the existing entries establish that caregiving matters and that its absence damages the developing brain; this one specifies a concrete caregiving *behaviour* — timely interactive repair — and ties it to a regulatory outcome, which is the behavioural granularity the caregiver-requirement claim otherwise lacks.

## Limitations and caveats

This is the weakest of the four entries on transfer, and I have priced that in. The sample is small (46 dyads) with 16 imputed cortisol values, and the design is correlational, so neither the robustness of the effect nor the causal direction is secure. More fundamentally, the dependent variable is infant HPA-axis reactivity — physiological stress regulation — which is several steps removed from the rule/harm-model update and self-valence stability that LOVE-4/LOVE-5 actually instrument. The paper grounds the *general* rupture-repair frame strongly; it grounds the *specific* "correction updates rule/harm models without self-valence collapse" claim only by extension. It is the frame-setting anchor (repair is real, repair regulates), not a mechanism map.

## Confidence reasoning

0.63 — supportive but the lowest-confidence of the set, held there by sample size, imputation, correlational design, and the substantial infant-physiology-to-computational-substrate transfer gap. Its value is precisely that it is the *empirical* still-face/repair citation the LOVE-7 node names (L2), complementing Tangney's taxonomy of *what* correction must avoid (shame-collapse) with evidence that *repair* is the event that does the regulating.
