# Moral emotions and moral behavior — the shame/guilt distinction (Tangney, Stuewig & Mashek 2007)

**Claims grounded:** INV-082 (loveability as safe-base structural prerequisite)
**Direction:** supports · **Confidence:** 0.70

## What the paper did

An authoritative Annual Review of Psychology synthesis of the self-conscious moral emotions — principally shame and guilt, with embarrassment, and the positive moral emotions (elevation, gratitude, pride) and other-oriented empathy. Its central, well-replicated claim is a distinction in the *object* of negative self-evaluation. Guilt is about a specific **behaviour** — "I did a bad thing" — and tends to motivate reparative, approach-oriented, constructive responses (confession, apology, undoing the harm). Shame is about the global **self** — "I am a bad thing" — and tends to motivate withdrawal, hiding, defensiveness, externalisation of blame, and appeasement. The review also covers the link between childhood abuse and later proneness to shame, psychobiological aspects of shame, and styles of coping with it.

## Why it matters for the claim

INV-082's safe-base function is operationally *defined* by the discrimination this paper establishes. The claim is that an agent with internalised loveability can take a correction and update at the behaviour/harm/rule level — "I caused harm, I made an error, here is the repair" — without that update generalising into a global verdict on the self: "I am unloveable, I must self-erase, I must appease." That is precisely guilt-locus versus shame-locus. The LOVE-4 (correction-without-annihilation) and LOVE-5 (love-mediated repair) failure-mode catalogues in the plan — repair-as-appeasement, repair-as-self-erasure — are the shame pathway by another name, and DEV-NEED-018's `punishment_avoidance_vs_repair_discriminability` readout is asking the experiment to separate exactly these two outcomes. Tangney et al. supply the empirical taxonomy those design targets are drawn from. The mechanism INV-082 proposes — that internalised loveability is what biases correction toward the reparable behaviour-locus rather than the collapse-prone self-locus — is the computational restatement of why secure, loved children tend toward guilt-reparation while shamed/abused children tend toward shame-withdrawal.

## Limitations and caveats

REE instantiates no shame or guilt; there is no affective phenomenology here to transfer. What transfers is structural: the *locus* of negative evaluation (behaviour vs global self) and its differential behavioural consequences, as a design discriminator the substrate must be able to produce and the governance scorecard must be able to read. Two honest complications. First, the adaptive/maladaptive split is not absolute — the review notes guilt can itself become maladaptive when it fuses with shame or generalises into chronic self-blame, so "route correction to a guilt-like channel" is necessary framing but not a sufficient mechanism; the behaviour-vs-self locus is the real discriminator. Second, and more interesting for the project: childhood-abuse history predicts shame-proneness, which means the safe base is *itself a developmental product*, not a fixed parameter. That is consistent with INV-082's developmental framing but it complicates any temptation to treat loveability as a settable substrate constant — the very conditions LOVE-1/LOVE-2 are meant to model are what determine which pathway later correction takes.
