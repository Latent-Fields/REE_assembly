# The case for incentive salience (Berridge, 2006)

*According to PubMed.* Berridge, *Psychopharmacology* 191(3):391-431. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-006-0578-x)

**What the paper did.** This is the canonical review setting out the incentive-salience ("wanting") account of mesolimbic dopamine, against the two rivals — hedonic "liking" and associative learning. Berridge marshals the dissociation evidence: dopamine is neither necessary nor sufficient for hedonic impact, and is not required for new learning, but *does* contribute causally to attributing incentive salience to reward-related stimuli. The key sentence for us is that dopamine activation can be *sufficient to enhance cue-triggered incentive salience*.

**Key findings relevant to the claim.** Three points carry the mapping. First, "wanting" is a distinct process, separable from both liking and learning. Second, it is *cue-triggered* — a Pavlovian stimulus acquires the power to pull behaviour toward its associated reward. Third, the attribution can be amplified at the moment of cue encounter without re-learning the reward's value; in fact the wanting can be dynamically enhanced even as liking and learned prediction stay fixed.

**How this maps to REE.** MECH-347 implements `GoalState.cue_pull(z_object, strength)` as a directional `z_goal` nudge with explicitly *no benefit gate* and *no revaluation*, plus `agent.cue_recall_wanting(cue_type, drive_level, simulation_mode)`. That is, almost line for line, an instantiation of cue-triggered incentive salience: a cue makes its object more "wanted" and pulls the goal toward it without re-deriving the object's value. Berridge's central claim — that wanting is dissociable from value-learning and can be cue-evoked — is the biological licence for an ungated, revaluation-free pull. The dependence on `drive_level` in the REE primitive matches Berridge's insistence that incentive salience integrates Pavlovian information *with physiological state signals*.

**Limitations and caveats.** Two cautions. (1) The review's "wanting" is dynamically modulated by dopamine/physiological state at the moment of cue re-encounter; REE's `cue_pull` uses a fixed `cue_recall_gain` (0.05) and derives strength from SD-049 proximity rather than a dopamine-analog state signal. So the *no-gate* premise is faithful, but the *magnitude control* is a simplification. (2) This is a review, and the strongest evidence is rodent; the claim should lean more on the empirical sibling entries (Smith 2011; DiFeliceantonio 2016) for mechanistic weight.

**Confidence reasoning.** 0.74, supports. High conceptual fidelity for the no-revaluation premise, balanced against review-level status and the missing state-modulated gain. This is the framing source; the two empirical entries do the load-bearing mechanistic work.
