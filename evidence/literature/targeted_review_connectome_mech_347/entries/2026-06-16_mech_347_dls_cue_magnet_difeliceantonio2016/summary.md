# Striatal stimulation makes one reward cue a stronger motivational magnet (DiFeliceantonio & Berridge, 2016)

*According to PubMed.* DiFeliceantonio & Berridge, *European Journal of Neuroscience* 43(9):1203-1218. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/ejn.13220)

**What the paper did.** Rats were trained in an autoshaping paradigm that separates sign-trackers (animals drawn to the reward-predictive lever cue) from goal-trackers (animals drawn to the reward dish). The authors then microinjected a mu-opioid agonist (DAMGO) or a dopamine indirect agonist (amphetamine) into the dorsolateral striatum and watched what happened to cue attraction, including when the lever was suddenly moved and when a new instrumental response was required to earn the cue.

**Key findings relevant to the claim.** Limbic activation in DLS *dynamically amplified* attraction toward a previously-learned Pavlovian cue, in an individualised way — sign-trackers' attraction to the lever cue grew, goal-trackers' attraction to the dish cue grew — and the amplification was *selective and competitive*: one cue became a stronger "motivational magnet" at the expense of a competing cue. Two control results show this was motivation, not habit: sign-trackers flexibly followed the cue to a *new* location after it was moved, and they worked harder on a *new* nose-poke to earn the cue.

**How this maps to REE.** MECH-347's `cue_pull` is a directional pull of `z_goal` toward the strongest-perceived cued object — and in StepHarness the strongest-perceived type is derived from SD-049 per-type proximity field views. DiFeliceantonio & Berridge give that a behavioural face: incentive salience makes the dominant cue a magnet that pulls action toward it, *competitively* over other cues, and the "followed-to-a-new-location" result shows the pull is toward the cued object's identity rather than a cached motor habit. That flexibility is exactly what a goal-latent nudge (rather than a hardwired action) is supposed to produce.

**Limitations and caveats.** The boundaries are real. (1) The effect was strongly *individual-difference dependent* (sign- versus goal-trackers) and required limbic drug activation; a uniform fixed-gain `cue_pull` does not model that heterogeneity or its state-dependence. (2) The amplification was *competitive* — one cue at the expense of another — which implies `cue_pull` ought to arbitrate across competing cues, not independently nudge toward each; REE's "strongest-perceived type" selection captures this only partially. (3) Rat DLS microinjection to a latent nudge is a substantial transfer.

**Confidence reasoning.** 0.72, supports. Behaviourally this is the most direct cue-pull analog in the batch — a cue literally pulling flexible approach toward an object — but I hold transfer risk higher (0.40) because of the pharmacological induction and the phenotype-dependence, neither of which REE represents.
