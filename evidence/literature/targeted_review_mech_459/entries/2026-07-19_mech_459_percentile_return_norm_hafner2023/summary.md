# DreamerV3 (Hafner et al. 2023) — the source of MECH-459's prescription, read back critically

MECH-459 names this paper directly, so the first job of this entry is not to ask whether the paper agrees with the claim — it will, by construction — but to check that what the claim attributes to it is actually there, and then to be honest about how much that agreement is worth.

It is there. All three prescriptions check out against the text. On return normalisation, the paper divides by `max(1, Per(R,95) - Per(R,5))` and states the rationale plainly: scaling returns down without scaling up small returns accelerates exploration under sparse rewards without sacrificing final performance under dense ones. The `max(1, ·)` is not incidental — it is the whole point, and it is precisely the floor that REE's `(adv - mean)/(std + 1e-8)` lacks. On symlog, the paper rejects running-statistics normalisation explicitly because it "introduces non-stationarity into the optimization" — the target moves when the normaliser moves. REE stacks a Welford *running* reward std, so this criticism lands on the substrate as well. On the critic, the paper adopts discrete twohot regression over scalar regression, motivated by bimodal reward and return distributions under sparse reward. That is exactly the regime a zero-forage REE episode occupies.

The translation to REE is where care is needed. DreamerV3 normalises *returns* feeding an imagination-rollout critic inside a learned world model; MECH-459 targets a per-episode *advantage* standardiser in a model-free raw-view actor-critic. The structural argument — an unfloored two-sided divisor destroys magnitude information and re-inflates a near-zero advantage vector to unit variance — is scale-free and transfers cleanly. The empirical result does not: DreamerV3's evidence is aggregate performance across 150+ tasks under one hyperparameter set, and at no point does it ablate the normaliser as the single cause of a competence floor. So it tells us the corrective is sound engineering practice; it does not tell us how much of REE's ~0–1 foraging floor the normaliser owns.

The sharper caution is circularity. MECH-459 was written *from* this paper. That the paper agrees is therefore not corroboration — it is the claim's own premise read back. I have set confidence at 0.78 rather than higher for exactly this reason, and mapping_fidelity (0.68) is deliberately the binding term. The claim's own `what_would_answer` gets this right: the adjudicating evidence is the shape-preserving knock-out at `mech457_fanout.py::train_rawview_ac_rl`, not the citation. What this entry legitimately establishes is narrower but still load-bearing — that the mechanism MECH-459 proposes is a *recognised, named, independently-motivated* failure mode in the RL literature rather than a post-hoc rationalisation invented to explain four inconvenient null results.

That narrower point matters more than it might look, because it is what licenses the claim's most consequential corollary: if the two-sided normaliser really does divide magnitude out by construction, then the eliminations of the capacity, drive-schedule, reward-coupling and credit-horizon axes (V3-EXQ-769/770/771/772) are conditional on the instrument, not facts about the mechanisms. That is a claim about the measuring apparatus, and an apparatus argument is exactly the kind that a well-documented prior failure mode can support and a knock-out must settle.
