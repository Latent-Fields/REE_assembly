# Wang, Zhang & Gao (2025) — independent indictment of the exact operator

Of the three papers in this pull, this is the one that carries real evidential weight for MECH-459, and the reason is simple: it was not the paper the claim was written from, and it targets the operator the claim actually indicts.

DreamerV3 normalises *returns*, one level up from REE's pathology and inside a learned world model. Wang et al. go straight at per-batch advantage standardisation in model-free policy gradient — the same `(adv - mean)/std` shape as `mech457_fanout.py::train_rawview_ac_rl` — and report that it is harmful despite being near-universal in advantage actor-critic implementations. They name two mechanisms. The first is that dividing by a small standard deviation produces disproportionately large gradient updates, and that this bites hardest early in training when advantages are small. That is MECH-459's noise re-amplification form restated in someone else's words, arrived at independently. The second is that the normalisation parameters are themselves estimated per mini-batch and so carry their own estimator variance.

The second mechanism is where I would caution against over-claiming. REE's normaliser is per-*episode*, not per-mini-batch, so the estimator-variance argument does not straightforwardly apply — only the small-denominator argument does. That is still the load-bearing half for MECH-459, since a zero-forage REE episode is by construction the small-denominator case: the advantage vector holds critic error plus exploration jitter and nothing else, and unit-variance rescaling turns that into a full-magnitude noise gradient. But the fit is partial and should be recorded as partial.

Two further limits keep confidence at 0.62 rather than higher. It is an unreviewed preprint. And while the extraction confirmed the qualitative claims and the mechanisms, it did not surface verbatim effect sizes or the benchmark table — so the *direction* of harm is supported here while the *magnitude* is not. Nothing in this entry licenses a quantitative prediction about how far REE's foraging floor would move under probe K.

The most interesting wrinkle is that their remedy diverges from ours. Wang et al. conclude that the fix is better value estimation, not a floored one-sided denominator — they treat bad advantages as a symptom of a bad critic. MECH-459 prescribes both, listing a distributional twohot critic alongside the percentile normaliser, so the two are not in conflict. But it does mean the paper supports MECH-459's *diagnosis* while pointing toward a different *treatment* than the sanctioned knock-out installs. Practically, that is worth holding onto when probe K reports: a null on the normaliser knock-out alone would not refute this paper, and would arguably redirect attention to the critic-calibration readout that probe R (V3-EXQ-782) is already measuring. The two probes are less independent than their framing suggests, and this paper is the reason to expect that.
