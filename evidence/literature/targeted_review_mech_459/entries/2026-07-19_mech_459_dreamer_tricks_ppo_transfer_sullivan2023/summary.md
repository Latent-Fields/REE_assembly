# Sullivan et al. (NeurIPS 2023) — the transfer null, and why it should temper probe K's prior

MECH-459 makes an inference that deserves to be named explicitly: it takes a prescription validated inside DreamerV3 and imports it across an algorithm boundary into REE's model-free raw-view actor-critic. This paper is the only one I found that ran that exact inferential step as an experiment — importing DreamerV3's tricks into PPO — and the headline result is a null. The tricks "do not transfer as general improvements to PPO," established across more than 10,000 compute-hours. That is not an underpowered miss; it is a well-funded, peer-reviewed negative result, which given the publication bias against nulls should probably be weighted *up* rather than down.

I have logged this as `mixed` rather than `weakens`, and the reason is the second half of their finding. PPO with the tricks performed comparably to PPO *with* reward clipping, and significantly outperformed PPO *without* it. So the machinery was not inert — it was substitutive. It recovered what reward clipping already provided. Read carefully, that says the benefit of DreamerV3-style normalisation is specifically about robustness to unbounded reward magnitude. Which is interesting for REE, because magnitude robustness is exactly the property MECH-459 argues REE's stacked normalisers have already achieved — and achieved too well, to the point of destroying the signal along with the scale.

Three boundaries keep this from being a straightforward strike against MECH-459, and the first cuts both ways. Their evaluation is dominated by dense-reward Atari. MECH-459's pathology lives in sparse, zero-forage episodes where the advantage vector contains only critic error and exploration jitter. In a dense-reward regime the advantage vector is genuinely informative, the denominator is not small, and MECH-459 would itself predict no effect from changing the normaliser. So the null may be regime-explained rather than mechanism-refuting — which also means a positive probe-K result would not contradict this paper. Second, the tricks were tested as a bundle; no component-level attribution is possible, and a bundle-level null cannot be read as evidence against the percentile normaliser specifically. Third, and I think most important: they *added* a corrective to a working baseline, whereas probe K *removes* a suspected-harmful operator from a floored baseline. Those are not the same experiment. Adding a fix to a healthy system tests whether it helps; removing a pathology from a broken one tests whether it was load-bearing.

Where this genuinely bites is on prior-setting, and it should be recorded before probe K runs rather than after. The honest position going in is that the cross-algorithm transfer of DreamerV3's normalisation has been tried once at serious scale and did not generalise. If probe K returns a null, this paper is the reason that outcome should read as unsurprising rather than as a puzzle demanding a further round of single-axis probes — and the MECH-457 re-derive brake, which has already fired three times on that claim family, would then be the correct governance response rather than a premature one. If probe K instead moves the floor, the regime argument above is what distinguishes REE's sparse case from their dense one, and that distinction should be stated in the result rather than discovered afterwards.
