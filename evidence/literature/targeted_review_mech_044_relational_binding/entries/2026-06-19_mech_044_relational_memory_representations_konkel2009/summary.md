# Konkel & Cohen (2009) — Relational memory and the hippocampus: representations and methods

**Claim tested:** MECH-044 (hippocampal systems participate in relational binding and comparison)
**Direction:** supports · **Confidence:** 0.72
**DOI:** [10.3389/neuro.01.023.2009](https://doi.org/10.3389/neuro.01.023.2009) · *Frontiers in Neuroscience*, 2009

## What the paper does

This review states the relational-memory theory of hippocampal function in its mature form. The core proposal is that the hippocampus forms representations of *arbitrary or accidentally occurring relations* among the constituent elements of an experience — that what the hippocampus contributes is not a particular task or a particular content type but a *representational format*: the relation. The authors anchor the argument in a then-recent finding that amnesic patients with hippocampal damage are *similarly* impaired across probes of spatial, sequential, and associative relations. The methodological argument that follows is that hippocampal function should be specified in terms of the representations it supports, not catalogued task-by-task, because the same relational machinery surfaces across superficially different paradigms.

## Why it bears on MECH-044

Konkel & Cohen supply the theoretical frame that MECH-044 instantiates. Where Olsen et al. (2012) establish that relational binding/comparison happens *online and early*, this paper establishes *what is being bound* — relations as a representational currency, distinct from item or stimulus-response codes. That distinction is exactly the one REE leans on: MECH-044 says the hippocampal anchor tracks *spatial relations between entities, not just absolute positions*. The contrast between a relational representation and a bare positional/item representation is the conceptual hinge of the claim, and this is the paper that draws it cleanly.

The second contribution is the empirical *generality*. The finding that spatial, sequential, and associative relations are impaired together by hippocampal damage underwrites REE's choice to treat relational binding as one capacity rather than a bag of per-modality tricks — which is what justifies a single MECH-044 mechanism feeding the ARC-080 coregistration primitive rather than separate spatial, temporal, and associative gadgets.

## Limitations and caveats

The honest limitation is the instrument. The evidence cited here is amnesia/lesion: it shows the hippocampus is *necessary* for relational memory, but lesion data are coarse in time and cannot localise relational binding to the online perception-action window that MECH-044 specifies. That temporal reading rests on the companion Olsen et al. eye-movement/MEG line, not on this paper. There is also a scope point: Konkel & Cohen are talking about *memory for* relations, and reading the theory as support for *in-the-loop perceptual relational comparison* is a defensible extension but an extension nonetheless. Finally — the cross-entry caveat — this paper, Olsen et al. (2012), and Ryan et al. (2015) are all from the Cohen/Eichenbaum/Ryan tradition; they are mutually reinforcing within one school rather than independent convergence, and the three confidence values should be read together with that in mind.
