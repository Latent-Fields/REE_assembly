# Resolving New Memories: the Memory Resolution Hypothesis (Aimone, Deng & Gage 2011)

According to PubMed ([DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2011.05.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2011.05.010)), this canonical *Neuron* review takes a critical look at how the dentate gyrus and adult neurogenesis contribute to memory, and argues that the field's focus on "pattern separation" is too narrow. The authors propose a *memory resolution* hypothesis: broadly-tuned immature adult-born granule cells and highly specific mature granule cells contribute different kinds of information, and the fidelity — the resolution — of a memory depends on the balance between these contributions rather than on pattern separation per se.

For Q-057, the value of this paper is conceptual but precise. Its core distinction — a broad, low-specificity recall regime supported by immature cells versus a narrow, high-specificity regime supported by mature cells — is the closest biological precedent I found for the exact contrast Q-057 draws between "immature single-cue authority" and "mature convergent-weak-input recall." It says, in effect, that recall specificity is a maturational variable, and that the same circuit can operate in qualitatively different retrieval modes depending on the maturity of its constituent units. That is a useful constraint on how REE should think about MECH-362 and ARC-019: cue-authority may not be a fixed property but a regime that shifts as the underlying representation matures from broad to specific.

Two caveats keep my confidence moderate, and both are worth carrying forward as failure signatures. First, the authors themselves flag a reconciliation problem that should make REE careful with borrowed vocabulary: the "pattern separation" measured in behavioural tasks does not cleanly match the computational and electrophysiological conception of the term. If REE imports "pattern separation/completion" language into the Q-057 discussion without operationalising it, it risks inheriting exactly this equivocation. Second, the maturation axis here is the immature-versus-mature status of *individual* adult-born neurons, not the organismal development of CA3 cue-authority — and resolution is framed as a graded continuum of tuning width, not a discrete gate. An REE account that posits a sharp developmental gate before action-release may be over-discretising what the biology treats as a continuum.

I have set evidence_direction to **supports** with confidence 0.58. The source is a well-cited, canonical review (strong source quality), but it is theoretical, predates much of the causal optogenetic work, and operates at the cell-maturation level rather than the systems-development level Q-057 is really about. So it earns its place as the mechanistic-precedent entry for the single-cue-vs-convergent-recall distinction, while staying clearly an analogy rather than direct evidence.
