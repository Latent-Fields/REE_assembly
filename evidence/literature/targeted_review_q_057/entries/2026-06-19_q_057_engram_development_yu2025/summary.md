# Long-Term Memory Engrams From Development to Adulthood (Yu & DeNardo 2025)

According to PubMed ([DOI 10.1002/hipo.70032](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.70032)), this 2025 *Hippocampus* review synthesises what is currently known about how the memory engram — the physical trace of a memory — transforms as memories evolve from recent to remote, and crucially how that transformation differs between the developing and adult brain. The authors concentrate on four regions: hippocampus, retrosplenial cortex, medial prefrontal cortex, and anterior thalamic nuclei. The adult sections emphasise the distinct contributions of engram cells across encoding, consolidation, and retrieval, with synaptic plasticity carrying the consolidation load and coordinated cross-regional activity supporting long-term storage. The developmental sections review how the *same* engram machinery, when immature, produces infant memory generalization and infantile amnesia.

The reason this is relevant to Q-057 is that it speaks directly to the question we are actually asking: is REE-v3's cue-authority problem partly a developmental immaturity rather than a mature design property? The review's central developmental claim — that early-life engrams are encoded and consolidated *differently* and tend toward generalization rather than specific, cued recall — is exactly the shape of the hypothesis behind Q-057's first sub-question. A trace that exists but generalizes too broadly, or that has not yet been bound to a stable convergent cue set, is the biological analogue of "immature single-cue authority." That maps cleanly onto MECH-362 (the CA3 developmental anchor) and ARC-019.

Where the paper is honest, and where I want to stay honest, is the limit of what is actually known. The authors state plainly that, compared with adults, much less is understood about how — or even *to what extent* — early-life memories undergo consolidation at all. So the review supports the *framing* of Q-057 (developmental gates are real and they shape retrieval) without licensing any specific ordering of those gates. If REE wanted to commit to a particular gate sequence before a trace can influence action release (sub-question 3), the biology does not yet adjudicate it. There is a second caveat worth flagging: engram persistence in this account is a distributed, multi-region phenomenon. A CA3-only story about cue-authority maturation is probably incomplete; retrosplenial and prefrontal maturation are part of the same clock.

I have set evidence_direction to **supports** with confidence 0.62. The source quality is high (specialist venue, 2025, NIH-funded review) and the conceptual mapping is good, but I deliberately held confidence below the source-quality score because the mapping fidelity is moderate: the review describes developmental gating qualitatively and never operationalises the single-cue-versus-convergent-input distinction that REE instruments. It is strong corroborating context for treating cue-authority as a developmental regime, and a good pointer toward the prefrontal/retrosplenial dimensions REE may be under-representing — but it is not direct mechanistic evidence for any one REE gate.
