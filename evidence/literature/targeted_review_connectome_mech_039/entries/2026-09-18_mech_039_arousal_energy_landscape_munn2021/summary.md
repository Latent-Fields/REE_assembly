# Munn, Muller, Wainstein & Shine (2021) -- The ascending arousal system shapes neural dynamics to mediate awareness of cognitive states

*Nature Communications 12:6016 -- doi:10.1038/s41467-021-26268-x -- PMID 34650039*

## What the paper did

Fifty-nine healthy adults were scanned at 7T during rest (2 mm isotropic, TR 586 ms), and timeseries were extracted from two subcortical hubs of the ascending arousal system: the noradrenergic locus coeruleus (LC) and the cholinergic nucleus basalis of Meynert (BNM). The authors identified phasic bursts in each -- a threshold of 2 s.d. above the local mean, yielding 148 LC-leading, 130 BNM-leading and 316 simultaneous events across the cohort -- and asked what happened to cortical dynamics in the seconds around them. The dynamics were summarised as a low-dimensional *energy landscape*, where the energy of a brain state is inversely related to its probability of occurrence, so that frequently-visited states are valleys and rarely-visited ones are hills. The authors are explicit about the picture: "Brain states evolve along the energy landscape topography, much like a ball rolls under the influence of gravity down a valley and requires energy to traverse up a hill." A second cohort of fourteen expert meditators, pressing a button when they noticed their attention had drifted from the breath, provided a behavioural anchor for state change.

## The finding that matters here

The two channels moved the landscape in opposite directions, as predicted a priori. Phasic LC bursts produced "a flattening or reduction of the energy landscape", making otherwise improbable state transitions accessible; BNM peaks produced "an accentuation of the well by raising the energy landscape", making transitions less likely. The authors motivate this by analogy with activation energy in chemistry: noradrenaline's multiplicative gain lowers the barrier between steady states, acetylcholine's response gain raises it, so the same system becomes variable or rigid depending on where the two channels sit.

This is as close as the human literature gets to a direct measurement of what MECH-039 asserts. The claim's architecture is that modes are stable regions in a continuous control-channel space rather than separate modules, and that switching is a trajectory through that space rather than a flag flip. Both halves are present here in neurobiological form. The stable region is the energy well -- a set of states the system settles into and must be *driven out of*, which is what makes "stable" a measurable property rather than a label. The channel is neuromodulatory tone, and it does not select a mode; it changes how deep the wells are, and therefore how readily the system leaves whichever one it is in. Mapped onto `control_plane.md`'s channel list, LC tone behaves like the arousal-volatility and interruptibility channel and BNM tone like precision-and-gain routing, and it is worth noticing that this is the same pairing REE already leans on elsewhere (the MECH-104 Aston-Jones and Cohen basis, and SD-069's phasic burst on the selection softmax).

## Why this is the strongest entry for the non-degeneracy precondition

MECH-039 will not permit a verdict at all unless the named channels show non-zero independent cross-condition variance: a run in which every channel moves in lockstep cannot discriminate a stable region in a continuous space from a discrete module. That precondition is easy to state and easy to fail quietly. What makes this paper unusually useful is that its two channels move the landscape in *opposite* directions within one dataset. Two quantities with opposite-signed effects on the same dependent measure are not a collapsed single arousal scalar wearing two names, which is the degenerate case the precondition is guarding against. That is a stronger constraint than a mere correlation-is-below-threshold argument, and it comes from data rather than from design.

## Limitations, and what the paper does not establish

The honest reading has to hold three things back. First, the LC is roughly 2-3 mm across and sits against the fourth ventricle; these are BOLD signals from very small nuclei at 2 mm, and although the authors control for CSF explicitly, if the physiological confound *is* the signal then the channel dissociation -- the paper's load-bearing result for us -- is pulsatile artifact rather than arousal. Second, the energy landscape is a derived statistical object, computed from the likelihood of BOLD change, not a measured potential. A landscape whose estimated topography shifts with arousal is consistent with attractor dynamics; it does not demonstrate them, and a non-attractor system with arousal-dependent autocorrelation would look the same. Third, and most relevant to governance: the paper shows that a channel changes the *depth* of wells. It never asks whether REE's three named modes -- task-engaged, default-mode-like, emergency -- each occupy a distinct well, nor whether transitions between them are graded or abrupt. The clustering half of MECH-039's confirming signature and the continuity half are both untested here. The meditator arm, which ties landscape change to a reported shift in awareness, rests on fourteen people and should be treated as suggestive.

Confidence is set at 0.80. Mapping fidelity is the highest in this pull (0.85) because the paper's own prose is close to a restatement of the claim, including the ball-in-a-valley image and the insistence that neuromodulation reshapes the landscape rather than flipping a state. Source quality 0.85 for the 7T acquisition, the directional a-priori hypothesis confirmed in the predicted direction, and the second cohort -- discounted for the small nuclei and the small replication arm. Transfer risk 0.30: the step from human neuromodulatory tone to engineered REE control scalars is real, but it is the *direction* of the effect and not its magnitude that MECH-039 needs.
