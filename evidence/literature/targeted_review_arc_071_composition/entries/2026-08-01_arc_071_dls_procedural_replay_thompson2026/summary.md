# Thompson, Rollik, Waked et al. 2026 -- DLS procedural replay independent of hippocampus

Thompson et al. report direct rodent evidence that procedural-memory replay can occur in the dorsolateral striatum during offline consolidation without hippocampal dependence. Mice learned a multistep nose-poke sequence; DLS lesions impaired learning/execution, and post-training DLS AP5 disrupted next-day consolidation. Neuropixel recordings plus PP-Seq identified task-aligned DLS neural sequences during waking and awake-like replay during sleep. Replay events preserved within-sequence ordering and between-sequence task order, and replay content predicted subsequent performance improvement.

The load-bearing extension beyond the older Albouy 2013 entry is the hippocampal-lesion result. Large bilateral hippocampal lesions did not impair procedural learning in this task, and DLS replay features persisted: awake-like sequences were still replayed offline, sequence ordering remained, and ordered combinations still followed the behavioral sequence. This materially strengthens the ARC-071 R6 / MECH-322 carve-out story because the relevant replay is not merely hippocampal-striatal coupling inferred from human fMRI; it is DLS procedural replay in mice, preserved after hippocampal ablation.

## REE mapping

For ARC-071, this supports the claim that policy-composition/habit-chunk machinery belongs in a striatal/DLS-like procedural system, not only in the hippocampal rollout system. The paper fits the existing ARC-071 shape: repeated execution of a multistep behavioral sequence produces task-phase neural sequences in DLS, and offline replay of those sequences is linked to consolidation and later performance.

For MECH-322, this is direct biological support for a sleep/offline replay route into procedural consolidation. The replay content was shaped by salient behavioral events, with positive and negative outcomes having opposing effects on replay events, and replay content predicted later performance improvements. That supports MECH-322's value-conditioned carve-out in direction: sleep replay can help consolidate procedural/habit content, but REE should keep the audit flag and corroboration deadline because biological replay is not a safety guarantee.

For MECH-121 and the hippocampal replay cluster, the paper is a boundary condition, not a refutation. It challenges hippocampus-as-universal-orchestrator language for nondeclarative/procedural memory, but it does not weaken hippocampal replay claims scoped to episodic, spatial, or schema transfer. The correct REE update is parallel replay systems: hippocampal replay for map/episodic/schema consolidation, and striatal replay for procedural/chunk consolidation.

## Caveats

The authors explicitly note that selective disruption of striatal replay is still needed to prove replay's causal contribution to procedural memory consolidation. The evidence currently shows DLS offline processing is needed, DLS replay exists, replay is outcome-shaped, replay predicts later improvement, and replay survives hippocampal lesions. That is strong but not the final causal intervention on replay itself.

The task is a skilled motor sequence in mice. Mapping to REE's chunked policy primitives is high-fidelity for procedural sequence consolidation and lower-fidelity for cognitive/rule chunks unless later evidence shows the same striatal replay logic beyond motor sequences.

## Disposition

Evidence direction: supports ARC-071/MECH-322/MECH-323. Confidence 0.84.

No new claim is required. MECH-322 already exists and already resolves the older R6 safety-vs-realism escalation. This source strengthens the biological footing and narrows the old Albouy caveat; it does not require a redesign beyond preserving the existing parallel replay-system framing.
