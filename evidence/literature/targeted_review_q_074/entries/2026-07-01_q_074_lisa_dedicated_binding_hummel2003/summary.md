# LISA: relational mapping needs dedicated dynamic binding (Hummel & Holyoak 2003)

**Claim tested:** Q-074 -- Is analogy a separate V6 operator (MECH-421), or does it emerge from MECH-300 traversal over a shared symbolic map?

**What the paper argues.** LISA (Learning and Inference with Schemas and Analogies) is Hummel and Holyoak's symbolic-connectionist theory of relational inference. Its central commitment is that structure-sensitive reasoning -- analogical access, mapping, inference, schema induction -- requires *dynamic binding* of roles to their fillers, implemented in LISA through temporal synchrony: units that fire together encode "this object plays this role." The pay-off is a system with connectionist flexibility *and* symbolic structure-sensitivity. The price, which the authors are explicit about, is capacity: synchrony can only hold bindings at one hierarchical level at a time, so LISA maxes out at roughly 4-6 role bindings, or 2-3 propositions, in play at once. Published in *Psychological Review* 110(2):220-264 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.110.2.220)).

**Why it speaks to Q-074 -- and why I logged it as `weakens`.** This is the principled case for the *first* horn: analogy is not free. If you want to map "the atom is like the solar system," you have to keep straight that the nucleus corresponds to the sun and the electrons to the planets, and *not* the reverse -- and Hummel and Holyoak's argument is that distributed representations, on their own, cannot maintain that role-filler structure. Something has to bind. So relative to REE's emergence hypothesis (that MECH-421 falls out of the relational stack), LISA is a counter-weight: it says structure-sensitive mapping needs a dedicated binder, and a stack that merely blends representations will produce systematic binding errors -- illusory conjunctions, role confusions -- on exactly the hard analogies the mechanism is supposed to handle. Hence `weakens` for the pure-emergence reading.

**The honest complication.** LISA establishes that analogy needs binding; it does *not* establish that MECH-300 cognitive-map traversal cannot itself *be* the binder. That distinction is the whole game for Q-074. If traversing the shared symbolic map already implements role-filler binding as a side effect of how entries are addressed, then the "dedicated operator" and "emergent from traversal" horns quietly converge -- you get the binder for free from the map machinery, and LISA's requirement is satisfied without a bolted-on module. So the correct reading is that LISA weakens *pure* emergence (analogy is not a no-cost by-product of distributed representation) without deciding the build question (traversal might supply the binding LISA demands). It also carries the standard transfer discount: LISA is a psychological model of *human* analogy, one step removed from REE's substrate.

**Confidence.** 0.60. The source is foundational and heavily cited (source quality 0.85), but mapping fidelity is only moderate because "analogy needs binding" does not cleanly adjudicate "build a separate module" versus "traversal already binds." The load-bearing takeaway for the eventual V6 design fork is the *capacity* signature: if the LISA thesis holds, whatever REE does for analogy inherits a hard working-memory bottleneck (a handful of simultaneous bindings), which is a design constraint worth pricing in now rather than discovering later. Q-074 remains `substrate_conditional` and do-not-build-in-V3; this entry sharpens the fork without forcing it.
