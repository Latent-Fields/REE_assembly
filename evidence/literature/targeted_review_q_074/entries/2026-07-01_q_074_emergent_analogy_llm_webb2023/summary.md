# Emergent analogical reasoning in large language models (Webb, Holyoak & Lu 2023)

**Claim tested:** Q-074 -- Is analogy a separate V6 operator (MECH-421), or does it emerge from traversal over a shared relational substrate?

**What the paper did.** Webb, Holyoak and Lu ran GPT-3 (text-davinci-003) head-to-head against human participants on a battery of analogy tasks: a text-based matrix-reasoning task modelled on Raven's Standard Progressive Matrices, letter-string analogies, four-term verbal analogies, and story analogies. GPT-3 had no analogy-specific training and no worked examples -- the tasks were posed zero-shot. It nonetheless displayed, in the authors' words, a "surprisingly strong capacity for abstract pattern induction," matching or exceeding the human group in most conditions; a preliminary look at GPT-4 was better still. Published in *Nature Human Behaviour* 7(9):1526-1541 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-023-01659-w)).

**Why it speaks to Q-074.** The question is whether REE should build analogy as a dedicated operator or expect it to fall out of the relational-inference stack. Webb et al. is the cleanest behavioural demonstration that a *general* system -- one built to predict the next token, with no analogy module anywhere in it -- can nonetheless reason analogically across several task families. That is precisely the shape of the emergence horn: capability the designer never explicitly installed, appearing because the underlying substrate is rich enough. If a plain language model gets analogy "for free," the prior that MECH-421 *must* be a separate operator weakens.

**Where I have to be careful.** Two caveats, and neither is minor. First, the genuineness of the effect is contested. Hodel and West, among others, showed that GPT-3 fails simple perturbations of the very letter-string analogies it appears to solve -- swap the alphabet, shuffle the surface form -- while humans stay robust. That is exactly the failure signature that should worry us: analogy is supposed to *buy* robust generalization, and brittle pattern-completion that collapses under trivial variation is not the thing we want to claim emerged. (A later counterfactual-task paper pushes back the other way, so the debate is live rather than settled.) Second, the substrate is wrong twice: an LLM is not MECH-300 cognitive-map traversal, and "emerges at trillion-token scale in a language model" is not the same proposition as "emerges from a compact symbolic map." Scale and training distribution may be doing the work here in a way that would not transfer to REE's leaner substrate.

**Confidence.** 0.55 -- moderate support for the emergence horn, and deliberately not higher. The venue and the direct human comparison are strong (source quality 0.80), but the contested-genuineness literature plus the double substrate mismatch keep mapping fidelity and transfer risk both middling. Read alongside the Tolman-Eichenbaum entry (a mechanistic emergence argument) and the LISA entry (the dedicated-binding counter-argument), this is the *behavioural* leg of the emergence case: suggestive that analogy need not be hand-built, silent on whether REE's particular substrate is the kind that would deliver it. Q-074 is `substrate_conditional` and do-not-build-in-V3, so this stays context for the V6 fork, not a trigger.
