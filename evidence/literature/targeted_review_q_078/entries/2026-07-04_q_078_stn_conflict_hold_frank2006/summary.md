# Frank 2006 — the STN "hold your horses" conflict gate

According to PubMed. Source: Frank MJ. "Hold your horses: a dynamic computational role for the subthalamic nucleus in decision making." *Neural Networks* 19(8):1120-36, 2006. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neunet.2006.03.006) · PMID 16945502.

## What the paper did

Frank extends an earlier BG decision-making network — which had already captured Parkinson's response-selection deficits and made confirmed predictions in patients and under pharmacological challenge — by adding the subthalamic nucleus (STN), the one major BG structure the prior model omitted. The addition is not cosmetic. In the model, the STN, driven by cortex via the hyperdirect pathway and projecting diffusely to pallidum, implements a **dynamic, conflict-sensitive raising of the response threshold**: when multiple cortical response candidates are strongly co-active (high response conflict), the cortico-subthalamic-pallidal loop transiently makes it *harder* to commit to any response — "hold your horses." The key result is that by controlling *when* a response is executed, the STN changes *which* response is ultimately selected, particularly when several candidates compete. The model also reproduces BG population dynamics during selection, generates Parkinsonian-like oscillations under simulated dopamine depletion, and shows that STN lesions relieve those oscillations at the cost of impaired conflict-sensitive decisions.

## Why it speaks to Q-078

Q-078's constitution list names, verbatim, "hyperdirect-STN conflict-hold" and "threshold/widening" as candidate constitutional levers whose under-specification could explain why a formed signal reaching E3 authority fails to convert to committed action. Frank 2006 is the canonical mechanistic model of exactly that lever. It shows that the commit threshold is not a fixed parameter and is not a property of any single channel's salience — it is set *dynamically as a function of inter-candidate conflict* by a dedicated circuit. That is a constitutional rule for lawful access to commitment: two systems with identical upstream signal strengths but differing conflict-hold machinery will convert differently. This is direct support for the reading that the commit-timing/threshold machinery is an architectural component, not something you can buy with more salience.

There is also a sharp, testable prediction embedded here. If REE's conversion failures are constitutional-via-missing-conflict-hold, they should **concentrate where inter-candidate conflict is high** — where several candidates reach E3 near-simultaneously — rather than being uniform across the board. A parametric weakness (signal too quiet) predicts uniform failure; a missing conflict-hold predicts conflict-selective failure with premature commitment to noise. That is a discriminating signature the paired experiment could look for.

## The mapping and its limits

The caveat is the same shape as for the Gurney entry: this is a BG model validated against Parkinson's and pharmacology, not a test of REE's E3 pathway, and it evidences *one* lever (conflict-gated threshold) rather than the whole selection constitution Q-078 hypothesises. So it raises the prior on the constitutional reading for that specific sub-mechanism and hands the experiment a concrete conflict-selective prediction, but it does not by itself decide the broader constitutional-vs-parametric question. The transfer from a biophysically-grounded BG circuit to REE's arbitration remains analogical.
