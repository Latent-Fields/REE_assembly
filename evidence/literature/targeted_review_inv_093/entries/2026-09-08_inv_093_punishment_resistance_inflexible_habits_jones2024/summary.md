# Jones et al. (2024) -- Punishment resistance for cocaine is associated with inflexible habits

**Claim tested:** INV-093 (skill optimisation must not trade harm sensitivity for competence)
**Direction:** supports | **Confidence:** 0.65

## What the paper did

The question is whether resistance to punishment during drug seeking is a consequence of the behaviour having become habitual. To ask it properly you have to measure both things in the same animals, and that is what this design does. Male and female rats were trained to self-administer cocaine, then exposed to punishment -- random footshock delivered contingent on seeking. Separately, outcome-devaluation tests established whether each animal's responding was under goal-directed or habitual control, and crucially these tests were run both *before* and *after* the punishment phase. Parallel experiments used food rather than cocaine.

The result: punishment resistance was associated with continued use of habits, punishment sensitivity with increased goal-directed control. The finding I keep returning to is the temporal one -- punishment-resistant rats showed persistent habitual responding *after* punishment despite not having shown habits before it. In the food experiments males followed the same pattern; females showed habitual responding both before and after. The authors' conclusion is that punishment resistance reflects habits that have become inflexible and persist under exactly the conditions that ought to force a return to goal-directed control.

## Why this matters for INV-093

Dickinson (1985), the neighbouring entry in this directory, establishes that refinement produces autonomy from outcome *value*. INV-093 needs autonomy from *harm*, and I flagged in that entry that treating a newly aversive outcome as a special case of a revalued one is an argument REE has not made. This paper is the empirical step across that gap. Both variables are measured in the same animals, and they co-vary: the more habitual the control, the less the behaviour bends to an aversive consequence.

That coupling is the substantive contribution, and it is stronger than it first appears. If harm sensitivity were an independent property, a refinement mechanism might simply happen to leave it alone, and INV-093 would be a reasonable thing to check rather than an invariant to enforce. What this paper suggests is that harm sensitivity and degree of automatisation are two faces of one variable -- which is exactly why the panel cannot be collapsed into a single score. A collapsed metric would be summing a quantity with its own shadow.

The post-punishment finding is the one I would build a probe around. In the resistant animals, exposure to the aversive signal drove behaviour *further* into automaticity rather than recruiting outcome-sensitive control. The corrective feedback made things worse, in precisely the population where it mattered. If a REE refinement mechanism does this, then the natural remedial design -- notice the harm-sensitivity loss, apply a corrective signal, retrain -- is not merely insufficient but actively counterproductive, and INV-093's acceptance test needs to be run *after* any such correction rather than treating the correction as a fix.

The sex difference is worth carrying forward too, in a modest form. Females showed habitual responding throughout the food experiments, males only after punishment. Whatever the biology, the methodological lesson holds for us: a refinement mechanism can pass the invariant on average while failing in an identifiable subpopulation, so a sweep that reports pooled means is not sufficient.

## Limitations and caveats

The primary preparation is cocaine self-administration, and that is a serious confound rather than a formality. Drug-induced striatal plasticity is a plausible common cause of both punishment resistance and habitual control, which would make their association a shared consequence rather than the dependency the paper argues for. The parallel food experiments are the right control and are the reason the entry is here at all, but they are secondary in the design and carry a sex difference the cocaine arm does not fully mirror -- so they mitigate the confound without dissolving it.

Second, the design is correlational across individual differences. Nobody manipulated how habitual the animals were and watched harm sensitivity follow. So this establishes coupling, not the causal direction INV-093 asserts, and the claim's own falsifier -- a sweep of refinement strength -- remains unrun in any literature I have found.

Third, footshock is a crude, exogenous harm signal. INV-093 is written about harm sensitivity, residue accumulation and commitment integrity in an agent with an endogenous harm channel; the sibling claim INV-092's evidence on selective permeability is a much better guide to what that channel should look like. And as with every entry here, residue accumulation and commitment integrity get no coverage at all -- three of the four panel axes are simply unevidenced by anything I could find, which is a finding this pull should hand to governance rather than hide.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality 0.70: a careful within-animal design that measures both variables, with sex balance and a food control -- unusual rigour for this question -- but single-lab, unreplicated, in a young specialist journal. Mapping fidelity 0.70: the habitual-control-to-punishment-insensitivity coupling is exactly the relation INV-093 needs, discounted because it is correlational rather than a refinement sweep. Transfer risk 0.50, the joint highest in this directory, because an addiction preparation carries a pharmacological confound with no REE analogue whatever, and reading a drug-seeking result as a general statement about skill refinement asks the food arm to carry more weight than its secondary status supports. Aggregate 0.65.
