# Hutcherson, Bushong & Rangel (2015) -- altruistic choice as attribute weighting, not a moral term

## What the paper did

Subjects chose between real monetary prizes for themselves and for an anonymous other while being
scanned. Rather than treating generosity as a psychological kind and asking which region "does" it,
the authors fitted a multi-attribute drift-diffusion model in which a single relative-value signal
accumulates to a boundary, and the two payoff streams -- self and other -- enter that accumulation
as separately weighted attributes. They then asked whether that one mechanism could reproduce the
choice proportions, the reaction-time structure, and the BOLD responses in ventral striatum, TPJ
and vmPFC simultaneously. It could.

## The finding that matters here

The interesting result is a negative one, and the authors state it plainly: the model explains when
and why generous choices are slower or faster than selfish ones, and why they evoke larger TPJ and
vmPFC responses, *without* invoking a competition between automatic and deliberative processes, and
*without* assigning any reward value to generosity as such. The RT asymmetry and the regional
activations are the two observations most often read as evidence that something extra -- a moral
override, a prosocial reward, a cost of defection -- is running alongside ordinary valuation. This
paper shows those observations fall out of ordinary valuation with different attribute weights.

There is a second, sharper implication the authors draw: because the accumulator is noisy, when
one's own payoff is weighted more heavily than the other's, some generous acts are simply errors.
Generosity, on this account, is not always the output of a generous representation.

## How this bears on ARC-012

ARC-012 asserts that E3 does not require an explicit ethical cost term. The natural objection is
that without such a term there is nothing for the ethical content to *be*. This paper answers that
objection in the only way an architectural claim can be answered -- by exhibiting a mechanism in
which the ethical content lives in the weighting of attributes the valuation process was already
integrating, and showing that the mechanism accounts for the data a separate-term account was
introduced to explain. The mapping to E3 is close in shape: a relative value signal, accumulated,
with the ethically loaded dimension entering as one more weighted contributor rather than as a
penalty applied after the fact.

I want to be careful about the direction of the inference, though. What is licensed is "a separate
ethical term is not necessary *to explain these data*". What is not licensed is "no architecture
ever needs one". And there is a specific place where the analogy strains. In this task the
experimenter individuates the attributes: the subject is handed "money to me" and "money to them"
as pre-separated quantities, and the only open question is the weights. E3 does not get that for
free. Deciding *what the ethically relevant attributes of a situation are* is upstream of weighting
them, and nothing in this paper speaks to whether that individuation can also be done without an
explicit ethical representation. If ARC-012 fails, I would expect it to fail there rather than at
the weighting stage.

## Confidence

0.78. The source quality is high and the mapping fidelity is unusually good -- it is rare to find a
paper whose headline negative result is the same negative an architectural claim is making. I have
held it below 0.8 because of the individuation gap above and because the transfer is from one-shot
human economic choice to a sequential embodied selector, which is a real distance even when the
formal machinery looks the same.
