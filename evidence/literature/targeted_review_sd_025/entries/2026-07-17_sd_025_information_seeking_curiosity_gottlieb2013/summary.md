# An information-seeking drive is a legitimate, formalizable primitive (Gottlieb et al. 2013)

According to PubMed, Gottlieb, Oudeyer, Lopes & Baranes (2013, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2013.09.001)) review the mechanisms by which animals and machines devote time and energy to gathering information. Their central move — and the reason this paper matters for SD-025 — is to unify three traditionally separate literatures: reinforcement-learning intrinsic motivation, active oculomotor sampling in natural vision, and the psychology/neuroscience of curiosity. Out of that synthesis they draw a *family* of exploration mechanisms that runs from automatic biases toward novelty and surprise at one end, to systematic searches for learning progress and information gain (curiosity-driven behaviour) at the other. The claim is that exploration is not a residual of reward-seeking but is guided by intrinsic drives that bias action selection in their own right, and that these drives can be given explicit computational form.

SD-025 is a design_decision, and design decisions earn their keep by being principled instances of something real rather than ad hoc bonuses. This review is close to the ideal grounding for that: it establishes that an information-seeking / curiosity drive biasing action selection independently of extrinsic reward is a well-attested, cross-domain primitive, and it is precisely the machine-learning-meets-neuroscience bridge REE lives on. SD-025 adds a uniform curiosity term to hippocampal CEM trajectory scoring — "the same computation everywhere" — and lets approach behaviour *emerge* because DA-expanded regions (SD-024) happen to carry more representational structure and therefore score higher. That is a recognisable member of Gottlieb et al.'s novelty/information-bias family: a uniform intrinsic bias whose directional consequences fall out of where the information is, not out of the agent "knowing" it is approaching reward. The review licenses the architecture.

I want to be careful not to over-claim, because the same review that grounds the drive also pressures SD-025's specific form. Gottlieb et al.'s *stronger* mechanisms are learning-progress and information-gain based — seek where you can most reduce your uncertainty — and they explicitly note that pure novelty biases can get trapped, endlessly drawn to novel-but-uninformative stimuli, unless a learning-progress term rescues them. SD-025's operationalization is nearer the automatic-novelty end: it follows *representational density* in the RBF map (with novelty = density × (1 − familiarity)), which is a proxy for "there is structure here to distinguish," not an explicit expected-information-gain computation. Its only guard against perseveration is the familiarity EMA — a visit-count discount, which is a reasonable but ad hoc stand-in for a learning-progress signal. So a strict reading of this review supports the *decision* to have an information-seeking drive more firmly than it supports SD-025's particular density-following functional form, and it flags the exact failure mode (getting stuck on high-density-but-already-learned regions) that SD-025's familiarity term is trying to patch.

Confidence 0.68. Source quality is high (a canonical, heavily-cited review in the right venue and the right domain-bridge). Mapping fidelity is moderate: it grounds the drive and situates SD-025 in the mechanism family, but does not endorse the density-specific measure and leans toward learning-progress variants SD-025 does not implement. Transfer risk is low-to-moderate because the review is explicitly framed as design-relevant computational principles — which is exactly how REE consumes it. Net: solid grounding for the SD-025 design decision, with an honest asterisk that the specific functional form is the weaker member of the family the review surveys.
