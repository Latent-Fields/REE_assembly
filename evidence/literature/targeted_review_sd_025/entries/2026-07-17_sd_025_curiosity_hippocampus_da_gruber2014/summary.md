# Curiosity engages the dopamine-hippocampus circuit (Gruber, Gelman & Ranganath 2014)

According to PubMed, Gruber, Gelman & Ranganath (2014, *Neuron*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.08.060)) asked how an intrinsic motivational state — curiosity — shapes what we learn. Participants rated their curiosity about the answers to trivia questions and were then tested on those answers, plus on incidental material (faces) shown during the waiting period. Two behavioural results: memory was better for high-curiosity answers, and — the more striking part — memory was also better for the *incidental* material encountered while in a high-curiosity state, both immediately and after a one-day delay. The fMRI story is what makes this relevant to REE: high curiosity enhanced activity in the midbrain (SN/VTA) and nucleus accumbens, and the curiosity-driven memory benefit was supported by anticipatory activity in midbrain *and* hippocampus and by functional connectivity between them. In other words, intrinsic curiosity recruits the same dopaminergic circuitry as extrinsic reward, and it does so in concert with the hippocampus.

For SD-025 this grounds two design commitments simultaneously. First, that curiosity is a genuine intrinsic drive with a dedicated neural substrate — reinforcing the Gottlieb et al. framing but with human empirical data rather than review synthesis. Second, and more specific to REE's architecture, that the substrate for curiosity is precisely the *midbrain-dopaminergic + hippocampal* circuit. SD-025 places a curiosity trajectory-scoring bias on the hippocampal RBF layer, and SD-024 places a DA-modulated representational-density expansion on the same layer; ARC-057 asserts these two co-modulate the same hippocampal representation. Gruber et al.'s finding that intrinsic curiosity and extrinsic reward converge on a shared dopaminergic-hippocampal circuit is close to a biological warrant for that pairing — it says the components REE couples together are, in the brain, coupled together. The demonstration that a curious state enhances *incidental* encoding is also a nice echo of SD-025's "uniform computation everywhere" property: curiosity raises the gain on whatever is being represented, not just on the object of curiosity.

The caveats are real and I want them on the record. The curiosity construct here is *epistemic* — anticipatory interest in the answer to a factual question — and it is read out as a *memory-encoding benefit*. SD-025's curiosity is an *online action-selection bias*: a bonus added to CEM trajectory scoring in proportion to representational density, steering the agent toward information-rich regions. These share the label "curiosity" and, plausibly, a circuit, but they are different computations measured on different axes (encoding gain versus trajectory choice). So this paper supports the *drive and its substrate* far more than it supports SD-025's specific functional form (density-following approach emergence). It also lives on the encoding/consolidation axis rather than the online-planning axis where SD-025 operates — the same online-versus-offline gap that qualifies the MECH-232 McNamara entry applies here. And it is human fMRI mapped onto REE's abstract RBF terrain by functional analogy, not shared substrate.

I set confidence at 0.66. Source quality is high — a well-known, well-replicated study in a top venue with converging behavioural and imaging evidence. Mapping fidelity is moderate: it validates the drive's existence and, importantly, its DA-hippocampus coupling (the SD-024 × SD-025 pairing ARC-057 depends on), but not SD-025's density-following action-selection mechanism. Transfer risk is moderate for the epistemic-to-spatial and encoding-to-planning shifts. Net: this entry is the empirical partner to the Gottlieb theory grounding — together they support the SD-025 design decision and its architectural coupling to SD-024, while leaving the specific density-following functional form for REE's own experiments (V3-EXQ-767/768) to validate.
