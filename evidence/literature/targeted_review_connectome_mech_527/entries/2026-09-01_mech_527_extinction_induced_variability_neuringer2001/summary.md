# Stability and variability in extinction (Neuringer, Kornell & Olufs, 2001)

This paper is filed mixed, and the mixture is the whole reason it is here: it supports MECH-527's trigger about as cleanly as a behavioural study can, and it supplies the sharpest evidence in this pull *against* the claim's attractor-escape framing. Both halves matter, and a review that reported only the first would be doing the claim a disservice.

Rats were trained under three schedules -- one reinforcing repetitions of a particular three-operandum sequence, one reinforcing sequences only if they varied, and one yoked so that reinforcement depended on neither -- and then placed in extinction. The authors report that "in all cases, rare sequences increased during extinction--variability increased--but the ordering of sequence probabilities was generally unchanged, the most common sequences during reinforcement continuing to be most frequent in extinction." They read this as the animals generally doing what worked before while occasionally doing something very different.

The first clause is close to an ideal operationalisation of MECH-527's trigger. A previously successful response continues to be emitted, reward stops, and behavioural variability rises. There is no uncertainty manipulation and no near-tie between options -- the animal is not deliberating between two comparable alternatives, it is persisting with a strategy that has stopped paying. That is unresolvedness driving variation, and it is exactly the distinction MECH-527 draws against MECH-440's near-tie formulation. The yoked schedule matters here too: because the effect appears even where variability was never reinforced, it cannot be dismissed as the animals having previously been trained to vary.

The second clause is the caution. If injected variation existed principally to escape a false bottom, the signature we would expect is reorganisation -- the dominant response losing its rank as the search relocates. What these rats did instead was keep the dominant sequence dominant and add rare variants underneath it. Variability increased in the tail of the distribution without dislodging the attractor. So "escape a false bottom" describes a function this mechanism may serve only weakly, and REE should not assume that raising variation under stuckness will by itself move the policy somewhere better. That is a design warning for the eventual falsifier as much as a comment on the claim.

Two limits keep me from treating the negative half as decisive, and one of them cuts against my own reading. Extinction is total non-reinforcement -- a cruder and stronger manipulation than "persistent failure to reduce the active need/goal error". An agent in a genuine false bottom is still receiving some return, which is exactly what makes the bottom attractive, and that regime is simply not tested here. And preserved rank ordering over three-operandum sequences is a limited probe of basin escape: the response space is small and the sessions short, so a mechanism that would eventually reorganise the distribution might have had neither room nor time to show it. I therefore record the negative half as a genuine caution rather than a refutation -- and note that it is precisely why MECH-527's own registration text is right to demand a false-bottom ecology in which a better alternative actually exists and is reachable.
