# Peng et al. 2026 -- StateBridge: an orthogonal-Procrustes rung that works, within one model

arXiv 2608.13317, submitted 13 August 2026; the abstract page says accepted to COLM 2026, which I have not independently confirmed, so this is banked as a preprint. StateBridge takes a sender agent's final-layer hidden states and maps them into the receiver's input-embedding space by a closed-form orthogonal Procrustes transform fitted on vocabulary-embedding anchors, then rescales norms and softly pulls the result toward the input-embedding manifold. No training of the bridge. It reports best or tied-best on 22 of 26 model-task pairs over Qwen3-4B/8B/32B and OLMo3-7B-Think, with the largest gains on the hard benchmarks and a small loss on GSM8K attributed to formatting.

For MECH-538 this is the ladder in miniature and it has the right controls. A random-noise prefix collapses the Qwen3-4B average from around 80% to 48.8%, which is the random-projection floor INV-105 demands. Replacing Procrustes with ridge regression -- the affine rung above it -- is tried and does not help. Removing norm calibration or vocabulary anchoring costs a couple of points. So the least expressive transformation was tried first and it sufficed; that is precisely the inference pattern MECH-538 says licenses "the sender already contained an appropriately structured representation".

For ARC-139 the thought needs correcting. StateBridge aligns a model's own output hidden states to that same model's input space. The authors say so: "Our current setting assumes a shared model and a fixed prefix length. Future work could extend the method to heterogeneous models." The OLMo3 experiment switches both agents to OLMo3 to test whether the method survives a change of architecture; it does not bridge Qwen to OLMo. The thought's reading that "some incompatibilities may be shallow" holds within a model and says nothing about the case ARC-139 is about, where two independently trained systems have different geometry by construction. That difficulty is absent here.

Mixed at 0.5: a clean, well-controlled demonstration of one rung, and no evidence either way on cross-system legibility.
