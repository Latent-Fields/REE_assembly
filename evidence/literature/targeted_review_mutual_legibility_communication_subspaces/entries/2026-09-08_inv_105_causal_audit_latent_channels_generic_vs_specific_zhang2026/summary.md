# Zhang & Emu 2026 -- a second, independent audit: generic channel effects versus example-specific content

arXiv 2607.26773, 29 July 2026, cs.AI; the PDF carries an AAAI 2027 copyright line, so it may be accepted there, but I bank it as a preprint. Zhang and Emu run a causal audit of latent communication on LatentMAS with Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B across GSM8K, ARC-C and MATH-500. Four message settings -- no message, an other-example message generated through the same interface and length-matched, a compute-matched self-generated message, and the current-example message -- support five measurements that separate message presence, message identity, the value of example-specific content, and the additional value of a separate agent.

The verified headline is the decomposition. On GSM8K the Qwen3-4B overall effect of -1.00 percentage point "decomposes into a -6.17-point effect retained by an other-example message and a +5.17-point effect attributable to example-specific content; both component directions reverse at 8B". On MATH-500 the 4B gain of 15.00 points is 8.33 from the generic channel and 6.67 from example-specific content, while at 8B the generic component dominates. A null aggregate hid two large opposed components; the same aggregate at another scale hid a different split.

For INV-105 this is corroboration from a second group of the same standard Cheng, Das and Ramnath arrived at, which is what turns a control from one paper's preference into a norm. It also adds a rung REE should take seriously: the self-substitution comparison shows that example-specific content and other-agent value are distinct, because a receiver can gain as much from its own compute-matched latent as from another agent's. "The sender supplied it" therefore needs its own control, separate from the mismatched-pair test.

Limits: small cells (40 to 100 examples, often one seed), one released system, LLM maths benchmarks. What transfers is the decomposition logic, not any number. Supports at 0.68.
