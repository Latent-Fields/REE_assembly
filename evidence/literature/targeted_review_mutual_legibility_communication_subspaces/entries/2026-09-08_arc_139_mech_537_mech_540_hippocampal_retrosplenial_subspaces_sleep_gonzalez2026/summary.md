# Gonzalez et al. 2026 -- rotated subspaces and a plasticity-stability split across interfaces in sleep

Verified exactly as cited: Nature 655(8121):192-201, DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10481-z, PMID 42129569, PMC13317024, online 13 May 2026; the December 2025 bioRxiv version is a separate PubMed record (41509324). Gonzalez and colleagues from the Buzsaki lab recorded up to 1,024 channels across DG, CA3, CA2, CA1 and retrosplenial cortex in behaving mice and used partial CCA -- CCA that accounts for a measured third area -- to find low-dimensional communication subspaces. The structural findings are the ones the thought reports: membership is biased to deep sublayers and higher-firing, burstier cells; the same CA1 pool participates in different subspaces for different partners; and those subspaces are rotated relative to one another, with principal angles approaching orthogonality.

That is direct support for ARC-139's federation picture -- a private population, several context-specific interfaces over it -- and for MECH-537's premise that the consumer-facing subspace is a rotated, non-dominant subset of the sender. I have scored both as supports.

MECH-540 is where care is needed, and the claim's own notes already model the right posture. The abstract's sentence is: reactivation of CA1-CA3 subspaces, but not CA1-RSC, during post-experience sleep correlated with replay, "reflecting a plasticity-stability balance in the input-output transformation". The full text adds that CA1-CA3 subspace correlation rises during post-maze NREM and especially during ripples, that replay is likelier when it is high, that CA1-RSC does not show this, and that CA1-RSC weights are more correlated pre-to-post -- "stronger plasticity for intrahippocampal compared to hippocampal-neocortical communication". So sleep acts on interfaces differentially. That is the adjacent, weaker result MECH-540 claims as motivation, and it is real.

But two things must not be over-read. First, the paper measures subspace correlation, principal angles and replay sequence scores; it never computes anything like a bridge-complexity metric, so it cannot be cited for sleep reducing L(A->B). The campaign's own statement that this remains a REE hypothesis is confirmed. Second, the CA1-RSC story is not simply "stable": the text reports post-sleep CA1-RSC subspaces as more aligned with the maze subspace by weight overlap while projected correlations fell relative to pre-maze, and high CA1-RSC correlation during ripples went with lower replay fidelity. That last point is MECH-540's adverse signature S4 in biological dress -- more cross-system alignment is not automatically better -- and it is worth keeping in view when the sleep assay is eventually designed.
