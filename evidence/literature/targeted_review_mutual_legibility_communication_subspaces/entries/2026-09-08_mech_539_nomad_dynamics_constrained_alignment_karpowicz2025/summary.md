# Karpowicz et al. 2025 -- NoMAD: alignment is more stable when dynamics constrain it

One correction first. The thought's bibliography lists this as "Nature Biomedical Engineering / open manuscript record, 2025". It is Nature Communications 16:4662, DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-59652-y, PMID 40389429, PMC12089531, published 19 May 2025; the 2022 bioRxiv is its preprint. The claim-level notes for MECH-539 cite only "NoMAD (Karpowicz et al.)" and are not wrong, but the location-doc bibliography should be corrected by whoever next edits it.

The paper's argument is the one MECH-539 borrows. Intracortical BCI decoders drift as the recorded population changes; unsupervised manifold-alignment methods fix this by mapping new-day activity back onto an old-day manifold, but the existing ones "treat each time step as an independent sample and do not account for latent dynamics". NoMAD instead fits an LFADS model of latent dynamics on Day 0, freezes it, and trains a small alignment network so that Day K activity produces generator-state distributions matching Day 0 (KL between fitted Gaussians, plus Poisson reconstruction). The effect is large: on an isometric force task over 95 days, median R2 0.91 with a 208-day half-life and no failures, against 0.65/77 days for ADAN and 0.59/45 days with 51 failures for aligned factor analysis; the reaching dataset shows the same ordering.

For MECH-539 this is clean motivation. The claim says pointwise state correspondence across an interface is insufficient between predictive systems, because a bridge can match states and still destroy transition geometry. NoMAD's comparators are pointwise alignments, and they are the ones that decay. The registered REE diagnostic -- map the initial state only and let the receiver run natively, versus re-mapping every step -- is the same distinction viewed from the receiver's side.

The transfer limit is exactly the interesting one. NoMAD aligns a population to itself across days against a single fixed dynamics model. MECH-539 is about a mapping between two systems that each have their own dynamics and, in REE, are both still learning. The authors' own limitations say the method assumes a stable manifold, consistent behaviour, and has only been tested on single behaviours. So this shows that dynamics-constrained alignment is better than pointwise alignment when a shared generator exists; it does not show that cross-system dynamic compatibility can be achieved when one does not. Supports, 0.6.
