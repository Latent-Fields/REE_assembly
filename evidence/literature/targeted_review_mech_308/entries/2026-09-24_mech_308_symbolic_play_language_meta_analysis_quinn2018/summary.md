Quinn, Donnelly and Kidd pooled 35 studies that measured both symbolic play and language in young children. They report a significant small-to-medium association (r around .35) that held across ages 1 to 6, across comprehension and production measures, and across concurrent and longitudinal designs.

For MECH-308 this is the most direct quantitative support available for the claim that language tracks play maturation. The stability of the effect across design type matters: a longitudinal association is at least compatible with play maturation preceding language gains, which is what the claim's "tracking" language needs.

It does not reach the claim's stronger content. The claim says language is a game instantiated inside play_mode, not a parallel system. A moderate correlation is equally well explained by a shared representational capacity (the classic Bates-era "common symbolic underpinning" view), by shared caregiver input, or by general cognitive level. The meta-analysis also does not test the specificity clause of acceptance criterion A1: whether play milestones track language better than motor or sensory milestones.

Only abstract-level summaries were retrieved in this session (publisher and preprint pages were blocked), so moderator details and the exact confidence interval are not checked. Confidence 0.52: good evidence for correlation, weak evidence for the mechanism.
