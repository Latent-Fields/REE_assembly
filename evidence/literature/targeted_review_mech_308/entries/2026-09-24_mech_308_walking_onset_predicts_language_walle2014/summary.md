Walle and Campos tracked 44 infants every two weeks from 10 to 13.5 months and found that starting to walk was associated with a significant increase in both receptive and productive vocabulary, independent of age. A second, age-matched comparison of crawlers and walkers at 12.5 months replicated the difference, and a naturalistic observation found that social-environment factors predicted language only among walkers.

MECH-308's acceptance criterion A1 needs language milestones to track play milestones more strongly than motor or sensory milestones at matched ages. This paper shows a motor milestone with an age-independent language link, so "language correlates with play" is not by itself distinctive; a motor milestone does it too. A head-to-head comparison would be needed.

The paper does not set motor against play. The authors' own account leans toward walking changing the infant's social interactions, and MECH-308 could read that as locomotion opening more play and joint-frame episodes. So this is a specificity challenge, not a refutation.
