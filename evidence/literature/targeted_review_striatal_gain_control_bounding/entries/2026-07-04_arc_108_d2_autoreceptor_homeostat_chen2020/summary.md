# The bound the raw scalar was missing: the D2 autoreceptor homeostat

Chen, Ferris & Wang (2020), *Pharmacology & Therapeutics*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pharmthera.2020.107583)

The V3-EXQ-711 autopsy listed one dependency as explicitly unresolved: "striatal gain-control / DA-homeostasis bounding (lit not yet pulled)." This review is that dependency, named and characterised. The dopamine D2 autoreceptor (D2AR), on the somatodendritic and terminal compartments of DA neurons, provides *tonic negative-feedback control* over dopamine neuron firing, DA synthesis, reuptake and release. It is, functionally, a homeostat: it holds extracellular dopamine within a set-point band by clamping the very drive that would push it out. That is precisely the object ASG-3 abstracts — "the tonic-DA-homeostasis loop that would set the bound endogenously."

The mapping onto our repair is close to one-to-one at the level of function. Our bounded parity controller replaces an unbounded multiply with a controller that seeks a *parity setpoint* with actuator saturation — a configured `parity_ceiling_ratio` plus an anti-windup `M_cross` clamp. The biology's version of "seek a setpoint with saturation" is exactly a negative-feedback autoreceptor loop: raise the drive and the loop pushes back proportionally, keeping the output bounded. Chen et al. make the failure direction explicit too — dysregulation of this feedback disrupts DA homeostasis and is implicated in substance-use vulnerability. Remove the negative feedback and the system loses its band. This is what lets me rewrite ASG-3 from "honest simplification" to a *grounded* divergence: the FUNCTION we preserve (a homeostatically-bounded, parity-restoring modulation) is a real, named neural primitive; what we abstract away is only its *endogenous, emergent* implementation (a distributed multi-stage feedback) in favour of a configured ratio. That is ARC-106's load-bearing-not-decorative test passed on the nose — the grounding is a mechanism, not a naming flourish.

There is a second, sharper reading that feeds the ASG-4 question. The autoreceptor homeostat acts at *multiple stages* — firing, synthesis, reuptake, release — a distributed, graded negative feedback. Our current bound is a single hard box on `M_cross`. The biology suggests that a one-point hard clamp is a coarser instrument than the distributed soft feedback it stands in for, and predicts our clamp will be more brittle at the edges than a soft, multi-stage normalization would be. That is a genuine design signal for whether the hard clamp should later give way to a soft normalizer (the ASG-4 escape hatch), not just a citation.

Direction *supports*: the biological bound the ASG-3 row assumes demonstrably exists and is a tonic negative-feedback loop. I have held confidence at 0.7 rather than higher because this is a molecular/pharmacological review of the D2AR over dopamine *transmission* generally — it does not itself measure that this autoreceptor loop is what bounds the ascending *spiral gain* specifically (that inference comes from composing this homeostat with the Belin/Haber spiral anatomy), and REE's controller bounds a cross-loop *weight* whereas the D2AR bounds DA *concentration* — a function-level, not variable-level, correspondence. Source quality high, mapping fidelity high, transfer risk modest.
