# Where the two bounds meet: dopamine grades the normalizer

Kohnomi, Ebihara & Kobayashi (2016), *Neuroscience Letters*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neulet.2016.10.049)

The two bounding pillars — the tonic-DA homeostat (ASG-3) and the striatal lateral-inhibition normalizer (ASG-4) — read as if they were independent knobs in the divergence ledger. This paper shows they are not, and that is its whole value. Using dual whole-cell recordings of unitary IPSCs between medium-spiny-neuron pairs in the rat nucleus accumbens shell, Kohnomi et al. show that dopamine *grades* the strength of MSN→MSN lateral inhibition in a concentration-dependent, stepwise fashion: D1 receptors carry the suppression at low dopamine, with D2 and then D3 recruited as dopamine rises, progressively disinhibiting MSN activity toward maximal mesolimbic drive. So the normalizer that bounds loop competition (pillar 2) is itself dopamine-gain-modulated (pillar 1). The bound is a *coupled* system, not two constants.

For REE this directly informs the ASG-4 successor question. If a soft normalization is ever to replace the hard `M_cross` box, this paper argues its strength should not be fixed — it should be coupled to the same dopaminergic signal that drives the ascending gain, because that is how the biology wires it. The controller REE built treats the ASG-3 setpoint and the ASG-4 clamp as separate configured scalars; Kohnomi et al. say the faithful version co-tunes them from a shared drive. That is a concrete design constraint for V3-EXQ-713-and-beyond, registered here so it is not lost.

But I have marked the direction *mixed*, and the reason is important enough to be the headline caveat rather than a footnote. The dopaminergic effect here is *disinhibitory* — higher dopamine *reduces* lateral inhibition. That cuts against a naive "more dopamine = more bounding" story. What it actually says is subtler and, I think, more interesting: dopamine simultaneously *drives* the ascending spiral (pushing a channel up) and *relaxes* the striatal normalizer (releasing competition), so the healthy bound is a *balance* struck between drive and disinhibition, not a monotone brake. That is very likely part of how the real system trades competition (keep options open) against commitment (let one channel win) — which is the exact trade-off the whole conversion-ceiling programme is circling. It means REE should not model "add dopamine → add bound"; it should model a balance, and getting that balance wrong in either direction is a candidate failure mode.

Confidence 0.58: solid paired-recording electrophysiology, but a short *Neuroscience Letters* report, specific to the accumbens shell, and the coupling REE would actually need is inferred from the pharmacology rather than tested in a selection task.
