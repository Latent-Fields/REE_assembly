# The same loop, in both regimes: homeostat and runaway

Gowrishankar, Gresch, Davis, Katamish, Riele, Stewart, Vaughan, Hahn & Blakely (2018), *Journal of Neuroscience*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0055-18.2018)

Where Chen 2020 gives the homeostat as a review-level principle, this paper shows both regimes of the loop — bounded and runaway — inside one experimental system, and the runaway has exactly the dynamical signature of the 711 blow-up. In wild-type dorsal striatum, tonic D2-autoreceptor activation constrains presynaptic dopamine by driving dopamine-transporter (DAT) phosphorylation and surface trafficking and by controlling tyrosine hydroxylase — the negative-feedback bound doing its job. In the neuropsychiatric-disease-associated DAT variant Val559, the *same* tonic D2AR activation flips sign: it drives surface expression of efflux-prone transporters, which raises extracellular dopamine, which — in the authors' words — *overwhelms DAT-mediated DA clearance capacity*. A homeostatic loop, deprived of its saturating constraint, becomes a positive-feedback amplifier that swamps the downstream normalizer.

That is the 711 failure drawn in wet biology. Our pre-repair ascending `M_cross` maturation was a plastic positive-feedback loop with no saturation — the three-factor update kept compounding through the eligibility trace until `M_cross` range hit 4897.8, five orders of magnitude above the un-gained baseline. Gowrishankar et al. give the biological instance of precisely this shape: tonic drive → plastic amplifier (transporter trafficking) → rising output → more drive, with the clearance capacity overrun. The anti-windup `M_cross` clamp we added (ASG-4) is, functionally, the saturation this loop is missing in the Val559 state. And the pairing with the healthy dorsal-striatal case makes the deeper 709→711 point concrete: it is *one loop with two regimes*, and the difference between "bounds" and "runs away" is whether the saturating constraint is intact — which is exactly why the fix is a controller, not another gain-magnitude guess.

One detail earns its own note for the successor design. The bound-vs-runaway outcome here is *region-specific* — dorsal striatum shows the Val559 runaway, ventral does not, because the D2AR regulatory networks differ between regions. Translated: whether a homeostatic loop stays bounded can depend on loop-specific parameters. That is a live hint that REE's parity controller might eventually need per-loop (limbic vs motor) bounding constants rather than a single global `parity_ceiling_ratio` — worth holding as an open question for the V3-EXQ-713 successor, not acting on now.

I have marked this *mixed* rather than *supports*. It supports the existence of the tonic homeostatic bound, but its actual headline is the *pathological* runaway in a disease variant — so it is doing double duty as the cautionary failure-signature as much as positive support. And the honesty caveats matter: the runaway is engineered by a specific transporter mutation in adolescent male mice, region- and sex-specific; mapping its presynaptic-efflux positive feedback onto our cross-loop-weight maturation is an analogy of *loop dynamics* (positive feedback + no saturation → blow-up), not of substrate. Confidence 0.62 — strong mechanism and a near-perfect dynamical match, discounted for the disease-variant specificity and the substrate gap.
