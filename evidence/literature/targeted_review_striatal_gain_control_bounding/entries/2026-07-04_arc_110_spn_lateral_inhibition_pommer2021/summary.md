# The soft normalizer the hard clamp stands in for

Pommer, Akamine, Schiffmann, de Kerchove d'Exaerde & Wickens (2021), *Journal of Neuroscience*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1037-20.2021)

This entry grounds the *other* bounding pillar — the one ASG-4 abstracts. The 711 repair added two bounds: a forward parity ceiling (the ASG-3 setpoint, grounded by the autoreceptor literature) and a hard box clamp on the ascending `M_cross` entries. ASG-4 flags that hard box as a divergence: the biology uses "soft striatal lateral-inhibition normalization," not a box. Pommer et al. show that soft normalizer exists and is real machinery. Using whole-cell recording with optogenetic activation of presynaptic neurons in mouse striatal slices, they demonstrate that spiny projection neurons (SPNs) — the principal striatal cells — make inhibitory GABAergic synapses onto *each other* via axon collaterals, forming a genuine local lateral-inhibition network spanning both direct- and indirect-pathway SPNs. And that network is *tunable*: activating 5-HT1B receptors scales down the strength of SPN→SPN inhibition (without touching the fast-spiking-interneuron inhibition), so serotonin sets a gain on the lateral-inhibition normalizer.

Two things follow for the ledger. First, ASG-4 can move from "honest simplification" to a grounded divergence with a named substrate: the biological bound on one channel dominating is a distributed, mutually-inhibitory (divisive-normalization-flavoured) network among the competing projection neurons, and our hard `M_cross` box is a coarse stand-in for it. Second — and this is the useful part for the roadmap — the divergence row already anticipates that "a soft normalization can replace the clamp if the biology demands graded bounding." This paper is direct evidence *for* that escape hatch: the biological normalizer is soft and, moreover, gain-tunable, which a fixed hard clamp cannot emulate. If the V3-EXQ-713 line ever shows the box is brittle at the edges, this is the design to reach for.

I have kept the direction *supports* but confidence only 0.6, and the reason is a caveat the authors themselves put front and centre: the *functional* role of SPN–SPN lateral inhibition in action selection is, in their words, "currently unknown." So what this paper establishes is the substrate and its tunability — the mechanism ASG-4 abstracts is real and modulable — not a demonstrated causal claim that lateral inhibition is what prevents loop runaway. There is also a topological mismatch to be honest about: this is *intra*-striatal, within-arena inhibition between projection neurons, whereas REE's clamp bounds a *cross*-loop ascending weight. The analogy is functional (competitive normalization that keeps one unit from dominating its neighbours) rather than a one-to-one wiring correspondence. Source quality high (J Neurosci, Wickens lab); mapping fidelity moderate for those two reasons; transfer risk moderate.
