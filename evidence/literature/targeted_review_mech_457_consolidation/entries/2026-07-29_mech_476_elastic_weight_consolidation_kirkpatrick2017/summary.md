# Kirkpatrick et al. (2017) -- Elastic Weight Consolidation: trace-selectivity in REE's own idiom

Where the three neuroscience entries establish the *definition* (Krakauer), the *dissociation and its sleep-bound divergence* (Walker), and the *behavioural-tagging third arm and synapse-specific target* (Moncada; Bin Ibrahim), this paper closes the loop back to REE's actual substrate. Kirkpatrick et al.'s Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC), published in PNAS ([DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1611835114](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1611835114)), is a neural-network mechanism, so the mapping is ML-to-ML rather than biology-to-ML, and the central contrast MECH-476 draws becomes exact rather than analogical.

EWC adds a quadratic penalty that anchors each parameter to its value at the end of the previous task, with a per-parameter stiffness proportional to that parameter's *importance*, estimated by the diagonal of the Fisher information matrix. Important weights are held rigid; unimportant ones stay plastic. The network thereby retains earlier tasks while continuing to learn -- overcoming catastrophic forgetting without freezing the whole model. Read against REE, this is illuminating in a specific way: REE's landed retention lever, the global KL anchor to the installed BC snapshot (V3-EXQ-792), is precisely the *undifferentiated limit* of EWC -- what you get if you set every parameter's stiffness to the same constant. EWC is what happens when you make that stiffness trace-selective. So the biology's synapse-specific STC (Bin Ibrahim) and REE's global KL anchor are not two unrelated worlds; EWC sits between them as the computational realisation of "protect the parameters that carry the competence, let the rest move."

This bears directly on two of MECH-476's assertions. First, the claim that retention is supplied by the *update constraint*, not by continued demonstration: EWC is an update-side constraint and nothing else -- no replayed data, no continued imitation -- and it retains prior tasks, which is a clean ML corroboration of REE's V3-EXQ-789 result that the imitation-auxiliary schedule axis does not carry retention. Second, the claim that trace-selective protection is a strictly richer, *buildable* target than a single global coefficient: EWC demonstrates exactly that in neural networks, and it does so with better retention than a uniform penalty. That makes the "global KL cannot express which traces are protected" complaint concrete and constructive -- there is a known, published way to make the constraint selective.

The caveat is real and I have made it a failure signature rather than burying it. EWC assumes discrete tasks with identifiable boundaries and a stationary previous optimum from which to compute Fisher importance. REE's BC-install-then-RL-erode is one continuing task with a moving objective and no clean boundary at which to snapshot importance. So the *concept* -- importance-weighted, per-parameter, update-side consolidation -- transfers as a design target, but a literal EWC port is non-trivial, and EWC's diagonal-Fisher approximation is itself imperfect and known to degrade over long task sequences. Adopting it in REE would trade the global-KL crudeness for a different, approximation-bound one, not reproduce STC's synapse-exact selectivity. Confidence is 0.72: high source quality (a landmark, much-cited result), high conceptual mapping fidelity because it is REE's own kind of object, tempered by the task-boundary assumption REE's single-task erosion violates. It supports the *direction* of MECH-476 -- trace-selective, constraint-supplied retention is real and superior -- without implying REE can pick EWC up unchanged.
