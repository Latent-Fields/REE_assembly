# Bin Ibrahim, Wang & Sajikumar (2024) -- what trace-selective consolidation would have to be

This 2024 review in *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* ([DOI: 10.1098/rstb.2023.0237](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2023.0237)) is the current authoritative statement of the synaptic tagging and capture (STC) hypothesis, and it is the entry that grounds the most distinctive -- and least comfortable -- piece of MECH-476's content: trace selectivity. STC says cellular consolidation is *synapse-specific*. An induced synapse sets a local molecular "tag"; that tag captures diffusible plasticity-related products (PRPs) synthesised elsewhere; and late-phase, protein-synthesis-dependent plasticity is therefore conferred selectively on the synapses that were tagged. Crucially, tag-setting and PRP-synthesis are *separable* operations, and neuromodulation gates the availability of the PRPs.

The reason this matters for MECH-476 is a direct line from the V3-EXQ-792 autopsy, which flagged that a single global KL coefficient cannot express *which* traces are protected. Bin Ibrahim et al. tell us what the biology does instead: it protects specific traces via local tags, so consolidating one memory does not indiscriminately shield every other synapse. REE's landed protection mechanism -- a KL anchor to the installed BC snapshot -- is the opposite in the one respect that counts here. It is a single divergence penalty applied across the whole policy/value parameterisation. It can say "don't move far from the snapshot," but it cannot say "keep *this* foraging competence and let *that* stale habit decay." STC is therefore the reference for what trace-selective consolidation would have to look like, and by extension it measures how crude REE's current mechanism remains *even when it works*. This is why the entry supports the claim while simultaneously being uncomfortable for it: MECH-476's SUPPORTED verdict on the dose/interval arms would establish that REE has *a* consolidation process, but Bin Ibrahim makes clear it would still be a globally-scoped one, and trace-selectivity would stay an unbuilt target.

I want to be careful not to overstate the mapping. STC is a cellular and synaptic hypothesis about hippocampal and cortical plasticity. REE has no synapses, no tags, and no diffusible PRPs. The correspondence is conceptual -- local and selective versus global and undifferentiated -- and it is a hypothesis review rather than a single decisive measurement. So the value of this entry is to *name the gap precisely* and to warn against a specific overreach: mapping any one REE lever onto "consolidation" conflates tag-setting with resource-supply, two things STC shows are separable and independently failable. I set confidence at 0.64. Source quality is high (current, from a leading STC group), but the fidelity is to the *target and the divergence*, not to a REE result, and I do not want the index to read this as evidence that REE has, or can cheaply acquire, trace-selective consolidation. It cannot -- and saying so clearly is the point of including the paper.
