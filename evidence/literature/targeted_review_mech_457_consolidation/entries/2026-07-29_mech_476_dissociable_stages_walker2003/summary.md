# Walker, Brakefield, Hobson & Stickgold (2003) -- the dissociation, and where the mapping stops

According to PubMed ([DOI: 10.1038/nature01930](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01930)), Walker and colleagues showed that human motor-skill memory does not simply "strengthen" after acquisition -- it moves through at least three dissociable stages, with wake and sleep making distinct contributions, and with a twist that is the paper's real signature: reactivating a consolidated memory in the waking state returns it to a *labile* condition that then has to reconsolidate. For MECH-476 this cuts two ways at once, which is why I have filed it as `mixed` rather than as supporting evidence.

On the supporting side, the paper is a strong independent instantiation of the claim's central structural assertion. MECH-476 says acquisition and retention are dissociable capabilities served by separate substrate. Walker says the post-acquisition timeline is itself decomposable into stages that respond to different manipulations -- consolidation, reconsolidation, and the wake/sleep contributions to each. That is the same shape of argument: the thing that happens *after* you learn is not one process but several, and you can knock one out while leaving another intact. It also re-uses the interference-resistance operationalisation that Krakauer formalised, so the two papers reinforce each other on the definition MECH-476 adopts.

On the diverging side -- and this is the part I want the governance record to be honest about -- Walker localises the consolidation that matters to *sleep*, not to elapsed time. Overnight sleep produced gains that an equivalent interval of waking did not. REE has no sleep and no replay; its demonstrated protection pathways (the distributional critic of V3-EXQ-788, the KL anchor of V3-EXQ-792) are awake, online, and continuous. So when MECH-476's falsifier varies an "A->B interval" of offline optimisation steps, it is *not* varying the same variable Walker varied. A positive REE interval effect would be a mechanism-divergent, weaker analog of biological consolidation; a negative one would not contradict Walker at all, because REE simply lacks the sleep-stage structure his interval indexed. The reconsolidation finding -- reactivation re-opening a labile window -- has no REE analog whatsoever today.

This is precisely the divergence MECH-476 was asked to record: REE's protection is awake / online / trace-undifferentiated, whereas the biology's is sleep- and replay-dependent and, per Walker, cyclically re-labile. I have written both halves into the failure signatures so that a future reader cannot mistake a REE interval result for confirmation of the biological process, in either direction. Confidence is 0.6: the study itself is first-rate and robust, but its load-bearing mechanistic content is the content REE does not share, so the mapping fidelity is deliberately held low. Its worth to the dossier is as much in marking the boundary of the transfer as in supporting the dissociation.
