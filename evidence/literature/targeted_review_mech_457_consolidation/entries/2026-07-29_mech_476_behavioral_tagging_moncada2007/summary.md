# Moncada & Viola (2007) -- the empirical basis for the novelty-paired third arm

According to PubMed ([DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1083-07.2007](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1083-07.2007)), Moncada and Viola gave rats a *weak* inhibitory-avoidance training that on its own produced only short-term memory, and then showed that a single exploration of a novel environment close in time to that training was enough to convert it into a lasting long-term memory. Exposure to a familiar environment did nothing. The rescue required D1/D5 dopamine receptor activation and new protein synthesis in the dorsal hippocampus -- the signature of a plasticity-related-product capture process. This is the behavioural incarnation of synaptic tagging and capture, and it is the paper MECH-476 points at when it proposes a third experimental arm.

The reason this arm is worth building, and worth building cheaply, is that it is the sharpest discriminator in the whole falsifier between the two live hypotheses. MECH-476's failure mode is that "retention" turns out to be nothing more than a concurrent *regulariser* -- a KL coefficient that damps updates -- in which case the claim should collapse back into MECH-459/460. Behavioural tagging offers a prediction that a regulariser simply cannot make. A regulariser has no reason to care about an unrelated novelty exposure; there is nothing in "penalise divergence from the snapshot" that says a nearby dose of novelty should rescue an otherwise-decaying trace. So if a sub-threshold BC install becomes durable *only* when it is paired with REE's novelty drive inside the window, that is positive evidence for a genuine capture-like consolidation process and against the bare-regulariser reading. REE already owns the signal it needs: the landed RND exploration drive can play the role of Moncada's novel environment.

The caveat I have to be careful about is that the *mechanism* Moncada identified is specifically dopaminergic and protein-synthesis-dependent, with strict temporal contiguity between the weak trace and the novelty. REE's RND drive is an exploration bonus in the reward stream, not a protein-synthesis gate that captures plasticity-related products. So the transfer here is at the level of the behavioural *signature* -- weak trace plus nearby novelty yields durability -- not at the level of the capture machinery. This matters for how we read a null. If the REE novelty-paired arm shows no rescue, that could mean tagging is not the right target, or it could mean REE lacks any capture mechanism for the tag to exploit even though the tag was set. The two are not distinguishable from a single null, so I have written that ambiguity into the failure signatures. I have also flagged the prerequisite that MECH-476 already insists on: if the sub-threshold install sets no learnable trace at all, the arm is uninformative and self-routes `substrate_not_ready_requeue` rather than returning a false novelty-paired null.

Confidence is 0.68. The study is foundational and pharmacologically well-controlled, and the behavioural prediction transfers cleanly and is cheap to test. I hold it below the Krakauer design-source entry because the capture mechanism is biologically specific in a way REE's novelty signal does not reproduce, so the arm's positive result would be strong but its null would be mechanism-ambiguous.
