# Krakauer, Ghez & Ghilardi (2005) -- the design source for MECH-476's falsifier

According to PubMed ([DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4218-04.2005](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4218-04.2005)), this is the paper MECH-476 leans on when it defines consolidation as *resistance to retrograde interference*. The design is the A -> B -> A paradigm: learn task A, then after a variable interval learn an interfering task B (here a counter-rotation), then re-measure A. Consolidation is present exactly when B fails to disrupt A. That operational definition -- not a warm intuition about "memory getting stronger" but a specific, falsifiable assay -- is what MECH-476 imports wholesale, and it is why the claim insists the dependent variable is a *retained-fraction trajectory* rather than terminal competence.

What makes this the right entry point rather than merely a citation of convenience is that Krakauer varied precisely the two factors MECH-476 says REE has never varied. In experiment 2, resistance to interference emerged when the counter-rotation came 24 h after the original rotation but not when it came 5 min after -- an **interval** effect. In experiment 3, doubling the amount of initial rotation learning produced resistance even at the 5-min interval -- a **dose** effect. The concluding sentence of the abstract is almost a specification for the REE experiment: "rotation learning consolidates both over time and with increased initial training." If REE's retained fraction grows with BC dose and/or offline interval, MECH-476 is SUPPORTED and the build target becomes a consolidation window; if it is invariant to both, the claim is WEAKENED and withdrawn into MECH-459/460.

The paper also hands us a methodological landmine that we would otherwise have stepped on. In experiment 1, interference persisted even at a 24 h interval -- which naively reads as "visuomotor learning does not consolidate." The correct reading, which experiment 2 established by inserting washout blocks, is that this was *anterograde* interference (task B corrupting the *retrieval* of A), not a genuine absence of consolidation. The lesson for REE is sharp: our A -> B -> A design must measure the retained A cleanly, with the RL-phase's anterograde pull on the policy removed or controlled, or an invariance result is uninterpretable -- it could be a masking artefact rather than the absence of a process. I have recorded this as a failure signature because it is the single most likely way our falsifier could return a false WEAKENED.

The mapping caveat is that this is a *design* transfer, not a *mechanism* transfer. Krakauer's substrate is human visuomotor adaptation with cerebellar and explicit-strategy components; REE's is a behavioural-cloning-then-RL agent whose retention levers are a distributional critic and a KL anchor. The claim that dose and interval are the discriminating levers transfers with high fidelity because it is a claim about the *shape* of the consolidation function, which the assay measures directly. The physiology does not transfer and we should not pretend it does. I set confidence at 0.82 -- above the component mean -- because the paper's job here is to license the assay and establish that dose and interval are real consolidation levers in at least one biological system, and on that narrow, load-bearing point it is about as authoritative as the literature gets.
