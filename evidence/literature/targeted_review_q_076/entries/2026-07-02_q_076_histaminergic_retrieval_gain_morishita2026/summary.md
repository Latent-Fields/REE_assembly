# Morishita et al. (2026) — an infraslow histaminergic gain on retrieval

**Claim tested:** Q-076 — does the *interaction* of retrieval-readiness gain (MECH-425 Line A) and content-selective suppression (MECH-425 Line B) govern the interference-vs-forgetting tradeoff?

According to PubMed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.05.019)), this is the founding paper behind the whole MECH-425 intake — the "infraslow histaminergic dynamics" study that prompted Daniel's 2026-06-12 note. Morishita and colleagues show, in mice, that spontaneous pre-cue activity of histaminergic neurons in the tuberomammillary nucleus (TMN) fluctuates on an infraslow (0.05–0.1 Hz) timescale that tracks an integrated brain–body state, and that this state *sets how strongly an identical cue reinstates its associated memory*. Closed-loop cue delivery during high-histaminergic states enhances memory expression; brief optogenetic activation or inhibition just before the cue bidirectionally moves expression up or down; and direct activation of histaminergic terminals in the basolateral amygdala (BLA) is sufficient to enhance expression, while inhibition blunts the cue-evoked BLA population response.

For Q-076 this anchors *Line A* — the global, slowly-fluctuating, arousal-coupled reinstatement-gain layer — as biologically real and causal, not merely a modelling convenience. Memory expression fluctuates even for identical cues, and a single upstream state variable explains a large part of that fluctuation. That is precisely the "reinstatement gain" knob MECH-425 wants to expose as an explicit retrieval-time policy.

The reason this is a *partial* supports (confidence 0.56) rather than a strong one is that Q-076 is fundamentally an *interaction* question, and this paper supplies only one of the two factors. The histaminergic gain demonstrated here is content-blind: it scales the cue-evoked response and the memory expression as a whole. That is exactly Q-076's Line-A *main effect* — clamp the gain low and you get uniform under-reinstatement (apparent forgetting); raise it without any selective suppression and you get the "problematic interference" corner where everything becomes accessible at once. The paper does not demonstrate the content-selective suppression arm (Line B), and its single-trace behavioural readout cannot score competing co-encoded memories individually, so interference versus correct-trace reinstatement cannot be dissociated within this dataset.

There is one subtlety worth recording for governance. The MECH-425 notes observe (via Nomura 2019) that the histaminergic gain itself carries a context-dependent suppress arm — H1 facilitates, H2 inhibits, depending on the cue's reinforcement history. If that is right, Line A is not a purely monotone gain and the clean "Line A = gain, Line B = suppression" factorisation Q-076 assumes may be partly confounded inside the histaminergic system itself. That is a caution for how REE parameterises the two knobs: the biology suggests the gain and its suppress arm may be entangled rather than orthogonal. Either way, this paper grounds the existence and causal potency of the reinstatement-gain factor; the interaction with a separable competitor-suppression operator remains substrate-conditional on the MECH-425 v4 retrieval substrate.
