# Norman, Newman, Detre & Polyn (2006) — inhibitory oscillations as a strengthen/punish switch

**Claim tested:** Q-076 — does the *interaction* of retrieval-readiness gain (MECH-425 Line A) and content-selective suppression (MECH-425 Line B) govern the interference-vs-forgetting tradeoff?

According to PubMed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco.2006.18.7.1577)), Norman and colleagues present a learning algorithm in which oscillations in the *strength of global inhibition* do double duty. When inhibition is raised, the weakly-supported parts of a target memory fall below threshold and are exposed — the network then strengthens them. When inhibition is lowered, competitors that share features with the target intrude above threshold and are exposed — the network then weakens them. The sign of the Contrastive-Hebbian weight update flips with the phase of the inhibitory oscillation, so a single control axis produces both target consolidation and competitor punishment. They show the algorithm can store large numbers of correlated patterns without catastrophic collapse and still generalise.

This is the closest thing in the literature to a *working* demonstration of Q-076's core intuition. Daniel's source intuition for Q-076 was that "access to all memories could produce problematic interference; active management of which cues are retrievable would be needed" — i.e., that reinstatement gain without selective suppression is the failure corner. Norman et al. make exactly that mechanism explicit: the *level* of competitive inhibition is the axis that decides whether you retrieve the target or forget its competitors, and the interference-vs-forgetting outcome is a function of where on that axis the system sits. That is the interaction term Q-076 predicts, instantiated rather than merely asserted.

The mapping is mechanistically strong but structurally analogical, which is why I hold confidence at 0.62 despite high source quality. Two gaps matter. First, Norman et al. realise the strengthen/punish tradeoff as successive *phases of one oscillating inhibition parameter*, whereas MECH-425 factors retrieval control into two composable layers — a global reinstatement-gain scalar (Line A) and a separate content-selective suppression operator (Line B). If REE implements those as two independent static knobs, the Norman model supports the *principle* that inhibition level governs the tradeoff but does not validate the specific 2×2 factorisation. Second, the algorithm operates at learning/encoding time, while Q-076's predicted regimes are stated over retrieval-time reinstatement of already-stored competing traces. The read-side dissociation is therefore inferred from an encoding-side model.

For governance: this entry supports Q-076's *existence* claim — that a single competitive-inhibition mechanism can produce both retrieval and forgetting depending on its setting — without being able to run the retrieval-time (gain high/low) × (suppression on/off) design that would actually confirm the interaction *term* dominates the main effects. That design remains substrate-conditional on the MECH-425 v4 retrieval substrate (both knobs exposed, ≥2 co-encoded competing traces scorable individually). The paper is the strongest available theoretical prior for expecting the interaction Q-076 describes, and a caution that the discriminating signature may live in the *dynamics* of competition rather than in two cleanly separable scalars.
