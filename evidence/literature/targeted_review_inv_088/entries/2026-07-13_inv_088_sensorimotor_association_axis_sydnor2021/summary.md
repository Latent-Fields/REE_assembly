# Sydnor et al. 2021 (Neuron) — the sensorimotor-to-association axis of cortical maturation

**Claim tested:** INV-088 — *E3's z_world-reading evaluators (harm_eval, benefit_eval, goal/trajectory scoring) are strictly bounded by E1's z_world representational differentiation.*
**Direction:** supports · **Confidence:** 0.72

## What the paper did

This is a synthesis review, not a single experiment, but it is the definitive statement of a framework I keep leaning on: the human cortex does not mature uniformly. Development runs along an *evolutionarily rooted sensorimotor-to-association axis* — primary and unimodal sensory/motor cortices reach maturity early, while higher-order transmodal association cortices (the executive, socioemotional, mentalizing machinery) remain plastic and keep maturing into the third decade. The authors assemble structural, functional, and microstructural neuroimaging evidence across childhood and adolescence to show this is a single, coherent spatiotemporal program, and they link it to the hierarchical unfolding of plasticity-related neurobiology.

## Why it bears on INV-088

INV-088 is, stripped down, a developmental ordering claim with a functional consequence: you cannot productively train a world/goal evaluator on a world-representation that has not yet differentiated. The evaluator will fit noise. Sydnor et al. give me the biological existence proof for the *ordering* half of that. The world-reading representation (E1/z_world) is sensory-like and, in the brain, matures first; the evaluator (E3, harm/benefit/goal scoring) is association/PFC-like and matures last. Crucially, the review argues the staggered timing is not incidental — it exists *to enhance feature variation* between lower- and higher-order regions. That is the same logic as INV-088: the differentiation established upstream is the raw material the late-maturing evaluator needs, and the gap is what late plasticity exploits. This dovetails with the V3-EXQ-740a IV result already in hand (task-relevant z_world differentiation rising 0.048 → 0.245 across maturation onset), which is the substrate-side echo of the same ordering.

## Limitations and honest caveats

The review earns a "supports", not a strong one, because it evidences *temporal ordering and differentiation*, not the *functional bound* INV-088 actually asserts. Nobody in this literature is training an evaluator on a deliberately under-differentiated representation and measuring how badly it noise-fits — that is the REE experiment, not the neuroimaging one. Temporal precedence (sensory matures first) is necessary but not sufficient evidence for a causal representational bound; the two could in principle be co-scheduled by an upstream clock without one gating the other. There is also a pointed complication buried in the review: the same protracted association plasticity that produces the ordering is what confers risk for developmental psychopathology. So "sensory-first" is not a clean guarantee — a poorly-differentiated-but-still-plastic association stage is a documented failure mode, which is arguably *more* consistent with INV-088's noise-fitting worry than against it.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality is high (Neuron, a framework that has held up across many groups). I weighted mapping fidelity heavily because INV-088 is an architectural claim, and set it at 0.7 — the ordering maps cleanly, the functional bound is inferred not measured. Transfer risk is real but moderate (0.35): the sensorimotor-to-association axis is deep and evolutionary, so it is more plausibly substrate-general than a task-specific finding, but the human-imaging-to-REE step plus the ordering→bound inference keep it honest. Net 0.72: a solid supporting anchor for the developmental premise, explicitly not a demonstration of the bound itself.
