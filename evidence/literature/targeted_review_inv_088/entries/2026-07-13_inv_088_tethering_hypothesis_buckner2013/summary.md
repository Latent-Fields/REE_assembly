# Buckner & Krienen 2013 (TiCS) — the tethering hypothesis

**Claim tested:** INV-088 — *E3's z_world-reading evaluators are strictly bounded by E1's z_world representational differentiation.*
**Direction:** supports · **Confidence:** 0.6

## What the paper argues

Buckner and Krienen ask a mechanistic question the Sydnor review largely takes as given: *why* do association networks mature late and behave so differently from sensory cortex? Their answer is the tethering hypothesis. Sensory hierarchies are built under strong constraints — molecular gradients and cascades of early patterned activity that pin cortical identity to sensory input. As the human cortical mantle expanded rapidly in evolution, large stretches of cortex ended up too far from those anchoring signals to be captured by them. These "untethered" regions became the distributed association networks that fill the gaps between sensory hierarchies, and — freed from the early constraints — they mature late and wire up through protracted, activity-dependent development.

## Why it bears on INV-088

Where Sydnor et al. give me the *pattern* (sensory-before-association), Buckner & Krienen give me a candidate *mechanism* for it, and mechanism is what lets an ordering claim become a dependency claim. On the reading INV-088 needs, the differentiated sensory representation (E1/z_world) is the anchored scaffold; the evaluator (association-like E3) is the untethered machinery that assembles late, on top of and around that scaffold. That is a coherent story for why productive evaluator training would trail world-schema differentiation rather than lead it. It also fits the REE architecture's own layering: z_world is the anchored, input-driven representation; harm_eval / benefit_eval / goal scoring are the later, higher-order readouts.

## The honest tension

I am giving this a *moderate* supporting confidence, not a strong one, because the central metaphor cuts both ways. "Untethered from sensory gradients" can just as easily be read as association cortex being *decoupled* from upstream sensory structure — less dependent on it, not more. If you took that reading seriously, it would tension INV-088's strict bound: an evaluator that floats free of sensory constraints need not wait on sensory differentiation at all. My mapping leans entirely on the *developmental-timing* reading (association matures late, built on top of anchored hierarchies), and deliberately does not claim the paper demonstrates tight representational dependence — it does not. It is also an evolutionary/comparative argument about network topology, with no measurement anywhere near the training dynamics of a graded-quality evaluator.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality is good (a heavily-cited TiCS review), but it is a hypothesis paper, and the mapping fidelity is the limiting factor (0.55) precisely because of the double-edged untethering framing. Transfer risk 0.4: evolutionary/cross-species reasoning stretched to a maturational-bound claim in an artificial substrate. Net 0.6 — it earns its place as the mechanistic complement to Sydnor et al., strengthening the *why* behind the ordering, while I stay explicit that "untethered" is not the same as "dependent".
