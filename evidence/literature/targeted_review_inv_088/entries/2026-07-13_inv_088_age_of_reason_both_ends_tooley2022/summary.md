# Tooley et al. 2022 (J Neurosci) — "The Age of Reason", and a caveat for INV-088

**Claim tested:** INV-088 — *E3's z_world-reading evaluators are strictly bounded by E1's z_world representational differentiation.*
**Direction:** mixed · **Confidence:** 0.55

## What the paper did

This is the counter-weight I went looking for, and I found it in a genuinely good study. Tooley et al. scanned children aged 4–10 (a notoriously hard population to image cleanly) using careful pediatric acquisition, and asked how functional brain-network architecture reorganizes across early-to-middle childhood. The headline result: with age, cortical systems become more *segregated* — attention/perceptual systems pull apart from systems supporting abstract cognition (the past, the future, other minds). The associations were strongest at the two poles of the sensory-association gradient: visual cortex on the perceptual end, medial prefrontal cortex on the abstract end. More mature architecture, controlling for age, predicted better visuospatial reasoning.

## Why I logged it against INV-088

The authors are explicit that their finding *challenges* a classical theory — that cortical maturation proceeds strictly back-to-front, sensory areas first and association areas last. They see pronounced development at *both* ends of the gradient in early childhood. That is precisely the premise INV-088 leans on when it says the world-representation (z_world, sensory-like) differentiates and only *then* can the evaluator (E3, association/PFC-like) be productively trained. If both ends are developing prominently and concurrently at ages 4–10, strict temporal precedence of world-representation over evaluator is not a safe assumption, and the developmental-timing argument for INV-088 loses some of its footing. Intellectual honesty means this belongs in the folder next to Sydnor and Buckner, not omitted because it complicates the story.

## But it does not refute the bound

Here is the careful part, and it is why this is "mixed" and not "weakens". INV-088's actual assertion is not "sensory must finish before the evaluator starts." It is a *functional bound*: evaluator quality is bounded by representational differentiation — a poorly-differentiated z_world yields a noise-fitted evaluator. That bound is entirely compatible with the two ends co-developing. You can have concurrent maturation and still have the evaluator's achievable quality gated, at every moment, by how differentiated the world-representation currently is. Tooley et al. measure functional network segregation, not evaluator readout quality as a function of differentiation, so the study simply cannot speak to the causal bound. What it *does* do is remove strict temporal ordering from the list of things I can cite as support. Reading it as a hard refutation would over-claim; filing it as no-signal would be dishonest. So: a real caveat on the ordering premise, logged as such.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality high (0.85) — sophisticated methods, a hard population, a clean and well-argued result. Mapping fidelity is the limiter (0.55): it targets the ordering premise, not the bound INV-088 actually makes. Transfer risk 0.4 (human developmental imaging → REE substrate). Net 0.55, direction "mixed" — deliberately kept in the record as the honest counter-balance to the two supporting developmental anchors, and a reminder that INV-088 should be defended as a *bound*, not as a strict maturational sequence.
