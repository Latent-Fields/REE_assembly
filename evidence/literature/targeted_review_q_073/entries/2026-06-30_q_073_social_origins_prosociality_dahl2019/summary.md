# The Social Origins of Human Prosociality (Dahl & Brownell, 2019)

*According to PubMed* — Dahl A, Brownell CA. *Current Directions in Psychological Science* 28(3):274–279. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721419830386)

## What the paper did

A synthesis review of the developmental-psychology literature on how helping — the most-studied form of prosocial behaviour — emerges over the first two years of life. The authors advance two claims. First, children's motivations and capabilities for helping change in *quality* as well as quantity: helping "begins in participatory activities without prosocial intent in the first year of life, becoming increasingly autonomous and motivated by prosocial intent over the second year." Second, helping emerges through *bidirectional social interactions*, beginning at birth, in which caregivers scaffold the behaviour and infants are active participants.

## Key findings relevant to Q-073

This is the third leg of the triangulation for Q-073. Where the Davidov entry pins down *when the distress-concern channel comes online* (early — around 3 months) and the Tallot/Sullivan entry characterises *the substrate-gating of the threat channel*, this paper characterises *the maturation of the other-bound appetitive channel* — the drive to actually confer benefit on another. Its message is that this channel matures comparatively late and gradually: in the first year, what looks like helping is participatory and not yet genuinely benefit-directed; it is only across the second year that helping becomes autonomous and prosocially *motivated*.

Read together with the other two entries, the ordering Q-073 asks about takes a recognisable shape. The channel that *registers* harm/distress to others is up and stable early; the channel that is *motivated to do good for* others consolidates later. That is the "protection-first" silhouette REE encodes in MECH-408 on the ARC-059 social spine — register harm-to-others before instantiating benefit-to-others.

## How it translates to REE — and the principled hesitation

The mapping from "prosocial helping" to REE's "other-bound liking/wanting" stream is reasonable: both are about an agent being oriented toward another's benefit. But this paper carries the most important *mechanistic* caveat of the three, and it cuts against over-reading the ordering as architecture. Dahl and Brownell's central thesis is that prosociality is *socially scaffolded* — its development is driven substantially by caregiver interaction, not by an autonomous internal clock on affective streams. So the observed lateness of the appetitive channel may be an artefact of the *curriculum* (what the social environment elicits and rewards, when) rather than evidence of an intrinsic, substrate-internal ordering. For REE that distinction is load-bearing: it is the difference between *hard-coding* the suffering-before-liking ordering into the architecture and *letting it emerge* from a developmental training curriculum on the social substrate. This paper leans toward the latter reading. There is also the first-year over-attribution risk: if REE treats an early other-bound appetitive signal as already intentional and benefit-directed, it imports structure the developmental data say is not yet present.

The paper does not test Q-073's exploitation-vulnerability hypothesis (that early positive other-reward would be gameable) at all; like the other two, it speaks to *which channel matures when*, not to *why* that ordering is adaptive.
