# Caring babies: Concern for others in distress during infancy (Davidov et al., 2020)

*According to PubMed* — Davidov M, Paz Y, Roth-Hanania R, Uzefovsky F, Orlitsky T, Mankuta D, Zahn-Waxler C. *Developmental Science* 24(2):e13016. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/desc.13016)

## What the paper did

This is a longitudinal study of 165 Israeli infants followed from 3 to 18 months, with observational assessment at 3, 6, 12, and 18 months of two distinct things: (a) the infant's *concern for distressed others* — affective registration of, and attention to, another person's suffering — and (b) the infant's *prosocial behaviour* — acting in ways that benefit a distressed other. The design lets the authors separate the onset and growth trajectory of these two channels and ask whether early concern predicts later prosociality.

## Key findings relevant to Q-073

The headline result is an asymmetry in timing. Concern for distressed others is present "long before previous theories assumed" — observable and *moderately consistent across situation and age from as early as 3 months* — and it grows only modestly thereafter, plateauing during the second year. Prosocial behaviour, by contrast, *increases rapidly during the second year*. And early individual differences in concern for others predicted later prosocial behaviour on behalf of distressed others.

That is the precise shape Q-073 is asking about. The question is whether other-bound *suffering/threat* registration needs to come online before other-bound *liking/wanting* (the protection-first hypothesis), or whether the ordering is instead about exploitation-resistance (early positive other-reward being gameable). This paper supplies developmental evidence for the *first half* of that: the channel that registers harm-to-others is up and stable early, while the channel that acts to confer benefit-on-others is still climbing well into the second year. The predictive link — early concern forecasting later helping — is consistent with the suffering channel scaffolding the appetitive channel, which is the ordering REE encodes in MECH-408's developmental sequence on the ARC-059 social spine.

## How it translates to REE — and where the translation strains

The mapping is real but it is a mapping of *adjacent constructs*, not identical ones, and the honest reading has to hold that boundary. "Concern for distress" is an affective-resonance measure; REE's "other-bound suffering/threat" stream is about *valuing* harm-to-others inside action selection. "Prosocial behaviour" bundles helping, comforting and sharing; REE's "other-bound liking/wanting" is closer to wanting-good-things-*for*-another, i.e. vicarious reward. So the paper is best read as a developmental *prior* on stream ordering, not as a direct measurement of REE's two streams. There is also a structural caveat that cuts against an over-strong reading: the paper explicitly refutes discrete *stage* theories of empathy. So it supports an *ordering* (suffering-channel-first) but specifically *not* a hard developmental gate in which the suffering stage completes before the liking stage opens — the two overlap and grow gradually. If REE enforces the ordering as a clean gate rather than a staggered-onset-with-overlap, that is a divergence this paper would flag.

Critically, the paper is silent on the *why*. It tells us suffering-registration matures first; it does not adjudicate between Davidov's developmental account and Q-073's exploitation-vulnerability account of why that ordering would be adaptive. So it narrows the question without closing it.

## Confidence

I put this at 0.66 — supporting, but deliberately short of the 0.8 band. Source quality is high (large repeated-measures longitudinal sample, predictive validity, strong venue). The limiting factor is mapping fidelity (0.62): the constructs are one translation-step away from REE's, and we are reading human-infant ontogeny as a prior on an artificial agent's stream-ordering, which carries non-trivial transfer risk (0.40). It is good evidence that *something like* the ordering REE assumes is observed in human development; it is not evidence that the ordering must hold in REE, nor evidence for the particular protective/anti-exploitation rationale.
