# Developmental emergence of fear/threat learning (Tallot, Doyère & Sullivan, 2015)

*According to PubMed* — Tallot L, Doyère V, Sullivan RM. *Genes, Brain and Behavior* 15(1):144–154. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/gbb.12261)

## What the paper did

A mechanistic review of how Pavlovian fear/threat learning comes online across postnatal development, drawing mainly on rodent pup work (Sullivan lab) with human-infant parallels. The organising idea is that "adult-like features of threat learning emerge as the relevant brain areas become incorporated into this learning": amygdala maturation permits cue-based threat learning, hippocampal maturation later permits context-based threat learning, and prefrontal cortex modulates expression. The review ties this maturational schedule to the pup's transition to independence at weaning.

## Key findings relevant to Q-073

Two things matter for Q-073. First, the threat-valuation system has a *definable, substrate-gated onset* — it is not a free-floating channel that is simply weak early and strong later, but one whose content (cue vs context threat) becomes available only as specific structures are recruited. That supports the general premise behind REE's MECH-408 ordering: an affective stream can be said to "come online" at a particular developmental point relative to other systems.

Second — and this is why I logged the entry as *mixed* rather than *supports* — the review describes a distinct early-life period that *blocks* threat learning and instead *enhances* the pup's approach to and attachment with the mother. In other words, the earliest developmental default is not threat-dominance; it is threat-*suppression* in service of the caregiving bond, lifting only as the animal moves toward independence. If Q-073 were read as "the threat/suffering channel comes online first *and* dominates early behaviour," the biology of early attachment pushes directly back on it. The ordering REE wants may still hold for *registration* (cf. the Davidov entry, where concern-for-distress is online early), but the Sullivan work warns that an early-online threat channel can be deliberately gated down so it does not override the bond it depends on.

## How it translates to REE — and the limits

The translation is two steps removed, and the entry's caveat says so plainly. This is *self*-directed threat learning — the pup learning what is dangerous *to itself* — whereas Q-073 is about *other*-bound suffering/threat, registering harm to a conspecific. So we are transferring across both species (rodent → human/AI) and reference frame (self → other). The paper makes no other-bound measurement at all. Its value here is not confirmation but *calibration*: it shows that a threat-valuation system's developmental onset is real and substrate-gated (good for REE's ordering premise), while also showing that "online early" does not entail "dominant early," and that evolution sometimes installs an *active suppressor* on the threat channel precisely to protect a vulnerable approach/attachment relationship. That second point is an interesting inversion of Q-073's exploitation-vulnerability hypothesis: rather than delaying the *positive* other-bound channel because it is gameable, biology here delays the *negative* self-threat channel because premature threat sensitivity would damage the attachment the infant needs to survive.

## Confidence

0.52, mixed. Source quality is high for a mechanistic review, but mapping fidelity is low (0.40 — self vs other, and no direct other-bound data) and transfer risk is high (0.62). I would not let this paper move Q-073 toward resolution on its own; I include it because an honest governance record on the *ordering* question should carry the contrary, attachment-protective nuance, not just the confirmatory Davidov trajectory.
