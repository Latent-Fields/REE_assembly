# Moral Choice Blindness and Confabulated Arguments (Hall, Johansson & Strandberg, 2012)

What would rationalisation look like if you could isolate it? You would need someone to hold a moral
position, have the position silently swapped for its opposite, and then watch whether they defend what
they never said. Hall, Johansson and Strandberg did exactly that. They used a self-transforming paper
survey, a magic trick that reversed two of each participant's stated moral attitudes. Of 160 park
volunteers, 69% failed to spot at least one reversal. Only 47.2% of manipulated items were detected
overall, and 53% of participants argued unequivocally, on at least one item, for the opposite of what
they had just endorsed.

The detail I keep returning to is that abstraction made no difference. Detection was 44.4% for broad
moral principles and 50.0% for concrete current issues, and the authors do not treat that gap as
meaningful. If rationalisation were a failure peculiar to abstract symbolic content, concrete issues
should have protected people, and they did not. That sits uneasily beside the Walker et al. (2021)
entry in this directory, where concrete detail *did* weaken the pull of a euphemism. The two are
compatible if they are about different things. Concrete detail about an *act* anchors an appraisal
against relabelling. A concrete *proposition* gives the verbal system nothing extra to check its own
account against.

For REE the lesson is architectural rather than mechanistic. MECH-013's first failure mode,
rationalisation, needs a verbal system that will justify whatever it is handed. This paper shows
that such a system is the ordinary human case, not a pathology. So if REE ever gains a symbol channel,
its account of why an action was chosen has to be read as a hypothesis about the underlying ethical
state and checked against the residue field and harm streams directly. It cannot be read as a
measurement. The claim's REE-pathology list does not currently say this. It probably should, since it
is the most defensible thing the literature supports.

What the paper does not show is suppression. No aversive signal is present, measured or silenced;
what comes apart is the verbal report and a previously stated attitude. The stronger reading of
MECH-013, that language *suppresses* harm, is therefore untouched here. The setting was also designed
to make correcting the experimenter as easy and unawkward as possible, and the authors are candid that
the attitudes might have been constructed in context rather than stored, in which case there was
little for language to decouple *from*. Choice blindness is also largely one group's paradigm.

Confidence 0.55. This is strong, direct evidence for the weaker reading of the rationalisation limb, that language
generates justification unconstrained by the evaluation it purports to report. It is silent on the
stronger reading.
