# Neural Correlates of Maintaining Political Beliefs Against Counterevidence (Kaplan, Gimbel & Harris, 2016)

MECH-013's ideological-capture limb says that fixed symbolic frames override perception and residue.
The natural place to look for that is belief resistance: what happens when a firmly held proposition
meets evidence against it? Kaplan, Gimbel and Harris scanned 40 strongly liberal adults while they
read five counterarguments against each of 8 political and 8 non-political statements they strongly
endorsed. Belief fell more for the non-political statements. Challenges to political beliefs recruited
the default mode network, which the authors associate with self-representation and disengagement from
the external world. On individual items, greater resistance went with more dorsomedial prefrontal and
less orbitofrontal activity.

So far, so supportive: propositions bound to identity behave differently under evidence, and the
network they recruit is the self-referential one. The result I think matters most, though, points the
other way from the claim as written. People who changed their minds *more* showed *less* insula and
amygdala response to the counterevidence. Put the other way round, the people most captured by their
frame were the most affectively engaged. That is not a frame overriding affect. It looks much more
like a frame recruiting affect to defend itself, so that a threat to the proposition registers
something like harm.

If that is right, REE should rethink where it files ideological capture. The claim maps its failure
modes to four pathologies, and this finding sits much closer to "spurious residue from narrative
contamination" than to anything like suppression. It also gives a distinguishable prediction for any
future REE symbolic layer. Capture should appear as harm-like signal evoked by frame-threatening
evidence, not as an absent harm signal on frame-consistent harm. The two predictions come apart
cleanly, which is what a claim of this kind most needs.

The weaknesses are real. The sample is small and ideologically uniform, so "political" is confounded
with the content of liberal policy positions, and there is no comparison group. The counterarguments
were written to be persuasive and, by the authors' own account, often contained exaggerations or
distortions. Resisting a distorted argument is partly just good reasoning. No statement concerned harm
the participant could perceive, so the claim's specific phrase "override perception and residue" is
approached rather than tested. And the affect finding is a between-subject correlation in regions with
many functions.

Confidence 0.40, direction mixed. The paper supports the existence of identity-bound resistance to
evidence and contradicts the suppression form of ideological capture. Its value to REE is mainly the
second: it tells us which mechanism to build if the limb is ever tested.
