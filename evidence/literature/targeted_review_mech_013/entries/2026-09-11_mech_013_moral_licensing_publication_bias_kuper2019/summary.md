# Has the Evidence for Moral Licensing Been Inflated by Publication Bias? (Kuper & Bott, 2019)

MECH-013 lists five "common failure modes", and moral licensing is one of them: symbolic good acts
used to justify later harm. It is the most intuitively satisfying of the five. Most of us have caught
ourselves at it, or think we have. It is also the one on the least secure empirical footing, and
this paper is why.

The earlier meta-analysis by Blanken, van de Ven and Zeelenberg (2015) pooled 91 studies and 7,397
participants and put the effect at d = 0.31. It also noted, almost in passing, that published studies
showed larger effects than unpublished ones. Kuper and Bott took that seriously. Applying two
standard publication-bias corrections to the meta-analytic data, they got d = -0.05 from PET-PEESE,
which is nothing, and d = 0.18 from a three-parameter selection model, which is small but
significant. They also found that culture moderates the effect. Their conclusion is that both the
evidence for moral licensing and its size have probably been inflated by publication bias. That fits
the large replications that failed to find it.

This entry goes in as *weakens*, and I want to be precise about what it weakens. It does not touch
MECH-013's central idea that language decoupled from embodied signal fails in characteristic ways. It
weakens one item on the list, and the word "common" attached to it. If moral licensing exists, the
evidence says it is small, culturally variable, and in one of two defensible analyses, absent. REE
should not build symbolic moral credit discounting later harm-avoidance into any model as a default
dynamic. At the literature's headline size, that would mean encoding a publication artefact.

There is a second, quieter point. Even a robust licensing effect would map only loosely onto
MECH-013's mechanism. Licensing experiments manipulate recalled or performed good deeds and measure
later choices. They do not isolate *language* as the thing that decouples from harm. So the item's
fit to the claim was always looser than its neighbours'. My recommendation to governance is to
separate the limbs of MECH-013 by evidential standing. Rationalisation and euphemistic reframing have
reasonable support in this directory. Licensing should be marked contested, or removed from the
asserted list, until a REE-native test exists.

Confidence 0.58. This is good meta-science that agrees with independent replication failures. It is
held below 0.7 because the two correction methods disagree about whether anything survives, and
because it bears on one limb of the claim, not the whole.
