# Euphemistic Language Shifts Judgments of Harmful Actions (Walker, Turpin, Meyers, Stolz, Fugelsang & Koehler, 2021)

MECH-013 makes a strong and slightly vague assertion: that when language decouples from embodied
ethical signals, it can be used to explain harm away, or to abstract it until it no longer registers.
The first thing to ask of such a claim is whether words alone, with the act held still, move the
appraisal of harm at all. This paper answers that about as directly as a behavioural study can.
Across 1906 U.S. participants, describing an action with an agreeable term -- "enhanced interrogation"
rather than "torture" -- made the same action look more acceptable, and the disagreeable term did the
reverse.

Two further results matter more to me than the headline. First, the effect shrank, though it did not
vanish, when participants were given a detailed description of what was actually done. The label has
the most purchase where the act itself is least specified. Second, people judged the euphemistic
descriptions as largely truthful and distinct from lies, and rated the people using them as more
trustworthy and more moral than liars. The reframe is essentially free for the speaker.

The mapping I would draw for REE is this. If a future REE symbol channel feeds harm appraisal, it
should be expected to shift appraisal by label choice alone, and the size of that shift should fall as
the precision of the non-linguistic harm evidence rises. That is a more useful statement than
"language decouples from embodied signals", because it names what the decoupling depends on: language
takes over where the harm stream is weak. It also gives MECH-013's bureaucratic-dissociation limb a
mechanism. Bureaucratic language works less by lying than by removing the concrete detail that would
otherwise anchor the appraisal. And the dishonesty result gives the reputation-substitution limb
something real to stand on: a reframe that carries no reputational penalty is exactly the kind of move
a social-scoring system would reward.

The caveats are serious and I do not want to understate them. These are observers rating vignettes.
Nothing here tells us whether the person who *did* the thing, or who stood beside it, feels less when
they call it by a kinder name, and that is the case MECH-013 is most concerned with. The measure is an
explicit evaluation; the effect could live entirely in verbal judgment without touching anything
affective or residue-like. There is no physiology. And REE has no language substrate, so this
constrains the design of something that does not yet exist rather than testing anything that does.

Confidence 0.62: strong source, the right manipulation, but measured one step removed from the
agent's own harm signal. The paper supports the claim that language can move harm appraisal. It does
not show that language suppresses an agent's own harm signal, and it should not be cited as if it did.
