# State-dependent learning as the biological precedent for the reset-on-switch arm

Radulovic, Jovasevic and Meyer review state-dependent learning (SDL): the finding that information
acquired under one neurobiological state can be stored and retrieved *only* under that state, and is
behaviourally inaccessible outside it. The review catalogues the historical induction methods --
largely pharmacological (GABAergic agonists, ethanol, opioid agents) -- and identifies the
mechanisms that are actually established, principally inhibitory neurotransmission and microRNA
regulation, before proposing further cellular and circuit-level contributors. The authors are candid
that SDL, despite a long behavioural pedigree, "has not been subjected to rigorous neuroscientific
study," and they draw the implication outward to psychiatric conditions in which learning acquired
in one state fails to inform behaviour in another.

For MECH-028 the relevance is specific rather than general. The claim has two clauses, and it is the
second -- that ethical behaviour depends on the *preservation of learning across* mode transitions
-- that this paper speaks to. The claim's testable form builds a (C) arm in which the residue field
and the `e2_harm_s` weights are restored to their pre-switch snapshot at every mode switch, and asks
whether harm learned before a transition recurs after it. SDL is the reason that arm is not a straw
man. Biology genuinely contains a regime where a state boundary is enough to make prior learning
behaviourally absent, with the organism intact and the memory physically undestroyed. If the
transfer of harm learning across internal states were automatic, SDL would not exist.

The translation has a boundary I want to state plainly rather than bury. SDL is generated by
inducing a *pharmacological* state, and REE's mode switch is a reconfiguration of the control plane
-- a change in what the SalienceCoordinator gates, not a change in the chemical milieu of the
encoding substrate. These are analogous in effect and not in mechanism. There is a second, sharper
asymmetry: SDL is a retrieval gate. The memory is preserved and returns when the state is
reinstated, which is what makes the phenomenon interesting. REE's (C) arm does not gate retrieval;
it reverts the stored quantity. So this literature supports the premise that a state boundary *can*
block the behavioural expression of prior learning; it does not license the stronger reading that a
mode switch destroys what was learned. If the V3 result shows permanent, unrecoverable loss, that
exceeds what Radulovic et al. would predict, and the excess is REE's construction rather than
biology's.

Confidence is 0.66. Source quality is high and the phenomenon is robust; the deduction is held back
by mapping fidelity, because the review is about drug states and REE is about control-plane regimes,
and by the review's own acknowledgement that the neural account is incomplete. What I take from it
is permission to treat the preservation clause as a real question rather than an obvious one -- which
is exactly what the claim's non-degeneracy precondition already insists on.
