# Extinction, renewal, and the half of MECH-028 most likely to come back null

Bouton, Maren and McNally's Physiological Reviews survey is the reference account of extinction
learning. Its central thesis is that extinction does not erase the original association; it installs
a *new inhibitory* memory, and that inhibitory memory is bound to the context in which it was
acquired. The evidence is renewal: change the context after extinction and the extinguished response
returns. The review establishes that this holds for both Pavlovian and instrumental extinction,
locates Pavlovian extinction in amygdala-prefrontal-hippocampal circuitry where prefrontal inhibition
of fear-encoding neurons enables retrieval of the extinction memory, and identifies distinct
corticostriatal and striatopallidal pathways for instrumental extinction. The authors close by noting
that a fully integrated biobehavioural account still awaits.

I have marked this entry `mixed`, and the reason is the most useful thing in it. MECH-028's
preservation clause -- learning must carry across mode transitions for ethical behaviour to hold --
is supported by this literature *for one component and not the other*. Safety learning is the
context-bound, fragile half: it does not survive a context change, which is the whole content of
renewal. Threat learning is the robust half: the review's founding argument is that it was never
erased and returns readily. If REE's mode switch behaves like an ABA context change, then the (C)
arm's harm-recurrence readout is pointed at the component that biology says should transfer anyway.
That is not a criticism of the design; it is a prediction that (C) = (A) on harm recurrence is a
live and principled outcome rather than a null result to be explained away. The claim's own
FALSIFYING clause anticipates this -- "(C) = (A) because harm learning is entirely re-acquirable
within-mode" -- and this review is the reason to take that branch seriously in advance.

The corollary is a design point. MECH-028 specifies two readouts: harm recurrence, and the avoidance
*cost* (over-generalised suppression of approach to non-harm resources). On the extinction account
these are not interchangeable. Avoidance cost is the behavioural signature of an inhibitory,
safety-side representation failing to be applied -- the organism does not know, in the new context,
which things are safe -- and that is exactly the representation this literature says is context-bound.
A V3 run that reads only harm recurrence risks a null that says more about which half was measured
than about whether cross-mode preservation matters. Reading both, as the claim already requires, is
what makes the test diagnostic.

The caveat I cannot dissolve is that renewal is demonstrated across *external* contexts -- chambers,
odours, rooms -- and REE's mode is an internal control-plane configuration. Whether an internal
regime shift functions as a retrieval context is a real assumption, not a derivation, and the V3
design should say so out loud. Confidence 0.72: near-ceiling on source quality, held down by that
internal/external question, and by the fact that the strongest thing this paper does for MECH-028 is
sharpen the falsifier rather than support the claim.
