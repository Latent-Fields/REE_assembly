# A transition that consolidates without transferring -- and a warning about timing

Borquez and colleagues built, in rats, almost exactly the design MECH-028 proposes. Animals acquired
an operant lever-press response in context A and were extinguished in context B. A three-hour
retention interval then delivered one of three treatments -- sleep, sleep deprivation, or spontaneous
wakefulness -- and recall was tested either immediately or at 48 hours, in both the extinction
context and a novel one. The manipulation is a mode transition; the test is preservation across a
boundary. That is MECH-028's (A)/(B) contrast with a context probe bolted on.

The answer came back two-sided, which is why this entry is `mixed`. Sleep did enhance extinction
memory relative to both deprivation and quiet wakefulness at recent recall -- the transition clause
gets support. But the enhancement appeared *only in the extinction context*. The transition
consolidated the learning without making it transferable. And by remote recall at 48 hours, the
sleep-deprived animals had caught up in both contexts: long-term generalisation of extinction
happened independently of whether immediate post-learning sleep occurred at all.

Both halves of that result bear directly on the V3 design, and neither is comfortable. The first
half says MECH-028's two clauses are separable in a way the claim states them as joined: a mode
transition can do consolidation work while doing no preservation-across-boundaries work, so a run
that finds (B) < (A) has not thereby shown anything about cross-mode transfer. The second half is
the sharper one. The claim's own degeneracy section warns that the test is spoiled "if harm is
re-learned within one episode fast enough that a reset is behaviourally invisible." Borquez et al.
are the animal precedent for a neighbouring version of that: given enough elapsed time, the
deprived animals arrived where the slept animals already were. The benefit of the transition was in
the *rate*, not the endpoint. If V3's lives are long relative to whatever consolidation the substrate
performs, the (B)-vs-(A) contrast may be measuring a timing difference that has washed out by the
time the readout is taken. The design should decide in advance at what point in the life the
post-transition readout is sampled, and should probably sample it more than once.

Caveats are real and I will not soften them. This is appetitive operant extinction in rats, not harm
avoidance; a positive-reinforcement paradigm may consolidate on a different schedule from an aversive
one. The N is modest and the retention window is three hours, which is a far weaker intervention than
REE's permanent mode lock. The 48-hour catch-up has no clean V3 analogue -- rodent hours and V3
episodes are not commensurable -- so the timing lesson transfers as a qualitative caution about when
to read the dependent variable, not as a quantitative prediction. Confidence 0.59: the design
alignment is the best of the five entries, and the transfer distance is the widest.
