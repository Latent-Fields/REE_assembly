# Fatigue as a mode-shaping factor: the closest human analogue of the mode-locked arm

Ben Simon, Vallat, Rossi and Walker asked whether losing sleep makes people less willing to help
each other, and answered it three times over at three different scales. In the laboratory, one night
of total sleep deprivation reduced person-to-person helping, with fMRI showing deactivation across
social-cognition networks. Ecologically, within-subject reductions in a given night's sleep predicted
reduced helping the following day. At population scale, the single hour of sleep lost to the
Daylight Saving transition was associated with a drop in charitable giving across more than three
million donation records. The convergence is the paper's strength: a laboratory causal manipulation,
a within-person longitudinal signal, and a natural experiment all pointing the same way.

MECH-028's architecture doc states that "upbringing, environment, fatigue, trauma, and care" are
structurally relevant to ethical behaviour rather than incidental to it, and this is the empirical
paper that makes the fatigue term concrete. Sleep is the biological transition into REE's
offline-consolidation mode -- `ree_core/sleep/phase_manager.py` is named in the claim's substrate
list for exactly this reason -- and depriving someone of it is the nearest available human version
of the (B) mode-locked arm: a system prevented from making a mode transition it would otherwise make.
The result is that other-regarding behaviour degrades. For a claim whose transition clause could
easily be dismissed as rhetorical, that is worth having on the record.

I want to be careful about how far it carries, because two substitutions sit between this paper and
the V3 test. The first is on the readout. Helping and donating are approach-side prosocial acts;
MECH-028 measures harm recurrence and over-generalised avoidance, which are suppression-side. A
system that helps less is not thereby a system that harms more -- these are different failures, and
the moral ledger does not net them out. The second is on the manipulation. Total sleep deprivation
is blunt: it degrades attention, working memory and affect regulation at the same time as it blocks
the mode transition, so the prosociality deficit cannot be attributed to the missing transition
specifically. REE's (B) arm is in fact the cleaner experiment -- it raises the switch threshold out
of reach and leaves everything else running -- which means it should expect a *smaller* and more
interpretable effect than this paper reports, not a comparable one.

Confidence 0.61. The finding itself is about as solid as behavioural prosociality research gets, and
I have discounted it almost entirely on mapping rather than on source quality. What it licenses is
the direction of the transition clause and the claim that fatigue is architecturally relevant. What
it does not license is any expectation about effect size in a clean mode-lock, or the assumption
that a helping deficit and a harm-avoidance deficit are the same phenomenon measured two ways.
