# Umbricht, Huestis, Cone & Preston (2004) -- Effects of high-dose intravenous buprenorphine in experienced opioid abusers

**What the paper did.** In a controlled clinical research-unit study, six
non-dependent, experienced opioid-abusing participants received escalating
intravenous doses of buprenorphine (2, 4, 8, 12, 16mg) across separate sessions,
compared against sublingual and placebo conditions. Physiologic outcomes (heart
rate, oxygen saturation, blood pressure) and subjective drug-effect ratings were
tracked for 72 hours after each dose.

**Key findings.** Dose-response curves were flat above a moderate dose for most
physiologic and subjective parameters -- further dose escalation produced no
further increase in measured effect, a textbook pharmacological ceiling. This is
attributable to buprenorphine's pharmacology: it is a partial mu-opioid agonist,
meaning its intrinsic efficacy at the receptor is bounded regardless of how much
additional drug is administered, unlike a full agonist such as morphine.

**How this maps to Q-086.** This entry is included as a conceptual anchor rather
than a direct empirical test of Q-086's ecological-vs-representational
dichotomy. What it establishes cleanly, in a domain far removed from REE, is the
general form of the "calibration/representational pathology" hypothesis: a
readout can plateau purely because of the bounded properties of its own
transduction mechanism, even while the true underlying input (drug dose
administered, unambiguously increasing) continues to rise without limit. A ceiling
in an observed signal does not, on its own, distinguish "the true underlying
quantity has stopped increasing" from "the readout mechanism has a bounded
dynamic range regardless of the true input." For Q-086, the value of this paper is
in reinforcing that the calibration-pathology reading of z_harm_a's saturation
requires no exotic or REE-specific failure mode -- bounded-dynamic-range readouts
that flatten above some input threshold are an entirely ordinary, well-understood
class of phenomenon spanning receptor pharmacology, sensory transduction, and (per
the other entries in this pull) endocrine stress physiology.

**Limitations and caveats.** The domain here -- partial mu-opioid receptor
agonism in a clinical population of opioid abusers -- is a substantially more
distant analogy to REE's internal scalar affective/harm signal than the other
three entries pulled for Q-086 (neural pain coding, ecological predation-risk
physiology, chronic-inflammatory stress-axis blunting). This paper does not test
anything resembling chronic hazard exposure, ecological stress, or affective
integration; it is cited purely to establish the abstract, domain-general
principle that ceiling effects reflect the properties of a transduction
mechanism, not necessarily the absence of further "true" input. The sample is
also very small (n=6), typical for this kind of intensive-monitoring
research-unit pharmacology study but limiting generalizability even within its
own domain.

**Confidence reasoning.** Source quality is moderate: small sample but a

research unit, published in a reputable clinical psychopharmacology journal.
