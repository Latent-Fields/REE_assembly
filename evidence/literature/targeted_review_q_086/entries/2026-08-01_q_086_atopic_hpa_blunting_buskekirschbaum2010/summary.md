# Buske-Kirschbaum, Ebrecht & Hellhammer (2010) -- Blunted HPA axis responsiveness to stress in atopic patients is associated with the acuity and severeness of allergic inflammation

**What the paper did.** Twenty patients with seasonal allergic rhinitis and 20
non-atopic controls were exposed to a standardized laboratory acute stressor (the
Trier Social Stress Test) twice each, in a cross-over design: once during the
pollen season (high allergic-inflammatory burden, active symptoms) and once
during the pollen-free season (low burden). Salivary cortisol responses to the
acute stressor were compared across these two chronic-load states within the same
individuals, alongside eosinophil/basophil counts and symptom severity ratings as
independent markers of inflammatory acuity.

**Key findings.** Cortisol responses to the acute stressor were significantly
more blunted during the high-symptom-severity (pollen) period than during the
symptom-free period, and symptom severity correlated negatively with the
magnitude of the cortisol response (r=.53, p<.05) -- the worse the ongoing
allergic inflammation, the less the HPA axis discriminated an additional acute
stressor on top of it. Non-atopic controls showed no such seasonal difference,
ruling out a simple seasonal artifact. The cortisol awakening response (a
different, tonic measure) did not differ between conditions, so the effect is
specific to acute-stressor reactivity, not baseline HPA tone.

**How this maps to Q-086.** Q-086 frames z_harm_a's saturation as a binary: either
it is faithfully tracking chronic ecological suffering, or it reflects a
calibration/representational pathology in the readout. This paper's contribution
is to establish that the second horn of that dichotomy is not a purely
hypothetical failure mode dreamed up to explain away an inconvenient null result
-- hyporesponsivity of a physiological stress-response system as a function of
rising chronic load is a real, clinically documented phenomenon with a plausible
adaptive/pathological mechanism (down-regulation of glucocorticoid signaling under
sustained inflammatory pressure). If z_harm_a's plateau under high hazard density
reflects something structurally similar -- the readout's dynamic range compressing
precisely as the underlying chronic load rises, rather than the readout tracking
moment-to-moment hazard intensity -- that would be consistent with a genuine,
biologically-grounded allostatic mechanism, not proof of an REE-specific
instrumentation bug. Whether that compressed-range behaviour is the behaviour REE
*wants* from z_harm_a (faithful chronic-suffering integration) or not is a
separate, normative design question this paper cannot settle -- it only
establishes that the mechanism itself is real and well-precedented.

**Limitations and caveats.** The chronic load studied here is allergic and
inflammatory, not predation- or threat-related -- a materially different stressor
category from REE's hazard-density manipulation, even though both fall under
"chronically elevated physiological burden" broadly construed. The design is a
naturally occurring cross-over (pollen season vs not) rather than an
experimenter-controlled dose manipulation, so causal direction is not fully
separable: it is possible that pre-existing HPA hyporesponsivity predisposes to
worse allergic control, rather than allergic severity causing the blunting. The
acute stressor tested (TSST) is a single fixed-intensity social-evaluative
challenge, not a graded series of stressor intensities, so this paper speaks most
directly to "does chronic load blunt response to A stressor" rather than "does
chronic load blunt the SCALING of response WITH stressor intensity," which is
closer to what Q-086 actually needs to resolve.

**Confidence reasoning.** Source quality is moderate: a modest sample (20 per
group) but a methodologically strong within-subject cross-over design that
controls for individual trait-level variation, published in a respected
psychoneuroimmunology journal. Mapping fidelity is moderate: the general
mechanism (chronic load blunting acute-stressor responsiveness) is directly
relevant to the calibration-pathology reading, but the study does not test
graded-intensity scaling, only a fixed stressor under two load states. Transfer
risk is moderate: human clinical allergic-disease population to an RL agent's
internal scalar harm signal, and the stressor class differs from predation/threat.
Net confidence 0.58, direction "supports": it grounds the calibration-pathology
horn of Q-086 in a real, precedented biological mechanism rather than treating it
as a residual "something went wrong" explanation.
