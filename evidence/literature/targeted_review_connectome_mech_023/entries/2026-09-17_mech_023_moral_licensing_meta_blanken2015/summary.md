# The human case for path-dependence is also the case against path-*shape*

Blanken, van de Ven and Zeelenberg meta-analysed 91 studies of moral licensing — 7,397 participants —
and found the effect real but modest: Cohen's d = 0.31 for the prediction that people who have just
behaved morally subsequently become more willing to behave immorally. The only moderator with empirical
support was publication status, with published studies yielding larger effects; the authors are candid
that the primary literature is underpowered for the moderator questions everyone wants answered.

I have included this deliberately as the counterweight entry, because it is the best human evidence for
half of MECH-023 and the best human evidence against the other half.

The half it supports is the non-Markovian half, and it supports it about as cleanly as psychology
manages anything. Two people facing an identical present choice, matched on the situation in front of
them, diverge in what they will do according to what they did earlier. That is MECH-023's opening
sentence — two agents in the same state may differ because of how they arrived — stated as a laboratory
effect rather than an architectural intuition. If someone wanted to defend the Markov reduction of
responsibility, this literature is the obstacle in humans that splitter cells are in rodents.

The half it undercuts is the *geometric* half, and this is the more interesting contribution. The
licensing effect is standardly reported and theorised as domain-general: a prior good deed in one domain
licenses transgression in an unrelated one. The mechanism as characterised is a moral-credential or
self-concept ledger — a scalar the history contributes to, which is then spent anywhere. In REE's
vocabulary that is a *global cost shift*, and MECH-023 names the global shift explicitly as its
falsifier: if fork A and fork B′ — equal harm mass, distant region — produce the same divergence, the
geometry is not path-shaped and the claim fails at criterion (ii). The default human finding sits on the
falsifying side of REE's own line. That does not refute MECH-023, since the architecture is not obliged
to reproduce human moral accounting, but it does mean the B′ control cannot be treated as a formality.
It is the crux, and the nearest human literature predicts the wrong answer.

There is also a sign problem I should not paper over. Licensing runs good-history → subsequent
transgression. Residue runs harm-history → subsequent avoidance. The moral-cleansing literature supplies
the direction that matches REE, and cleansing and licensing are both reported in humans, which is
itself informative: a single scalar residue term cannot generate both directions. If REE's residue field
is modelling anything human here, it is modelling one arm of a two-armed phenomenon. That is a
reasonable scope decision, but it should be stated as one.

The one respect in which this entry's challenge is weaker than it appears is the meta-analysis's own
verdict on its evidence base. Domain-generality is a claim about moderators, and the authors say plainly
that the studies are not powered to resolve moderators. So "licensing is domain-general" is the field's
working characterisation rather than a securely established fact, and the strength of the B′ warning
should be discounted accordingly.

Confidence 0.55, with mapping fidelity deliberately low at 0.45 and transfer risk high at 0.65. What
this meta-analysis measures — self-image accounting over explicit, socially accountable moral acts — and
what MECH-023 measures — residue-weighted candidate ranking at a matched latent state with bit-identical
non-residue surfaces — share a shape and not a substrate. The entry earns its place by naming the
alternative hypothesis the experiment has to beat, not by supporting the claim.
