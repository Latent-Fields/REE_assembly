# Feasibility of dopamine as a vector-valued feedback signal in the basal ganglia

According to PubMed. Source: Warnberg & Kumar (2023), *PNAS* 120(32):e2221994120. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2221994120)

## What the paper did

Warnberg and Kumar set out to reconcile two views of the basal ganglia that have sat uneasily together: the classical reinforcement-learning account, in which midbrain dopamine broadcasts a scalar reward-prediction error (RPE) that updates corticostriatal synaptic weights, and the newer evidence that the basal ganglia behave less like a selector over a small discrete action set and more like a dynamical system with graded, continuous outputs. Their move is to let the dopamine signal in the striatum carry a *vector-valued* error feedback signal -- a loss gradient -- rather than a single homogeneous scalar loss. They implement this with a local "three-factor" corticostriatal plasticity rule: the update at each synapse is the product of the presynaptic firing rate, a postsynaptic factor, and the unique dopamine concentration perceived by that particular striatal neuron. They then show, by construction and simulation, that this rule increases the capacity to learn a multidimensional series of real-valued outputs, and -- the part that matters for biological plausibility -- that it does not require precise nigrostriatal wiring but tolerates the random varicosity placement and diffuse volume transmission that dopamine actually exhibits.

## Why it speaks to ARC-108

ARC-108 is, almost word for word, this substrate. The claim proposes that the cortico-striatal weights deciding which channel wins committed-selection authority on the E3 selector should themselves be *learned* via a three-factor rule -- Hebbian channel co-activation multiplied by a signed dopaminergic RPE -- rather than remaining the pure-arithmetic arbitration layer that ARC-107 (MECH-447/448/449) is today. The diagnosis motivating ARC-108 is that F monopolises 88-89% of committed-selection variance (MECH-439) precisely *because* no learned striatal weighting can re-weight channels through experience. Warnberg and Kumar supply the formal feasibility argument that the missing piece is buildable: a local three-factor rule with dopamine as the third factor is sufficient to learn graded, real-valued outputs, which is exactly the gating function ARC-108 wants to install. This is the cleanest direct support I found for the mechanism, and it is reassuring that the result survives the messiness of volume transmission, because REE's substrate cannot assume surgical per-synapse dopamine delivery either.

## The caveat I want on the record

The headline result -- high capacity to learn many independent outputs -- leans on dopamine being *vector-valued*, i.e. each striatal neuron perceiving its own dopamine concentration carrying its own component of the gradient. ARC-108's V3-minimal slice, as pulled forward on 2026-06-22, assumes a signed *scalar* RPE; the routed-efferent, per-channel refinement is explicitly parked in v4. So the three-factor learning rule transfers essentially one-to-one and gives ARC-108 strong support for "make the gating weights learned," but the specific capacity argument is partly a property of the vector signal, and we should not over-read it as evidence that a scalar RPE alone will dissolve the F-dominance ceiling. The honest reading is: the substrate is feasible and well-motivated; how much of MECH-439 it actually relieves in the scalar slice is an empirical question for the V3 experiment, not something this paper settles.
