## What the paper says

Aston-Jones & Cohen's integrative theory is the canonical account of the locus coeruleus–norepinephrine (LC-NE) system as a *slow, broadly-projecting neuromodulator* with two activity modes. **Phasic** LC activation is driven by the outcome of task-related decisions and facilitates the ensuing behaviour — it optimises *exploitation* of the current task. **Tonic** LC activation appears when task utility wanes and is associated with *disengagement* from the current behaviour and a search for alternatives — *exploration*. The control variable is a broadly-projecting **gain** parameter (not a value estimate); frontal areas (ACC, OFC) that monitor task utility are proposed to drive LC into these modes to optimise performance on short and long timescales.

## Why it grounds MECH-369

MECH-369's defining property is a slow modulatory state that biases the *decommit-friction / commitment-threshold / action-release landscape* **independently of represented goal value** — "the modulator changes the release/threshold landscape, not the value estimate." LC-NE adaptive gain is the textbook biological instance of precisely that: a value-*independent* slow modulator whose job is to set engagement-versus-release of the current behaviour. It is the right *class* of mechanism, expressed at the right *level* (slow, broadly-projecting, decoupled from the value of any particular option), to anchor MECH-369's "slow-modulator-class distinction" without reducing compulsion to inflammation or importing a fast biological switch.

## The polarity caveat — why direction is `mixed`, not `supports`

There is an honest inversion I have refused to paper over. Tonic LC-NE drives *disengagement and lability* — the opposite pole from compulsive *stickiness* (difficulty releasing a loop). So the paper grounds the *existence and form* of a value-independent slow release-modulator, but its documented *direction* is opposite to compulsion. MECH-369's stickiness polarity is therefore a free parameter the anchor does not directly evidence: one can read compulsion as a *failure to switch into the tonic disengagement mode* (gain stuck in exploitation), which is consistent with the framework, but Aston-Jones & Cohen do not demonstrate that pathological pole. I set `evidence_direction: mixed` to record both the strong class-level support and the unevidenced polarity. A second boundary: LC gain is *driven by* frontal utility monitoring, so "value-independent" must be read precisely — the modulator carries no value estimate *itself*, even though its upstream drive is utility-derived.
