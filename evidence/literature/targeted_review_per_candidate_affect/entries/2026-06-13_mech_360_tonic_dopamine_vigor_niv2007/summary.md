# Niv, Daw, Joel & Dayan 2007 — Tonic dopamine, opportunity cost, and response vigour

**Claim grounded:** MECH-360 (expression as emergent action geometry)

## What the paper did

This is the canonical normative account of *response vigour* — the rate and latency with which an animal acts, distinct from *which* action it chooses. The authors build an average-reward reinforcement-learning model in which an agent chooses both an action and the latency at which to perform it. Optimal control trades the energetic cost of acting fast against the benefit of getting reward sooner, and the term that mediates this is the *long-run average reward rate*, which functions as the opportunity cost of time. They argue this quantity is reported by *tonic* dopamine in nucleus accumbens — explaining, from a normative standpoint, why higher tonic dopamine produces more vigorous responding.

## Why it grounds MECH-360

MECH-360's thesis is that affective expression *begins as an emergent read-out* of candidate-level affective arbitration — hesitation, latency, approach angle, retreat, repeated retry — rather than as a separately-engineered output channel. The AE-9 readiness gate names "Niv 2007 tonic-DA vigour as the basal-ganglia expression-intensity partner" as a co-constitutive harvest target. Niv et al supply exactly that, in the *intensity* dimension: vigour is not separately specified — it *falls out of* the motivational/value landscape via a slow modulatory signal. That is the same logic MECH-360 applies to the whole behavioural geometry. It is the basal-ganglia read-out side of the affect vector: the affect/value state sets *how hard and how fast* the selected trajectory is enacted, which is one visible axis of expression.

## Limitations and the mapping caveat

The honest gap is that Niv's vigour is a *global scalar* — the average reward rate is shared across all actions, so tonic-DA vigour does **not** carry the *per-candidate* differentiation that is MECH-360's distinctive contribution (style inherited from the candidate-differentiated affect vector MECH-359). It grounds the *intensity* axis of expression, not the *style* axis (approach angle, oscillation between candidates, avoidance margin, decommitment, repair-seeking). And it is a normative model, not a direct measurement of expressive behaviour, so the step from "vigour" to "visible behavioural geometry" is an REE-side extrapolation. The complementary entry (Dael, Mortillaro & Scherer 2012) supplies the empirical body-action-geometry side that Niv lacks.
