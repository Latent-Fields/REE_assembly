# Emotional shaping of spatial encoding along the dorsoventral axis -- assessed against MECH-144 (Silva & Girardeau, 2026)

> Paired with `2026-08-09_mech_143_dorsoventral_valence_coding_silva2026` in `targeted_review_connectome_mech_143`. Same source, assessed separately against each claim because the direction differs: *weakens* for MECH-143, *mixed* here.

## What the paper did

A recent mini-review synthesising the rodent literature on spatial and emotional coding across the hippocampal dorsoventral axis, from a group whose primary experimental work is in this area. For MECH-144 this is close to an ideal secondary source: the ventral valence literature is the review's main subject rather than its background.

## Key findings relevant to the claim

The supporting material is substantial. Subsets of ventral neurons increase activity across different anxiogenic locations -- open arms of an elevated plus maze, centre of an open field. Population activity within the anxiogenic ensemble scales *monotonically* with the level of environmental anxiety, and ventral activity immediately before entering an anxiogenic zone predicts subsequent exploration. Optogenetic inhibition of ventral hippocampus or its projections reduces anxiety-related behaviour; activation enhances it. Ventral place cells remap more than dorsal ones when contextual emotional valence changes -- when a previously rewarded environment becomes aversive. And the overrepresentation of high-value reward locations increases from dorsal to intermediate hippocampus. All of that is MECH-144's first half, well supported.

Then there is the sentence that complicates the second half. These anxiety-related neurons show *minimal overlap* with classical place cells, which the review reads as the ventral hippocampus generalising across anxiogenic situations independently of their specific locations. If the cells carrying valence are largely not the cells carrying space, then valence is not embedded in the map geometry -- it is carried by a parallel population that happens to be co-located in the same structure.

A second complication, which the review raises itself: recent work finds ventral populations maintaining stable representations of a stimulus even when its associated valence is *reversed*, which the authors describe as challenging the valence-centric view of ventral coding in favour of identity-and-salience coding.

## How this translates to REE

MECH-144 makes two claims, and they come apart here in the same way MECH-143's do in the paired entry. The existence claim -- ventral CA1 carries spatially organised valence signals that can serve as a prior over R(x,t) -- is well supported and I would not hesitate to lean on it. The geometric claim -- that valence is *intrinsic to* the map rather than externally tagged onto it -- is where the review pushes back.

The distinction is not academic for us. An intrinsic-geometry reading licenses reading valence straight off R(x,t), with no separate channel and no binding problem. A separate-population reading requires a distinct valence channel that is bound to location only loosely, and it reopens the question of *how* the binding happens -- which is exactly the question the Q-020 Path A resolution was supposed to close. The review's emphasis on projection-defined routing (lateral hypothalamus, medial prefrontal cortex, nucleus accumbens shell, basolateral amygdala) points the same way: the locus of valence may be the output routing rather than the map.

There is a caution in the other direction too, and I want it on the record: the review notes that both ventral-BLA and ventral-NAc pathways can switch between driving preference and avoidance. Whatever REE reads off the ventral compartment, it should not assume a fixed sign.

## Limitations and caveats

Secondary throughout, and rodent-only. That second point bites harder than usual here, because MECH-144 leans substantially on primate abstract-value-map evidence (Knudsen & Wallis, Cell 2021) which is entirely outside this review's scope -- so this entry cannot speak to that half of the claim at all, in either direction. The salience-not-valence challenge is itself reported second-hand from a single recent study and should not be treated as settled. And "minimal overlap" is a quantitative statement the review does not put a number on; how much is minimal matters a great deal for whether the geometric reading survives.
