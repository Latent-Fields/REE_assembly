# Grover, Wen & Reinhart 2026 -- the framework verified, the key term still out of reach

The citation in ARC-140 and MECH-541 is exact: Nature Neuroscience, DOI 10.1038/s41593-026-02434-6, PMID 42697957, published online ahead of print on 4 September 2026, indexed as a Review. I obtained the abstract verbatim through PubMed, and it says what the claim notes say it says. The authors "formalize these approaches as 'restorative normalization' (RN) and 'compensatory amplification' (CA)", and they "evaluate the following four factors that enable CA to be effective: multiple realizability, multiscale neuroplasticity, precision readiness and activity selectivity". RN restores healthy-like dynamics; CA strengthens repurposed processes that support function. They work the interplay through stroke rehabilitation and point at neurodegeneration, psychiatry and aging as places where CA might delay decline or facilitate remission.

So the framework attribution is now verified rather than taken on trust, and the two claims may cite this paper as the origin of the RN/CA distinction.

What this pull could NOT do is the thing the chip most wanted. Nature serves an authentication wall for the full text; there is no PMC deposit; the Reinhart lab publications page redirects and offers no PDF; no preprint exists that I could find. The four factors are therefore verified as a list of names and nothing more. "Precision readiness" is not defined anywhere in the accessible text, so I cannot confirm or refute that it differs from REE's own precision and gain machinery. ARC-140's warning against silently equating the two must stand exactly as written, still untested. If a later session gets institutional access and finds the meanings to coincide, that is a wording change owed to ARC-140, and it should be treated as such rather than as pleasant corroboration.

One further reservation, which is mine rather than the claim's. The abstract frames CA as a design principle for intervention: the clinician amplifies a repurposed process. ARC-140 asserts something about the organism, that it reallocates causal responsibility after damage without being driven to. Those are different propositions, and a review about how to intervene cannot establish the second. The claim's own notes already call itself a derived REE hypothesis, which is the right posture; this entry does not change it. Supports at 0.5.
