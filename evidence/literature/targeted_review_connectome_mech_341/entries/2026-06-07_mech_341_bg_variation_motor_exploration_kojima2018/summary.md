# Avian basal ganglia as a source of behavioral variation enabling exploration (Kojima et al. 2018)

According to PubMed. Source: Kojima, Kao, Doupe & Brainard, *Journal of Neuroscience* (2018), [DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2915-17.2018](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2915-17.2018) (PMC6222063).

## What the paper did

The authors asked a question that bears directly on MECH-341: when a system learns by trial and error, where does the *variation* come from? The basal ganglia (BG) are uncontroversially involved in evaluating and selecting motor programs — the exploitation side. What was contested was whether the BG also *generate* the exploratory variability that selection feeds on. Earlier lesion work had muddied this: ablating Area X (the song-specific BG nucleus in zebra finches) had shown no lasting effect on several measures of vocal variability, which had been read as evidence that the BG do not supply exploration. Kojima and colleagues revisited this with a sharper readout. They lesioned Area X in adult male zebra finches and measured rapid *within-syllable* variation in fundamental frequency (FF) — a fast variability channel that can act as motor exploration. They also lesioned LMAN (the cortical output of the BG loop) and, separately, electrically perturbed LMAN.

## Key findings relevant to the claim

Area X lesions *permanently* eliminated rapid within-syllable FF variation. LMAN lesions did the same, consistent with the BG relaying variability through LMAN to the song motor pathway. And electrical perturbation of LMAN could *drive* FF fluctuations that mimicked the naturally occurring within-syllable variability. The within-syllable variation was elevated in juveniles and in adults singing alone — conditions independently linked to enhanced plasticity and elevated neural variability in Area X. Taken together, the BG are not merely an evaluator; they are a dedicated *source* of the rapid behavioral variation that constitutes the substrate for reinforcement-driven learning.

## How this maps to REE (MECH-341)

MECH-341 argues that E3 score aggregation over CEM-supplied candidates must preserve trajectory-class diversity rather than collapse a diverse pool onto a single deterministic ranking — and that without explicit diversity preservation, downstream action selection becomes deterministic regardless of proposal-layer diversity. The songbird circuit gives a biological existence proof for the *architectural shape* this requires: a structurally separate variability-generating pathway (Area X → LMAN) running in parallel with a deterministic precision pathway (HVC → RA), where the variability is precisely what makes reinforcement selection learnable. The strongest transferable point is the failure mode: remove or down-weight the diversity pathway and exploration collapses, leaving selection nothing to discriminate among. That is exactly the FP MECH-341 names — "diversity in candidates that collapses to one class after scoring."

## Limitations and confidence

The honest caveat is locus. The BG here inject variation at the *motor-output / proposal* stage, whereas MECH-341 is specifically about the *scoring/aggregation* step preserving diversity that already exists in the pool. So the paper most cleanly evidences the general principle ("a dedicated diversity circuit must hold parity, and ablating it collapses behaviour") rather than the precise scoring locus. There is also real domain transfer: songbird vocal motor learning is a canonical BG trial-and-error model, but it is not REE's CEM/E3 candidate machinery. The earlier null results are themselves instructive — they show that measuring "diversity" at the wrong granularity can falsely conclude the diversity circuit is inert, a methodological warning worth carrying into how we instrument candidate-pool diversity in V3. I score this **supports at 0.7**: causal manipulations in a strong venue (source quality 0.85), moderate mapping fidelity (0.6) because of the proposal-vs-scoring locus mismatch, moderate transfer risk (0.4).
