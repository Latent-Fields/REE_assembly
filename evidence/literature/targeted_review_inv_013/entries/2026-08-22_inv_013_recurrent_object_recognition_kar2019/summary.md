# Recurrence is critical to core object recognition -- INV-013 (ITERATIVE limb)

Kar and colleagues built this study in the right order, which is why it carries weight. They first used behaviour to find hundreds of "challenge" images -- images that primates solve but that feedforward CNNs fail on -- and only then went to the physiology. Recording at scale in macaque inferior temporal cortex, they found that behaviourally sufficient object-identity solutions emerged roughly 30 ms later for challenge images than for performance-matched controls, and that those late-phase IT population patterns were poorly predicted by feedforward CNN activations. The extra time is doing something, and that something is not in the feedforward sweep.

For INV-013's ITERATIVE limb this is close to the best available biological evidence. The limb asserts that selection proceeds through repeated refinement rather than a single forward pass -- in REE, the CEM-style proposal loop in `ree_core/agent.py` that produces candidates, scores them, and revises the candidate set within one decision, with stuck-state-gated widening via `difficulty_gated_proposal_entropy.py`. What Kar et al. add is the *necessity* form rather than the mere availability form: for a real, behaviourally-identified class of inputs, one pass demonstrably does not suffice. INV-013's own stated falsifier is the proposal-refinement loop being replaced by a single greedy argmax with no resampling. This paper says that in the biological case such a replacement would cost you exactly the hard inputs.

I want to be honest about the finding that cuts the other way, because it is in the same paper and it is not incidental. The authors report a **functional equivalence between additional nonlinear transformations and recurrence**: very-deep feedforward CNNs predicted the late IT responses about as well as shallower recurrent ones. Read carefully, that means recurrence is how the primate brain buys extra computation under a constraint on physical depth, not that iterative refinement is the only architecture that could deliver the result. INV-013 survives this cleanly, because it only asserts that an iterative mechanism exists and is exercised in the live loop. But any stronger REE argument -- that iteration is architecturally *necessary* rather than efficient -- overreaches what this evidence supports, and I have put that in the failure signatures rather than leaving it as a footnote.

The second boundary is the level of the mapping. The recurrence here lives inside perceptual categorisation in the ventral stream; REE's iterative mechanism lives in action selection. Both satisfy the abstract form "more than one pass within a single decision," and that is a genuine shared property, but it is a structural analogy rather than shared subject matter. A reader should not take this entry as evidence that REE's *planning* loop is biologically warranted in its specifics -- only that iterative refinement is a real and load-bearing computational strategy in a system that had the option of being purely feedforward and was not.

A third, smaller bound: the late-solution signature holds for a curated subset of images. Most images are solved in the feedforward sweep. So the result also tells us how much of ordinary processing is genuinely iterative, which is: less than all of it. That is arguably good news for INV-013, which requires that the mechanism exist and be exercised, not that it dominate.

Confidence 0.70, with mapping fidelity (0.60) as the limiting term rather than source quality (0.90).
