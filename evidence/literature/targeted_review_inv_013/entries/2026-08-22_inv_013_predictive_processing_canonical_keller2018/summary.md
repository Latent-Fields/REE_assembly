# Predictive processing as a canonical cortical computation -- INV-013 (PREDICTIVE limb)

Keller and Mrsic-Flogel's 2018 Neuron perspective is the reference statement of the position that prediction is not one cortical trick among many but the organising motif of cortical computation. Their argument is that cortical circuits continuously generate predictions of incoming input from internal models, and that a substantial part of what propagates forward is the mismatch rather than the raw input. They go further than most reviews in the genre by setting out how the framework might be implemented in cortical microcircuitry -- including a proposed division between neurons signalling positive and negative prediction error -- and, unusually and to their credit, by devoting a section to how the whole framework could be experimentally falsified.

INV-013 asserts three conjunctive properties of REE's cognition, and this paper bears on the first: PREDICTIVE, meaning that the substrate forms forward models rather than reacting purely to immediate observation. The value of the paper for that limb is not that it measures anything about REE -- it cannot -- but that it establishes the biological warrant for the commitment. If prediction is the canonical cortical computation, then a cognitive architecture without forward models is the one making the strange choice, and REE's E1 slow LSTM world model and E2's `world_forward`/`predict_next_state` are load-bearing structure rather than optional enrichment. That reframes the interesting question, and I think correctly: it is not whether to be predictive, but whether the prediction is conditioned on the right variables.

The mapping caveat here is the whole point and I want it stated plainly rather than softened. INV-013's own confirming condition is a **code audit** -- at least one forward-model component outputting a prediction of a future state conditioned on current state plus action, consumed by the live decision loop, and demonstrably not dead code. No paper discharges that condition. What this entry contributes is the prior, not the verdict. Anyone reading `literature_confidence` on INV-013 as evidence that REE's predictive machinery is wired in has misread the entry.

There is a second and sharper boundary. Keller and Mrsic-Flogel's falsification section turns on a distinction that maps almost exactly onto REE's own worry: a circuit can produce prediction-error-shaped responses without containing a generative forward model. That is the labelled-versus-genuine gap INV-013 explicitly delegates to ARC-002 ("is E2's prediction actually affordance-conditioned, not just world-dynamics-conditioned"). The paper does not close that gap for REE and does not pretend to; it names it in the biological case, which is at least a demonstration that the distinction is real and testable rather than philosophical hand-wringing. A further limitation in the same direction: the perspective is centred on sensory and sensorimotor prediction, whereas ARC-002's question is about affordance prediction, so the strongest form of REE's predictive commitment is adjacent to, rather than inside, the paper's evidential reach.

Confidence 0.72. Source quality is high and the mapping is honest but indirect: this is a warrant for an architectural choice, evaluated against a claim whose confirming condition is an audit of source files.
