# A hierarchy of intrinsic timescales across primate cortex -- INV-013 (MULTI-TIMESCALE limb)

This short 2014 Nature Neuroscience paper is the field's standard reference for the claim that cortex is temporally stratified. Murray and colleagues pooled single-unit data across seven primate cortical areas and several laboratories, measured the autocorrelation timescale of intrinsic spiking fluctuation in each, and found a hierarchical ordering: short in sensory areas, long in prefrontal. Their reading is that intrinsic timescales reflect areal specialisation for computations over different temporal ranges.

INV-013's third limb requires that REE's processing be stratified across more than one update rate -- not a single flat clock -- and names two mechanisms: the within-episode fast/slow split between E2 and E1, and the cross-episode split via `ree_core/sleep/` running at a slower-than-tick cadence. What Murray et al. contribute is the direct measurement that temporal stratification is an organising principle of the biological system rather than an engineering convenience adopted for tractability. That matters for how one reads INV-013's stated falsifier -- L-space collapsed to a single shared-rate latent with consolidation removed. On this evidence that would not be a simplification; it would be departing from the one architectural regularity the biology most clearly exhibits in the temporal domain.

Now the part that I think is actually the most useful thing in this entry, and it is uncomfortable. INV-013 carries its own non-degeneracy note, inherited from ARC-004's audit: `LatentStack.encode` currently smooths `z_beta`, `z_theta` and `z_delta` with a **single shared EMA constant** (`alpha_shared=0.3`). So REE's L-space is role-stratified but not clock-stratified, and the multi-timescale property is presently carried by the online/offline split rather than by graded per-layer rates. Murray et al.'s cortex is graded per area, measured in the spiking statistics themselves. The paper therefore supports the commitment while pointing at exactly the place REE has not yet earned the analogy it gestures at. That is not a falsification of INV-013 -- the limb requires *some* multi-timescale mechanism and the E2/E1-plus-sleep split supplies one -- but it is the sharpest available statement of what a fuller realisation would look like.

Two further limitations bound the entry. First, the quantity measured is *intrinsic* -- the autocorrelation of ongoing fluctuation during fixation and inter-trial intervals -- not the rate at which an area updates task variables, and crucially the paper does not demonstrate that areas **interact** across rates. INV-013's limb asks for coexistence *and* interaction; this supports coexistence well and interaction not at all. Second, the paper is silent on offline and consolidation cadences, which is where half of REE's multi-timescale property lives. Anyone hoping this entry underwrites the sleep half of the split will not find it here.

I have also weighted for the design: seven areas, pooled across laboratories and heterogeneous tasks, with substantial within-area heterogeneity behind a group-level ordering. The hierarchy is real and has replicated widely since, but no single area has one clean clock rate.

Confidence 0.74. Source quality 0.88, mapping fidelity 0.70 (the untested interaction requirement is the deduction), transfer risk 0.35.
