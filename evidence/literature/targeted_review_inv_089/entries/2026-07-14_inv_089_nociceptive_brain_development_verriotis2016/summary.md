# The nociceptive brain matures on its own terms, unlike other sensory systems (Verriotis, Chang, Fitzgerald & Fabrizi 2016)

According to PubMed, Verriotis, Chang, Fitzgerald & Fabrizi (*Neuroscience*, 2016; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroscience.2016.07.026)) review how nociceptive cortical processing develops from birth. Their framing statement is almost a restatement of INV-089's premise: "unlike other sensory systems, the pain system in healthy individuals cannot rely upon prolonged activity-dependent shaping through repeated noxious stimulation." Remarkably little is known, they note, about when and how the mature adult nociceptive cortical network assembles — but what is clear is that it does not follow the experience-driven refinement path of the exteroceptive senses.

This is the entry that speaks to the leg the Bastuji paper could not reach: the *maturation trajectory*, not merely the anatomical separateness. INV-089 says harm-evaluator quality — `harm_eval_z_harm`, reading `z_harm = HarmEncoder(harm_obs)` — is strictly bounded by `z_harm` representational differentiation, and that this differentiation follows a schedule distinct from `z_world`'s. The biology here is congruent: a healthy nociceptive system cannot be tuned by repeated painful experience the way vision or touch are sculpted by their input statistics, so its representational maturation is necessarily governed by a different (largely non-activity-dependent) process on a different timeline. That is precisely the shape of the REE claim that "productive harm-evaluator training cannot precede sufficient `z_harm` differentiation."

Two caveats keep me honest. First, the review candidly flags that the neurophysiological (EEG) and hemodynamic (fMRI/NIRS) infant measures "are not always consistent," so the exact rate and ordering of nociceptive-vs-exteroceptive cortical maturation is not pinned to one agreed curve — the *distinctness* is well-supported, the precise *schedule* less so. Second, and more subtly, the paper's point that pain development "cannot rely on activity-dependent shaping" cuts both ways for REE: it supports a distinct trajectory, but it also gently warns that the REE picture — train the harm-evaluator once `z_harm` has differentiated — may over-assume that experience-driven refinement is even the operative mechanism for harm. I map only the shared "distinct maturation schedule" content and deliberately do not import a specific shaping mechanism.

I have scored this **supports** at **0.74** — the highest of the INV-089 set — on high mapping fidelity (the review's thesis is nearly the claim's premise) from an authoritative Fitzgerald-lab source, tempered by its being a narrative synthesis across heterogeneous and sometimes-inconsistent infant measures rather than one decisive experiment.
