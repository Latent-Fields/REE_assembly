# Ho, Yang, Boddu, Bulkin & Warden (2025) -- Infralimbic parvalbumin neural activity facilitates cued threat avoidance

**eLife, 2025. DOI: 10.7554/eLife.91221. PMID: 40168058.**

What did they do? PV-Cre mice ran a two-way signaled active avoidance task -- a tone gives five seconds to shuttle across a chamber and avoid a footshock. The authors recorded calcium activity from parvalbumin (PV) inhibitory interneurons specifically in infralimbic cortex (IL) with fiber photometry, then silenced those same neurons optogenetically to ask whether the activity is causal rather than incidental.

What did they find? Two clean results, going in opposite directions of usefulness for MECH-357. First, the good news: IL-PV activity rises sharply and specifically during the avoidance movement itself -- not during reward approach, not during ordinary locomotion -- and silencing those neurons delays how fast the animal avoids and makes it freeze longer during the tone. That is about as direct a demonstration of "an infralimbic gate suppresses freezing and releases avoidance" as this literature offers, and it is anatomically the right region: IL, not prelimbic, not a diffuse vmPFC label.

Second, the complication, and it is not a minor one: this IL-PV freeze-suppression signal shows up within tens of trials of the animal's very first shock -- before it has learned to avoid at all -- and its size does not change with further training. MECH-357 does not just claim "an ilPFC-analog suppresses freeze." It stakes its falsifiability on suppression strength tracking a slowly-accumulated, learned avoidance-efficacy trace, specifically so it can be told apart from the reflexive, arousal-driven overrides already in REE (SD-037's orexin exit-threshold raise, MECH-281's urgency interrupt). A gate that appears almost immediately and stays flat across learning looks, on this evidence, more like the reflexive family MECH-357 is defined in contrast to than like the gradually-learned gate MECH-357 actually proposes.

So the honest read is mixed, not a clean win. The paper is strong evidence that SOME infralimbic circuit does exactly the job MECH-357's freeze-suppression piece describes -- but it is evidence against the specific claim that this job is done by a learned scalar rather than an early, largely pre-wired switch. It does not rule out a learned contribution living somewhere else (a different interneuron class, a slower synaptic process feeding the same gate) -- the authors flag SOM and VIP interneurons as unexamined -- but as far as this single population goes, "learned, not reflexive" did not hold up.

Caveats worth keeping in view: this is mouse, not rat or primate; the task is a two-way shuttle-avoidance paradigm, structurally simpler than REE's foraging-under-hazard survival leg; and "no learning effect" here is an absence-of-effect claim about one specific neural population's signal, not a comprehensive test of every candidate learned pathway into the same behavioral gate. Confidence is set at 0.72 -- high on how well the anatomy and behavior map onto MECH-357's pieces (a) and (b), lower where the specific "learned, not reflexive" prediction is directly tested and does not survive.
