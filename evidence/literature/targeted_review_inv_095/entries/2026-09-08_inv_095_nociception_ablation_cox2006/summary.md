# Cox et al. (2006) -- SCN9A channelopathy and the non-redundancy of the harm channel

## What the paper did

Cox and colleagues studied six children from three consanguineous families in northern Pakistan who
shared a phenotype the authors describe as "the complete inability to sense pain in an otherwise
healthy individual." They mapped the trait to chromosome 2q24.3 under an autosomal-recessive model,
identified three distinct homozygous nonsense mutations in `SCN9A` -- which encodes the
alpha-subunit of the voltage-gated sodium channel Na(v)1.7, strongly expressed in nociceptive
neurons -- and confirmed in heterologous expression that the mutant channels were functionally dead:
currents "were no greater than background." The conclusion is stated in strong terms: `SCN9A` is
"an essential and non-redundant requirement for nociception in humans."

What makes this study unusually valuable for INV-095 is not the genetics but the specificity of the
lesion. Each affected child "could correctly perceive the sensations of touch, warm and cold
temperature, proprioception, tickle and pressure, but not painful stimuli." They "were considered of
normal intelligence by their parents and teachers, and by the caring physicians," and "there was no
evidence of a motor or sensory neuropathy." One channel was removed; everything else that could
support protective behaviour was left standing.

## Key findings relevant to the claim

The behavioural consequences are the evidence. All six "had injuries to their lips (some requiring
later plastic surgery) and/or tongue (with loss of the distal third in two cases), caused by biting
themselves in the first 4 yr of life." All "had frequent bruises and cuts, and most had suffered
fractures or osteomyelitis, which were only diagnosed in retrospect because of painless limping or
lack of use of a limb." The index case was a street performer who "placed knives through his arms
and walked on burning coals, but experienced no pain. He died before being seen on his fourteenth
birthday, after jumping off a house roof."

That last sentence is the finding. An architecture with intact perception of touch, temperature,
pressure and proprioception, with normal intelligence, with an intact motor system, and with the
full benefit of caregivers and cultural instruction, nonetheless failed at self-preservation to the
point of death in adolescence -- because one signal was missing. The harm channel was not decorative
in this architecture. Nothing else it had could substitute for it.

## How this translates to REE

INV-095's testable leg asks whether REE's harm/residue machinery -- `z_harm_a`, `z_harm_s`,
`ResidueField`, feeding E3's harm-weighted trajectory scoring -- is functionally load-bearing for
commitment-relevant behaviour, or whether it is architecturally decorative and could be swapped for
noise with no behavioural consequence. The axiom's own text makes the prediction explicit: "if
existence had no value, harm signals would be noise rather than information."

Cox et al. supply the strongest available biological instance of the confirming side of that
contrast, and they supply it with a specificity control that a designed ablation struggles to
match. The natural experiment removes the harm channel and holds the rest of perception fixed, which
is precisely the comparison V3-EXQ-533 attempted and failed to make cleanly. Read against ARC-024's
existing entries -- Mobbs (2007), Fanselow (2022), Seymour (2004), which established that the
threat signal is spatially and temporally *graded* rather than binary -- this entry adds the
complementary half: the graded signal is also *necessary*. A structured signal that could be
deleted without behavioural cost would still be decorative.

## Limitations and confidence reasoning

The honest boundary is that this is the wrong manipulation for the falsifier as written. INV-095
specifies replacing the harm signal with distribution-matched random noise; Cox et al. describe
deleting it. Those come apart in a way that matters: deletion shows the channel is necessary, but
does not isolate whether the behavioural work is done by the signal's *informational content* or
merely by its *presence*. A REE experiment substituting noise is asking the sharper question, and
this paper does not answer it. It establishes only that the weaker claim -- the channel is not
inert -- holds in at least one existence-preserving architecture.

Two further caveats. First, the transfer is analogical rather than homologous: human Na(v)1.7 is a
peripheral afferent transduction channel, while REE's harm gradient is a learned model-side signal
positioned downstream, feeding trajectory evaluation. Similar function, different architectural
location. Second, Weisman, Quintner and Masharawi (2019) have argued that "congenital insensitivity
to pain" is a misnomer and that residual nociceptive-affective capacity survives in this population;
if they are right, the ablation is less complete than the canonical reading assumes, which would
weaken rather than reverse the inference.

Confidence 0.72. Source quality is near the ceiling -- linkage, three independent nonsense
mutations, electrophysiological confirmation, and a clinical description detailed enough to audit.
Mapping fidelity is the limiting term at 0.62, because the claim is architectural and the paper is
empirical about a different manipulation. I have weighted mapping fidelity heavily here, per the
calibration guidance for architectural claims: this is close to decisive evidence for its own
proposition and analogically informative for REE's.
