# Hudon & Stip (2025) — the named anchor, resolved; useful vocabulary, wrong topology

**Claim tested:** Q-096 (does INV-077's evaluation-channel-integrity discipline generalise to an ordinary human-AI conversational dyad?)
**Direction:** mixed

## Why this one is here at all

Partly for provenance. Q-096's registered source list reaches this paper indirectly: it points at the sibling repo `Latent-Fields/ai-cognitive-failure-taxonomy`, whose `docs/psychopathology_interaction.md` cites Hudon & Stip 2025 among its case-literature anchors. Pulling it in makes the chain checkable from inside the evidence record.

## What the paper did

A Viewpoint in *JMIR Mental Health* from a Montréal psychiatric group, introducing "AI psychosis" as a framework for how prolonged chatbot engagement might trigger or intensify psychotic symptoms in susceptible people. It offers four analytical lenses — AI as a psychosocial stressor; the digital therapeutic alliance; theory-of-mind disturbances enabling misattribution of sentience; and environmental risk factors — and recommends five domains of research and clinical action, including longitudinal study, embedded safeguards, and ethical governance frameworks.

## What transfers, and what does not

Two of the four lenses earn their keep.

The **theory-of-mind** lens is the more valuable. Misattribution of sentience names the specific step by which a user comes to read the system's assent as an *independent second opinion* rather than as an echo of their own framing returned to them in more fluent prose. Q-096's loop cannot close without that misreading — if assent were correctly perceived as an echo, no confirmation would accrue — and this is the clearest naming of it I found.

The **digital therapeutic alliance** lens supplies an uncomfortable corollary. The trust that makes the system useful is the same trust that suspends scepticism about it. If that is right, usefulness and epistemic risk are not cleanly separable by tuning, and "just make it less agreeable" is not a complete remedy. That is worth holding onto when reading the Cheng et al. result, which shows users actively prefer the sycophantic system.

What does *not* transfer is the paper's dominant framing, and I think this is a real mismatch rather than a difference of emphasis. The stressor model is one-directional: the AI acts *on* a vulnerable individual. Q-096 is explicitly about a bidirectional loop, in which the human's restatement of an AI-generated framing becomes the premise the AI conditions on next. Neither party is the agent and neither is the patient. A one-directional model will not represent that, which is why I filed this `mixed` rather than `supports` — it gives good vocabulary for the coupling while modelling the wrong topology.

## Limitations, stated plainly

This is the weakest source in the pull and should be read that way. It is a Viewpoint with no primary data at all — weaker than the Morrin Personal View, which at least synthesises case material — so it contributes framing and provenance and nothing evidential about prevalence, magnitude, or whether any of this happens outside susceptible populations.

The population caveat is identical to Morrin's and applies with equal force: Q-096 asks about an ordinary dyad; this is scoped to psychotic vulnerability. And I would enter one further reservation. "AI psychosis" is a contested and arguably premature coinage. It should not be imported into REE's vocabulary as though it named an established entity, and nothing in this entry should be read as REE endorsing the nosology.
