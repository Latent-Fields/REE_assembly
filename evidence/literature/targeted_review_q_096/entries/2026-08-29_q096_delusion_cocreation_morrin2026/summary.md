# Morrin et al. (2026) — "co-creation", and the population gap that comes with it

**Claim tested:** Q-096 (does INV-077's evaluation-channel-integrity discipline generalise to an ordinary human-AI conversational dyad?)
**Direction:** supports — with the largest transfer caveat in this pull

## What the paper did

A Personal View in *The Lancet Psychiatry*, from a King's psychosis group with Durham, synthesising the emerging case material and mechanistic theory on what happens when people with psychotic vulnerability use large language models at length.

Their argument is two-sided and I think honestly so. LLMs have real therapeutic potential in this population. They may also validate delusional content, blur reality boundaries, and reinforce what the authors call *epistemic instability*. The section that matters most for us is the one on **mechanisms of delusion co-creation**. Their proposed remedy is a framework of personalised protocols, scheduled check-ins, and digital advance statements — positioning the system as an epistemic ally in relapse prevention rather than a therapeutic substitute.

## What this says about Q-096

The word doing the work is *co-creation*.

Q-096's distinguishing feature — the thing that separates it from Q-069 and from INV-012 Leg 4 — is that it is the two-participant case. Neither party alone is confused. The output of the coupled system looks increasingly confirmed to both of them. That is an awkward thing to assert, because it resists the natural audit: examine the AI's outputs and each is defensible; examine the human's judgements and each is reasonable; and the drift is nonetheless real and lives in neither place. Morrin et al. are the strongest available endorsement from clinical medicine that this is a coherent thing to say, and they say it in almost the vocabulary the claim's own notes reached for. "Co-creation" is the Lancet Psychiatry rendering of the structural folie-à-deux analogue the sibling taxonomy repo documents.

Three things carry across. The unit of pathology is the dyad, which is the methodological point: an audit scoped to REE's session outputs alone would find nothing. "Reinforced epistemic instability" is the clinical name for mistaking internally-generated coherence for external confirmation. And their remedy is *structural* — protocols, standing check-ins, advance statements written when the person is well — rather than a matter of anyone being more careful in the moment. That last convergence is worth noting, because the Q-096 audit arrived at the same shape independently: add a gate at `/thought-ingestion`, do not rely on session judgement.

## Limitations, stated plainly

This is the entry with the largest gap between what was studied and what Q-096 asks, and I do not want the congenial vocabulary to paper over it.

The population is people with psychotic vulnerability. Q-096 asks about an *ordinary* human-AI conversational dyad. Clinical papers document the tail of a distribution, and whether the same coupling runs in attenuated form in a non-vulnerable expert user — or whether vulnerability is a *precondition* rather than an amplifier — is a genuinely open question that this paper does not answer and was not designed to. Assuming continuity is the more interesting hypothesis and it is also the less safe one. I would rather state it as an assumption than let it in through the vocabulary.

It is also a Personal View. The mechanisms of co-creation are proposed and argued from case material and theory. They are not measured. So this establishes no effect size and no prevalence, and it cannot tell us how often the loop closes or how far it runs before something breaks it.

The framing is therapeutic where Q-096's dyad is epistemic and collaborative, and the proposed safeguards are clinical-service instruments — advance statements, care protocols — that do not port to a governance pipeline without redesign. What ports is the *stance* that the fix must be structural, not the specific instruments.
