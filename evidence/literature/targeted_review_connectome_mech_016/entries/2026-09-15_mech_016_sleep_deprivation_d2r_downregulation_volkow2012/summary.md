# Volkow et al. (2012) -- a night without sleep downregulates ventral striatal D2/D3 receptors

**What they did.** Volkow and colleagues had already shown that sleep deprivation lowers [11C]raclopride binding in human striatum. What they could not say was whether that meant more dopamine competing for the receptor or fewer receptors. Here 20 healthy adults were scanned after rested sleep and after one night of sleep deprivation, each time on placebo and on methylphenidate. The logic: if deprivation raises dopamine release, methylphenidate's dopamine-boosting effect should be bigger when deprived.

**What they found.** Deprivation again reduced D2/D3 availability in ventral striatum, tracking lower alertness and higher sleepiness. The methylphenidate-induced dopamine increase, however, did not differ between conditions. The simplest reading is receptor downregulation. Rodent microdialysis after paradoxical sleep deprivation pointed the same way.

**How it maps to MECH-016.** The claim casts dopamine as the commitment channel and says sleep rebalances commitment against exploration. This paper shows that the dopaminergic gain apparatus in humans (postsynaptic sensitivity in the ventral striatum, the node most tied to motivated commitment) moves measurably within one night when sleep is withheld. If a gain drifts when the offline phase is removed, the offline phase is a good candidate for what normally keeps it in range.

**The honest limitation** is the deprivation inference itself. Knowing that something goes wrong without sleep does not tell us that sleep does something constructive; sleep might simply stop the waking process that produces the drift. Does 'recalibration' mean active restoration toward a set-point, or only the absence of further erosion? MECH-016's wording implies the former, and a deprivation design cannot separate the two. The behavioural correlate was also sleepiness, not choice behaviour, and receptor availability is a proxy for gain rather than a measure of it.

**Confidence 0.50.** Solid human data, clearly on the neuromodulatory substrate MECH-016 names, discounted for inferential distance.
