# Feher et al. (2026) -- a nap recalibrates cortical synaptic gain and plasticity in humans

**What they did.** Feher and colleagues (Nissen group, Geneva/Freiburg) brought 20 healthy adults into the sleep laboratory for an adaptation session and then two experimental afternoons: a one-hour nap, or an hour awake. Afterward they measured net cortical synaptic strength non-invasively, as the TMS intensity needed to evoke motor potentials and as waking EEG theta, and they measured LTP-like plasticity as the MEP increase after paired associative stimulation.

**What they found.** After the nap it took more TMS intensity to evoke an MEP, and wake theta was lower, which indexes reduced net synaptic strength. PAS also produced a larger MEP increase, so associative plasticity was more inducible. Nocturnal sleep was already known to do both. The finding here is that a short afternoon nap is enough.

**How it maps to MECH-016.** The architecture note speaks of sleep 'restoring entropy where collapse occurred too early' and 'reducing excessive rigidity'. At the synaptic level that is roughly what this study shows. Gain that accumulated during waking gets turned down, and the room for new learning returns. If waking saturates the system and sleep restores dynamic range, an REE agent without an offline reset should show creeping gain and falling learnability. That is a testable failure signature.

**The caution** is conceptual. Excitability and LTP inducibility are not precision weights on prediction error. The authors' word 'recalibrate' is tempting, but what they recalibrate is synaptic gain and plasticity headroom. That places the finding squarely in synaptic homeostasis territory (MECH-120), and it does not touch the monoaminergic channels MECH-016 names. It supports the family resemblance, sleep as a gain reset, more than the specific mechanism. The study is also small, recent and unreplicated, with no behavioural readout of rigidity or exploration.

**Confidence 0.45.**
