## Schlichting & Preston 2015 -- memory integration via overlapping populations (grounds the allocation gate's 'integrate' pole)

According to PubMed, Schlichting and Preston's 2015 review in *Current Opinion in Behavioral Sciences* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cobeha.2014.07.005)) is the most directly on-point of the biology entries for the *decision* content of MECH-391, because it is about the mechanism by which related memories become linked rather than kept apart. Their account: everyday flexible behaviour requires applying prior knowledge to new situations, and this is supported by *memory integration* -- related memories becoming interconnected through the recruitment of overlapping neuronal populations. A hippocampal-medial-prefrontal circuit carries this; abstracted representations in mPFC guide the reactivation of related memories during new encoding, so that related experiences are integrated in the hippocampus and can later be recombined for navigation, inference, and imagination.

MECH-391 is, at its core, an allocation gate that chooses among *integrate / partial_overlap / separate* for a new experience against the existing memory graph. Schlichting & Preston ground the substrate of the `integrate` choice and the `schema_fit` input to the policy: the overlapping-population mechanism is the actuator that an allocation decision to "link this trace to those" would engage, and the mPFC schema representations are exactly the abstracted prior knowledge the policy would consult when judging schema fit. This is higher-fidelity mapping than the systems-consolidation entries because it is about the link/no-link operation itself.

The honest limit -- and the reason I have written an explicit failure signature -- is that this paper grounds only *one pole*. It documents the mechanism and the benefit of integration; it does not say when integration should be *withheld*, and it does not price the cost of linking things that should not be linked. That one-sidedness is the same caveat the allocation-gate lit verdict already recorded against Sahay 2011 for INV-079: the literature evidences the value of linking and the value of separating, but the *false-linking cost* (over-integration as the substrate of false association / delusion) has no clean empirical home. So Schlichting & Preston strengthen the integrate side of the gate without resolving the policy that balances it against separation.

I rate this `supports` at 0.73 -- good source quality and the highest mapping fidelity in this set for the gate's decision content, discounted because it grounds the integrate pole alone and leaves the decision policy and the false-linking price as the novel REE overlay. `exp_conf` stays 0; promotes nothing.
