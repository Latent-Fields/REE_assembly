## Sekeres, Winocur & Moscovitch 2018 -- memory transformation loses detail (grounds INV-080's threat model, not its remedy)

According to PubMed, Sekeres, Winocur, Moscovitch and colleagues' 2018 *Hippocampus* paper ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.23009)) is the empirical backbone of Trace Transformation Theory, and it is the most important entry for INV-080 because it pins down, across species, *what consolidation actually does to a memory's content*. Using rat context-fear memory and human naturalistic episodic memory with behaviour and fMRI, they show that memories lose precision over time: context-specific and perceptually-detailed memories become less precise and more schematic, hippocampal engagement decreases as that detail is lost, and the medial prefrontal cortex is engaged increasingly during remote retrieval. Critically, when detail *is* retrieved at remote timepoints the hippocampus is re-engaged -- so consolidation is a *transformation* from detailed to schematic, not the production of a faithful copy.

This is the cleanest available statement of the failure mode INV-080 is written to forbid. INV-080 says a consolidated abstraction must retain a pointer to, and must not delete, the episodes it abstracts. Sekeres et al. show why that rule is necessary: the natural trajectory of a memory under consolidation is to *shed* its episodic detail in favour of gist. In MECH-391's lifecycle vocabulary, the `summarised` and `consolidated` states are lossy by construction -- the schematic remote memory is not the detailed episode, and the detail does not reliably survive the transition. A memory system that let the schematic output overwrite its source would be doing, deliberately and permanently, what biology does gradually.

But this is exactly why I have rated the entry `mixed` rather than `supports`, and the distinction is the load-bearing one for INV-080. The paper grounds the *threat* -- transformation degrades episodic detail -- but it provides no evidence of a *remedy*. There is no demonstration here that the brain keeps a recoverable raw episode behind the schematic gist; if anything, the finding is that detail is genuinely lost (the hippocampus only re-engages when detail can still be retrieved at all). So INV-080 should be read honestly as a *normative engineering rule that departs from the biological default*: REE chooses to retain the source pointer that biology often discards. The biology warrants the risk the invariant addresses; it does not mandate the invariant's prescription. That is the right way for a design rule to relate to its biological motivation, and recording it is the point of biology-before-formal-definitions.

I rate this 0.66, `mixed`, tagged primarily to INV-080 (threat model) and secondarily to MECH-391 (the lossy `summarised`/`consolidated` states). Source quality is high (cross-species, converging methods); the discount reflects that it grounds the problem, not the solution. `exp_conf` stays 0; promotes nothing.
