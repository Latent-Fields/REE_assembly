## Richards & Frankland 2017 -- transience is adaptive (grounds the 'retired' state, and sharpens INV-080)

According to PubMed, Richards and Frankland's 2017 *Neuron* review ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2017.04.037)) is the entry that keeps the memory-lifecycle cluster honest, because it argues the case *against* preservation as an end in itself. The predominant focus in memory neuroscience has been on remembering; Richards & Frankland press the neurobiology of *forgetting*. Their thesis is that transience is not a bug or a decay artefact but an adaptive function: forgetting (1) enhances flexibility by reducing the influence of outdated information on memory-guided decisions, and (2) prevents overfitting to specific past events, thereby promoting generalisation. They draw the parallel to machine learning explicitly -- transience is regularisation. The provocative conclusion is that the goal of memory is not faithful transmission of information through time; it is to optimise decision-making, and for that goal controlled forgetting is as important as persistence.

For MECH-391's lifecycle store, this straightforwardly grounds the `retired` state. Retirement is not the absence of a memory; it is a *designed, regulated operation* that the system performs because retaining everything is maladaptive. A six-state lifecycle that includes `retired` as a first-class state -- something a memory is deliberately moved into -- has its biological warrant here. That part is `supports`.

For INV-080 the relationship is more interesting, and it is why I tagged the entry to both claims and marked it `mixed`. INV-080 says consolidation_output must not replace source_episode_evidence -- an abstraction must not delete the episodes it abstracts. Read naively, that collides head-on with Richards & Frankland: if forgetting is adaptive, an invariant forbidding all deletion would itself be a failure mode, hoarding outdated and overfit detail and degrading exactly the decision-making memory is supposed to serve. The resolution -- and the design correction this entry forces -- is that INV-080 cannot mean "never forget". It must mean "no *silent, ungoverned* overwrite or loss of the evidence base". Retirement is permitted; what is forbidden is abstraction that destroys its source *without provenance, without a pointer, without the option of rollback*. The `retired` state and the provenance layer (MECH-392) are what let REE have principled transience while still satisfying INV-080. So this paper does not just support the invariant; it sharpens it, and the sharpening is the substantive contribution.

I rate this 0.64, `mixed`. High source quality; the discount and the mixed direction reflect that it both grounds the retired state and deliberately complicates a naive reading of the preservation invariant. `exp_conf` stays 0; promotes nothing.
