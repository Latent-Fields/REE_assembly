## Frankland & Bontempi 2005 -- systems consolidation as a staged, multi-state process (grounds MECH-391's lifecycle premise)

According to PubMed, Frankland and Bontempi's 2005 *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* review ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn1607)) is the canonical synthesis of how a memory's substrate home *changes over time*. Everyday episodic memories in humans depend initially on the medial temporal lobe, the hippocampus chief among them; as those memories mature they come to depend increasingly on distributed cortical networks. The review's contribution is to treat this not as a single fact but as a *staged* transformation -- recent and remote memories are organised differently, and the molecular and cellular events that carry a trace from one organisation to the next are themselves distinct.

The reason this grounds MECH-391 is upstream of any allocation-gate detail. MECH-391, and the memory-lifecycle store the MEM-8 node tracks, rest on a prior commitment: that memory is not a flat archive but a thing that occupies *distinct states over its lifetime*, each with its own substrate and its own operators. Frankland & Bontempi supply exactly that warrant. A trace that is hippocampus-indexed today and cortically-consolidated a month from now is, in REE's vocabulary, a trace that has moved from an `indexed` to a `consolidated` state -- and the biology says that movement is real, staged, and substrate-distinct, not a metaphor.

What the paper does *not* supply is the part REE adds. It does not enumerate six discrete labels, and it certainly does not describe a control-plane that *decides* whether a new experience should integrate with, partially overlap, or stay separate from existing traces. Those are the genuinely novel aspects of MECH-391 (verdict G1 in the allocation-gate intake), and no amount of systems-consolidation biology mints them for free. So I have set `mapping_fidelity` mid: the staging premise is strongly grounded, the specific state taxonomy and the policy are not.

I rate this `supports` at 0.74. The source quality is as high as this literature offers (a definitive NRN review), and the premise it grounds -- memory as a multi-state, time-dependent lifecycle -- is exactly the foundation MECH-391 is built on. The discount is honest: this is grounding for the *shape* of the claim, not its decision content. `exp_conf` stays 0; MECH-391 remains a `substrate_conditional` V4 candidate. This raises literature confidence only and promotes nothing.
