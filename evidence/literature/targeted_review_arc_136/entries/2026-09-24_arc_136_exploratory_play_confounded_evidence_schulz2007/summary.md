# Schulz & Bonawitz 2007 -- "Serious fun"

**What they did.** Preschoolers watched an experimenter demonstrate a two-lever toy. In one condition the demonstration was confounded (both levers pressed together, so it was unclear which did what). In the other it was not. Children were then offered that toy and a new one. After the confounded demonstration they chose the familiar toy over the new one, played with it more, and in their free play worked out which lever did what. After the unconfounded demonstration they preferred the new toy.

**Why it matters for ARC-136.** This is the information-sensitive-play anchor the claim's notes name (Gopnik/Schulz). It shows three things the claim needs. Play with no extrinsic payoff is aimed at a particular kind of state, one with an ambiguity that can be resolved. It is not novelty seeking: the children passed over a new toy to go back to the ambiguous one. And the play actually resolved the ambiguity. That is the claim's anti-randomness discriminator and its "learnable uncertainty" condition, in children, at the causal rung of the affordance ladder.

**Where it doesn't reach.** One age group, one session, one kind of ambiguity. It says nothing about how the play frontier moves over development. The ambiguity was set up by an adult, and children bring language and rich priors to it. For REE, this implies the ecology needs states whose uncertainty the agent can reduce by its own actions. Pure noise won't do, and neither will novelty.

**Confidence.** 0.64.
