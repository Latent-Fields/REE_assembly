# Pellis, Pellis, Ham & Stark 2023 -- play fighting and the social brain

**What it says.** Rats deprived of peer play as juveniles grow into adults with socio-cognitive deficits, and their medial and orbital prefrontal neurons are changed (less dendritic pruning). The detailed analysis the review summarises shows the benefit does not come from practising particular moves. It comes from the constant adjustments needed to keep play fighting reciprocal, so that it stays play and does not become a fight. What is trained is a general skill set that transfers to social and non-social contexts. A 2026 follow-up from the same lab (Ham, Iwaniuk & Pellis, Behav Brain Res, doi 10.1016/j.bbr.2026.116198) found that about a fifth of well-played rats still fail to get the benefit, with the same under-pruned prefrontal profile.

**Why it cuts both ways for ARC-136.** For the claim: play clearly does developmental work, and what it trains is general and transferable, which suits the claim's reading of play as the organism learning where it can. It also gives the claim its social rung. Against the claim: the key ingredient is reciprocity, a social-calibration mechanism that a learning-progress drive does not predict. And rodent social play has its own reward machinery, which looks like the dedicated play motivation ARC-136 says is unnecessary.

**How I read it.** The claim already disowns "play equals information gain" and allows bonding, practice and regulation, so this isn't a falsification. But it suggests the "no dedicated module" clause may hold for object and causal play (Schulz, Oudeyer) and fail for social play. The claim could be sharpened along that line.

**Confidence.** 0.52, recorded as `mixed`.
