# Ten, Kaushik, Oudeyer & Gottlieb 2021 -- humans monitor learning progress

**What they did.** Participants could switch freely between four learning activities of different difficulty, one of which could not be learned at all. The authors then compared models of how people chose what to work on next. People used competence (percent correct) to leave tasks that were too easy. But the models that fit best also included a learning-progress term: people kept at hard tasks they were improving on and left the unlearnable one instead of laboring at it.

**Why it matters for ARC-136.** The claim says play-like exploration samples *learnable* uncertainty, not whatever is most novel or most random. That is the anti-randomness discriminator in leg (1) of the falsifier, and this paper measures it in people. It also tells us what kind of intrinsic drive REE would need. A raw-novelty or pure uncertainty-reduction drive would be pulled toward the unlearnable task. A learning-progress term is not. That matters because the claim notes SD-025's existing drive might make the discriminator testable in V3, and this paper names the property the drive would have to have.

**Where it doesn't reach.** These are adults in a short lab task they were told to do. It evidences the rule for choosing what to learn, not spontaneous play, and it says nothing about safety or satiety gating or about migration across affordance spaces. Competence also mattered, so a learning-progress-only drive would be too simple.

**Confidence.** 0.70.
