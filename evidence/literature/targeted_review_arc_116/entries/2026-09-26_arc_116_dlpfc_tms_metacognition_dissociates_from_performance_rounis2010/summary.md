# Rounis, Maniscalco, Rothwell, Passingham & Lau (2010) -- Theta-burst TMS to prefrontal cortex impairs metacognitive visual awareness

**What they did.** Participants made masked visual discriminations and rated how visible each stimulus was. Theta-burst TMS was then used to depress bilateral dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, and the task was repeated. The analysis used signal detection theory, to separate metacognitive *sensitivity* (do the ratings track being right?) from mere shifts in how liberally people rated.

**What they found.** After DLPFC stimulation, discrimination was unchanged, but the visibility ratings tracked correctness less well. People reported lower visibility on trials they had in fact got right. So the readout degraded while the generating performance held.

**What it means for ARC-116.** This is the shape of result ARC-116 needs, turned around. The claim's falsifier says: degrade the reconstruction and see whether the readout survives. Rounis et al. degrade the readout and show the generation survives. Either direction of dissociation is evidence that two stages are distinct computations rather than one thing described twice. It also gives the REE falsifier a methodological template: a matched first-order performance, an SDT-style measure of readout quality, and a targeted perturbation.

**Why the confidence is low.** Bor, Schwartzman, Barrett and Seth (2017) tried hard to replicate this and could not. They added an active control site and ran a stricter within-subjects design. Their diagnosis was that the original effect depended on including data unsuitable for SDT analysis. I have entered that paper separately as weakening. The honest position is that this particular causal dissociation is unsettled. A further caveat: visibility ratings are reports, so what degraded may be partly the reconstruction-of-confidence rather than the felt readout itself. The paper cannot tell stages 2 and 3 apart, and those are exactly the stages ARC-116 cares most about separating.

**Confidence.** 0.45: the right kind of evidence, not yet trustworthy evidence.
