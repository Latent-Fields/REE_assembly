# Johansson, Hall, Sikstrom & Olsson (2005) -- Failure to detect mismatches between intention and outcome

**What they did.** People looked at pairs of faces and pointed to the more attractive one. On some trials the experimenter, by sleight of hand, gave them the face they had rejected and asked why they had chosen it.

**What they found.** Most of the swaps went unnoticed. And people explained their "choice" anyway, fluently, often citing features of the face they had in fact rejected. The authors call this choice blindness. It is the modern, controlled version of Nisbett and Wilson's old point that we tell more than we can know.

**What it means for ARC-116.** The claim says verbal reconstruction is a stage of its own, and that it may not substitute for the generating dynamics. Choice blindness is the clinical demonstration of why. The reconstruction takes the outcome in front of it and makes a story. It does not consult some privileged record of how the choice was made. As a psychiatrist I recognise the shape: confabulation is what reconstruction looks like when the check against generation fails. Here the check fails in healthy people, in a low-stakes task. The safety corollary ARC-116 draws also finds support. If an agent's confidence were derived from its own narrative, it would carry the narrative's unearned fluency. Choice-blind reasons do not sound less sure than real ones.

**Limits.** No independent readout was measured. We don't know whether these participants' felt confidence, probed nonverbally, would have flagged the swapped trials. That is precisely the ARC-116 prediction, and it is untested here. Later work in this paradigm has looked at confidence and detection rates, but this paper does not. Face attractiveness is also a domain where the generating signal is weak, and that flatters the effect.

**Confidence.** 0.55. Strong, replicated source for the stage-1/stage-3 gap; the stage-2 leg is inferred, not measured.
