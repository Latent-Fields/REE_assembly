# Hobeika et al. (2016) -- one general region, several specialised ones

## What the paper does

A coordinate-based meta-analysis of 27 functional imaging experiments on analogical reasoning. The design question is the one Q-083 asks: when a person maps a relation from one body of knowledge onto another, is there a single machine doing it, or one machine per kind of content? The meta-analysis contrasts semantic analogy against visuospatial analogy, and separately contrasts visuospatial analogy against matrix problems.

Two findings matter here. Left rostrolateral prefrontal cortex comes out as the most consistent activation across the whole set, and survives the semantic-versus-visuospatial contrast as domain-general and analogy-specific. But inferior and middle frontal gyri come out domain-oriented, and -- the finding I find most instructive -- visuospatial analogy and matrix problems engage partly distinct left/right networks and *separate subregions within left RLPFC*.

## The mapping to Q-083

Q-083 registers a fork: separate regularity-apprehension systems per domain, or one domain-general apprehender that inherits domain structure from its inputs. This paper is the imaging literature's answer, and the answer is "both, at different levels". There is a shared resource. It is genuinely shared across two quite different content types. It also sits on top of machinery that is not shared.

Read against the previous entry in this folder (Frost et al. 2015), the two converge from different methods on the same architectural shape: a common computation, per-domain instantiation. That convergence is worth something, because the methods fail in different ways -- behavioural individual-differences correlations and coordinate-based meta-analysis have close to nothing in common as sources of error.

For REE the implication is a design constraint rather than an answer: if one apprehension stage is to be shared across domains, the per-domain input encoders are not an implementation detail to be filled in later. They are where the biology puts most of the structure.

## What this cannot do

It cannot answer Q-083 as Q-083 is actually posed. The claim's `what_would_answer` is a transfer test -- does apprehending a rule in one domain accelerate apprehension in another under matched structure -- and co-activation is not transfer. A region that lights up for both semantic and visuospatial analogy is entirely compatible with two adjacent, functionally independent circuits that fMRI cannot separate. The paper hands me the demonstration of that limit itself: at finer resolution, within left RLPFC, visuospatial analogy and matrix problems come apart. If a within-domain dissociation appears the moment you look more closely, I should be slow to read the cross-domain co-activation as evidence of one machine.

Two further boundaries. The domains contrasted are semantic and visuospatial; Q-083 names causal, social, ethical, motor, spatial and relational, and the two that are hardest for the domain-general arm -- social and ethical -- appear nowhere in this dataset. And these are healthy adults performing explicit reasoning tasks, which is a narrower thing than the pre-linguistic regularity apprehension Q-083 is deliberately scoped to include.

## Confidence

0.72. The source is strong and the contrast is the right one, but mapping fidelity is the limiter: this is localisation evidence for a claim posed in terms of transfer, and I have weighted mapping fidelity heavily because Q-083 is architectural. It moves me toward the hybrid reading without settling it, and it does nothing about the substrate block -- V3 still has one domain and no transfer test can be run in it.
