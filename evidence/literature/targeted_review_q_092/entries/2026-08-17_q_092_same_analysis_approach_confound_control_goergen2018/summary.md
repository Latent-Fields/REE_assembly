# The same analysis approach (Görgen, Hebart, Allefeld & Haynes, 2018)

Q-092 specifies two guards beyond its permutation null: the held-out accuracy must exceed the same pipeline run on a matched untrained/random-policy control, and the discriminator must remain selectively sensitive to the reef axis under an orthogonal nuisance perturbation. Those are good instincts. This paper is the general statement of the principle behind them, and reading it makes me think Q-092 has got two-thirds of the way to a design that would actually protect itself.

Görgen and colleagues argue that novel analysis methods carry failure modes that cannot be predicted from the experimental design, and that the remedy is procedural rather than theoretical: apply **the same analysis pipeline** to things where you know what the answer should be -- the design variables themselves, simulated confounds, null data, and control data -- and see what the pipeline says anyway. They demonstrate it catching two real error classes in multivariate decoding. The first is a mismatch between counterbalancing (crossover designs) and cross-validation, which produces *systematically below-chance* accuracies. The second is linear decoding of a nonlinear effect: a classifier separating conditions on a **difference in variance** rather than a difference in means.

Both of those are reachable by Q-092's design, and I think the second is the more dangerous.

Consider the variance case concretely. Reef-OFF episodes plausibly have higher trajectory variance than reef-ON episodes -- no refuge structure to organise behaviour around, so excursions wander more. A linear classifier over trajectory-segment features will happily separate a high-variance class from a low-variance class. That separation is real, replicable, and will clear a label-permutation null decisively. It will also survive the matched untrained-policy control, because an untrained policy in a reef environment does not necessarily reproduce the *trained* variance asymmetry. And it means nothing more than "one condition is more variable than the other", which is emphatically not the "context-sensitive behavioural differentiation" Q-092 is asking about. This is the failure mode I would bet on if Q-092 returns a strong PASS, and it is cheaply detectable -- variance-match the classes, or report per-class feature variance alongside the accuracy.

The below-chance case is subtler and bears on Q-092's *falsifying* signature specifically. Q-092 currently reads a failure to clear the permutation null as evidence against the methodology detecting structure. But Görgen et al. show that a fold-structure mismatch produces accuracies that are systematically *below* chance -- a positively broken pipeline, not an absence of signal. If REE episodes are blocked by seed or by curriculum stage and the cross-validation folds are cut across that structure, Q-092 could report a clean-looking null that is actually a bug. The claim's falsifying signature would absorb it silently. The fix is the third instance of the Same Analysis Approach that Q-092 does not currently have: run the identical pipeline on **null data and on the design variables** (classify episodes by seed, or by fold index, where the true answer is chance) before trusting either direction of the real result.

So the bearing on Q-092 is `supports`, and specifically supports the parts of the design that are already there -- the matched control and the orthogonal-perturbation check are exactly this method, arrived at independently, which is some evidence the design is sound. But the support comes with a concrete, buildable addition rather than a pat on the head. All three additions surfaced across this Q-092 pull (an episode-budget power calculation, a within-class feature permutation, and null/design-variable control runs) are `complicated (buildable)` refinements to the experiment as specified, not new unknowns. None of them requires a probe; all of them are cheap relative to the run itself.

The honest transfer caveat: this is an fMRI methodology paper. Its specific confound catalogue -- scanner drift, run structure, trial counterbalancing -- does not transfer literally to an episode-level behavioural classifier, and its concrete recommendations are not directly executable in the REE setting. What transfers is the principle and the two named error classes. I have set transfer risk at 0.20 to reflect that, which is higher than the other two Q-092 entries precisely because this one is the least domain-general of the three.

Confidence 0.83. Source quality 0.87 (NeuroImage, worked demonstrations on simulated and real data, from a group with a long record on decoding methodology). Mapping fidelity 0.80: it warrants two of Q-092's three guards and prescribes a third the claim lacks.
