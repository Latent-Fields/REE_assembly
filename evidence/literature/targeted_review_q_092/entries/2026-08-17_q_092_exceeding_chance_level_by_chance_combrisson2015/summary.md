# Exceeding chance level by chance (Combrisson & Jerbi, 2015)

Q-092 asks whether a held-out, permutation-tested classifier over trajectory-segment features can detect context-sensitive behavioural differentiation between SD-054 reef-ON and reef-OFF conditions. Its confirming signature has three guards: clear a label-permutation null, exceed a matched untrained-policy control, and remain selectively sensitive to the reef axis under an orthogonal nuisance perturbation. This paper is the methodological warrant for the first guard, and it is a stronger warrant than I expected when I pulled it.

Combrisson and Jerbi did the adversarial thing: they ran classification pipelines on *pure random Gaussian signals*, where by construction there is nothing to decode, and asked what accuracies came out. In two-class problems with small sample sets they observed decoding accuracies of up to 70% or higher. The mechanism is unglamorous and inescapable -- the variance of the observed correct-classification rate scales inversely with sample size, so with few samples the chance that chance performance is (by chance) high becomes substantial. Their prescription is to obtain p-values from the binomial formula or from permutation testing, both of which account for sample size, rather than comparing against the theoretical 50%.

For Q-092 this does two things. The first is straightforwardly confirmatory: it converts "use a permutation null" from a defensible methodological preference into a hard precondition, because the failure mode it describes is exactly the one Q-092's design would hit. REE experiments run modest numbers of held-out episodes. At those sample sizes, an observed accuracy of 65% against a theoretical chance level of 50% is not evidence of anything, and a PASS declared on that basis would be a manufactured result. Q-092 already specifies the permutation null, so this is support rather than correction -- but it is support with teeth, because it means the null is not optional even if the observed accuracy looks comfortable.

The second thing is a constraint Q-092's `what_would_answer` does not currently state. The severity of the inflation is a function of held-out sample size, so the experiment design needs a *number*: how many held-out episodes per condition. A permutation null does control the false-positive rate correctly at any n, so nothing here is unsound -- but at small n the null distribution is wide, the test is underpowered, and a real effect will fail to clear. That is the more likely failure for Q-092 in practice: not a false PASS, but a false FALSIFY driven by an episode budget nobody chose deliberately. Worth fixing at design time, and cheap to fix -- it is `complicated (buildable)`, a power calculation, not a probe.

What this paper does **not** do deserves equal emphasis, and I have recorded it as a failure signature rather than leaving it implicit. A label-permutation null controls for small-sample inflation and for the absence of any label-feature relationship. It does not control for a *genuine* relationship that arises from something other than what you care about. If reef-ON and reef-OFF environments simply look geometrically different, a classifier over trajectory features will clear the permutation null decisively and correctly, and will have told you nothing about policy behaviour. Q-092 already anticipates this with its matched untrained/random-policy control -- which is exactly the right instrument, and is the subject of the Görgen et al. entry in this directory. Clearing this null is necessary and explicitly insufficient.
