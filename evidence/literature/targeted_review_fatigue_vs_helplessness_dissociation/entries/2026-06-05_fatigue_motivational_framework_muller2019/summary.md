# Müller & Apps 2019 — motivational fatigue, and the state/trait scoping line

**Paper.** Müller T, Apps MAJ. *Motivational fatigue: A neurocognitive framework for the impact of effortful exertion on subsequent motivation.* Neuropsychologia 2019;123:141–151. DOI [10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2018.04.030](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2018.04.030).

**What it does.** Müller and Apps propose that fatigue is fundamentally a *motivational* signal rather than an energy-depletion mechanism. Their thesis: levels of fatigue arising from effortful behaviour act on the systems that weigh the costs and benefits of exerting effort, so that as fatigue rises the perceived value of continued effort falls — a cost-benefit recalibration that reduces performance. They locate this in dorsal anterior cingulate cortex, anterior insula, and lateral prefrontal cortex (with basal-ganglia and dopaminergic contributions to effort valuation). Importantly for scoping, they separate two constructs: transient **state fatigue**, which "waxes and wanes during behaviours that require extended periods of cognitive control or motor output, typically impacting on the speed, accuracy or force of behaviours," and persistent **trait fatigue**, "a persistent feeling of exhaustion" of the kind seen in chronic illness and mood disorder.

**Why it is in this review.** It does two jobs the differentiation needs. First, it independently corroborates the Boksem & Tops and Meyniel picture — fatigue is a value/cost signal, not an empty tank — and so reinforces that the fatigue antecedent is effort-expenditure, categorically unlike the uncontrollable-aversion antecedent of helplessness. Second, and this is its distinctive contribution, the **state/trait split gives the V3-vs-V4 scoping line directly**. Transient state fatigue — the recoverable accumulator that rises with exertion and falls with rest — is the V3-minimal target and the thing the smallest computational form (Meyniel's two-bound accumulator) captures. Persistent trait fatigue, and fatigue that has acquired an affective colour shading toward anhedonia and depression, is V4-completeness territory. The framework is explicit that these are different things, which is exactly why a fatigue/stop-recover primitive should *not* be built as an SD-011 harm signal: the moment you wire it as aversive nociception you have imported the trait/clinical end and lost the recoverable, value-modulating state signal that is the actual V3 control need.

**Mapping and caveats.** In REE, this lands as: an effort term that down-weights continued goal pursuit inside the existing E3 scoring / value-arbitration layer, recoverable with rest, feeding the ARC-078 disengagement consumer — the homeostatic (SD-012) side, not the harm (SD-011) side. The caveats: this is a framework/review, not one decisive experiment; the dopamine and basal-ganglia attributions are integrative and do not import into REE, which has no dopaminergic effort module; and state-vs-trait is a useful scoping heuristic rather than a hard boundary. But for the question this directory asks — is fatigue distinct from suffering, and where should it live — the paper's answer is unambiguous: distinct, motivational/homeostatic, recoverable, and only continuous with depression at the trait end that V3 should not yet model.

**Confidence.** 0.74. A strong, well-aimed framework, but review-level rather than primary; mapping fidelity is good for the state/trait scoping and the motivational-not-depletion claim, and transfer risk is moderate because the cost/value framing aligns with REE's arbitration machinery even though the neuromodulatory detail does not transfer.
