# Meyniel et al. 2013 — the smallest computational form of stop-recover

**Paper.** Meyniel F, Sergent C, Rigoux L, Daunizeau J, Pessiglione M. *Neurocomputational account of how the human brain decides when to have a break.* PNAS 2013;110(7):2641–2646. DOI [10.1073/pnas.1211925110](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1211925110).

**What it does.** The authors give humans repeated bouts of effort (handgrip force, and an analogous cognitive task) with freedom to rest whenever they choose, and ask what variable governs the *timing* of stopping and resuming. Their answer is a single scalar they call "cost evidence." It accumulates roughly linearly during effort and dissipates during rest. A break is initiated when the signal reaches an **upper bound**; work resumes when it falls back to a **lower bound**. The model has three identifiable parameters: an accumulation slope Se, a dissipation slope Sr, and the amplitude A between the bounds. fMRI and MEG localise the accumulation/dissipation dynamics to **posterior insula and ventromedial thalamus** — proprioceptive and pain-matrix regions — *not* to reward circuitry or aversive-mood systems. Two manipulations sharpen the picture: increasing task difficulty selectively steepens Se (shorter work bouts, rest untouched), while raising the monetary stake slows Se, speeds Sr, and widens A — letting subjects "work closer to exhaustion" when it is worth it.

**Why it is in this review, and what it gives REE.** This is the load-bearing entry for part (b) of the verdict — the *smallest computational form* of a fatigue/stop-recover control signal. The model is, essentially, the primitive I want REE to register, already written in control-theoretic terms. Concretely: maintain a scalar `F`; during exertion `F += Se * effort_per_step`; during rest/low-effort `F -= Sr`; when `F >= F_upper` emit a stop/disengage signal; permit recovery until `F <= F_lower` before re-engagement. SD-012's existing `drive_level` and the incentive-token strength from the cue-recall work are natural modulators of the slopes and bounds — exactly the difficulty/incentive knobs Meyniel et al. identify. Two features matter for design discipline. First, *stop and resume are different bound-crossings*: a single threshold would lose the "recover" half and could chatter; the hysteresis is the point. Second, *incentive does not switch fatigue off* — it rescales it. A model where enough reward makes fatigue vanish would contradict the data.

**Relationship to MECH-342.** The two-bound, graded, bounded-accumulation, drift-to-a-release-bound structure is the same shape as MECH-342 (commit-maintenance release as a conflict-scaled drift to a release bound). That is the basis for the verdict's recommendation that fatigue *share the MECH-342 decommit consumer*: fatigue becomes a new deficit input feeding the release accumulator, distinct in SIGNAL (homeostatic effort expenditure) from MECH-342's existing R-c execution-readiness deficit, but routed through the same release actuator for the in-task disengage. The *recover* half — re-accumulation toward the lower bound during rest — is not in MECH-342 (which has a reengagement path but no homeostatic recovery integrator) and belongs on the SD-012/SD-017 side.

**Caveats.** This is a minutes-scale, within-task break model fit phenomenologically, not a metabolic measurement; REE has not yet operationalised "effort" (action cost? compute? plan depth?), and Se cannot be set until it does. It also captures the *within-task* accumulator, not the slower sleep-pressure variant (Process S, Borbély 2016 in this directory) — those are two accumulators on different time constants, both homeostatic, not one variable.

**Confidence.** 0.82. PNAS, converging fMRI+MEG, a formal model with recoverable parameters; mapping fidelity is high because the paper's model is the proposed REE primitive; transfer risk is low because the object is already domain-general — the open question is REE's own definition of per-step effort, not cross-species validity.
