# Borbély et al. 2016 — Process S, the homeostatic accumulator with an offline reset

**Paper.** Borbély AA, Daan S, Wirz-Justice A, Deboer T. *The two-process model of sleep regulation: a reappraisal.* Journal of Sleep Research 2016;25(2):131–143. DOI [10.1111/jsr.12371](https://doi.org/10.1111/jsr.12371).

**What it does.** This is the authors' own re-examination, three decades on, of the two-process model of sleep regulation. Sleep is governed by a homeostatic Process S and a circadian Process C. Process S is the part that matters here: it "increases monotonically during wakefulness and decreases during sleep." Its level tracks the *duration of prior wakefulness*; it is indexed physiologically by EEG slow-wave activity, which builds the longer one is awake and dissipates exponentially across non-REM sleep; and it is mechanistically tied to the accumulation of adenosine during waking and its clearance during sleep. The defining property is that Process S is a homeostatic deficit variable whose antecedent is simply *time-on-task / metabolic expenditure* — it rises because you have been awake and active, with no reference to whether experience was aversive or controllable.

**Why it is in this review.** It grounds the *slow* variant of the fatigue/stop-recover signal and supplies the recovery dynamic that the suffering pole lacks. The differentiation has two fatigue accumulators in play, on different time constants: Meyniel's within-task "cost evidence" (minutes; recover by resting) and Process S (hours-to-a-day; recover by *sleeping*). Both are homeostatic, both antecedent-driven by expenditure rather than aversion, and both have an explicit recover phase. That shared structure is the cleanest single argument for placing fatigue on the SD-012 homeostatic side and routing its longer-horizon consumer into SD-017 sleep timing — not onto SD-011. It also pins down the dissociator that recurs across this directory: a homeostatic accumulator resets offline; learned-helplessness withdrawal (Maier & Watkins) does not "sleep off" and is not gated by waking duration. Any model that lets a suffering-side stop signal recover with rest would be silently borrowing Process S dynamics that belong to the homeostatic system.

**Mapping and caveats.** For REE this transfers as the abstract dynamic, not the molecule: a homeostatic accumulator that rises monotonically with cumulative waking/effort and is reset by an offline (sleep) pass — which is precisely what REE's SD-017/sleep substrate is for. REE has no adenosine and its sleep machinery is partial and v3-pending, so this entry should be read as motivating the *shape* of the slow fatigue variable and its offline-reset consumer, not as a substrate import. The design discipline it enforces: do not collapse the two time-constants. The within-task break accumulator (Meyniel) and the across-day sleep-pressure accumulator (Process S) are two variables, both homeostatic; a fatigue primitive modelled as stop-only, with no recover integrator, omits the defining feature of both.

**Confidence.** 0.80. A foundational, repeatedly validated model; mapping fidelity is good for the homeostatic rise-with-waking / reset-in-sleep dynamic and the offline-recovery half, and transfer risk is moderate because the molecular substrate does not import but the accumulator dynamic — the part the differentiation actually needs — does.
