# Boksem & Tops 2008 — fatigue as a cost/benefit signal, not an empty tank

**Paper.** Boksem MAS, Tops M. *Mental fatigue: Costs and benefits.* Brain Research Reviews 2008;59(1):125–139. DOI [10.1016/j.brainresrev.2008.07.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brainresrev.2008.07.001).

**What it does.** Boksem and Tops review the human mental-fatigue literature and argue against the intuitive "depletion" account (fatigue = running out of a finite resource). In their reframing, fatigue is an *adaptive signal* produced by an integrated evaluation of the expected rewards and the energetical costs of continuing the current activity. As effort accumulates, the represented cost of persistence rises relative to its expected benefit; fatigue is the signal that this ratio has turned unfavourable, and it motivates a shift away from the current effortful goal toward activities with a better cost/benefit balance. They tie this to anterior cingulate / medial-frontal cost-benefit machinery and to the way fatigued performance is *recoverable* when incentive is raised — a fact a pure-depletion account cannot explain.

**Why it is in this review.** It supplies the antecedent half of the fatigue pole and the bridge to REE's existing disengagement consumer. Two of its claims are doing the work. First, the *antecedent* of fatigue-driven stopping is accumulated effort plus an opportunity-cost computation — not aversiveness and not uncontrollability. That is the categorical contrast with Maier & Watkins' suffering pole (also in this directory): helplessness-withdrawal is gated on a controllability appraisal of an aversive stressor; fatigue-disengagement is gated on a value recalibration of one's own effort. Second, the *output* — disengage and reallocate toward better-value behaviour — is the REE ARC-078 goal-disengagement consumer, which the project already grounds (Husain 2018 apathy, Treadway 2012 effort-cost, Kurzban 2013 opportunity-cost in ARC-068). This is the key subtlety the differentiation has to respect: fatigue and helplessness *share the disengagement consumer* (ARC-078) but arrive at it from opposite antecedents. The conflation risk DEV-NEED-002 names ("harm, hunger, fatigue, and survival pressure blur together") lives precisely at this shared output; the fix is to keep the SIGNALS separate upstream even though they converge downstream.

**Mapping and caveats.** In REE terms, accumulated effort should down-weight the *effective value* of the current committed goal — an opportunity-cost modulation of z_goal salience / E3 trajectory scoring — rather than injecting a negative-valence harm signal. The two failure signatures follow directly: a depletion model predicts insensitivity to incentive (contradicted), and an aversive-signal model predicts fatigue would not preferentially steer toward *more* rewarding alternatives (it does). The caveats are real: this is an integrative review, not one decisive experiment; "energetical cost" is loosely specified; and REE still has no fixed effort metric. The opportunity-cost framing also overlaps grounding REE already holds, so this entry's marginal contribution is specifically the *fatigue-as-cost-antecedent* link, not a new disengagement mechanism.

**Confidence.** 0.76. An influential and well-aimed review, but a secondary synthesis rather than primary data, which caps source quality; mapping fidelity is good for the antecedent claim and the framing is domain-general enough to transfer to REE's value-arbitration layer.
