# Badre & Nee 2018 -- three discontinuous zones, not one gradient

ARC-105's `notes` say the claim is written DISCRETE, at roughly three zones named schematic / contextual / sensorimotor, on the authority of Badre & Nee 2018. That is the kind of assertion worth checking rather than inheriting, so I read the full text rather than the abstract. It holds. The authors state that the evidence "appears to consistently support three major functional subdivisions" and that these are "functionally discontinuous, violating a linear, progressive rostro-caudal gradient". The three are sensory-motor control in caudal frontal cortex (effector movements), contextual control in mid-lateral PFC (present-moment behavioural control from maintained context), and schematic control rostrally (control signals derived from episodic or temporally structured context). ARC-105's wording tracks the source closely.

Why this matters for REE and not merely for neuroanatomy: the design fork ARC-105 is deciding is whether goal abstraction should be a *scalar* -- one continuous granularity dimension a goal representation slides along -- or a small set of *distinct levels*. Those are different substrates with different failure modes. A continuous scalar makes abstract-goal loss under subgoal switching a matter of drift along a dimension; discrete levels make it a matter of the wrong level being read. Badre & Nee's discontinuity finding is a direct argument for the second, and it is the reason the claim is written the way it is.

The paper also supplies the asymmetry ARC-105 needs. An intracranial recording study they cite found theta-gamma phase-amplitude coupling increasing both within PFC and between PFC and premotor/motor sites during hierarchical tasks, with a directional analysis showing PFC theta-phase encoding predicting premotor gamma modulation more strongly than the reverse. Abstract-to-concrete influence dominates concrete-to-abstract. For a multi-level z_goal that is a design constraint, not a decoration: the superordinate level must be able to constrain the subordinate one more than it is perturbed by it, which is exactly what "maintaining the abstract goal while executing concrete subgoals" requires.

Now the part that complicates ARC-105 rather than supporting it, and which I think governance should see plainly. ARC-105's description anchors on "rostral PFC holds abstract/superordinate goals, caudal PFC concrete action rules, with asymmetric top-down control" -- a 2009-vintage framing. Badre & Nee 2018 explicitly revise this: on an anatomical meta-analysis of connectional asymmetry, anterior mid-DLPFC rather than rostrolateral PFC is the apex of the frontal control hierarchy. So the ordering is not simply "more rostral = more superordinate". ARC-105's *discrete-levels* commitment survives this intact; its *rostro-caudal anchor sentence* does not, quite, and the Nee & D'Esposito 2016 entry filed beside this one is the primary study behind the revision. I have recorded that as a failure signature rather than smoothing it over, because a REE implementation that assumed a clean monotonic abstraction axis would be importing a superseded reading.

One more caveat I do not want lost. These zones are levels of control abstraction over task representation in general. ARC-105 reads a *goal-granularity* axis off them. That is a defensible narrowing -- goals are what control signals are about -- but it is a narrowing, and the paper does not itself distinguish "abstract goal" from "abstract rule" or "abstract context".

Confidence 0.82. The source says what the claim says it says, on the specific point the claim turns on; held below 0.85 because it is a synthesis rather than a primary test, and because it partially undercuts its own claim's inherited anchor.
