# Badre & D'Esposito (2009) -- a representational-granularity axis for goals

**Claim strand:** C -- granularity-matched goal hierarchy.
**Wires to:** ARC-051 (multi_level_wanting_goal_hierarchy) and the prospective candidate `granularity_matched_goal_hierarchy` (V4-scoped).

## What the review establishes

This is the field-defining review asking whether the rostro-caudal functional gradient in frontal cortex is genuinely *hierarchical*. Surveying neuroimaging, anatomy, electrophysiology, and development, it argues that progressively more anterior frontal regions control progressively more *abstract* action representations: dorsal premotor (BA6) for concrete, temporally-proximate motor decisions; caudal PFC (BA44/45) for intermediate abstraction; mid-dorsolateral PFC (BA9/46) for more abstract action decisions; and frontopolar / rostrolateral PFC (BA10) for meta-level task goals. The hierarchy-specific signature is asymmetry: "Activation in anterior frontal regions accounted for variance in activation in caudal regions but not vice versa."

## Mapping to REE

Daniel named the "granularity-level systems" as one of the systems his maintenance thought interacts with. This review supplies the neural grounding for treating superordinate goals and concrete subgoals as living at *different granularity levels*, with abstract goals (rostral) able to be maintained while concrete subgoals (caudal) are executed. That is a representational-granularity axis layered onto ARC-051's multi-level wanting hierarchy -- and it is the part of Daniel's thought most clearly *not* yet captured in REE, which has region-granularity as a tunable lever but no explicit multi-scale goal-representation substrate. Hence the candidate is V4-scoped.

## The qualification I will not gloss

Two honesty constraints. First, "abstraction" is not one thing -- domain-generality, relational integration, temporal abstraction, and policy abstraction are conflated across the studies, so mapping a single goal-granularity scalar onto the anatomical axis is loose. Second, and more important, the 2018 synthesis (Badre & Nee, *TICS* 22(2):170-188) revises the simple gradient: the evidence "consistently supports three major functional subdivisions" that are "functionally discontinuous, violating a linear, progressive rostro-caudal gradient," and "proposals of a unidimensional rostro-caudal gradient ... also require revision." The implication for REE is concrete: a model with a *small number of discrete* goal-granularity levels (superordinate / contextual-intermediate / concrete-motor) aligns with current consensus better than a continuous granularity scalar. The candidate claim should be written in the discrete form. Confidence 0.62, direction supports-with-caveat.
