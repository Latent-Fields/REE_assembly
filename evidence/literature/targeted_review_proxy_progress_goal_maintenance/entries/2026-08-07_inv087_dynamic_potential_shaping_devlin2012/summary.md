# Dynamic Potential-Based Reward Shaping (Devlin & Kudenko, 2012) -> INV-087

**Claim tested:** INV-087 (`proxy_tethering_constraint`) -- direction **supports**, confidence **0.72**.

## What the paper did

Ng, Harada & Russell (1999) proved that an additive shaping reward preserves the optimal-policy set if and only if it is potential-based, `F(s,a,s') = gamma*Phi(s') - Phi(s)`. That proof carries a quiet assumption: the potential `Phi` of a state is *fixed* for the duration of learning. Devlin & Kudenko point out that this assumption "often is broken, especially if the reward-shaping function is generated automatically" -- i.e. whenever the potential is itself being learned or updated online. They generalise the potential to a time-indexed function `Phi(s,t)` and prove that the shaping reward written as the difference *actually experienced across the transition*, `F(s,t,s',t') = gamma*Phi(s',t') - Phi(s,t)`, still guarantees policy invariance in the single-agent case (and consistent Nash equilibria in the multi-agent case). They demonstrate it in a tabular grid-world.

## Why it matters for INV-087

INV-087 states its own biggest weakness in its `notes`: "the Ng et al. invariance is exact only for a stationary, state-only potential; REE's maintenance proxy is learned and non-stationary, so the potential form is a DESIGN TARGET / limiting-case certificate, not an off-the-shelf guarantee." This paper is precisely the result that narrows that gap. REE's maintenance/progress proxy is a *learned estimate of goal-proximity that keeps changing* -- exactly the `Phi(s,t)` case. Devlin & Kudenko show the tethering constraint does not collapse under that non-stationarity: as long as REE writes the proxy's contribution as the potential difference computed with the *current* proxy at each step, the fact that the proxy is still learning does not, on-policy, pull the true-goal optimum around. In other words, "tether the maintenance proxy to a potential difference over goal-proximity" is implementable *while the proxy learns*, not only in the converged limit. That is the difference between INV-087 being a limiting-case aspiration and a buildable guard.

## The mapping caveat (this is where it stops)

The guarantee is **on-policy**. The telescoping cancellation that makes the potential difference invariant depends on the potential values along the *actually experienced* trajectory being mutually consistent. REE is not purely on-policy: its E3 trajectory selection and its reverse-replay wanting-spread (MECH-217) reuse past transitions, and any replayed transition carries a *stale* potential value from an earlier time. For those, the `gamma*Phi(s',t') - Phi(s,t)` terms no longer cancel down the episode, and the divergence risk INV-087 warns about comes straight back. So this certificate covers the **on-policy write path** of the proxy; it does **not** cover replay-based credit assignment, and it says nothing about REE's multi-drive, per-drive-non-stationary objective, where the single-objective invariance argument has to be re-derived drive by drive. It also does not choose the potential: a well-formed but useless `Phi(s,t)` is still policy-invariant and still useless.

I did not rate it higher than 0.72 because a guard that is certified only on-policy, on a substrate that leans on off-policy replay for its wanting landscape, is a partial certificate -- exactly the kind of "looks safe, is safe only in the regime you don't fully occupy" gap this claim exists to police.
