# Nee & D'Esposito 2016 -- asymmetric hierarchy, but the apex is in the middle

This entry is filed `mixed`, and the split is worth being explicit about because it is the kind of thing that gets flattened into "supports" if nobody writes it down.

Nee and D'Esposito used fMRI with dynamic causal modelling across manipulated levels of abstraction in lateral prefrontal cortex, asking not merely which regions activate but which drive which. They found asymmetrical interactions indicative of genuine hierarchical processing -- efferent and afferent connection strengths were not balanced, which is what distinguishes a hierarchy from a set of peers. That half supports ARC-105 straightforwardly. ARC-105 needs cross-level influence to be directed, because "maintaining the abstract superordinate goal while executing concrete subgoals" is only coherent if the superordinate level has some privileged purchase on the subordinate one. A flat or continuous organisation would not give that.

The other half cuts against the claim as currently worded. Contrary to the dominant assumption at the time -- and contrary to the anchor sentence in ARC-105's own description, which says rostral PFC holds abstract/superordinate goals -- the relative strength of efferent versus afferent connections placed *mid*-LPFC at the apex of the hierarchy. Under cognitive demand, connectivity modulations ran *toward* mid-LPFC, which the authors read as integration: the apex is where abstract and concrete information are synthesised for control, rather than an abstract summit issuing orders downward. Individual differences in these dynamics tracked measured cognitive ability, which at least suggests the organisation is doing something behaviourally consequential.

The design implication for a REE multi-scale `z_goal` is not trivial. If ARC-105 is implemented by reading its anchor sentence literally -- most abstract level on top, control flowing strictly downward -- it implements an architecture this study argues against. The alternative the paper suggests is that the *controlling* level sits where the two granularities meet, and that upward influence into it is part of the mechanism rather than noise. Both readings are consistent with ARC-105's core discrete-levels commitment; they differ on which level is privileged and on whether influence is one-way. I have recorded both as failure signatures.

The caveats are methodological and I weight them seriously. DCM apex results are statements about relative connection strengths among the nodes you chose to model, conditional on the model space you compared; they are not anatomical facts, and they move when the model space moves. Beyond that, there is a two-step mapping gap: the study manipulates abstraction of task *rules*, not goal granularity, and it does not test goal maintenance across subgoal switching at all -- which is ARC-105's actual falsifier. That is why mapping fidelity here (0.68) is the lowest of the three entries I have filed for this claim.

I include it despite that gap because it is the primary evidence behind the revision that Badre & Nee 2018 later synthesised, and ARC-105's description has not yet absorbed it. A claim whose stated biological anchor has been superseded in a specific, documented way should carry that on its evidence record rather than in nobody's head.

Confidence 0.7, direction mixed.
