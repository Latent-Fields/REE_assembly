# Koechlin et al. 1999 -- holding the main goal while the subgoal runs

ARC-105's `what_would_answer` field states the test in one sentence: superordinate-goal maintenance survives concrete-subgoal switching iff the abstract goal is encoded at a granularity level distinct from the subgoal, and collapsing both to one level causes abstract-goal loss on subgoal switch. Koechlin et al. 1999 is the study that first isolated the *functional operation* named in that sentence, which is why it belongs in this file even though it long predates the claim.

Subjects performed tasks in which a main goal had to be kept in mind while concurrent subgoals were executed -- what the literature came to call branching. Bilateral frontopolar prefrontal cortex activated selectively for this. The controls are what give the result its force: the frontopolar response did not appear for working-memory load on its own, nor for dual-task performance involving sequential allocation of attention. So the operation is not "hold more items" and it is not "switch between tasks faster". It is specifically maintaining a pending superordinate goal across the execution of something subordinate to it.

That dissociation is the useful thing for REE. V3's `z_goal` is single-scale, and ARC-105 exists because a single-scale goal representation has no obvious way to keep the abstract goal alive while the concrete subgoal occupies the representation. Koechlin et al. establishes that in humans this is a separable function with its own substrate rather than a by-product of capacity -- which is an argument that the REE substrate will need a dedicated provision for it too, not merely a bigger `z_goal`.

The gap between this paper and ARC-105 is one I want stated sharply, because it is the sort of thing a later reader would otherwise take as settled. A dissociable region that holds a pending goal is consistent with *two* different architectures. One is ARC-105's: the abstract goal is encoded at a distinct granularity level, and that is why the subgoal cannot overwrite it. The other is a protected separate *store* holding a goal in the same format, shielded by buffering rather than by representational level. Koechlin et al. does not adjudicate between them. So this entry supports ARC-105's premise -- that abstract-goal maintenance needs its own provision -- considerably more strongly than it supports ARC-105's specific answer about granularity.

Two further discounts. This is 1999 fMRI with subtraction logic and the sample sizes of that era. And frontopolar cortex has been reinterpreted repeatedly since; in particular Badre & Nee 2018, filed alongside this entry, moves the apex of the frontal control hierarchy to anterior mid-DLPFC and away from rostrolateral PFC. That does not invalidate the branching dissociation, but it does mean the tidy "frontopolar = top of the goal hierarchy" story this paper helped establish is no longer the consensus reading.

Confidence 0.72. Foundational and directly on ARC-105's functional question; capped by era-appropriate methods and by a genuine ambiguity between "separate store" and "distinct level" that the claim depends on and the paper does not resolve.
