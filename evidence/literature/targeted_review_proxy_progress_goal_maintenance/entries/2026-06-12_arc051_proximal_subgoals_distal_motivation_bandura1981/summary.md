# Bandura & Schunk (1981) -- proximal subgoals sustain a distal goal that bare distal goals do not

**Claim strand:** G -- cross-level credit: subgoal achievement reinforces commitment to the superordinate goal.
**Wires to:** ARC-051 (multi_level_wanting_goal_hierarchy), INV-034 (goal maintenance necessary for ethical agency), and the prospective candidate `cross_level_subgoal_credit`.

## The experiment

Children (7-10y) with marked arithmetic deficits and disinterest pursued identical self-directed math material under one of three goal framings -- **proximal subgoals** (a per-session target), a single **distal goal** (same total content, end-of-program target), or **no goals** -- plus a no-treatment control. Only the *granularity of the goal* varied. Proximal subgoals produced the strongest gains on every measure: subtraction skill (treatment x phases F(3,36)=12.55, p<.001), persistence on hard problems (+90% post-treatment vs +22% distal, +39% no-goals, -27% control), and free-choice intrinsic interest (90% of proximal children chose to do subtraction vs ~40% in the other groups). The abstract's blunt summary: "Distal goals had no demonstrable effects."

## Why it is load-bearing for the maintenance thesis

This is the cleanest empirical statement of the premise behind Daniel's whole thought: a bare superordinate goal does not, by itself, sustain long-horizon effort -- the distal goal alone behaved like *no goal*. Motivation toward the distal target was carried by the intermediate, attainable subgoals. That is cross-level credit running upward: subgoal attainment maintains commitment to the parent goal. It extends MECH-217 (which propagates value *along* a trajectory) to propagation *up* a hierarchy, and it underwrites INV-034's framing of goal maintenance as an achievement that requires structure rather than a default property of holding a goal in memory.

## Caveat

The mechanism the paper credits is *self-efficacy* -- a human self-appraisal with no clean computational analog -- and the design conflates two things a model would want separated: subgoals as more-frequent success signals vs subgoals as capability information. It is also small-N (~10/cell), single-domain, four sessions, so effect magnitudes are noisy. The most transferable datum is the *distal-goal null*: it is a direct caution against any REE design expecting a bare superordinate-goal representation to sustain effort without intermediate structure. Confidence 0.60, direction supports. (Goal-gradient corroboration: Hull 1932; Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng 2006, *JMR* 43(1):39-58 -- people accelerate effort as *perceived* proximity to a reward rises, and illusory progress alone speeds completion.)
