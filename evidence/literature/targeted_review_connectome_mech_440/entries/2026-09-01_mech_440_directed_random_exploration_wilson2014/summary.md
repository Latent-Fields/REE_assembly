# Humans use directed and random exploration to solve the explore-exploit dilemma (Wilson, Geana, White, Ludvig & Cohen, 2014)

The Horizon task manipulates one thing cleanly: how many choices remain after the current one. If information has value only insofar as you can act on it later, then a long horizon should increase exploration and a short one should not. What the authors find is that both of the candidate exploration strategies respond -- participants become more information-seeking (directed exploration, preferring the less-sampled option) *and* show greater decision variability (random exploration) when the horizon is longer. Both are used; both scale with the manipulation; and they are estimated as separate parameters within the same model of the same subjects.

For MECH-440 the tempting reading is the shallow one -- exploration is state-dependent, therefore leg (ii) is supported -- and I want to resist it, because the interesting content is more specific. What sets the magnitude of *random* exploration here is the horizon: an explicit feature of the task that the subject computes over and that is supplied to the choice process from outside it. The noise level is not discovered by the noise process observing its own success. It is set by a control variable computed elsewhere. That is architecturally the same shape as the LMAN variability channel in songbirds, as the slow gain-setting process in Dhawale et al., and as the noradrenergic gate in Dubois et al. -- and it is architecturally unlike a per-parameter sigma expected to find its own schedule from a local confidence EMA.

So the entry is filed mixed, and the mixture is genuine rather than a hedge. It supports the premise underneath leg (ii): REE does want a noise magnitude that varies with circumstance rather than a flat floor, and V3-EXQ-687's non-propagating temperature was not that. It weakens the specific proposal that the variation should emerge from the noise parameters themselves, because in every case where we can see how biology or human behaviour sets the level, the level is set by something else and handed in.

The transfer here is the longest of the three behavioural entries and I would not want it doing much work on its own. These are humans making explicit choices between two bandit arms with an experimentally manipulated horizon. MECH-440 concerns per-parameter weight noise in a trajectory-selection head that has no horizon variable and computes no explicit information value. "Random exploration" is a fitted decision-noise parameter in a choice model, not an observed behaviour, and it is not the same object as committed-action entropy in E3. The inference that REE's noise magnitude ought likewise to be externally gated is an architectural argument I am making from a convergent pattern, not a finding this paper reports. Read it as corroboration of the pattern the two animal entries establish more directly, and weight it accordingly -- which is why confidence sits at 0.72 with mapping fidelity the limiting term.
