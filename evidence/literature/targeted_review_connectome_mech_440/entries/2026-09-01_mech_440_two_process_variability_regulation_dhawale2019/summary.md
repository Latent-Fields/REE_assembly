# Adaptive Regulation of Motor Variability (Dhawale, Miyamoto, Smith & Olveczky, 2019)

This is the paper the MECH-440 review was commissioned to find, and it answers the commissioning question directly rather than by analogy. The authors trained rats to make ballistic forelimb movements to a target for water reward in automated home-cage rigs, accumulating behavioural datasets of millions of trials -- a scale that lets them fit and compare models of how variability is regulated instead of merely asserting that it is. Their conclusion is stated plainly in the abstract: "motor variability is regulated by two distinct processes." A fast process modulates variability as a function of recent trial outcomes, increasing it when performance is poor and decreasing it when performance is good. A slower process tunes the *gain* of the fast process, and it is keyed not to outcomes but to the uncertainty in the task's reward landscape. Simulations indicate the pairing optimises reward accumulation across a wide range of time horizons while still promoting learning.

The relevance to MECH-440 is structural. MECH-440 asserts that a learned per-parameter sigma will be state-conditioned (leg ii) *and* self-annealing (leg iii) as two properties of one quantity, inherited together from NoisyNet. This paper measures those two functions apart. They run on different timescales, they are driven by different signals -- outcomes versus reward-landscape uncertainty -- and, crucially, they stand in a hierarchical rather than a parallel relationship: the slow process does not itself inject variability, it sets the gain of the thing that does. That is not a mechanism that could be recovered by asking one sigma to discover its own schedule from a local confidence signal. If biology needed one parameter to carry both signals, it did not build it that way.

What makes this more than a modelling preference is that the decomposition is what the data forced. The fast process is the one that raises variability when performance is poor; on a single-process account, that same parameter would also have to encode the reward landscape's uncertainty, and the fits do not support it. So the honest reading is that MECH-440's conjunction was a design choice made at registration, taken over wholesale from an algorithm, at a point when the biology available to the claim (Aston-Jones & Cohen; Tervo et al.) spoke to state-conditioned gain but not to whether annealing was the same mechanism. It is the sort of over-scoping that is easy to commit and hard to notice, because both legs are individually plausible and the source algorithm bundles them.

The same result cuts the other way for MECH-527, which is why the record tags both claims with per-claim directions. MECH-527 posits that variation should be injected when a settled strategy keeps failing -- keyed to failure, not to uncertainty. The fast process here is exactly that: variability rises when recent outcomes are poor. And the uncertainty term, which MECH-527 explicitly wants to exclude as a trigger, enters at the gain-setting layer one level above, which is a rather elegant vindication of the distinction MECH-527 draws. It is worth noticing that MECH-527's registration text does not cite this paper; it should.

I have held confidence at 0.86 rather than higher, and the reason is mapping rather than source quality. The measured quantity is trial-to-trial kinematic variance in a ballistic forelimb reach. MECH-440's observable is categorical entropy over committed actions in a trajectory selector; MECH-527's is escape from a strategy basin. Neither is what was measured, and the "two processes" are a fitted decomposition -- well-powered and tested against simpler alternatives, but a decomposition of behaviour, not two identified circuits. "Dissociable" here means separable in the regulation of variability, not proven to have separate substrates. One further limit deserves stating because it bears on how far the MECH-527 half can be pushed: the paper varies performance but does not manipulate confidence independently of it, so it supports the failure-trigger half of MECH-527 and is silent on the confidence-without-resolution half that gives that claim its name.
