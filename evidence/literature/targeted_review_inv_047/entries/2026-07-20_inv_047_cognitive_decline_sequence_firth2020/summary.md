# Firth et al. (2020) -- the right instrument for INV-047, returning a partly discordant answer

According to PubMed, this paper applies an event-based model -- a data-driven progression model that estimates the sequence in which biomarkers or test scores become abnormal, without relying on a priori diagnostic labels or fixed cut-points -- to neuropsychological data from posterior cortical atrophy and typical Alzheimer's disease patients at the UCL Dementia Research Centre. The authors extended the classic model to cope with the heavily non-Gaussian distributions that cognitive test scores produce through ceiling and floor effects. The recovered sequences differ by syndrome: PCA declines vision-first, typical AD memory-first. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.12083)

INV-047 is a claim about an *ordering*. That makes this the methodologically correct instrument to hold it against, and I have weighted it accordingly even though it is a modelling paper rather than a mechanistic one.

On the claim's structural half it is straightforwardly supportive, and usefully so. Decline is recoverable as a staged sequence; it is not uniform; and -- the part I find most telling -- two syndromes sharing the same underlying proteinopathy produce *different* cognitive orderings. That is a real difficulty for any account on which diffuse amyloid does the explanatory work, since the same diffuse pathology yields systematically different phenotypic sequences depending on where it lands. INV-047's instinct that the staged profile needs an explanation beyond uniform damage looks right.

But that same observation cuts at INV-047's derivation. If the ordering tracks lesion topography, then it is topography rather than any single canonical dependency structure that sets the sequence -- and INV-047 derives its ordering from a phase-dependency relation. Two syndromes, two orderings, one pathology is not what a single dependency-ordered pipeline predicts.

Then the direct conflict: typical AD comes out **memory-first**, and INV-047 places precision/confidence loss before episodic memory failure. I flagged this as a failure signature and I want to be exact about how much it costs the claim, because it is easy to over-read. The battery covers memory, vision, arithmetic, and general cognition. It contains **no metacognitive or confidence measure whatsoever**. An event-based model cannot order an event it never measured. So the absence of a confidence-first stage here is a measurement gap, not a negative result -- and the same is true of INV-047's third and fourth stages (contextual identity loss, global disorientation), neither of which has a corresponding event in this battery. What this paper genuinely establishes is that among the functions it *did* measure, memory goes first in typical AD. INV-047 is not refuted by that; it is left untested on three of its four stages.

Confidence 0.6. High-quality work, unusually well matched in form to the claim, discounted because it can only see a quarter of what the claim asserts. Read alongside the Hanseeuw cohort -- which *does* measure an awareness signal and finds it moving years earlier -- the most defensible current reading is that INV-047's ordering is plausible but has never been tested on a battery instrumented for all four of its stages. That is a concrete, buildable next step rather than an irreducible unknown, and it is worth noting that it is a measurement-design gap of exactly the kind the SD-068 harness note already flagged on the experimental side.
