# D2-D3 heteromers: the mechanism by which the dopamine dissociation could fail (Maggio et al. 2014)

**Claim tested:** Q-075 -- Are ARC-086's vulnerability axes grounded in *dissociable* receptor systems, such that receptor-selective interventions double-dissociate the axes *rather than the axes collapsing into a single dimension*? This entry addresses the dopamine limb -- and, deliberately, the collapse horn.

**What the paper reviews.** Maggio, Scarselli, Capannolo and Millan review the "novel dimensions" of dopamine D3 receptor function, and the theme is that D3 does not act in molecular isolation. D3 forms homomers, and -- the part that matters for us -- D3-D2 heteromers. Within these receptor complexes, one protomer can *transactivate* the other: a signal arriving at D3 can recruit pathways normally coupled to D2, and vice versa. Ligands designed to be selective (PET agonists like PHNO, bitopic/allosteric antagonists like SB269,652, partial agonists like aripiprazole) behave differently on heteromers than on isolated receptors. So D3 is functionally distinctive *and* pharmacologically entangled with D2 at the level of the receptor complex. According to PubMed, published in *European Neuropsychopharmacology* 25(9):1470-1479 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euroneuro.2014.09.016)).

**Why I logged it `mixed` -- it cuts both ways on purpose.** Q-075 makes a strong prediction: receptor-selective interventions produce *double-dissociated* effects on their predicted axes, "rather than the axes collapsing into a single affective dimension." That framing invites a counterweight, and this paper is it. On one hand, the review supports "D3 is functionally non-redundant" -- there *is* something distinct about D3 worth assigning to its own axis, consistent with the dopamine limb of the grounding hypothesis. On the other hand, and more pointedly, it supplies the molecular mechanism by which the double-dissociation could *fail*: if D2 and D3 heteromerize and transactivate, then a "D3-selective" drug does not produce D3-selective *signalling* -- it can harness D2-coupled pathways. A behavioural experiment built on D2-versus-D3 selectivity can therefore be confounded at the receptor level. If REE assigns the goal/salience-coupling axis and the commitment-release axis to dopamine subtypes that physically cross-talk, those two axes may not stay separable under intervention. That is the collapse-into-one-dimension null Q-075 poses, given a concrete mechanism.

**Where the mapping is indirect.** This is molecular pharmacology, not a behavioural axis study, so inferring *axis collapse* from *heteromer cross-talk* is a cross-level inference -- suggestive, not demonstrative. Heteromerization shows a route by which dissociation *could* fail; it does not show that REE's specific precision/goal/commitment axes *do* collapse. And the usual scope caveat is in full force: Q-075 is `out_of_domain`, V3 has no dopamine receptor subtypes, the receptor rung is out of scope for the substrate, and this raises literature grounding without promoting, demoting, or licensing any V3 experiment.

**Confidence.** 0.50. The review is authoritative and well-cited (source quality 0.75); the discount is the cross-level inference plus out-of-domain transfer. I included it precisely because a literature file for Q-075 that only stacked up supporting dissociations would be dishonest -- the claim's own null (axis collapse) has a named, well-documented molecular substrate in the dopamine system, and pricing that in is the point. Taken with the serotonin entry (dissociable, per a theoretical model) and the kappa-opioid entry (dissociable in affect but CRF-fused), the trio's net message is: the receptor systems Q-075 nominates are *individually* plausible axis substrates, but the clean, non-crossing double-dissociation the claim needs is not something the current literature hands over -- two of the three limbs carry explicit entanglement warnings.
