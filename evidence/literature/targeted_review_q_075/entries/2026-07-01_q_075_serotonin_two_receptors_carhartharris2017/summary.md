# A tale of two serotonin receptors (Carhart-Harris & Nutt 2017)

**Claim tested:** Q-075 -- Are ARC-086's latent vulnerability axes grounded in *dissociable* neuromodulator-receptor systems, such that receptor-selective interventions produce double-dissociated effects rather than the axes collapsing into a single affective dimension? This entry addresses the serotonin limb: does the precision/terrain axis map onto dissociable 5-HT subtypes?

**What the paper argues.** Carhart-Harris and Nutt propose a bipartite model of brain serotonin, hung on its two most-studied receptors. Postsynaptic **5-HT1A** signalling, they argue, mediates *passive coping* -- tolerating a stressor, stress moderation -- and is the brain's default adaptive response to adversity. **5-HT2A** signalling mediates *active coping* -- changing one's situation or one's relationship to it -- and is characterised by enhanced plasticity, a raised capacity for change. Crucially for our purposes, the two pathways are separately druggable: conventional SSRIs preferentially enhance the 5-HT1A pathway, while 5-HT2A-agonist psychedelics enhance the 5-HT2A pathway. According to PubMed, published in the *Journal of Psychopharmacology* 31(9):1091-1120 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0269881117725915)).

**Why it speaks to Q-075.** Q-075's serotonin prediction is that the precision/terrain axis of ARC-086 is grounded in dissociable serotonin subtypes -- specifically 5-HT2A-cortical narrative versus 5-HT1A-hippocampal terrain. For that prediction to have any purchase, serotonin cannot be a single lumped tone; there has to be genuine functional dissociability between subtypes, and there have to be selective handles to move one without the other. This paper supplies exactly that: two receptors, two distinct adaptive functions, two different drug classes that engage them differentially. It is the receptor-level dissociability the axis-grounding hypothesis presupposes.

**Where the mapping strains.** Two honest gaps. First, the paper offers a *theoretical bipartite model*, not a factorial double-dissociation experiment. Q-075's strong form asks for a crossover -- a 5-HT2A-selective drug that moves axis A but not axis B, and a 5-HT1A-selective drug that does the reverse. Carhart-Harris and Nutt argue dissociable *roles*; they do not run the 2x2 that would show the axes stay separate under selective perturbation. Second, "passive versus active coping" is not obviously the same construct as "precision/terrain." The paper grounds a coping-style dimension; Q-075 needs a *vulnerability axis*, and the specific cortical-narrative-vs-hippocampal-terrain assignment is finer and more structural than anything the paper commits to. If REE's precision/terrain axis does not line up with the coping dimension, the receptor grounding has been transferred to the wrong variable.

**Confidence and scope.** 0.55 -- moderate support. The synthesis is influential and pharmacologically anchored (source quality 0.70), but it is a model rather than an interventional double-dissociation, and the construct gap plus the out-of-domain transfer keep fidelity mid-range. And the scope caveat is load-bearing here: Q-075 is explicitly `out_of_domain`. Its test domain is clinical pharmacology -- receptor-selective RCTs and naturalistic cohorts -- not the V3 substrate, which has no 5-HT receptor subtypes at all. So this entry raises literature grounding for the serotonin limb of the axis-dissociability hypothesis and *nothing more*: it promotes no claim, demotes no claim, and must not be read as licensing a V3 experiment. The V3-tractable sibling is the MECH-087 cross-plane non-rescue test, which asks a plane-level, not receptor-level, dissociation question.
