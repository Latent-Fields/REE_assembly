# Dynorphin/kappa-opioid as the dysphoric limb of stress (Bruchas, Land & Chavkin 2009)

**Claim tested:** Q-075 -- Are ARC-086's vulnerability axes grounded in dissociable receptor systems? This entry addresses the opioid limb: does the commitment-threshold axis have a substrate in a kappa/mu opioid dissociation?

**What the paper reviews.** Stress carries both aversive and motivating properties, and Bruchas, Land and Chavkin marshal the evidence that the *aversive* side is carried by the endogenous neuropeptide dynorphin acting at the kappa-opioid receptor (KOR). Activation of the dynorphin/KOR system produces dysphoria and conditioned place aversion, and it potentiates drug-seeking and stress-induced reinstatement of relapse. Mechanistically, KOR sits downstream of, and is co-activated with, corticotropin-releasing factor (CRF) -- the review's throughline is a CRF-dynorphin/KOR cascade that translates stress into negative affect. The aversive signature of KOR is separable from the *rewarding* actions of abused drugs, which run through the mu-opioid system. According to PubMed, published in *Brain Research* 1314:44-55 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brainres.2009.08.062)).

**Why it speaks to Q-075.** Q-075 predicts the commitment-threshold axis maps onto a kappa/mu opioid overlay. The kappa side of that overlay needs a receptor system that (a) is real and selectively targetable, and (b) carries a signal distinct from mu-opioid reward. This review delivers both: KOR is a well-characterised, druggable receptor whose dysphoric/aversive signature is dissociable from mu reward. As a candidate substrate for an aversive commitment-cost or a raised-threshold-to-commit signal, it is the right kind of object -- a separable receptor axis rather than a lump of "opioid tone."

**Where I get uneasy about the mapping.** Two things, and the second is the more serious. First, the paper's construct is *dysphoria, aversion, and relapse* -- not commitment, and certainly not "commitment entropy." The jump from "KOR carries aversive affect" to "kappa/mu governs a commitment-threshold overlay" is a large REE-specific interpretive leap that this literature does not make for me; I am grounding a receptor system that is *plausibly* involved, not one the authors tie to commitment. Second, and this is the honest counter-signal: the review's central mechanism is the *tight coupling* of CRF and dynorphin/KOR. If the commitment-threshold axis is grounded in a receptor system that is mechanistically fused to the stress/harm (CRF) system, then perturbing it will move both -- which is precisely the *collapse into a single affective dimension* that Q-075 explicitly worries about. So this paper simultaneously supplies the kappa handle Q-075 wants and flags a reason the double-dissociation might fail: the candidate commitment substrate does not look cleanly independent of the harm substrate.

**Confidence and scope.** 0.50 -- weak-to-moderate support, and deliberately so. The review is strong and well-cited (source quality 0.75), but mapping fidelity is low: the commitment reading is unsupported by the text, and the CRF-KOR coupling cuts against clean dissociation. As with the whole Q-075 file, this is `out_of_domain` by design -- V3 has no opioid receptor subtypes, the receptor rung is out of scope for the substrate, and the entry raises literature grounding without promoting, demoting, or licensing any V3 experiment. If anything, the useful contribution here is the *warning*: one of Q-075's own predicted axes may be entangled at the receptor level with the harm stream, which is a specific, testable-in-principle threat to the double-dissociation the claim rests on.
