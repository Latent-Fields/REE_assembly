# The price of your soul (Berns et al., 2012)

**Claim tested:** MECH-035 -- VALENCE is vector-valued and ranked without scalar collapse.
**Direction:** supports | **Confidence:** 0.62

## What the paper does

Berns and colleagues built a paradigm around a simple, uncomfortable offer: real money to renounce a
personal value. Participants were scanned while deciding, item by item, whether to sell. Some values
went. Others did not, at any offered price. The comparison that matters is between those two
populations of item, within the same participants, in the same task.

Values that were refused recruited left temporoparietal junction and ventrolateral prefrontal cortex
-- regions the literature associates with retrieval and application of semantic and deontic rules.
They did not recruit the valuation circuitry that a cost-benefit weighing would be expected to engage.
The authors' reading: sacred values are processed by looking up a rule, not by computing a price.

## What it says about MECH-035

MECH-035 has two halves and the rest of this directory speaks almost entirely to the first. The vector
half -- is VALENCE multi-dimensional, is it collapsible -- is well served by Vamplew, Abel, Hayden and
Niv, Levy and Glimcher, Suzuki. The second half has been going unexamined, and it is the half REE
actually cares about most: *constraint-first*. The claim says a candidate dominated on HARM is
rejected even when a scalar blend would prefer it for gains elsewhere. That is not a statement about
arity. It is a statement about there being a dimension on which no exchange rate is offered.

This paper is the nearest biological instance of that structure I could find. A dimension is held out
of the trade, and the holding-out has its own substrate, dissociable from the machinery that does the
trading. It is evidence -- weak but real, and otherwise absent from this pull -- that constraint-first
comparison is an architecture cognition actually implements, not merely a design preference REE has
adopted for ethical comfort.

## Limitations and what could go wrong

I want to be careful here, because this is the entry where I most want the result to be true and
therefore the one where my own reading needs most discipline.

The dissociation is regional, not causal. "A different region was active" does not establish "a
different algorithm ran". Increased TPJ and vlPFC activity for refused items fits rule retrieval, but
it also fits moral emotion, self-referential processing, or the reappraisal of an offer experienced as
insulting. The design does not separate these, and reverse inference from prefrontal and temporo-
parietal coordinates to a named cognitive operation is exactly the move the field has grown cautious
about since 2012.

Worse for the claim: a sufficiently large scalar weight is behaviourally indistinguishable from a
constraint at any price a laboratory can afford to offer. Refusal may reflect reputational and
self-concept costs entering the same utility computation at a very high coefficient. That possibility
is not addressed and probably cannot be, within this paradigm.

And the scope is one dimension. MECH-035 asserts ranking across six named streams. A single protected
dimension sitting on top of an otherwise scalar comparison would produce this paper's result and would
not satisfy the claim.

Sample size is not stated in the abstract and I did not obtain the full text, so it is unverified
here. Nothing above turns on it, but a governance reader should know.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality 0.72 -- Philosophical Transactions B, a genuinely inventive paradigm, but a single-site
2012 fMRI study in a domain where replication has been uneven. Mapping fidelity 0.60: it tests the
constraint half and says nothing at all about the vector half, so it covers less than half the claim.
Transfer risk 0.55 is the highest in this pull and is what holds the aggregate at 0.62 -- three
transfers stack (personal moral commitments to a HARM stream, humans to an artificial substrate,
regional dissociation to comparison rule), and the last is the one I would expect to break. Retained
despite all of that because no other source I found tests constraint-first comparison at all, and a
literature record that covers only the easy half of a claim is worse than one that flags the hard half
as thinly evidenced.
