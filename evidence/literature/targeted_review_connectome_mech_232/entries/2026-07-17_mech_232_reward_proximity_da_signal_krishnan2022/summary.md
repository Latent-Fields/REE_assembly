# Reward-proximity dopamine signal maintains CA1 map quality (Krishnan et al. 2022)

According to PubMed, Krishnan, Heer, Cherian & Sheffield (2022, *Nature Communications*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-34465-5)) asked what circuit mechanism lets reward expectation modulate the strength of hippocampal spatial memories. Using two-photon calcium imaging of CA1 pyramidal cells in mice running a virtual linear track, they show the familiar picture — place cells over-represent reward locations and this over-representation scales with reward expectation. The novel part is the causal handle: VTA dopaminergic axons projecting into CA1 carry a *ramping reward-proximity signal* that depends on reward expectation. When the authors extinguish reward expectation, the animals stay engaged with the track, yet three things happen together: the over-representation of reward vanishes, place-field remapping across the environment increases, and trial-to-trial place-field reliability drops. Optogenetically inhibiting VTA dopaminergic neurons largely reproduces this constellation. Their conclusion is that a dopaminergic VTA→CA1 reward-proximity signal is what stabilises and enriches the cognitive map around reward.

This is close to a purpose-built test of the premise underneath MECH-232. Our claim is that dopamine does not paint an explicit positive-valence field onto hippocampal terrain; instead it raises the *quality* of place representations — fidelity, stability, associative richness, density — at reward-relevant locations, and that this implicit representational expansion is how approach-valence enters the map. Krishnan et al. supply the mechanistic spine of that story with a real dopaminergic pathway (VTA→CA1) whose manipulation moves exactly the variables SD-024 was built to model: over-representation (density), reliability (stability), and remapping (the inverse of durable, easily-reactivated maps). Critically, removing the DA signal degrades the map *without* abolishing behavioural engagement — the animal is still there, still moving, just building a poorer reward map. That dissociation between "the representation is degraded" and "the agent is disengaged" is precisely the shape REE leans on when it argues the valence lives in representational quality rather than in an added motivational gradient.

Where I want to stay honest: this paper carries the *representational* leg of MECH-232 and is essentially silent on the *behavioural* leg. MECH-232's load-bearing, discriminating prediction — the CRUX our V3-EXQ-766/766a experiment was built around — is that DA-expanded representational density, with the value weights zeroed out, still produces approach, while an explicit-gradient value-follower falls to chance. Krishnan et al. never zero out value and never test whether map quality *alone* drives approach; they measure the encoding-side correlates. So they raise my confidence that "DA controls map fidelity/stability/density at reward" is real biology, but they do not adjudicate "and that, not a valence tag, is the approach mechanism." A second, subtler caveat: their signal is literally called a *reward-proximity* signal and it ramps toward reward. A sceptic could read that ramp as itself a graded, valence-like quantity — which is the exact implicit-versus-explicit boundary MECH-232 is trying to draw. The paper doesn't resolve that ambiguity; it locates the signal on dopaminergic axons and shows it controls map quality, which is the reading REE takes, but the wording leaves room.

On confidence I land at 0.82. Source quality is high (converging correlational imaging plus optogenetic causal manipulation, strong venue). Mapping fidelity is high for the SD-024 substrate premise (DA modulates density/stability/reliability) and moderate for the MECH-232 CRUX (approach without an explicit gradient), which this paper does not touch. Transfer risk is moderate — rodent VR navigation to REE's abstract RBF hippocampal terrain is an analogy of function, not a shared substrate. Net: strong support for the mechanism REE implemented, not yet evidence for the behavioural claim REE still has to win experimentally.
