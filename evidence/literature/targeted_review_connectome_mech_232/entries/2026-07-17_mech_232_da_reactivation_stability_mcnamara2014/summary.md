# Dopamine stabilises hippocampal representations of new goals (McNamara et al. 2014)

According to PubMed, McNamara, Tejero-Cantero, Trouche, Campo-Urriza & Dupret (2014, *Nature Neuroscience*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3843)) provide a clean causal complement to the Krishnan et al. imaging work. In freely moving mice, they optogenetically burst-stimulated hippocampal dopaminergic fibres arising from midbrain neurons while the animals explored novel environments. Two effects followed. First, the stimulation enhanced the reactivation of CA1 pyramidal-cell assemblies during subsequent sleep/rest — the offline replay that is thought to consolidate spatial memory. Second, when the same stimulation was applied during learning of new goal locations, it improved the later recall of the neural representations of space and stabilised behavioural memory performance. The headline is that midbrain dopamine promotes the hippocampal network dynamics associated with memory *persistence*.

For MECH-232 this is targeted support for one specific facet: stability and durability. Our claim decomposes representational expansion into several correlated properties — higher information density, finer spatial resolution, *more stable place fields*, more easily reactivated maps — and argues dopamine raises all of them at reward-relevant locations without adding an explicit valence field. McNamara et al. take the stability/reactivation strand and show it is causally dopamine-dependent: more dopamine at goal-relevant moments yields more durable, more reactivatable spatial representations. That maps directly onto the "stability" supporting metric in the V3-EXQ-766 leg-1 readout and onto SD-024's premise that dopamine acts on representational quality rather than value magnitude. Where Krishnan et al. give us density/over-representation and reliability, McNamara et al. give us persistence — together they cover most of the representational-expansion vocabulary MECH-232 uses.

The honest boundaries are two. First, this is an *offline* effect: the mechanism operates on reactivation during sleep/rest and on next-day recall, whereas MECH-232 as instantiated in REE is an *online* CEM-scoring / terrain mechanism. The paper supports "dopamine makes reward-relevant hippocampal representations more durable," but it does so on the consolidation timescale, and I should not overstate its fidelity to REE's online planning loop — that is a real mapping gap, not a quibble. Second, like Krishnan et al., it is entirely silent on the discriminating CRUX of MECH-232: whether representational quality, stripped of any explicit positive gradient, is what produces approach. It shows dopamine upgrades the representation; it does not test what the upgraded representation does to behaviour in the absence of a valence tag. So it strengthens the premise and leaves the load-bearing dissociation to REE's own experiment.

I set confidence at 0.72. Source quality is high — a causal optogenetic manipulation in a top venue with both physiological (assembly reactivation) and behavioural (recall, memory stability) readouts. Mapping fidelity is moderate: it evidences one facet (stability/durability) well but not density or the approach dissociation, and it crosses the offline-versus-online boundary. Transfer risk is moderate-to-high for that same reason. It belongs in the file as a second, converging causal pillar for the representational-expansion premise, sitting a notch below the Krishnan entry which speaks to more of the MECH-232 vocabulary at once.
