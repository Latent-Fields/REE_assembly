# System consolidation of memory during sleep (Born & Wilhelm, 2012)

This review sets out the active systems consolidation account: during slow-wave sleep, newly encoded
memories are reactivated and redistributed to long-term stores. Two of its claims bear directly on
CDQ-011, and they happen to be the two halves of the prescription the row was sent to find.

The first is selectivity. The review states that the consolidation process during sleep "is selective
inasmuch as it does not enhance every memory", and that SWS preferentially consolidates memories with
relevance for future plans. The second is transformation: system consolidation "produces qualitative
changes in memory representations such that the extraction of explicit and conscious knowledge from
implicitly learned materials is facilitated". Consolidation abstracts gist and rule; it does not
sharpen verbatim fidelity.

Put together, these say something fairly precise about when a consolidation pass will measurably
improve a frozen held-out readout. The selectivity half is about the probe's *support*: a battery
that does not overlap what was replayed is measuring the wrong population, and will return a diluted
or absent effect regardless of how good the consolidation was. The transformation half is about the
probe's *functional form*: if the process changes representational form rather than improving
reproduction accuracy, then a per-element reconstruction error is measuring exactly the quantity the
process is least committed to preserving. A readout improves across sleep when it is scored on the
abstracted structure, over the material the pass actually visited. Both failures are available to
INV-063 leg B simultaneously, and the 701b readout commits both at once -- it is a reconstruction
error over an unselected held-out battery.

The honest accounting on evidential weight matters here more than usual. This is a narrative review
from the laboratory that produced much of the primary evidence it synthesises, including the Wilhelm
2011 entry in this same batch -- same senior author, overlapping data. It corroborates that entry but
is not independent of it, and INV-063's aggregate literature confidence should not treat it as a
fourth data point. I would rather flag that explicitly than let four entries look like four
independent confirmations when they are closer to three.

The explicit/implicit framing at the centre of the transformation claim also has no REE counterpart
whatever: `agent.e2.world_forward` has no conscious readout and no explicit/implicit distinction. Only
the weaker proposition survives the transfer -- that consolidation changes the representation's form
rather than sharpening its fidelity -- and that weaker proposition is the one cited. Confidence 0.66;
source quality 0.7, mapping fidelity 0.65, transfer risk 0.45.
