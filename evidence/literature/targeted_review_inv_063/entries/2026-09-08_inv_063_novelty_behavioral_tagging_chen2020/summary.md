# Exposure to novelty promotes long-term contextual fear memory in juvenile mice (Chen, Tsai & Hsu, 2020)

## Why I went looking for this paper

INV-063's C2 leg -- the knee -- is the claim's whole distinctive content. Strip it out and what
remains is INV-050 observed on the function side: more intake, proportionally more offline benefit.
The claim asserts something stronger and more interesting, that there is a *minimum*, a floor below
which offline function does not degrade gracefully but collapses. Having read the local-sleep
literature and found only graded, proportional relations, I went looking specifically for any
demonstration that environmental novelty can produce a categorical rather than proportional memory
outcome. This is what I found, and it is the only such instance in the pull.

## What the paper did

Chen and colleagues trained juvenile mice on contextual fear conditioning using a deliberately weak
protocol -- one that reliably produces memory at short delays and no memory at long ones. They then
paired that same weak training with five minutes of exploration in a novel environment, close in time
to the training session. The weak trace became a long-term memory. A familiar environment did not do
this; the effect required dopamine receptor activation and new protein synthesis in dorsal
hippocampus; and novelty exposure increased the overlap of the CA1 ensembles engaged by the two
experiences. This is the behavioural tagging account: novelty supplies plasticity-related proteins
that a marginal trace can capture and use to stabilise itself.

The shape is what matters here. This is not "more novelty, proportionally better memory". It is a
conversion: without the novelty the trace is transient, with it the same trace persists. A threshold,
crossed.

## How far this actually carries

Not very far, and I would rather say so at length than have this entry cited as though it settled
something. Three disjunctions sit between this result and INV-063, and any one of them alone would
block a strong inference.

There is no sleep in the design. The consolidation window is the hour or so after training, awake.
INV-063 is a claim about four *offline* functions, and this paper's mechanism operates upstream of
the offline period entirely. Second, the independent variable is a discrete supplement -- five
minutes in a novel box -- not a graded level of ambient environmental entropy sustained across a
waking period, which is what our `world_rule_shift_interval` ladder manipulates. The threshold
demonstrated is about the presence of novelty within a time window, not about an intake *rate*
falling below a floor. Third, the mechanism is dopaminergic protein capture, which belongs to waking
encoding, not to replay, schema consolidation, or world-model updating.

There is a fourth point which is subtler and which I think is the genuinely useful thing this paper
tells us. The effect appears only for *weak* training. Strong training produces long-term memory
with no novelty supplement at all; the threshold exists because the trace was marginal to begin with.
Mapped across to our substrate, that predicts an intake floor would be visible only in an agent
already operating near its competence margin -- and given the documented action-learning competence
floor in V3, floor and artifact become very hard to distinguish precisely in the low arms where the
knee is supposed to show up. That is a design warning, not a supporting datum.

## Confidence

0.42, the lowest in this pull, and set well below the mean of its own components on purpose. The
study itself is competent (0.7) with pharmacological and ensemble-level converging evidence. But
mapping fidelity is 0.35 and transfer risk 0.75, and for a claim whose distinctive content *is* a
threshold, an analogy about thresholds drawn from a different pathway, without sleep, under a
different manipulation geometry, is suggestive rather than probative. What it earns is modest and
worth having: the knee is not a priori implausible, and novelty-gated discontinuities in
consolidation do exist in biology. It does not tell us that offline sleep function has one.
