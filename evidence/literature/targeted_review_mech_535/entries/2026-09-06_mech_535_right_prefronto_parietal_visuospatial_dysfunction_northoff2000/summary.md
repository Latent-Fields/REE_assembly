# Northoff et al. (2000) -- right prefronto-parietal dysfunction and visuospatial deficits in akinetic catatonia

**Question served:** Q2 (has any clinical account located the deficit in a representation?) -- the nearest positive hit.

## What the paper did

Ten acute akinetic catatonic patients were compared with ten psychiatric controls (matched for age, sex, medication and underlying diagnosis but without catatonia) and twenty healthy controls on Tc-99m ECD SPECT regional cerebral blood flow and a neuropsychological battery that included tests of right-parietal visual-spatial ability. Catatonic patients showed reduced perfusion in right lower and middle prefrontal and right parietal cortex, and poorer visuospatial performance. In both control groups visuospatial ability correlated with right parietal rCBF; in the catatonic group it did not, and instead attentional measures correlated with motor symptoms, visuospatial ability and right parietal rCBF. The authors call the findings preliminary and read them as right lower prefronto-parietal dysfunction "closely related to psychomotor disturbances."

## Why it is the closest thing to a Q2 hit

Every other clinical mechanism account in this pull locates the deficit in control: top-down modulation, GABA-A hypofunction in OFC, NMDA hypofunction, dopamine blockade of the selection loop. This is the one dataset in which a *spatial-processing* measure is degraded and travels with the motor signs. If one wanted a clinical foothold for the idea that some catatonic presentations are representational rather than gating failures -- INV-088's bound operating in a patient rather than in a fishtank -- this is where one would start. It is also the empirical basis for Northoff's later proposal (BBS 2002) that right parietal cortex supports the on-line monitoring needed to terminate a movement.

## Why it is nonetheless scored mixed

MECH-535 needs a specific dissociation: goal *proximity* preserved, goal *direction* lost. This study measures general visuospatial ability; nothing in the battery could separate a magnitude channel from a direction channel, and the 978 exemplar's signature (magnitude decodable at r2 ~0.71 while the directional head's argmax sits on one cell) has no counterpart here. The patients' motor sign is akinesia with posturing -- a termination pathology -- not ambitendency, so the paper cannot speak to whether spatial degradation produces approach/withdraw cycling. And the correlation pattern (visuospatial ability coupled to parietal perfusion in controls, decoupled in patients) is at least as consistent with a disconnection as with a degraded representation. So the honest reading is: the clinic has *touched* the representational axis, once, coarsely, in a small SPECT series; it has not located ambitendency there. Q2 remains essentially null for the specific structure MECH-535 asserts.

## Caveats and confidence

n = 10, SPECT spatial resolution, medicated-matched but not medication-free controls, and a correlational design from 2000 that has been extended but not replicated in the direction/proximity form. Confidence 0.4: moderate source quality, moderate mapping, high transfer risk.
