# Girard, Cuzin, Guillot, Gurney and Prescott (2003) -- basal ganglia persistence in a robotic survival task

**Question served:** Q3 (action persistence: needed for competence, or protective against degraded/near-tie input?). The most direct source found.

## What the paper did

The Gurney-Prescott-Redgrave (GPR) basal ganglia model was embedded in a Khepera robot that had to "survive" in a two-resource arena (reload on bright tiles, reload on dark tiles, with internal energy and potential-energy variables), and compared against a classical winner-takes-all (WTA) selector in the same robot and environment. The GPR model differs from WTA in two ways the authors isolate: a thalamocortical positive-feedback loop that increases the salience of the currently selected act ("behavioural persistence"), and a control pathway (BGII/STN) that keeps that feedback from running away. Three experiments: baseline survival, a "dithering" test in which a metabolically irrelevant Grooming act competes with the current winner, and an energy-saving test with a Rest act.

## What it found

Bouts of the resource acts "generally last longer with the GPR architecture than with the WTA. This can be explained by effects of persistence, allowing an action to remain active for some time after its raw salience has fallen below that of other actions." Persistence "serves to avoid dithering, the main issue of most engineering architectures of action selection": a robot that is starved of potential energy and dirty "may hesitate to display either an ingesting or a grooming act," and "lacking this possibility, the WTA robot necessarily oscillates between these behaviours," whereas the GPR robot's oscillation length can be set by its persistence weights (their Figure 8). And -- the sentence that carries MECH-536 -- "Despite its lack of persistence, the WTA robot was able to survive during Experiment 1 by increasing the frequency of its reloading actions," though it spent less time "in a comfort zone well away from the boundary of viability." The authors are explicit that persistence is a dial, not a good in itself: interruption is sometimes adaptive (an animal eating in a dangerous place should look up), so "an efficient action selection system should be able to control the level of behavioural persistence according to circumstances," and without the control pathway "a winning act could reinforce itself endlessly preventing itself from being deselected."

## How this bears on MECH-536 and MECH-535

MECH-536's functional claim is that BG-like persistence is protective rather than necessary: a memoryless greedy actor forages competently given a good representation (978's `local_view_greedy` at 45.75 resources per episode), and what persistence buys is robustness when the inputs are degraded or near-tie. Girard et al. state the first half in their own words -- the persistence-free selector survives -- and show what persistence adds: no oscillation between near-tie acts, a wider viability margin, energy saving. They also mark both pathological poles of the dial: too little and the selector dithers; too much and it perseverates. That is the shape of MECH-536's prediction (latch removes the cycle; excess latch is perseveration) as seen from the BG-modelling tradition. For MECH-535 the support is at the level of dynamics: a memoryless selector whose two options cross in value every step *necessarily* oscillates, which is exactly what a magnitude-conditioned reflex does on two adjacent cells. The caveat is that their oscillation is between two different acts driven by two motivations, not approach/withdraw toward one goal, so the cause differs even though the trajectory shape is the same.

## Caveats and confidence

The comparison varies persistence with inputs held adequate; the representation is never degraded, so the paper does not run MECH-536's dissociating test (latch plus direction-blind input should give cycle gone, competence flat). Five or six runs per condition, hand-tuned weights, a modest venue. The phrase "protective against representational degradation" is REE's extension of what the paper shows about near-tie inputs. Confidence 0.6, the highest in this pull for MECH-536 -- because the paper's own framing is the claim's functional statement, not because the test has been run.
