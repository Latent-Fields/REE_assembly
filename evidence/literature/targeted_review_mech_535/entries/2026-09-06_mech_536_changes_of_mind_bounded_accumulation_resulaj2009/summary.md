# Resulaj, Kiani, Wolpert and Shadlen (2009) -- changes of mind in decision-making

**Question served:** Q3 (perceptual decision commitment and changes of mind).

## What the paper did

Subjects judged the net direction of a noisy random-dot motion display and indicated their choice by moving a handle to one of two targets. Although "they received no additional information after initiating their movement, their hand trajectories betrayed a change of mind in some trials." The authors extend the bounded evidence-accumulation model that already accounts for choice and reaction time: noisy evidence accumulates to a bound that triggers the initial decision and movement, and the brain then "exploits information that is in the processing pipeline when the initial decision is made to subsequently either reverse or reaffirm the initial decision" if that continued accumulation crosses a second, revision bound. The model reproduces how often changes of mind occur, how they depend on difficulty, and the fact that they are predominantly corrective.

## Why it bears on MECH-536

MECH-536 says basal-ganglia-like persistence is protective against noisy or degraded input rather than necessary for competence. This paper is the canonical human demonstration of the protective half at the level of a single decision. The momentary evidence sign flips constantly -- that is what noisy motion is -- yet the hand reverses on only a minority of trials, mostly at intermediate difficulty, and mostly for the better. A memoryless actor that re-decided from the instantaneous evidence at every moment would reverse far more often; that is the vacillation the 978 reader shows on two adjacent cells, and the reversal-suppressing bound is the latch MECH-536 says would remove it. In REE's own vocabulary this is ARC-107 root C's post-commit latch and MECH-047's asymmetric enter/exit thresholds, observed in a human hand, with one refinement the claims do not state: a well-set latch stays *revisable* when the pipeline evidence is strong, so the alternative to vacillation is not rigidity.

## What it cannot say

The protection demonstrated is against noise in evidence that is informative on average. Nothing here concerns evidence that carries no direction at all, which is MECH-535's case and exactly the situation MECH-536's dissociating prediction is about (latch plus direction-blind input should give cycle gone, competence flat). Commitment to a categorical perceptual choice is an analogue of holding an action across foraging steps, not the same thing. Healthy humans, no basal ganglia measurement. Confidence 0.45: very high source quality, moderate mapping.
