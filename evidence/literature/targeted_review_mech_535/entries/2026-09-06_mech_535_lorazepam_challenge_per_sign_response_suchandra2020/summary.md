# Suchandra, Reddi, Aandi Subramaniyam and Muliyala (2020) -- the lorazepam challenge test, sign by sign

**Question served:** Q4 (the benzodiazepine analogy) -- recorded **as analogy only**.

## Why this entry exists, and what it is not

MECH-536's notes and the psychiatric_failure_modes Subtype III section both reach for a clinical echo: lorazepam restores movement in catatonia without treating the underlying illness, much as a post-commit latch would abolish the 978 reader's ambitendency without restoring its foraging. The chip asked that this analogy be pulled and tagged so that governance reads it as corroborating context and not as support (feedback_lit_exp_decoupled: a resembling paper does not raise a claim's confidence). That is what this entry does. Its `evidence_direction` is `unknown`, its mapping fidelity is set very low, and it carries `support_tag:analogy_only`.

## What the paper did

In a psychiatric emergency setting at a tertiary neuropsychiatric centre, 57 patients with catatonia received a lorazepam challenge at either 2 mg (n = 37) or 4 mg (n = 20), with the Bush-Francis scale scored before and after. There was no between-group difference in total-score change. At the item level, "significant response [was] noted to both doses (stupor, mutism, staring, posturing, withdrawal, ambitendency, automatic obedience) and others selectively to 2 mg (echolalia, rigidity, negativism, mitgehen)." The authors propose 2 mg as the optimal challenge dose and, more interestingly for this pull, read the per-sign variability as pointing toward "neurobiological underpinnings for individual catatonic signs that may be potentially different."

## What the analogy does and does not carry

Ambitendency resolves under GABA-A potentiation. So the shape of MECH-536's prediction -- a manipulation that removes the sign without addressing what produced it -- has a documented clinical instance at the level of the specific sign. That is the whole of what can be taken. A dose-response study says nothing about whether the sign arose from a degraded representation or from a gating failure; the two operations being compared (raising cortical inhibition in a human brain; adding a k-step latch to a frozen policy) share one abstract property and no mechanism; and GABA-A has no counterpart in an argmax reader over `z_world`. Two further points cut against even the analogy being tidy: the authors' own reading is that different catatonic signs may have different substrates, which undercuts any inference from one sign's benzodiazepine response to a shared locus; and Northoff (2002) describes an ambitendency-like presentation -- days standing before a wardrobe, unable to decide -- in a lorazepam *non*-responder.

## Caveats

Observational, single centre, no placebo, item-level comparisons without correction. Confidence 0.2, and that number is about the analogy's documentation, not about either claim.
