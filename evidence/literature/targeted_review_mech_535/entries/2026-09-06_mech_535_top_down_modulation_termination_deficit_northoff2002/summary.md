# Northoff (2002) -- what catatonia can tell us about top-down modulation

**Question served:** Q1 (mechanism accounts of catatonic signs; locus of the deficit). Also a structured null for Q2.

## What the paper is

Northoff's BBS target article is still the reference mechanistic account of catatonia. It contrasts catatonia with Parkinson's disease: both are akinetic, but PD is a *bottom-up* striatal dopaminergic deficit that impairs initiation, whereas catatonia is a *top-down* deficit in which GABA-A-mediated orbitofrontal / medial prefrontal dysfunction alters "vertical modulation" of the caudate and other basal ganglia (motor signs) and disturbs "horizontal" orbitofrontal-prefrontal/parietal cortico-cortical connectivity (emotional and behavioural signs). The clinical anchor is a set of ball experiments in 32 acute, unmedicated akinetic patients: almost all could catch and throw the ball -- they could plan, initiate and execute -- but most "remained in a final posture keeping the ball in a position against gravity, apparently unable to change posture and terminate the respective movement." Posturing, in this account, is a failure of *termination*. Right posterior parietal cortex is proposed as necessary for "on-line monitoring and consecutive termination of movements," on the grounds that right parietal lesions produce posturing and catalepsy without initiation deficits (Saver et al. 1993).

## What it says about the locus

The deficit is placed squarely in the control plane: top-down modulation of the selection loop, and the termination side of commitment in particular. Nothing in the article locates any catatonic sign in the *goal representation*. The word "ambitendency" does not appear. The nearest thing to an ambitendency vignette is a lorazepam non-responder who stood for days in front of her wardrobe and reported "a blockade of my will with contradictory and ambivalent thoughts about my dresses since I couldn't decide myself" -- read by Northoff as a blockade of will, i.e. a volitional locus, and noted as a case that did *not* respond to GABA-A potentiation. Patients were unaware of their own posture ("the objective position and the corresponding subjective experience of the spatial position seem to be decoupled") -- a spatial-representation element, but about one's own body, not about where the goal lies.

## How this bears on MECH-535 and MECH-536

Two things follow, pulling in different directions, which is why the entry is scored *mixed*. First, the representational route MECH-535 registers is genuinely un-owned in the clinical mechanism literature: the canonical account puts the deficit in top-down control and termination, and the only ambitendency-like case is attributed to will. So MECH-535's "distinct from MECH-202B and SD-036" framing is not merely distinct from REE's own prior routes; it is also distinct from the field's. Second, Northoff's phenotype is a persistence-*excess* pathology -- the movement cannot be ended -- which is the perseveration pole of MECH-536's prediction, whereas the 978 reader's ambitendency is a persistence-*absence* pathology. Both produce something stationary (posturing / stupor / the boundary-press fixed point) but by opposite dynamics. A reader who conflates "stuck" phenotypes across these two mechanisms will misread MECH-535; the entry is here partly to prevent that.

## Caveats

This is a hypothesis paper built on small series (n = 10-32), SPECT and neuropsychology, and it is now more than twenty years old; the follow-on literature (Hirjak, Northoff and colleagues) has broadened rather than overturned it. The null on a goal-direction locus is a null of omission, not a considered rejection: the article was never asked the question. And human catatonia carries affective and autonomic dimensions -- intense uncontrollable anxiety, responsive to lorazepam -- with no counterpart in a gateless argmax reader over `z_world`.
