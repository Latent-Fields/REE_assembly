# Caspi et al. (2014) — The p Factor: One General Psychopathology Factor in the Structure of Psychiatric Disorders?

**Claim grounded:** MECH-371 (global-instability / broad control-plane vulnerability axis — the p-factor analogue)
**Direction:** supports · **Confidence:** 0.72

*According to PubMed* ([DOI: 10.1177/2167702613497473](https://doi.org/10.1177/2167702613497473)).

## What the paper did

This is the landmark paper that introduced the *p factor* to clinical science. Using the Dunedin Multidisciplinary Health and Development Study — a roughly thousand-person birth cohort assessed repeatedly from adolescence to midlife over 20 years — Caspi and colleagues modelled the structure of psychopathology while taking seriously its dimensionality, persistence, co-occurrence, and *sequential* comorbidity (the tendency of disorders to follow one another over a life). They fit confirmatory factor models of increasing parsimony to the full cross-disorder covariance.

## Key findings relevant to the claim

Psychiatric disorders were initially explained by three higher-order factors — Internalizing, Externalizing, and Thought Disorder — but were explained *even better* by a single **general psychopathology dimension**, which the authors named the **p factor** by deliberate analogy to the *g* factor of general intelligence. The factor is not a statistical curiosity: higher p scores were associated with more life impairment, greater familiality, worse developmental histories, and more compromised early-life brain function. The authors' interpretive punchline is the one that matters most for REE: p "explains why it is challenging to find causes, consequences, biomarkers, and treatments with specificity to individual mental disorders," and it motivates *transdiagnostic* rather than disorder-specific approaches. In other words, a single broad dimension of vulnerability loads across the entire space of disorders, raising joint susceptibility to many at once.

## How it translates to REE

MECH-371 posits "a global-instability / broad control-plane vulnerability axis (a p-factor / general-psychopathology-factor analogue)" that "loads across ALL failure modes" — a single broad dimension of regulatory fragility that raises joint susceptibility to multiple REE failure modes simultaneously, distinct from and orthogonal to any single-axis failure. This is the one psychiatric-failure axis the claim notes has *no existing REE home* (the others map onto owned substrate: precision → ARC-005/MECH-251, commitment → E3/MECH-090, provenance → MECH-094, residue → ARC-013/SD-010/011, and so on). Caspi et al. are the empirical referent for that axis: they establish, in humans, that such a transdiagnostic-loading general dimension *exists* and predicts elevated risk across the whole space. This grounds MECH-371's central premise and its prediction that a subset of agents/patients carry elevated cross-axis risk rather than a single-axis fragility. It is exactly the lit anchor the claim's own notes and the ETH-8 readiness gate (L4, Caspi & Moffitt) call for before any promotion.

## Limitations and caveats

The decisive caveat is the gap between *existence* and *mechanism*. Caspi et al. establish that a general psychopathology dimension loads transdiagnostically; they do **not** identify what it *is* mechanistically. MECH-371 goes further — it asserts the analogue is specifically *instability in precision/gain/threshold setpoint regulation* (ARC-005), i.e. a control-plane stability scalar. That mechanistic identification is REE's own hypothesis and is neither made nor tested in the source. So this paper supports the *axis / transdiagnostic-loading* half of the claim, not the *control-plane-mechanism* half. Two further cautions: (1) the p factor is a latent statistical structure, not a demonstrated single cause, so a REE substrate that builds in a global-instability scalar *by construction* risks manufacturing the dimension rather than discovering it; and (2) part of the p-factor signal can reflect measurement and comorbidity-counting artefacts. The PRECISION GUARDRAIL in MECH-371's notes — that a global-instability axis does not erase the distinctness of the specific failure modes — is the right discipline and is consistent with the source, which treats p as an *additional* general loading layered over, not a replacement for, specific structure.
