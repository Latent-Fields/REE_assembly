# Inferences on a multidimensional social hierarchy use a grid-like code (Park, Miller & Boorman 2021, Nature Neuroscience)

This paper asks a narrower and cleaner version of the question TEM asks: can the same grid-cell
machinery long known for physical navigation represent an abstract space with MORE THAN ONE
dimension at once, and use that representation to infer relations it was never directly taught?
Park, Miller and Boorman trained participants on a two-dimensional abstract social hierarchy --
think of it as two independent "who outranks whom" axes rather than one -- built entirely from
discrete pairwise comparisons, never shown as a map or picture. They then asked participants to
judge relations between people who had never been directly compared. Behaviourally, participants
could do this by geometric inference within the learned two-dimensional space. Neurally, entorhinal
cortex and medial prefrontal cortex carried a hexagonally-periodic, grid-like signal over the
reconstructed two-dimensional space during exactly these novel inferences, and that grid-like signal
tracked with decision-value computations in mPFC and temporoparietal junction.

The reason this belongs in a pull about a general relational-possibility topology is specific: most
of the hippocampal cognitive-map literature (including the Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine entry alongside
this one) demonstrates that the map generalizes beyond physical space to non-spatial content, but
usually keeps to a single relational axis (a path, a rank, a similarity gradient). This paper is one
of the few with direct human neural evidence that the SAME machinery can hold two independent
relational dimensions simultaneously over the same set of entities, and interpolate correctly in
that joint space. That is a real, if partial, existence proof for the structural headroom the
intake's candidate claim needs: if the entorhinal-hippocampal system already juggles two axes for a
social hierarchy, "the topology has a requires axis AND an enables axis AND a conflicts-with axis
over the same possibility set" is not obviously beyond its representational capacity.

Where this stops short of the candidate claim is important to say plainly. Both axes in this
paradigm are the SAME kind of relation -- ranked ordering / relative standing -- just along two
independent social dimensions (e.g. competence and popularity, in the kind of hierarchy used in
this literature). The intake's candidate topology needs qualitatively DIFFERENT relation types
co-present (requires vs. enables vs. prevents vs. substitutes-for), which is a stronger and
different claim than "the map can be multidimensional." This paper also uses a static, discrete,
already-fully-learned hierarchy and a forced-choice inference task -- it says nothing about whether
or how such a structure gets discovered or revised WHILE an agent is pursuing a goal, which is the
crux of the intake's Section 8 formulation. So this entry is best read as narrowing one specific
worry (can the substrate even represent more than one relation dimension at once? -- yes) without
touching the harder, still-open worries (multiple relation TYPES, and revision through action).

Confidence: 0.72. Source quality is high (converging behavioral and dual-region neural evidence in a
well-controlled human paradigm). Mapping fidelity to SD-004's general claim is good; mapping fidelity
to the candidate claim's typed-multi-relation and in-action-revision requirements is only partial,
which is why confidence sits below the TEM entry in this same pull despite comparably strong source
quality.
