# Emotional shaping of spatial encoding along the dorsoventral axis -- assessed against MECH-143 (Silva & Girardeau, 2026)

> This paper is assessed twice, once per claim, because its direction differs between them. The companion entry is `2026-08-09_mech_144_dorsoventral_valence_coding_silva2026` in `targeted_review_connectome_mech_144`, where the direction is *mixed*. Here it is *weakens*.

## What the paper did

Silva and Girardeau synthesise the rodent literature on how spatial and emotional coding differ between dorsal and ventral hippocampus, and propose a framework for how the two poles interact. It is a mini-review, not primary data, but it is recent, tightly scoped, and written by a group whose own experimental work sits in this exact area.

## Key findings relevant to the claim

The review's dorsal-side synthesis is a problem for MECH-143, and it is worth quoting the shape of it rather than paraphrasing loosely. Following an aversive event -- footshock, predator odour -- dorsal place cells remap *even with no physical change to the environment*. Place fields shift toward locations associated with positive outcomes and toward sites linked to negative stimuli, concentrating around emotionally salient regions; the review calls this goal-directed remapping. A subset of dorsal neurons fires selectively at reward sites regardless of spatial position -- reward cells -- encoding positive valence independently of spatial context, within the dorsal compartment.

The discussion states the position outright: dorsal place cells remap in response to various aversive and rewarding stimuli, consequently embedding valence-related information within a spatially anchored framework.

That first point deserves emphasis, because it removes the most attractive defence of MECH-143. One could otherwise argue that dorsal maps look value-free simply because the environment did not change. Here the environment did not change and the map remapped anyway.

## How this translates to REE

MECH-143 and MECH-144 were registered together as a clean partition -- dorsal value-free, ventral value-laden -- and that partition was the mechanism by which Q-020 was resolved via Path A. This review says the partition is not clean. The dorsal compartment embeds valence too; it simply does so at higher spatial precision and lower weight.

I think the defensible version of the dissociation is quantitative rather than categorical: a gradient of increasing valence weight and decreasing spatial precision from dorsal to ventral, rather than a boundary between a value-free map and a valenced one. That reframing is not fatal to ARC-007, but it does mean the no-new-value-computation constraint has to be re-derived on a gradient rather than inherited from a dissociation that the literature does not support. Notably the review also reports that the overrepresentation of high-value reward locations *increases* from dorsal to intermediate hippocampus -- which is exactly the shape a gradient account predicts and a categorical account does not.

## Limitations and caveats

Everything here is second-hand. I have not independently assessed the primary sources behind these statements, and this entry should be weighted as synthesis rather than as data -- hence source_quality capped at 0.70 despite the review being recent and directly on point.

More importantly for fairness to MECH-143: the review does not isolate the specific manipulation the claim rests on, namely value varied while goal location is held constant. It therefore does not refute Duvelle et al. What it refutes is the broader generalisation REE drew *from* Duvelle et al. Those are different targets and the distinction should survive into whatever governance does with this. The review is also rodent throughout, so the primate abstract-value-map work underpinning MECH-144 is outside its scope.
