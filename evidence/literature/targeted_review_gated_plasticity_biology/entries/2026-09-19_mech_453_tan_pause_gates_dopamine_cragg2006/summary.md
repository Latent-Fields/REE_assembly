# Meaningful silences: how dopamine listens to the ACh pause (Cragg, 2006)

## The claim under test

MECH-453 -- registered as a candidate and, until now, carrying zero literature entries of its own --
says something quite specific: dopamine should *not* continuously update every recently-active
selector component. A striatal cholinergic tonically-active-neuron pause should open a narrow
plasticity window, and the three-factor learned-gating updates of ARC-108 should be admitted only
within it. The 2026-05-04 smoothened_da_ach intake stages four further candidates that inherit the
same window, including `plasticity.striatal_da_ach_windowed_write`.

The gate on all of this was: does the biology actually put a window there, or is the window a
convenient engineering fiction?

## What Cragg establishes

It puts a window there, and the mechanism is more pointed than a coincidence story. Cragg's review
assembles the evidence that striatal acetylcholine acts *presynaptically* -- on dopaminergic
terminals -- to determine how opposing patterns of dopaminergic-neuron activity are transduced into
actual dopamine release. The cholinergic state is not a bystander that happens to correlate with the
dopamine signal. It is upstream of what that signal becomes.

She then adds the temporal fact: reward-related TAN and DAN activities are temporally coincident, but
differently variant with reward probability. The synthesis is that TAN pauses enhance the contrast,
or salience, of dopamine signals carried by reward-related bursts -- and, she notes, by
reward-omission pauses too. The two systems are functionally cooperative rather than antagonistic.

For REE the consequence is the one MECH-453 needs: the same dopaminergic burst does not mean the same
thing inside and outside the pause. Eligibility becomes a function of window state, not only of
prediction-error magnitude. That is the whole difference between MECH-453 and a garden-variety
three-factor rule, and it now has biology under it.

## Where I would hold the line

Two gaps, and both should shape the falsifier rather than be smoothed over.

Cragg's language is *contrast enhancement*. The pause sharpens a signal that is transmitted either
way. MECH-453 is written as *admission* -- updates admitted only within the window, which reads as a
hard gate with suppression to zero outside it. Graded contrast and categorical admission make
different predictions, and the strong version is the interesting one, so MECH-453's falsifier ought to
test it explicitly rather than inherit it by assumption.

The second is a level shift, and it is the kind that quietly launders a claim's provenance. Cragg's
mechanism sits at dopamine *release*. MECH-453 places its window on *credit assignment* -- which
selector components get updated. Those are adjacent stages, not the same stage. A release gate makes
less dopamine arrive; a credit gate decides what the arriving dopamine is allowed to modify. The
review grounds the first. REE is claiming the second. That is a defensible architectural move but it
should be written down as a move, not presented as the paper's finding.

## Confidence

0.77. High-quality review from the field's principal authority on presynaptic cholinergic control of
striatal dopamine, discounted for being a review rather than a primary test, and for the
release-versus-credit level shift that MECH-453's own wording will have to absorb.
