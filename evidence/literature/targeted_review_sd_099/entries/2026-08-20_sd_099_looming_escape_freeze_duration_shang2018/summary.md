# Divergent midbrain circuits orchestrate escape and freezing responses to looming stimuli in mice

Shang, Chen, Liu, Li, Zhang, Qu, Yan, Zhang, Liu, Liu, Guo, Li, Wang & Cao (2018), *Nature Communications* 9(1):1232. DOI 10.1038/s41467-018-03580-7. PMID 29581428. PMC5964329.

## What the paper did

The authors presented mice with an overhead looming disc -- the standard simulated-predator visual threat -- and scored the full behavioral sequence that follows, not just the initial reflex. Two response patterns emerged, cleanly dichotomous rather than a continuum. Most animals (44 of 58, ~76%) escaped first and then froze: escape latency 154 +/- 32 ms, escape bout 0.52 +/- 0.09 s, followed by a freezing bout of 28 +/- 4.5 s. A minority (14 of 58, ~24%) skipped escape and went straight to freezing, with a similar onset latency (163 +/- 29 ms) and a freezing bout of 21 +/- 5.3 s. The paper's own scientific target is the circuit dissociation behind the two patterns -- parallel superior-colliculus pathways to the parabigeminal nucleus versus the lateral posterior thalamus -- but the behavioral timing is reported as a primary, precisely quantified characterization, not incidentally.

## Why this bears on SD-099's persistence-window question

The question this pull was commissioned to answer is: after a defensive-orienting episode resolves into a decision, how long does that decision measurably bias subsequent action selection? This paper does not measure a valence-gated approach/withdraw choice directly -- it measures something adjacent and, for the purpose of calibrating an order of magnitude, more useful: the full temporal anatomy of one complete defensive-orienting-to-resolution episode, end to end, in the exact species and stimulus modality (sudden unanticipated onset, undirected general threat) that SD-099's own trigger channel is modeled on.

Read that anatomy against the two candidate denominations. The FAST component -- detection to motor commitment -- is genuinely fast: 154-163 ms. If SD-099's bias window were meant to model this initial commitment latency, Option C's 60-125ms would be in the right neighborhood, perhaps even generous. But that is not what the window models. `orienting_post_override_bias_ticks` fires only AFTER the gate has already released into a decision -- it governs how long the RESULTING decision continues to bias what happens next. The biological analog of that quantity is not the 154ms latency; it is the 21-28 SECOND freezing bout that follows it. That number is two orders of magnitude larger than even Option A's upper bound (1.25s), let alone Option C's.

I want to be exact about the direction of the argument, because it would be easy to over-claim. This paper does not license setting the REE window to 21-28 seconds -- nothing here says SD-099's abstract latent-space agent should inherit a mouse's literal freeze-bout duration, and the paper's dichotomous escape-or-freeze choice is not SD-099's graded approach/withdraw/resume decision. What it licenses is narrower and still decisive for the question actually asked: whichever of Option A or Option C is closer to biological reality, Option A (625ms-1.25s) is the one on the correct SIDE of a two-order-of-magnitude gap, and if anything undershoots rather than overshoots what the closest available real measurement shows. A design that assumes the bias clears within one theta cycle (Option C) is modeling the motor-commitment latency, not the post-commitment defensive state -- a category confusion the paper's own two-stage decomposition (fast escape, THEN sustained freeze) makes visible.

## Limitations

Three gaps, stated plainly. First, this is escape-or-freeze, a binary innate reflex pair, not SD-099's three-way valence-gated decision (approach/withdraw/resume) computed from a harm-versus-benefit comparison. Second, the paradigm is a single acute trial with no repeated-choice structure -- it says nothing about whether the bias, once established, applies uniformly across several subsequent action-selection events (as REE's tick-count design implies) or decays within that window. Third, mouse innate visual defense circuitry maps onto REE's abstract PAG-analog gate only architecturally, not quantitatively -- I am not claiming REE's bias window SHOULD equal 21-28 seconds, only that the biological analog of "post-decision persistence" sits far closer to seconds than to sub-100ms.
