# State-conditioned exploration beats constant exploration (Bakopoulos & Chalkiadakis, AAMAS 2026)

This paper (arXiv:2509.03219, accepted as a full paper at AAMAS 2026) is the most direct machine-learning analogue I found for MECH-343's central claim. The authors build a generic adaptive-exploration framework in which the exploration signal is a function of state, f(s), tied to an uncertainty estimate. On a TD3 backbone they compare several instances on the six hardest MuJoCo continuous-control tasks (Walker2d, Hopper, Swimmer, Ant, Humanoid, Humanoid Standup), 15 runs each. The decisive internal control is f(s)=c -- a *constant* exploration signal, which essentially reproduces standard fixed exploration. The state-conditioned variants (f(s)=RND model-disagreement, and f(s) scaled by the Q-value standard deviation) consistently outperform that constant control and also beat off-the-shelf fixed exploration methods (RND, UCB, Noisy Nets).

Map this onto Q-056. MECH-343 predicts that *stuck-state-gated* proposal entropy should outperform both entropy-off and always-high entropy. The f(s)=c arm here is the literature stand-in for "always-high / fixed" exploration, and the state-conditioned f(s) variants are the stand-in for "gated" exploration. The result -- conditional beats constant -- supports the half of the MECH-343 prediction that says a gated regime dominates a fixed-high regime. It is encouraging that this holds on the *hardest* control tasks, since Q-056's discriminating environment is explicitly a hard-goal/blocked-path setting.

The mapping caveat is the load-bearing one and I want it explicit: the paper reports no *pure-exploitation* (entropy-OFF) baseline. So it evidences "gated > constant-high" but not the full three-way "gated > both off and always-high" discrimination that Q-056 and EXP-0176 are built to test. Two further gaps: the gating variable is generic epistemic uncertainty / model-disagreement, not REE's specific difficulty-or-stuck-state trigger; and there is no goal-progress or harm-constraint structure, so MECH-343's predictions about commitment latency rising-then-falling and no-harm-increase are simply out of this paper's scope. The authors also note their tests use dense rewards and flag sparse-reward domains -- closer to REE's blocked-goal regime -- as future work.

evidence_direction `supports`, confidence 0.60. It is peer-reviewed and directly on the gated-vs-constant axis, but it is one methods paper, it leaves the entropy-off arm untested, and the gating signal is not REE's. It moves Q-056 toward "the architecture is sound in principle" without retiring the need for the three-arm experiment.
