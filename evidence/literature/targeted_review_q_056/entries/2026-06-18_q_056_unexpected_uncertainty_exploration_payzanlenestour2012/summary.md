# Uncertainty modulates exploration -- but two motives compete, and one suppresses it (Payzan-LeNestour & Bossaerts 2012)

According to PubMed, this Frontiers in Neuroscience paper ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2012.00150)) is the most useful corrective in the Q-056 set, because it both supports the gating principle and refuses to let it become naive. Using human behaviour in a restless multi-armed bandit plus matched simulation, the authors argue that uncertainty-driven exploration is governed by a *dilemma* between two motives: (i) speeding up learning about the unknown, which may beget novel reward, and (ii) *avoiding* the unknown because it may be dangerous. Both estimation uncertainty and "unexpected uncertainty" (environmental volatility) modulate exploration, and the brain appears to differentiate the two motives. The authors are candid that the prior evidence for uncertainty-driven exploration is mixed -- which is itself the point.

This maps onto MECH-343 in two ways. First, it supports the core Q-056 premise: exploration is *conditioned on a state signal* (uncertainty) rather than held at a fixed rate, which is consistent with stuck-state-gated proposal entropy beating a fixed regime. Second -- and this is why it is the most valuable entry for governance -- it independently motivates the *constraint* MECH-343 already builds in. MECH-343 does not predict "more entropy when stuck, full stop"; it predicts difficulty-gated entropy with *no harm increase and no random churn*. The avoid-the-unknown motive documented here is the behavioural analogue of exactly that harm constraint: when the unknown is hazardous, adaptive agents *suppress* exploration. A version of MECH-343 that raised entropy under difficulty without the harm gate would be contradicted by this literature.

The caveats keep it at `mixed`, confidence 0.52. The paradigm is a restless bandit, not a sequential blocked-path goal environment; "unexpected uncertainty" (volatility) is related to but not identical with REE's difficulty/stuck-state trigger; the paper is older (2012) and its support for gating is at the level of principle, not a direct test of difficulty-gated proposal entropy against off and always-high arms. I have set it `mixed` deliberately: it endorses gating while flagging that the empirical base for uncertainty-driven exploration is contested and that more-when-stuck can be the wrong policy without a safety constraint -- both of which Q-056's three-arm experiment, not the literature, must resolve.
