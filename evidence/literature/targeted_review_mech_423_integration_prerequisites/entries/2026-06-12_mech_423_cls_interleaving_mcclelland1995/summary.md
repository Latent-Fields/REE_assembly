# McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly (1995) — interleaving as the precondition for shared-representation integration

**Claim grounded:** MECH-423 (cross-model super-additivity) — readiness condition R3 (offline/consolidation transfer must be active).
**Direction:** supports (necessity of the prerequisite). **Confidence:** 0.72.

## What the paper did

This is the paper that turned catastrophic interference from an embarrassment into an architectural argument. The authors showed, in connectionist simulations, that a single shared-weight network *can* extract the deep structure latent in an ensemble of experiences — but only if each item is learned gradually and *interleaved* with the others. Train the same network on one body of material to completion and then switch to a second, and the second sweep overwrites the first (catastrophic interference). Their resolution: the brain runs two complementary systems. The hippocampus rapidly stores individual episodes; it then *replays* them, interleaved, into neocortex, which integrates them slowly into a structured, shared representation. The slow, interleaved cortical system is where generalisable structure lives; the fast hippocampal system is the buffer that makes the interleaving possible.

## Why it speaks to MECH-423

MECH-423 asserts that integrating E1 (world model), E2 (affordance model) and the object spine over a shared L-space latent (ARC-004) yields capability gains *exceeding* the sum of the isolated modules. The whole claim presupposes that the shared latent is actually integrating cross-module experience rather than just co-locating it. CLS theory is the canonical statement of *when* that integration is realisable: it requires an offline engine that interleaves traces so the shared representation forms gradually. In REE that engine is MECH-121 replay / sleep consolidation. This is precisely Daniel's readiness intuition (2026-06-12) made rigorous — below an active interleaving regime, the integrated and isolated arms of EXP-0380 are indistinguishable by construction, because no cross-module structure has been built for super-additivity to act on.

The paper also supplies the sharpest *failure signature* for the experiment's non-degeneracy gate: if EXP-0380's integrated arm trains its modules in a focused, blocked schedule (E1 to convergence, then E2), CLS predicts catastrophic interference and a *sub-additive* outcome — which would be an artefact of the missing interleaving prerequisite, not a refutation of super-additivity. The readiness gate must therefore certify that consolidation/replay is active and interleaving *before* the super-additivity comparison is scored.

## Limitations and mapping caveats

CLS is an argument about avoiding interference during sequential learning. It establishes that interleaving is *necessary* for integration; it does not by itself establish that integration yields gains *above* an additive baseline (the specific super-additive magnitude MECH-423 asserts). The connectionist-cortex analogy to REE's L-space is structural rather than an identity — REE's modules are explicit predictors over a shared latent, not a generic backprop net. So this entry grounds the prerequisite, not the headline result, and its mapping fidelity is rated accordingly (0.66).

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality is near-maximal (one of the most cited and validated papers in computational memory). I hold overall confidence at 0.72 rather than higher because the paper certifies a *necessary condition* for the readiness gate, not the super-additive effect itself, and the transfer from connectionist cortex to REE's substrate is close but not exact.
