# Margulies et al. (2016) — the integration apex: a transmodal hub sitting above the sensory streams

**Claim grounded:** MECH-423 (cross-model super-additivity) — the DMN-analogue readiness condition (an offline integration hub must exist for cross-module integration to have a locus).
**Direction:** supports. **Confidence:** 0.66.

## What the paper did

Margulies and colleagues decomposed resting-state connectivity in humans and macaques and recovered a *principal gradient* of cortical organisation: a single continuous axis with primary sensory and motor cortex at one end and transmodal cortex — the default-mode network — at the other. The striking finding is geometric. DMN regions sit at the *greatest* geodesic distance along the cortical surface from primary sensory/motor landmarks, and they sit *precisely equidistant* from them. That topography is the structural signature of a region built to integrate information across modalities while being maximally insulated from any single immediate input stream. The authors argue the DMN's cognitive role — abstract, self-referential, simulation-heavy processing detached from the here-and-now — follows from its position at the apex of this hierarchy.

## Why it speaks to MECH-423

Daniel's readiness intuition included a DMN-style offline integration condition: the suspicion that super-additivity stays null until the integration machinery is itself doing cross-module work, and that a DMN-analogue offline integration hub is part of that machinery. This paper supplies the biological reality behind that condition. Cross-module integration needs a *locus* — a representational apex that sits above the individual sensory and affordance streams and combines them while detached from immediate input. The DMN is that apex in cortex; in REE the functional analogue is the shared L-space latent (ARC-004) together with its offline consolidation regime, where traces from E1, E2 and the object spine are combined away from online sensorimotor demands.

The failure signature this grounds for EXP-0380: if the integrated substrate offers no apex detached from the sensory/affordance streams — no representational level where the modules are genuinely combined rather than merely co-located — then there is nowhere for super-additive integration to happen and the integrated arm collapses to within-module processing. The readiness gate's R-DMN/offline-integration condition is checking exactly for the existence and activity of that apex.

## Limitations and mapping caveats

This is the entry with the most analogical mapping in the set, and I have rated it accordingly. REE deliberately does *not* implement a discrete DMN module — the project's standing position (project_attention_distributed_precision_selection) is that attention and integration are distributed across the substrate rather than localised. So the paper supports the *functional necessity* of an integration apex, not a one-to-one substrate component. It is also a structural connectivity result: it shows the hub exists, not that it is actively integrating on any given trial. A static hub with no online integration is itself the degenerate-readiness case the gate must exclude.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality is high (a landmark cross-species connectivity paper). I hold overall confidence to 0.66 because the mapping is functional-analogical rather than substrate-identical, and the evidence is topographical rather than a dynamic measurement of integration. It earns its place by grounding the *biological plausibility* of the offline-integration-hub readiness condition, which would otherwise rest on assertion alone.
