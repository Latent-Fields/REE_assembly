# Am I Self-Conscious? (Or Does Self-Organization Entail Self-Consciousness?) -- Friston (2018)

Friston's starting point is deceptively small: living systems are the kind of thing that keeps returning to a set of characteristic states rather than dissipating or wandering off. He then makes the move that gives the paper its bite: this recurrence, formalised as a Lyapunov function, is mathematically identical to maximising Bayesian model evidence, or equivalently minimising surprise. On this reading, every biological process, from natural selection down to a single afferent update, is "doing inference" in the technical sense, whether or not anything is home to experience it. Mind and selfhood, he argues, appear specifically when this self-evidencing acquires "temporal thickness" -- when the organism is not just inferring the present hidden causes of its sensations but inferring the consequences of its own future action. Consciousness, on this account, just is inference about one's own future, nothing added.

The paper is written as notes for a public essay literally titled "Consciousness is not a thing but a process of inference," and that line could be dropped into INV-069's own notes almost unchanged. INV-069 asserts the self is "the dynamically sustained process maintaining high temporal-depth verisimilitude across time, not a static representation" -- Friston's attracting-state/self-evidencing picture is a general physics-flavoured argument for exactly that shape of claim, extended from organisms in general to conscious selfhood in particular. The parallel is close enough to be useful as conceptual grounding: REE's D_self(t) is a concrete, computable instance of "temporal thickness" (an EMA with an explicit look-back weighting, lambda), and V_self(t), the coupling/precision-weighted aggregate over self-tagged latents, is a concrete instance of an "attracting state" that the process must keep re-approaching rather than a value it simply holds.

Where the mapping runs out is at the level of testability. Friston's argument is that self-organising systems in general are, by construction, engaged in exactly this kind of self-maintaining inference; it is closer to a definitional/physical claim about living systems than to a falsifiable prediction about any one implemented self-variable. It offers no perturbation-and-recovery paradigm, no readout analogous to per_stream_vs['z_self'], and, most relevant to how INV-069 actually failed its first test, no account of what should happen when the recurrent machinery nominally implementing this self-evidencing process is present but untrained. The 2026-09-24 V3-EXQ-1078 autopsy found exactly that: an untrained, contractive SelfRecurrenceCell that "recovers" from perturbation purely by virtue of its architecture (any contraction below roughly 0.93/tick passes trivially), which is a vacuous pass rather than evidence of a genuine self-maintaining process. Friston's paper cannot adjudicate that distinction; it argues that self-organisation of the right functional shape entails self-consciousness, but says nothing about how to tell a trained maintaining process apart from an untrained but formally similar dynamical system.

Confidence is set at 0.62: strong conceptual and vocabulary alignment (temporal thickness maps to temporal-depth verisimilitude, attracting state maps to the D_self trajectory, self-evidencing maps to V_self aggregation) earns real credit, but the domain transfer from "any self-organising biological system" to "a specific gated recurrent cell in a V3 agent" is wide, and the paper is a theoretical/opinion piece rather than a tested model, so it cannot bear weight on the trained-substrate precondition the autopsy showed is decisive. This entry supports INV-069 as a conceptually well-motivated claim; it is not evidence that the claim is true of the current V3 substrate, and it does not resolve the open frozen-maintenance (arm b) DV question either.
