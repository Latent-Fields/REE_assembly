# Mapping Sub-Second Structure in Mouse Behavior (Wiltschko et al., 2015)

This is the founding paper for Motion Sequencing (MoSeq), one of the most influential methods in modern computational ethology. The authors used 3D depth-camera imaging to track freely moving mice and fit an autoregressive hidden Markov model (AR-HMM) to the resulting pose-dynamics time series. The model discovers, without being told in advance what to look for, that mouse behavior decomposes into a vocabulary of brief (~300ms), stereotyped, reused movement modules -- "syllables" -- strung together according to well-defined transition probabilities. Two mice performing superficially different overall activity can be shown to be drawing from the same underlying syllable vocabulary in different sequences, and the transition structure itself (not just which syllables occur) carries behaviorally meaningful information, for example about genotype or drug state.

This paper is close to a direct existence proof for the "discovery-oriented" half of GOV-BEHADJ-1's proposed two-channel analysis. Where the source thought asks, informally, "what behavioural structure is present that we did not think to measure?", MoSeq answers that question with a specific, statistically principled generative model: rather than hand-selecting candidate discrete states (refuge/explore/feed/etc., as the thought's own hypothesis-directed channel proposes) and then checking whether behavior transitions among them make sense, MoSeq lets the state boundaries and their number emerge from the data itself, constrained only by the generative model's assumptions. The paper also supplies a useful, self-contained caution that belongs directly in GOV-BEHADJ-1's own anti-Goodharting section: MoSeq requires choosing a model-order hyperparameter -- how many distinct syllables the model is allowed to discover -- and there is no single objectively correct value for this choice. In other words, even the most rigorous available unsupervised discovery method does not eliminate researcher degrees of freedom; it relocates them from "which metric did I choose to report" to "how many latent states did I let the model find." Mapping fidelity is set at a moderate 0.55: REE's Fishtank environment is a discrete or low-dimensional grid world, not a continuous 3D physical space captured by depth video, so MoSeq's specific pipeline (pose extraction, AR-HMM over continuous dynamics) cannot be transplanted directly. What transfers cleanly is the modeling philosophy -- formal generative segmentation in place of informal metric engineering -- and the general shape of the answer (discrete latent states plus an explicit, estimable transition structure), which is a genuinely more rigorous target for GOV-BEHADJ-1's "Discover" step than anything the source thought itself specifies.
