# Quantifying Generalization in Reinforcement Learning (Cobbe, Klimov, Hesse, Kim & Schulman, 2019)

Deep RL benchmarks have historically evaluated agents on the same distribution of levels or environments they were trained on, which the authors argue conflates two very different things: has the agent learned a genuinely general strategy, or has it simply memorized enough about a small, fixed set of training environments to perform well on them specifically? To separate these, they built CoinRun, a procedurally generated 2D platformer with an effectively unlimited supply of structurally similar but visually and layout-distinct levels, and systematically compared agent performance on training levels versus a held-out set of unseen levels drawn from the same generative process. The headline result is a substantial, reproducible generalization gap: agents that look highly competent within their training distribution can perform dramatically worse on structurally similar but genuinely novel levels, and this gap is invisible under standard within-distribution evaluation. The paper also shows which interventions narrow the gap -- deeper convolutional architectures and supervised-learning-style regularization (L2, dropout, data augmentation, batch normalization) -- establishing that the phenomenon is both real and at least partially addressable.
