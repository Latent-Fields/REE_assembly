# Measurement Schmeasurement: Questionable Measurement Practices and How to Avoid Them (Flake & Fried, 2020)

The open-science reform movement of the past decade has focused heavily on questionable RESEARCH practices -- p-hacking, selective reporting, HARKing (hypothesizing after results are known). Flake and Fried argue this focus has left a companion problem under-addressed: questionable MEASUREMENT practices, meaning decisions researchers make when constructing, selecting, adapting, or reporting a measurement instrument itself that raise doubts about whether it validly captures the construct it claims to measure, independent of any subsequent analytic choices made with the resulting data. They situate this within the established psychometric literature on construct validity, and propose a structured set of transparency questions researchers should be able to answer about how a construct was operationally defined and how that definition maps onto the specific instrument used to measure it -- questions that, if answered transparently, make a study's inferential claims properly evaluable and its replication meaningful.
