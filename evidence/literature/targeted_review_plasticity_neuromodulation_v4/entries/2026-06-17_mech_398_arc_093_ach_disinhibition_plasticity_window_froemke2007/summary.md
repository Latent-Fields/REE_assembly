# A cholinergic disinhibition window as the mechanism of plasticity gating (Froemke, Merzenich & Schreiner 2007)

According to PubMed ([DOI: 10.1038/nature06289](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06289)), Froemke and colleagues asked the question Kilgard & Merzenich left open: *how* does the cholinergic basal forebrain refine cortical circuits? Using in vivo whole-cell recording in adult rat auditory cortex, they paired tones with nucleus basalis stimulation and watched the synaptic conductances directly. The sequence is the finding. Within seconds, NB pairing produced a rapid, stimulus-specific *reduction of synaptic inhibition*. Over the following hours — long after the brief NB stimulation had stopped — excitation to the paired input grew large, and inhibition slowly crept back up in an activity-dependent way until it rebalanced the new excitation. The receptive field settled with a new preference for the paired stimulus. They proposed that this *restricted period of disinhibition* is a fundamental mechanism for receptive-field plasticity and a synaptic memory trace for stimuli that have acquired behavioural significance.

For the opening-side plan this is the mechanistic backbone. Kilgard & Merzenich established *that* a basal-forebrain gate exists; Froemke et al. show its functional shape — and that shape is exactly what **MECH-398** and **ARC-093** need to be honest about. The cholinergic gate does not raise a steady plasticity level. It opens a *transient, state-conditional window* (the disinhibition) that then *self-closes* by rebalancing. ARC-093's architectural commitment is that encoder and residue learning rates are multiplicatively gated by a state-conditional plasticity scalar held separate from the content carried by LC-NE / 5-HT / orexin (MECH-313 / MECH-104 / MECH-203 / SD-037). Froemke grounds the "state-conditional, time-limited, separate from content" half of that: plasticity gain here is a gated *event*, not a persistent attribute, and it is set by a neuromodulatory state rather than by the sensory content itself.

The mapping is to functional shape, not implementation. REE's encoder has no explicit excitatory/inhibitory interneuron decomposition, so "disinhibition" does not transfer literally. What transfers is the temporal signature: a neuromodulator-gated gain window that opens fast and self-rebalances. This carries a design consequence I want recorded as a failure signature — in the biology, the *same* E/I machinery that opens the window also closes it. A REE design that gates the opening (MECH-398) without a coupled closure/rebalancing mechanism (the PV-clock MECH-399 proposes) risks runaway excitation, because the biological window is never simply left open. The second signature is that the disinhibition was stimulus-*specific*: only the paired input's inhibition dropped. A single global REE gain scalar that opens plasticity for the whole encoder at once would over-generalise relative to this targeted window — which is part of why Q-072 (one global scalar vs per-substrate gates) is a live fork rather than a foregone conclusion.

I hold this at confidence 0.78. The source quality is very high (Nature, in vivo whole-cell, the definitive mechanism paper), and it grounds both the existence-of-a-window claim and its state-conditional, self-rebalancing character. I discount for the absent E/I microcircuit in REE and for the fact that this is adult sensory-cortex physiology rather than a developmental critical period — so it speaks more directly to ARC-093 / MECH-398 (state-conditional adult gain) than to the developmental closure-timing claim MECH-399 makes.
