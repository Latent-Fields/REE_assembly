# Critical-period timing is set by parvalbumin-interneuron maturation (Hensch 2005)

According to PubMed ([DOI: 10.1038/nrn1787](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn1787)), Hensch's 2005 review is the field-defining synthesis of what opens and closes cortical critical periods. The headline is that the *timing* of the window is not set by a clock or by excitatory plasticity, but by the functional maturation of a specific, late-developing subset of parvalbumin-positive (PV) inhibitory interneurons. As inhibition matures and the excitation-inhibition balance shifts, the window opens; structural consolidation then closes it through proteolytic reorganization of the extracellular matrix — the perineuronal nets that progressively enwrap those same PV cells. Crucially, Hensch shows that direct control over the window's timing, duration, and closure is achievable by manipulating the E-I balance, and he frames critical-period plasticity as a *continuum of local-circuit computations ending in structural consolidation*, not a binary switch.

This grounds the two later, deferred pillars of the opening-side plan. **MECH-399** proposes a PV-analog inhibitory-maturation clock — a time-since-onset accumulator that monotonically lowers the plasticity-gain *ceiling*, replacing MECH-334's current binary `on_phase3_entry` crystallize transition. Hensch is the direct warrant: in biology, closure is exactly a maturational process that gates a continuous decline in plasticity, implemented through inhibitory maturation, not a flag flip. **Q-072** asks whether REE needs one global plasticity scalar or per-substrate scalars; Hensch's observation that visual, auditory, and somatosensory cortices have *distinct* critical-period timings is the biological argument for the layer-specific answer.

Two failure signatures fall out, and both are warnings against over-simple REE designs. First, Hensch is explicit that canonical homosynaptic plasticity at excitatory synapses alone (LTP/LTD) *fails* to predict in vivo critical-period manipulations — the driving force is inhibitory maturation. A REE closure clock built as a pure time-since-onset accumulator, decoupled from any inhibitory-maturation or E-I-balance analogue, would be unfaithful in precisely the way those excitatory-only models were. Second, the area-specificity and the continuum framing both cut against a single global binary clock: MECH-334's current `on_phase3_entry` transition is a reasonable V3 placeholder, but the biology says the mature form is per-substrate and graded.

I want to be careful about scope and about double-counting. REE has no PV interneurons and no perineuronal nets, so the transfer is to the *functional form* — closure as a continuous, inhibitory-maturation-gated lowering of a plasticity ceiling, with area/layer-specific timing — not to the cellular machinery. The closure-side detail in this review overlaps with the existing `targeted_review_critical_period_crystallization` entries (Pizzorusso 2002 on PNNs, Morishita 2010 on the Lynx1 cholinergic brake), which already ground MECH-334; I have deliberately *not* re-tagged MECH-334 here, restricting this entry's claim_ids to MECH-399 and Q-072 so the closure side is not double-counted. And MECH-399 is explicitly deferred in the plan — it only earns its keep once a continuous gain scalar (PLW-3) exists for it to clamp. This entry closes its grounding debt in advance; it does not authorise building it. Confidence 0.80: a synthesis rather than a single primary result, but by the principal investigator of the field and resting on one of the most replicated closure mechanisms in systems neuroscience.
