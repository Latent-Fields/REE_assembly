# Acetylcholine and noradrenaline jointly gate cortical plasticity (Bear & Singer 1986)

According to PubMed ([DOI: 10.1038/320172a0](https://doi.org/10.1038/320172a0)), Bear and Singer set out to resolve a confusing literature. Earlier work had blamed noradrenaline depletion for the loss of ocular-dominance plasticity after 6-hydroxydopamine infusion, but other noradrenaline-depleting methods left plasticity perfectly intact. Their resolution is the result that matters here: in kitten striate cortex, ocular-dominance plasticity was abolished only when *both* the cholinergic and the noradrenergic cortical innervations were destroyed. Lesioning either system alone did nothing. They proposed that acetylcholine and noradrenaline facilitate synaptic modification through a common, conjunctive mechanism — and noted, as part of untangling the old contradiction, that 6-OHDA itself interferes directly with cholinergic action.

This paper pins down the *compositional form* the opening-side gate should take. The plan's PLW-3 readiness gate is explicit: the natural composition for **MECH-398** is `ACh-gate × LC-NE-gate`, multiplicative, where the LC-NE signal already lives in REE as content (MECH-313 tonic, MECH-104 phasic) and is reused as an *input* to the plasticity-gain gate without the gate collapsing into it. Bear & Singer is the direct biological warrant for that design choice. The conjunctive requirement — neither system sufficient alone — is exactly a multiplicative gate: knock out one factor and the product still requires the other. A gate built on a single neuromodulatory input would mis-predict redundancy and be more brittle than the substrate it models.

It is also the cleanest support for the plan's *hard separation rule*. The framing note insists that the plasticity-GAIN signal must not be conflated with the content signals (LC-NE for choice, 5-HT for state, orexin for affect). Bear & Singer's own history is a cautionary tale about conflation: two decades of contradictory plasticity-modulation results came partly from crude single-transmitter manipulations that bled across channels (their 6-OHDA / ACh cross-interference observation). The lesson for REE is structural — keep the learning-rate gate architecturally distinct from the content channels, or risk reproducing the same confound in silico. I have recorded both points as failure signatures.

The caveats are real and I have not hidden them. This is a 1986 lesion/depletion study, and ocular-dominance plasticity is a developmental critical-period phenomenon, whereas MECH-398 targets state-conditional gain that need not be developmental. The pharmacological specificity of 1980s lesion methods is exactly what the paper itself was written to question. So the transfer is to the *compositional property* — conjunctive ACh × NE gating, neither sufficient alone, gain-channel separate from content — not to the developmental timing or the lesion pharmacology. On that narrow but load-bearing point the evidence is strong and has been independently corroborated since, which is why I set confidence at 0.79 with mapping fidelity weighted high.
