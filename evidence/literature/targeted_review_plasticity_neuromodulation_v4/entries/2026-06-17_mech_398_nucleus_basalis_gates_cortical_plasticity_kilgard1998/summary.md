# Nucleus basalis activity gates adult cortical plasticity (Kilgard & Merzenich 1998)

According to PubMed ([DOI: 10.1126/science.279.5357.1714](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.279.5357.1714)), Kilgard and Merzenich asked a deceptively simple question: what licenses the adult cortex to rewrite its sensory maps? Their answer was the cholinergic basal forebrain. When they paired a tone with episodic electrical stimulation of the nucleus basalis (NB) in adult rats, primary auditory cortex underwent massive, progressive receptive-field reorganization. The same tone delivered without NB stimulation did essentially nothing. The basal forebrain, they argued, acts as a teacher — a diffuse neuromodulatory signal that marks which stimuli matter and thereby gates whether cortex incorporates them into long-term representation.

This is the cleanest available biological referent for what `plasticity_neuromodulation_v4:PLW-3` proposes as **MECH-398**: an ACh-analog basal-forebrain plasticity-*gain* gate. REE has built the machinery that *closes* the plasticity window — INV-074's crystallization necessity, MECH-334's EWC write-protect, the MECH-333 closure half — but it has nothing that *opens and gain-gates* it. Kilgard & Merzenich is the evidence that such an opening-side gate is not optional decoration: remove the basal-forebrain signal and adult cortex stops remapping even as it continues to perceive and act. That is precisely the asymmetry the plan's PLW-1 framing names — REE owns the lock but not the key.

The translation to REE is to the *gating role*, not the pharmacology. MECH-398 would be a scalar in [0,1] multiplying encoder learning rates and residue write magnitudes, computed from REE's own state proxies (MECH-205 surprise EMA for novelty, SD-032a salience-coordinator mode for attentional focus, drive level plus sustained z_harm for arousal). The paper says such a signal exists and is causally necessary; it does not say it must be acetylcholine, nor that the auditory-map readout transfers.

Two failure signatures discipline the mapping. First, NB pairing narrowed, broadened, or left receptive fields unchanged *depending on the paired acoustic input* — the gate licenses input-driven change rather than imposing a fixed plasticity direction. A REE gate that merely scales one global learning rate, divorced from what the encoder is currently exposed to, would miss this: the biology gates plasticity *on*, but the content of the change still flows from the input stream. Second, plasticity required temporal *pairing* — NB activation coincident with the stimulus. A gain scalar that is not bound to the encoder's moment-to-moment exposure would mislocalise plasticity onto whatever happened to be present when the state scalar rose.

I hold this at confidence 0.80. The source quality is about as high as neuroscience offers (Science; the definitive paradigm for this question), and the gating role maps cleanly onto MECH-398. What I discount for is the genuine transfer gap: this is exogenous electrical stimulation in rat sensory cortex, and REE's analogue is an endogenously computed scalar in an artificial encoder. The paper establishes the *existence and necessity* of an opening-side gate — it does not, on its own, adjudicate whether that gate must be state-*conditional* in REE's specific sense (that is ARC-093's burden) or how it should close (MECH-399).
