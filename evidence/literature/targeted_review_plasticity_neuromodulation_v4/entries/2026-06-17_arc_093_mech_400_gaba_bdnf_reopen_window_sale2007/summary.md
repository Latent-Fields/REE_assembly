# The plasticity window is re-openable in adulthood (Sale et al. 2007)

According to PubMed ([DOI: 10.1038/nn1899](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1899)), Sale and colleagues showed something that, on the closure-side view alone, looks impossible: they reopened a critical period in adults. Amblyopia — visual acuity lost to abnormal early experience — was thought untreatable once the window had shut. Yet environmental enrichment in adult amblyopic rats restored normal visual acuity and ocular dominance. The mechanism was a reduction of GABAergic intracortical inhibition, accompanied by increased BDNF expression and reduced perineuronal-net density. The causal anchor is the rescue control: when they pharmacologically *enhanced* inhibition with a benzodiazepine, the recovery was prevented. Lower the inhibition and the window reopens; hold the inhibition up and it stays shut.

For the opening-side plan this is the evidence that closure is not a one-way door, and that matters in two directions. It *supports* **ARC-093**'s reading that plasticity gain is a continuous, state-conditional quantity that can be raised again — not a binary flag that latches permanently. And it grounds **MECH-400**, the BDNF-analog trophic window-*duration* knob, as a genuine biological lever operating in the reopening direction: BDNF rose as the window reopened. At the same time it *weakens* any REE assumption that crystallization (INV-074 / MECH-334) is strictly irreversible. That is why I have set this entry's `evidence_direction` to **mixed**: it confirms the re-openable-gain pillars while undercutting a too-rigid binary-closure model. REE should keep a recovery pathway in mind even as it builds the closure machinery — the biology never throws the key away entirely.

The honest limit is the confound, and I have made it a failure signature rather than buried it. Environmental enrichment is a sledgehammer: it simultaneously lowered GABA, raised BDNF, and stripped perineuronal nets. The paper shows these factors *co-move* to reopen the window; it does not isolate BDNF as the lever. So a REE design that attributes reopening to a single BDNF-analog duration knob (MECH-400) would over-claim specificity relative to what this experiment can support. This is exactly the kind of philosophy-right / mechanism-wrong trap the `feedback_biology_before_formal_definitions` rule exists to catch: the *phenomenon* (reversible, gradable closure) is well evidenced; the *attribution to one molecular knob* is not, here.

The transfer, as throughout this review, is to functional form. REE has no GABA, no BDNF, no perineuronal nets. What carries over is the property — closure is reversible and the gain ceiling can be re-raised — not the molecular implementation. And MECH-400 is the plan's explicitly lowest-priority, deferred pillar; this entry closes its grounding debt without authorising anyone to build it. Confidence 0.76: a clean adult-reopening result with a strong causal control (the benzodiazepine rescue), discounted for the multi-factor confound and the genuine animal-to-algorithm transfer gap.
