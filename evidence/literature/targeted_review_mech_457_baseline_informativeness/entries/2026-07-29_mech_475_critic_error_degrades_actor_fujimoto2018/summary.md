# Fujimoto et al. 2018 (TD3) — critic error actively degrades the actor, and fixing the critic reverses it

ICML 2018 · [arXiv:1802.09477](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.09477).

**What the paper did.** Fujimoto, van Hoof and Meger take the well-known observation from value-based RL — that function-approximation error produces *overestimated* value estimates and suboptimal policies — and show it persists in the *actor-critic* setting, where the actor is trained on the critic's estimates. They then propose TD3: take the minimum of two independently-trained critics to cap overestimation, delay policy updates until the value estimate settles, and smooth the target policy with noise. On the MuJoCo/OpenAI-gym continuous-control suite the method outperformed the prior state of the art in every environment tested.

**Why it is the ML anchor for MECH-475's causal core.** The claim's hard content is not "the critic is imperfect" but "the imperfect critic *actively degrades* the actor — competence lands below its own control, not on a plateau." Terminal-only measurement hides this; a plateau and a below-control collapse can look similar if you only read the endpoint. Fujimoto is the cleanest general-domain demonstration that critic error is *not inert*: it accumulates and propagates into a measurably worse policy, and — this is the part that matters for REE — correcting the critic *reverses the sign of the outcome*. That is precisely the shape of REE's own positive control, V3-EXQ-788, where a distributional critic retains 1.839 of installed competence while the scalar critic degrades it. TD3 says, in a domain with none of REE's idiosyncrasies, that "make the baseline informative and the outcome flips" is a real and reproducible phenomenon, not a REE-specific artifact. That materially strengthens the plausibility of MECH-475's decisive falsifier — re-running the three destructive treatments with the 788 distributional critic in place and expecting the inversion to reverse or flatten.

**The load-bearing caveat.** TD3's diagnosed pathology is *overestimation bias* — the critic is systematically too high — whereas MECH-475's measured pathology is *uninformativeness/flatness*: `std(V)/std(G) = 0.041`, a critic that barely varies with the return, so the advantage carries variance rather than signal. These are cousins (both "the critic is wrong, so the actor gets worse") but not the same error, and TD3's specific corrective — the minimum of twin critics — targets bias, not flatness. So this entry supports the *structural* prediction (critic error is destructive and reversible) and the *direction* of REE's proposed fix, but it does not license the *identity* of the mechanism or a *magnitude* of reversal to expect. I have been careful not to let "TD3 fixes actor-critic" slide into "TD3 is REE's fix"; it is the existence proof for the causal shape, nothing more.

**Confidence.** 0.66, `supports`. Highly-cited, strong empirical result, and crucially *same domain* (actor-critic RL), so transfer risk is low (0.35). Mapping fidelity is held at 0.60 because the structural claim transfers cleanly while the specific error mode does not — which is the honest boundary of what a bias-focused paper can say about an uninformativeness-focused claim.
