# Stalnaker et al. 2012 — the action-value teaching signal in dorsomedial striatum is action-specific

According to PubMed. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0832-12.2012) · J Neurosci 32(30):10296–10305.

**What the paper did.** Rats performed a task in which the reward predicted by each of two actions was repeatedly violated, deliberately eliciting reward prediction errors (RPEs). The authors recorded 1076 single units across posterior dorsomedial (pDMS) and dorsolateral striatum, separating fast-firing neurons from phasically-firing ones, and asked whether striatal RPE-like signals are *action-specific* — that is, whether the error signal that would update an action's value is addressed to that action, or is a global scalar like the midbrain dopamine RPE.

**Key finding.** A subpopulation of fast-firing pDMS neurons (19 of 196, ~10%) showed the RPE signature — phasic increase to unexpected reward, decrease to unexpected omission, increase to cues predicting high value — but, crucially, *linked to a specific action* (the animal's anti-preferred action), unlike the dopaminergic RPE, which is not action-specific. Such a signal could update the value of a particular action rather than broadcasting a single error to every representation.

**Why it belongs in this dossier.** MECH-475 is a claim about a *critic*: it says REE's value critic is flat and uninformative on the policy's own state distribution, so the advantage it produces carries variance rather than signal, and adding optimisation therefore degrades rather than plateaus. Samejima 2005 established that action-*value* is explicitly represented in dorsal striatum; Stalnaker adds the *teaching-signal* half — the error signal that keeps such an action-value estimate informative is itself action-specific and lives in dorsomedial striatum, distinct from the global dopamine RPE. So the biology sketches exactly the object REE is missing: a well-formed action-critic is one that receives action-addressed credit. A critic driven only by a global, non-action-specific error, or one whose value estimate is flat, is the neural picture of REE's `std(V)/std(G) = 0.041` — an advantage that cannot tell the actor *which* action was better.

**Honest limits.** This is correlational rodent data, the action-specific RPE population is thin (~10% of pDMS fast-firing units), and — like the other two biological entries — it shows the *existence and form* of the informative signal, not the *consequence* of removing it. It does not, by itself, demonstrate that an uninformative critic makes competence fall below its own control. That destructive step is what the lesion result (Rothenhoefer: compromised value channel → erratic, negative-feedback-weighted choice) and the computational entries (Fujimoto 2018; Tucker 2018) carry. Stalnaker's job here is to pin the *locus and form* of the teaching signal, complementing Samejima on the value-representation side.

**Confidence.** 0.58, `supports`. Source quality is solid (large single-unit sample, well-controlled RPE elicitation). Mapping fidelity is 0.55 because it grounds the locus/form of the informative critic, not the destructiveness. Transfer risk 0.5 for the rodent-to-REE step. It is the weakest-confidence of the three biological entries and is deliberately weighted as corroboration of the locus resolution rather than independent evidence for the claim's causal core.
