# Climbing towards NLU: on meaning, form, and understanding (Bender & Koller, 2020)

Every other entry in this pull addresses one of MECH-011's four positive assertions. This one addresses its hedges, which are the part of the claim most likely to be quietly dropped precisely because nothing currently supports them. MECH-011 states that "language-learned ethics is brittle without embodiment" and that "purely linguistic agents risk ethical drift and rationalisation loops." Both are asserted without argument. Bender and Koller supply the argument.

Their thesis is a priori rather than empirical: meaning is a relation between linguistic form and communicative intent, and a system exposed only to form has no route to the intent side of that relation, however much form it sees. The octopus thought experiment makes the point vivid — an intelligence intercepting a conversation it cannot ground can learn to continue it plausibly without ever having access to what it is about. The authors' target is the over-reading of benchmark performance as understanding, and their remedy is conceptual hygiene about the distinction.

For REE the translation is direct, and it clarifies something the claim leaves implicit. If an agent acquires an ethical constraint through a symbol channel alone, it holds the constraint's form with no access to its referent. What would supply the referent? REE's answer, embedded in MECH-011's closing sentence, is harm sensing and residue continuity — the agent knows what "harm" means because it has a harm signal, not because it has read sentences containing the word. That is why the claim insists language is a multiplier and not a substitute, and Bender and Koller give the reason the distinction is load-bearing rather than merely cautious.

There is a second and more immediately practical consequence, which concerns how such an agent should be evaluated. If a rationalisation loop is a real failure mode, then the quality of an agent's verbal ethical justifications is close to the worst available measure of its ethical competence — a loop optimises exactly that surface. Taken together with the Doll findings on confirmation bias, the picture is uncomfortable: an ungrounded linguistic agent could become simultaneously more articulate about its ethics and less responsive to the harm it causes, with articulacy rising as responsiveness falls. Any future REE evaluation attaching to this claim should measure behaviour under harm contingencies, never the acceptability of the justification.

I hold this at modest confidence and want to be explicit about why. It is an argument, not a measurement — it gives grounds for expecting brittleness, not evidence of it. The thesis is also actively contested, and the six years since publication have seen multimodal and embodied-agent work shift the terms of the debate considerably; citing it as settled would misrepresent the field. Most importantly, it concerns semantic understanding generally, whereas MECH-011 concerns ethical learning, and the extension assumes ethical terms are grounded the way ordinary referential terms are. That is a substantive philosophical position rather than a neutral step, and a reasonable person could reject it while accepting everything else in the paper. The right use of this entry is as the articulation of a constraint MECH-011 depends on and had not previously cited — not as its proof.
