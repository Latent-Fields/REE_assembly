# Experiential reward learning outweighs instruction prior to adulthood (Decker, Lourenco, Doll & Hartley, 2015)

This is the entry in the pull that makes MECH-011 harder rather than easier to believe as written, which is why it is worth including. The claim asserts that language *accelerates* generalisation. Decker and colleagues ran the instructed-learning paradigm across development and found that the linguistic channel's effect on veridical learning was, in the most mature participants, net negative — and that this got worse, not better, with age.

The design sets inaccurate instruction against trial-and-error experience across children, adolescents and adults. Adults were markedly biased by the false instruction. Children and adolescents estimated the stimulus values more objectively, learning what the environment actually contained rather than what they had been told it contained. Susceptibility to instructional bias, in other words, is something one acquires.

There is a naive reading of this that REE should resist: that language is simply bad for learning and the young are epistemically superior. That is not what the data support, and it ignores everything language buys — the counterfactual reasoning and vicarious knowledge transfer that MECH-011's third and fourth assertions name, neither of which this task gives instruction any opportunity to provide. The instruction here was false and the environment was fully observable through experience. Under those conditions a channel carrying second-hand information can only hurt. The result is better read as establishing the *cost side* of a trade whose benefit side this paradigm deliberately excludes.

But the cost side is what MECH-011 under-specifies, and the developmental direction is genuinely informative for REE's curriculum ordering. If susceptibility to linguistic override increases as the system matures, then the period of experientially-grounded learning is not merely a prerequisite that makes language *meaningful* later — it is an active protection against a miscalibration that language introduces and that deepens as the agent's capacity to represent instruction grows. That reframes "language-learned ethics is brittle without embodiment" from a statement about semantic grounding into a statement about calibration hygiene, and it suggests the symbol channel in a future REE substrate should be gated on accumulated residue rather than switched on at initialisation. It is also, notably, consistent with the ordering intuition REE has arrived at elsewhere on quite different grounds.

The caveats are real and I do not want to oversell the mapping. A developmental difference in susceptibility to instruction has several candidate explanations that this design cannot separate: children may simply trust the experimenter less, or hold the instruction less well in working memory, or engage differently with the task. Any of those produces the observed pattern without licensing "grounding protects calibration." Reading it my way is an interpretation. And the transfer risk here is the highest of the five entries in this pull — human developmental trajectories map onto artificial curricula loosely at best, and the dependent measure is reward-value estimation rather than anything with residue's affective character. I have marked this `mixed` rather than `supports` deliberately: it backs one half of MECH-011 and puts real pressure on the other.
