# Vicarious extinction blocks the return of learned fear (Golkar, Selbing, Flygare, Ohman & Olsson, 2013)

MECH-011 claims that a social channel transmits "negative knowledge" — what not to do. The obvious reading is warnings: one agent tells another that something is dangerous, saving it the cost of finding out. Golkar and colleagues demonstrate the inverse, and in doing so raise a question MECH-011 does not currently ask, which is whether the socially transmitted signal is better or worse than the directly experienced one.

The design is straightforward. Participants were fear-conditioned, then extinguished either in the usual first-person way — repeated exposure to the conditioned stimulus without the aversive outcome — or vicariously, by watching someone else undergo that safe exposure. Vicarious extinction produced better extinction. More strikingly, it blocked the return of fear, which standard extinction did not.

That second result is the one worth dwelling on, because return-of-fear is the standard demonstration that extinction does not erase a learned association so much as overlay it with a competing one. A manipulation that blocks reinstatement is doing something qualitatively different from a manipulation that merely extinguishes faster. For REE, this maps onto the second of MECH-011's assertions — accelerated generalisation of residue — more precisely than one might expect. What generalised better here was an affective trace, tested under exactly the conditions that normally expose a trace as context-bound. If REE's residue is a care-marker with affective continuity rather than a propositional record, then "generalisation of residue" and "resistance to the return of fear" are closer to being the same measurement than the vocabularies suggest.

There is also a methodological warning embedded here that applies to REE's own experiment harness. The vicarious advantage did not show up as a faster extinction curve; it showed up in the reinstatement test afterwards. A run scored on within-session learning curves — which is the default in most harnesses, REE's included — would have recorded these two conditions as roughly equivalent and missed the entire effect. Any future experiment attaching to MECH-011's second assertion needs a generalisation or reinstatement probe built in from the start, or it will be structurally incapable of detecting the thing it is meant to test.

Now the caveat that governs everything above, and the reason this entry's mapping fidelity is the lowest in the pull. The channel here is observational, not linguistic. Nobody was told anything. Participants watched another person's face and body while that person safely encountered the stimulus. MECH-011 is a claim about *language*, and it is a live possibility — not a remote one — that what makes vicarious extinction work is affective resonance rather than transmitted propositional content, in which case a symbolic channel would not reproduce it at all. That would be a genuinely important negative result for REE, since it would mean language is not simply a more efficient version of the social channel but a different mechanism with different properties. Given that ree-v3 currently has neither a symbol channel nor a multi-agent environment, the right use of this entry is as a specification of what such a substrate would have to be able to express, and as a reminder that the social/linguistic distinction is doing real work that MECH-011 currently elides.
