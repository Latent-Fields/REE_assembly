# Mobbs et al. (2020) -- Space, Time, and Fear: Survival Computations along Defensive Circuits

## What the paper did

Mobbs, Headley, Ding and Dayan review the defensive-behaviour literature -- rodent ethological work, human flight-initiation-distance studies, virtual-predator fMRI -- and organise it around a single variable: defensive distance, the spatial and temporal remove between an agent and a threat. Their synthesis is that this variable does not merely scale the intensity of the response; it switches the *kind* of computation performed. Proximal threats, they write, are addressed by "a limited repertoire of reflexive and myopic actions, reflecting reduced decision and state spaces and model-free architectures." Distal threats, by contrast, "allow increased information processing supported by model-based operations, including affective prospection, replay, and planning." The two regimes are not sealed off from one another: they are "often intertwined," and conditions of relative safety permit model-based operations to instruct model-free control.

## What it says about MECH-021

This is the paper in the pull that speaks to what MECH-021 actually measures. The other four bear on whether a widthed now exists (Chaudhuri, Wolff) or on whether temporal depth formally affords planning (Friston). None of them contains a hazard. This one is about nothing else.

MECH-021 claims that E3 commitment behaves as a control surface integrating predictions across horizons rather than reacting to the current sensory timestamp, and it operationalises that as the anticipatory-restraint fraction: of trajectories that would have reached a hazard within the E2 rollout horizon, the fraction rejected or de-committed *before* realised harm rises above zero. Mobbs et al. report that precise dissociation as the organising principle of mammalian defence. When the hazard lies beyond the immediate horizon, control is prospective, model-based, and expressed as restraint in advance -- which is REE's anticipatory fraction. When the hazard is inside that horizon, control collapses onto reflexive responses triggered by the present situation -- which is REE's reactive arm. The claim's predicted pattern is, in broad outline, a documented feature of biological agents.

It does something else valuable, which is to make MECH-021's degeneracy precondition concrete. The claim already warns that the test is degenerate "if the harm-approach episodes are so short that predicted and realised harm coincide within one theta window." Mobbs et al. turn that from a methodological footnote into the empirically expected default: under proximal threat the system does not merely fail to integrate, it actively contracts its decision and state spaces. If REE's hazards are reachable in too few steps, the substrate should be expected to look reactive in *every* arm. That is a design warning for whoever builds this falsifier, and I would weight it heavily.

## Limitations and caveats

The mapping caveat that matters most is a difference in independent variable, and it is easy to miss. Mobbs et al. vary defensive distance -- a property of the world. MECH-021 varies `theta_buffer_size` and `rollout_horizon` -- properties of the agent -- with the environment held fixed. These are dual manipulations of the same underlying ratio (prospective horizon against time-to-harm) *only if* the agent's internal window is what limits its effective prospection. But that is precisely the proposition under test. Treating this paper as direct evidence for the REE manipulation would therefore be quietly circular, and I have kept mapping fidelity at 0.82 rather than higher to mark it.

Two further cautions. First, this is a review of mammalian defensive circuitry -- periaqueductal grey, amygdala, hypothalamic and prefrontal structures with deep evolutionarily-specified organisation. REE has none of that; its hazard sensitivity is learned. A shared behavioural signature does not license an inference to shared mechanism. Second, and this is the one that should temper any triumphant reading of a positive result: the authors treat reactive model-free control under imminence as *adaptive*, not as a deficit. A low anticipatory-restraint fraction in REE would therefore not by itself demonstrate a broken control surface -- it might demonstrate a correctly-calibrated one operating in a short-horizon environment. The claim's insistence that realised harm must *also* fall is what protects against this confusion, and it looks well-judged in light of this paper.

Finally, the intertwining point cuts against clean measurement. If model-based operations instruct model-free control even in biology, then a sharp dissociation between anticipatory and reactive restraint may not exist to be found, and a partially-anticipatory substrate may be the correct outcome rather than an equivocal one.

## Confidence reasoning

0.75, the highest in this pull. Source quality 0.80 -- *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, authors spanning the ethological (Mobbs) and computational (Dayan) sides, synthesising a substantial primary literature; capped below a decisive single experiment because it is a review. Mapping fidelity 0.82, the payoff for being the only source addressing the claim's actual dependent variable. Transfer risk 0.42, reflecting the evolutionarily-specified-circuitry gap.

What this entry establishes is that MECH-021 is asking a question biology answers affirmatively in outline: horizon does govern whether avoidance is prospective or reactive. What it cannot establish is that REE's internal window is the term that sets that horizon in REE -- which remains exactly what the experiment must show.
