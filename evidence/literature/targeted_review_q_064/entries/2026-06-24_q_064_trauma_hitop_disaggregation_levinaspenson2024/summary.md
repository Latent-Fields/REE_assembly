# Levin-Aspenson & Greene (2024) — Rethinking trauma-related psychopathology in HiTOP

*According to PubMed.* Levin-Aspenson HF, Greene AL, *Journal of Traumatic Stress* 37(3):361-371, 2024. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/jts.23014)

## What the paper did

This is a position piece on a specific awkwardness in dimensional nosology: how to place trauma-related psychopathology within HiTOP. The authors observe that the dominant definitions of post-traumatic syndrome — PTSD above all — *build the purported cause into the symptom category* (you cannot have the diagnosis without the stressor criterion). They argue this creates content-coverage problems, within-category heterogeneity, and excessive comorbidity, and that HiTOP's symptom-covariation approach has so far left a gap here. Their proposal: **disaggregate symptoms from their purported causes** — develop a detailed taxonomy of *traumatic events* (type, duration, subjective meaning) *alongside* the evolving HiTOP symptom model, rather than fusing the two.

## Key findings relevant to Q-064

Q-064's most demanding sub-requirement is the predisposition/expression factoring: a vector representation is only preferred if it keeps "which axes are fragile" (predisposition) separable from "which failure mode is currently expressed" (expression, gated by developmental/plasticity/environmental machinery), so that *the same predisposition can yield different expressed syndromes*. The Ringwald meta-analysis speaks to registry economy and the Michelini RDoC–HiTOP interface to the mechanism-vs-symptom layering; this paper is the cleanest statement of the *separation principle itself*.

Its core argument — keep the *causal/perturbation* taxonomy distinct from the *symptom-dimension* taxonomy, because different features of a perturbation map to different symptom sequelae across spectra — is structurally identical to what Q-064 asks of REE: do not fold "what made this axis fragile / what perturbed it" into "which symptom dimension is now expressed." And the paper names the failure of *not* doing so: PTSD, by building the cause into the symptom set, is heterogeneous and over-comorbid. That is exactly the anti-pattern Q-064's two-layer vector is designed to avoid.

## How it maps to REE

For REE, the lesson is a design constraint on the vector representation: the predisposition layer (ARC-086 vulnerability axes, and whatever perturbs them) and the expression layer (the realized failure mode) must be *separate coordinates*, linked by a context-gated map, not merged. The paper supplies a real precedent that doing the merge produces precisely the pathologies — heterogeneity, runaway comorbidity — that a clean factoring removes. It also supplies a useful caution about the *shape* of the map: different features of the same perturbation produce different sequelae, so the predisposition→expression mapping is high-dimensional and gated, not one-to-one. That matches Q-064's insistence that expression is "gated by developmental timing / plasticity / environment" rather than read directly off predisposition.

## Limitations and caveats

The analogy is close but not exact. The paper concerns *external* causes — traumatic events — whereas Q-064's predisposition layer is an *internal* latent vulnerability space (which axes are constitutionally fragile). "Disaggregate external cause from symptom" and "disaggregate internal predisposition from expression" are siblings, not twins; REE's distinction additionally requires the predisposition itself to be a stable, separable internal vector, which a trauma-taxonomy argument does not address. The scope is also narrow — trauma-related psychopathology — and the claims are *argued*, not empirically demonstrated. So this entry supports the *principle* of two-layer factoring strongly while contributing little to whether REE's specific axes will turn out separable.
