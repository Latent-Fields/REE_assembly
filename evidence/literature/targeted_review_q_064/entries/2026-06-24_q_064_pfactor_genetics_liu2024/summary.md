# Liu, Lichtenstein, Kotov, Larsson, D'Onofrio & Pettersson (2024) — Genetic etiology across the continuum of the general psychopathology factor

*According to PubMed.* Liu Y, Lichtenstein P, Kotov R, Larsson H, D'Onofrio BM, Pettersson E, *Molecular Psychiatry* 29(10):2921-2928, 2024. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-024-02552-2)

## What the paper did

Comorbidity across psychiatric disorders can be summarized by a latent *general psychopathology* factor — the "p factor" — that captures the variance shared, to varying degrees, by every dimension of psychopathology. This study asked whether the *whole continuum* of that factor, from mild to extreme, has the same genetic origin. Using Swedish national registers, the authors estimated the sibling association in p factors across 580,891 sibling pairs and the group heritability in 17,170 twin pairs, with the p factor built from 10 register-based diagnoses.

## Key findings relevant to Q-064

Two findings map straight onto Q-064's premises. First, the sibling association in p was **linear across the entire range, including the extreme tail** — mild and severe psychopathology are not genetically distinct kinds. Second, **twin group-heritability (0.42–0.45) closely matched the individual-differences heritability (0.41)**, indicating the same — largely *common* — genetic variants influence both normal and extreme variation in p. The authors conclude the whole continuum of the p factor shares a common genetic origin driven by common variants.

For Q-064 this is direct human evidence for two of the three mechanics the vector representation needs:

- **Comorbidity = axis-overlap.** Comorbidity across the 10 diagnoses is captured by a *shared latent factor* — i.e. disorders co-occur because they load a common dimension, exactly the "comorbidity as overlapping axis activation" the claim proposes.
- **Pleiotropy = one mechanism loading multiple axes.** A shared set of common genetic variants drives the p factor across many disorders — a single genetic *mechanism* loading multiple phenotypic axes is the definition of pleiotropy the claim uses.

It also touches the **predisposition layer**: the heritable p factor is a genetic *vulnerability* quantity, and its moderate heritability (≈0.41) means genetic risk is partial, not deterministic — consistent with Q-064's "genetic-risk ≠ inevitability" and the requirement that predisposition be kept separable from expressed syndrome.

## How it maps to REE

If REE encodes vulnerability as a vector over ARC-086 axes, this study shows the human analogue behaves as the claim hopes on the comorbidity and pleiotropy fronts: shared latent loading explains co-occurrence, and a shared genetic mechanism produces cross-disorder loading without a separate cause per disorder pair. The predisposition/expression split is also supported in spirit — heritable vulnerability is a real, measurable quantity that under-determines the expressed outcome.

## Limitations and caveats

The decisive caveat is dimensional, and it pushes against the claim as much as it supports it. This study's central object is a **single general factor**. Its power is precisely that *so much* shared variance concentrates onto one dimension. Q-064, however, requires a *multi-axis* vulnerability space whose axes are "empirically separable (non-degenerate, discriminable loadings)." The more strongly a single p factor explains comorbidity, the more it suggests the latent axes are *not* independent — and the harder it becomes to satisfy Q-064's hard precondition that the ARC-086 axes be separable rather than collapsing toward one dominant dimension. So the p-factor literature simultaneously validates the *comorbidity-overlap and pleiotropy* mechanics of the vector and warns that the *separable-multi-axis* assumption is exactly where it could turn vacuous. Add the obvious transfer gap — these are human register diagnoses, not REE mechanisms — and the entry lands as genuinely mixed.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality high (very large, well-designed family + twin study). Mapping fidelity moderate — it directly evidences two of Q-064's three premises but via a single factor rather than a multi-axis space, which stresses the third. Net **0.70, mixed**: strong support for comorbidity-as-overlap and pleiotropy-as-shared-mechanism, counterbalanced by being the clearest evidence that the latent structure may be too low-dimensional for a non-degenerate multi-axis vector.
