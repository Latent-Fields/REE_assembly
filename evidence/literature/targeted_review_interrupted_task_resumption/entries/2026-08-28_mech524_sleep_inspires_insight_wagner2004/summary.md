# Wagner, Gais, Haider, Verleger & Born 2004 — sleep as an active trigger, not a passive vault

MECH-524 proposes two alternate triggers for re-seeding resumption processing: a spare-compute signal during wakefulness, or sleep entry itself. This paper is the cleanest evidence I found for treating "sleep entry" as a licit, active trigger rather than a hand-wave — sleep here does not just protect a memory from decay, it visibly works on it.

The design is simple and well-controlled. Subjects trained on a sequence-learning task that had a hidden abstract rule buried in it — get the rule, and performance jumps qualitatively; miss it, and you only improve gradually through practice. After training, subjects were retested following one of three intervals: a night of sleep, a night of wakefulness, or a day of wakefulness (controlling for time of day, not just elapsed hours). More than twice as many subjects discovered the hidden rule after sleep as after either wake condition. Sleep, specifically — not merely time passing, not circadian phase — roughly doubled the rate of insight. And there is a nice mechanistic tell inside the data: reaction times slowed across the sleep interval before the insight appeared, which the authors read as a signature of active restructuring of the memory representation, not passive storage.

For MECH-524 this is direct support for treating a sleep-entry event as parallel to the awake spare-compute signal, both capable of triggering re-seeding of a parked goal's processing — in REE's case, MECH-285's sleep-replay sampler biased toward the interruption_snapshot, alongside the awake MECH-293 probes. It would be easy to assume sleep is simply "protective downtime" for a parked goal and treat any sleep entry as an opportunity to pass the baton to MECH-285. This paper argues for something stronger and more specific: sleep is doing generative work on the unresolved representation, which is a better match for what MECH-524 actually wants from the sleep branch (advancing toward resolution, not just preserving state).

There is a precondition in the data that I think deserves to be a design constraint, not a footnote: sleep produced *no* insight benefit in subjects who had not received the initial training. Sleep did not manufacture a solution from nothing — it needed prior engagement with the problem to work on. That maps cleanly onto a constraint I want MECH-524 to inherit: the sleep-replay re-seeding pathway should require that an interruption_snapshot actually exists, i.e. that the goal was genuinely attempted and then interrupted, rather than firing generically on any unresolved MECH-292 bank entry regardless of whether real progress had ever been made on it. A goal that was ranked but never engaged is not the same kind of "unresolved" as one that was actively worked and then cut off, and this paper is evidence that sleep's active-restructuring benefit specifically requires the latter.

The honest limitation: this is a discrete rule-insight paradigm (you either see the hidden rule or you don't), not the graded, continuous process of reorienting and resuming a goal-directed action sequence that MECH-524 is actually about, and the design does not separate REM from non-REM contributions — that finer-grained question is exactly what the companion Cai et al. 2009 entry in this pull addresses.
