# Christoff, Gordon, Smallwood, Smith & Schooler 2009 — spare capacity is not idle capacity

I chose this paper specifically to test whether "spare-compute signal" in MECH-524's trigger condition means what I want it to mean, and the answer it gives is more interesting, and more honest, than a clean confirmation.

The design is an online experience-sampling fMRI study: subjects perform a sustained, moderately demanding concurrent task while intermittently probed about whether their mind had just been wandering, and whether they were aware of it at the time. The interval immediately preceding each probe is the window of interest. The headline finding replicates the standard story at first — mind-wandering recruits default-mode network regions, the network classically associated with internally directed, stimulus-independent thought. But the second finding is the one that matters for this claim family: mind-wandering also recruits executive-control network regions, concurrently, and these two systems are ordinarily treated as antagonistic — task-positive executive activity is supposed to suppress task-negative default-mode activity, not co-occur with it. The authors read this cooperation as evidence that mind-wandering is a distinct mental state in its own right, not simply "executive control switched off, default mode switched on."

This directly shapes how I want MECH-524's trigger condition read. The proposal is a spare-compute signal composed of low SD-037 broadcast-override, low z_harm_a, and low dACC choice_difficulty — and it would be easy to misread that as "the agent is doing nothing, so resources are free." Christoff et al. argue against exactly that reading: the state they characterize is not idle, it is executive resources actively engaged alongside associative, internally-directed processing. That is a better match for what the trigger should be detecting — reduced task-directed threat/harm arbitration load (low SD-037, low z_harm_a), not reduced cognitive engagement altogether. The dACC choice_difficulty term fits particularly well here, since dACC is the canonical executive-monitoring/conflict-detection structure, and its co-recruitment alongside default-mode activity in this paper is close to a literal description of "executive machinery still running, just not on choice-difficulty from the primary task."

There is a finding here I am not going to smooth over, because I think it is a genuine open question rather than a solved detail: neural recruitment in both networks was strongest when subjects had no meta-awareness that they were mind-wandering. The state this paper characterizes is, if anything, more pronounced when it is not being explicitly monitored. That sits uncomfortably next to a design instinct I might otherwise have — that MECH-524's re-seeding trigger should be a legible signal the agent's own arbitration machinery can see and reason about. This paper's data lean the other way: the analogous biological state seems to run best as a covert background process, not a flagged, self-aware reallocation. I am registering that tension honestly rather than picking a side, which is part of why I think the trigger-design details here belong with the open Q-claim on V_s-reconciliation rather than being pre-committed inside MECH-524 itself.

The transfer risk is real and worth stating plainly: default-mode-network and executive-control-network are human fMRI functional labels with no validated mapping onto REE's SD-037/z_harm_a/dACC signal architecture. What I am drawing from this paper is a functional analogy — spare capacity looks like co-activated, cooperating systems, not idle ones — not a claim that REE's substrate literally instantiates these two networks.
