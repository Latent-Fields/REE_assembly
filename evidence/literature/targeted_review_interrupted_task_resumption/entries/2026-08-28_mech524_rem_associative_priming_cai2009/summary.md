# Cai, Mednick, Harrison, Kanady & Mednick 2009 — which sleep, and reproducing what

Wagner et al. 2004 told me sleep actively restructures an unresolved representation. Cai and colleagues ask the more surgical question I actually need answered before writing MECH-524's trigger condition: which sleep, and restructuring toward what?

Their design is a same-day nap paradigm, which lets them compare REM sleep, non-REM sleep, and quiet wakeful rest of matched duration within otherwise similar conditions — a cleaner isolation of sleep-stage than Wagner et al.'s full-night sleep-vs-wake comparison. The measure is the Remote Associates Test, a classic probe of associative/creative cognition: given three seemingly unrelated words, find the one word that links all three. REM sleep, and only REM sleep, improved performance on items that had been primed earlier in the session — quiet rest and non-REM sleep of the same length did not produce the same gain. The authors attribute this to the cholinergic and noradrenergic neuromodulatory profile that characterizes REM specifically. And there is a second finding I think is easy to undervalue: the creative benefit was independent of how well subjects remembered the specific items they'd been tested on. REM was not simply making the primed material more retrievable verbatim — it was doing something to the associative structure around that material, dissociable from item-level memory strength.

Both findings sharpen MECH-524's sleep-entry trigger in specific, testable ways. First: if the active-restructuring benefit this claim family is leaning on is a REM-specific phenomenon rather than a generic sleep phenomenon, then MECH-285's re-seeding of the interruption_snapshot into sleep replay should not fire uniformly on any sleep-phase entry — it should be gated by a REM-analogous internal state signal, which is exactly what the existing MECH-272 state-gated routing substrate is for. A trigger that fires equally across all sleep phases is predicted, on this evidence, to under-deliver relative to one that waits for the right phase. Second, and this is the subtler point: the dissociation from item-specific memory means I should not specify "successful re-seeding" as "the agent reproduces the interruption_snapshot's exact content." What this literature suggests sleep is actually doing is generalized associative restructuring around the parked goal's content — building new connections to it, not replaying it verbatim. That has a real implication for how MECH-285's output should be evaluated once this mechanism gets built and tested: watch for improved integration and re-engagement with the goal's neighborhood, not a literal snapshot-reconstruction accuracy score.

I want to flag the honest gap directly rather than paper over it: Remote Associates Test performance is a proxy for associative cognition, not goal-directed task resumption, and there is no established mapping from human polysomnographic REM/non-REM categories onto REE's MECH-272 sleep-phase substrate. Calling anything in REE "REM-analogous" is a mapping decision this paper cannot make for us — it can only tell us that phase-specificity is worth taking seriously once that mapping gets made.
