# Salvucci 2010 — the tax the snapshot is built to avoid

If Altmann & Trafton tell me why a preserved trace still needs a cue, Salvucci tells me what happens when the cue arrives but the context has to be rebuilt anyway — and it is this paper, more than any other in the pull, that argues for the specific *richness* of MECH-524's proposed interruption_snapshot.

Salvucci's model is built around a simple, well-measured phenomenon: when you resume a task after an interruption, you do not simply pick up where you left off. You have to re-encode — reconstruct — enough of the task state to know what step comes next, and that reconstruction takes real, measurable time before productive resumption begins. He calls that gap resumption lag, and the finding I find most useful is not just that it exists but that it varies systematically with *when* the interruption landed. An interruption at a natural subtask boundary — you'd just finished one coherent chunk of work — costs less to recover from than one that lands mid-subtask, because a boundary leaves less in-progress context that has to be re-derived from scratch. Finish a paragraph, then get interrupted, and there is not much to reconstruct. Get interrupted mid-sentence, and there is.

This is the clearest architectural argument in the whole pull for why MECH-524 does not propose reusing SD-039's existing, simpler goal-snapshot payload and instead proposes something richer: z_world, z_self, z_goal, the CEM planning stage, the rule-arbitration weights in play, and what had already been attempted. That entire list is a bet that the thing worth preserving is exactly the in-progress context Salvucci shows humans have to expensively reconstruct when it is not preserved. The snapshot is not there to make the goal more memorable (that would be the Zeigarnik-shaped, and largely unsupported, framing); it is there to make the reconstruction step itself unnecessary.

The boundary-timing result also hands the mechanism a concrete, falsifiable prediction rather than a vague design intuition: if the interruption_snapshot is doing its job, its benefit — the resumption-lag reduction it buys — should scale with how "mid-rollout" the interruption was, largest for interruptions that land deep inside a CEM stage with partial rule-arbitration weights in flux, smallest for ones that happen to land right at a clean stage boundary where little context would have needed reconstructing anyway. That is a real experimental handle, not just a plausibility argument.

The honest limits: this is a four-page CHI paper, not a full journal treatment, and the domain is a human office-editing task interrupted by a secondary task — the "subtask boundaries" are things a person recognizes as natural break points in text or photo editing, not CEM rollout stages in a continuous-control world model. Whether REE's CEM-stage boundaries behave anything like the boundaries Salvucci's participants used is untested here and worth flagging as its own open question rather than assumed. What transfers cleanly is the structural claim — reconstruction has a cost, that cost depends on interruption timing, and a preserved context is the direct antidote — and that is exactly the shape of argument MECH-524 needs.
