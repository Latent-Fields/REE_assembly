# Scalabrini et al. 2020 — dissociation has two shapes, not one

The gap this entry is meant to close is honest in a way I want to keep honest: MECH-524's own notes already named the dissociative pole ("trigger stuck too restrictive, or the interruption_snapshot never captured in the first place") and then flagged it as ungrounded rather than reaching for the nearest-sounding citation. Reading Scalabrini, Mucci, Esposito, Damiani, and Northoff's 2020 review of Janet's dissociation-as-disrupted-integration framework, I think the honest next step is not just "here is a citation" but "here is a reason the pole as currently written might actually be two poles."

Janet's original clinical account, which this paper is explicitly trying to re-ground in modern neuroscience, does not treat dissociation as one thing. It separates *compartmentalization* symptoms (amnesia — content becomes inaccessible) from *detachment* symptoms (depersonalization/derealization — content is present but subjectively estranged, not engaged) from structural dissociation of personality more broadly. The paper's own contribution is a hypothesis about why: three proposed levels of neuronal integration — regional binding, network synchronization, global-scale integration — with different levels of disrupted integration producing different symptom clusters. I want to be careful here, because the paper itself is careful: it says the exact neuronal mechanisms connecting disrupted integration to these different symptom clusters "remain unclear." This is a structural hypothesis, generously supported by preliminary fMRI work from the same lab, not a settled finding.

But the structure is exactly what MECH-524's own notes are missing. As written, the restrictive pole reads as a single failure: the trigger doesn't fire, or the snapshot was never captured, and either way nothing gets resumed. Janet's compartmentalization/detachment split suggests these are not the same failure. A compartmentalization-shaped failure in REE terms would be a *capture* failure — the interruption_snapshot's content (z_world, z_self, z_goal, the in-flight CEM stage, the rule-arbitration weights) is never bound together at the moment of interruption, so there's nothing to reseed even if the trigger later fires perfectly. A detachment-shaped failure would be a *coupling* failure — the snapshot exists, the spare-compute or sleep-entry trigger genuinely fires, but the reseeding into MECH-293's probes or MECH-285's replay never actually engages with it, the way a depersonalized patient can be alert and functional while nothing they're alert to feels like theirs to act on. MECH-524's notes as they stand describe only the second shape. This paper is my reason to think the first shape deserves its own name in a future design pass, not to be folded silently into the second.

What I don't want to overclaim: this is a theoretical review synthesizing a 130-year-old clinical framework into a still-speculative three-level neural model, backed by preliminary data from the authors' own line of work. The regional/network/global hierarchy is offered as a hypothesis, and I am borrowing only its shape — a taxonomy that distinguishes capture-failure from coupling-failure — not any claim about REE needing an analogous multi-level integration architecture to implement it. That would be reading much more into this paper than it is actually claiming. The honest confidence here is moderate: strong enough to change how MECH-524's dissociative pole should eventually be specified, not strong enough to specify it outright.
