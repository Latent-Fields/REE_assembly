# Eisenberg & Miller (1987) — The relation of empathy to prosocial and related behaviors

According to PubMed. Eisenberg, N., & Miller, P. A. (1987). *The relation of empathy to prosocial and related behaviors.* Psychological Bulletin 101(1): 91–119. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3562705/)

## What the review did

This is a meta-analytic review of the human literature on whether empathy predicts prosocial behaviour. Its enduring contribution is not the headline "empathy relates to prosociality" but the finding that the *strength* of that relation depends sharply on how empathy is operationalised. Physiological indices of empathic arousal, and facial/expressive measures, correlate with actual prosocial behaviour more strongly than self-report measures do — and the review makes the point, later much cited, that physiological assessments are less subject to voluntary, top-down manipulation by the subject than facial or verbal responses are. Verbal self-report is the channel most under the subject's strategic control and, correspondingly, the weakest predictor of what they actually do for others.

## Why it matters for Q-091

Blair and Ayers establish that masking is possible and that a language-only judge is foolable. Eisenberg & Miller supply the missing piece: the measurement principle that tells REE what a *good* evaluation looks like. The generalisable finding is that the trustworthiness of an empathy measure is inversely related to how much voluntary control the subject has over it. A physiological response is informative precisely because you cannot decide to have it; a verbal report is uninformative in proportion to how freely you can choose what to say. This is the exact axis Q-091 lives on. A fluent language system has maximal voluntary control over its linguistic channel — that is what fluency *is* — so by this principle the language channel is the worst possible basis for inferring genuine other-directedness, and an evaluation built on it will be captured by whatever output the system judges will read as caring.

The translation (see `mapping`) is that REE's empathy evaluation should be anchored to a signal the agent cannot strategically produce — the machine analogue of the involuntary physiological index. In REE's own vocabulary the candidates are costly other-directed action under live alternatives, and residue accrued after exploiting a cooperating other (MECH-407): behaviours whose cost makes them hard to fake, playing the role the autonomic measure plays in humans. That is what converts Q-091 from a worry into a design specification for the evaluation.

## Limits and mapping caveats

The transfer here is by analogy of a *property*, not of a measure. REE has no autonomic nervous system, so there is no literal physiological index to import; what carries over is the abstract lesson — prefer the involuntary, hard-to-fake channel — instantiated in whatever REE-native signal has that property. Two further caveats: the review concerns how well a measure tracks empathy that is *present*, whereas Q-091 is about a fluent system *masking* empathy that is absent, so the application requires one extra step of reasoning; and it is a 1987 human developmental/social-psychology synthesis that long predates artificial agents. It grounds the measurement principle robustly but does not itself test it in any machine.
