# Chen, Xia, Liu, Zhang, Zuo, Wu, Lai & Hu (2026) -- Reactivating Conflicting Evaluative Memories during Sleep Reduces Decision Ambivalence

**Verification note:** bioRxiv preprint (doi 10.64898/2026.03.11.710434, posted 13 Mar 2026). Verified 2026-09-03: the DOI resolves via a bioRxiv lookup redirect; title and full eight-author list matched (the citation's "Chen D, Xia T, Liu J, Hu X" is the standard first-three-plus-last-author abbreviation of the same list). Not peer reviewed.

## What the paper did

Chen and colleagues built a three-day paradigm to ask whether sleep can reconcile genuinely conflicting evaluative memories about the same thing. On Day 1, participants learn that a set of cues predicts one valence (positive or negative outcomes). On Day 2, they learn the opposite valence for the same cues -- a direct evaluative reversal, not just new unrelated information. That night, during NREM sleep, half of the reversed cues are re-presented to trigger memory reactivation; the other half serve as a within-subject control. On Day 3, participants complete evaluation and memory tests, with decision ambivalence indexed by how curved their mouse-tracking trajectories are en route to a choice (more curvature/hesitation implying more ambivalence). The headline result is that cueing reduces next-day decision ambivalence, most clearly in the negative-to-positive direction, and that this reduction tracks whether the two conflicting memories were actually integrated after sleep -- not simply whether the cue was reactivated. Electrophysiologically, stronger cue-elicited delta power during NREM predicted next-day ambivalence reduction, and spindle activity tracked memory integration success.

## Findings relevant to MECH-275 and MECH-273

MECH-275 proposes a general mechanism: sleep-phase full-Bayesian aggregation across many episodes, correcting systematic biases in whatever domain of attribution is being aggregated, with self-attribution (MECH-273) as one named specialisation and other-attribution (MECH-274) as another. This study is a fairly direct instantiation of the general mechanism in a domain MECH-275 does not itself name -- evaluative/valence conflict about an external cue -- via NREM reactivation. What makes it useful evidence rather than just a suggestive analogy is the paper's own careful language: ambivalence reduction was not automatic on reactivation, it was conditional on successful integration of the two conflicting memories. That is meaningfully different from simple erasure or suppression of one side, and it is the kind of dissociation MECH-275's schema-revision framing (explicitly not noise-fit, not deletion) would predict. The connection to MECH-273 is real but more indirect, since MECH-273 is specifically about aggregating self-attribution signals (SD-003/MECH-256 outputs) rather than external-cue valence -- both draw on the same underlying sleep-phase aggregation mechanism, but this study tests a sibling domain rather than MECH-273's own.

## REE mapping and limitations

The caveat that matters most here is one MECH-275 itself should care about: confidence versus calibration. Genuine Bayesian aggregation should produce a revised belief that is more accurate, not merely one that is held more confidently. This study's language of "integration" is a positive sign, and the fact that ambivalence reduction tracks successful integration rather than mere reactivation is somewhat reassuring on that front -- but decision ambivalence measured via mouse-tracking curvature is not a direct test of whether the post-sleep evaluative belief is better calibrated against any ground truth. The study simply cannot rule out that some portion of the effect is ordinary attractor collapse -- becoming more confident toward one valence without becoming more correct. The second limitation is the marked directional asymmetry: the effect is clearly present for negative-to-positive reversals and weak or absent for positive-to-negative ones, which meaningfully constrains how far this generalises to a valence-symmetric REE mechanism (REE's own MECH-054 already treats harm and benefit channels as distinct, so an asymmetric empirical result is at least not architecturally surprising, but it does limit the strength of the inference here).

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality sits at the preprint ceiling (0.6): a genuine causal within-subjects sleep-reactivation manipulation with converging behavioural and electrophysiological measures, but unreviewed. Mapping fidelity is capped at 0.5 specifically because of the confidence-versus-calibration gap described above -- the study's design cannot distinguish the two readings that matter most for MECH-275. Transfer risk (0.55) reflects the valence asymmetry and the distance between this human evaluative-learning paradigm and REE's own attribution/aggregation architecture. The aggregate, 0.5, is genuine but appropriately modest support: a real demonstration that sleep-phase reactivation can reconcile conflicting evidence about a target, read with the honest acknowledgment that it does not settle whether the reconciliation is calibrated.
