# Identity prediction errors update reward-identity expectations in OFC (Howard & Kahnt, 2018)

*According to PubMed.* Howard & Kahnt, *Nature Communications* 9:1611. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-04055-5)

**What the paper did.** The authors used a transreinforcer reversal-learning task with functional MRI in humans, designed so that the *identity* of an expected appetitive food-odour reward could be violated while its *value* was held matched. The point of that design is surgical: it lets you ask whether the brain tracks "which outcome is coming" separately from "how good the outcome is." They then asked whether prediction-error signals in the midbrain are evoked by identity violations alone, and whether those signals relate to outcome representations in orbitofrontal cortex.

**Key findings relevant to the claim.** Midbrain fMRI responses to identity errors and to value errors were correlated, suggesting a common origin — but, crucially, identity errors were present even with value matched. Changes in *reward-identity expectations* encoded in OFC were directly related to midbrain activity. The headline for our purposes is that OFC carries an outcome-*identity* expectation, updated by an identity-specific teaching signal, distinct from a pure value cache.

**How this maps to REE.** MECH-346 changes the seed of `z_goal`: when the incentive token bank is active, the goal latent is seeded from the most-wanted object's `z_object` (an identity-bearing latent) rather than from a raw single `z_resource` value snapshot. Howard & Kahnt is close to the cleanest biological warrant one could ask for that the brain maintains a distinct outcome-*identity* representation capable of driving prospective expectation independent of cached value. That is precisely the "what" half of the what/where factorisation the claim leans on (Dayan 1993; Barreto 2017; Schaul 2015 as the computational lineage). If the brain only ever held a scalar value at the goal node, this experiment would have found nothing once value was matched out.

**Limitations and caveats.** Two honest gaps. First, this is BOLD-level evidence in OFC, not a demonstration that an identity representation is literally used as a *generative seed* for a downstream goal or policy latent — the experiment shows the representation exists and is updated, not that it is consumed the way `update_z_goal` consumes `z_object`. Second, identity and value error signals were *correlated* in the midbrain, which is a mild caution against treating the identity seed as wholly value-free; REE's design keeps the firing gate (benefit/drive threshold) intact, which is consistent with that coupling, but a reader could fairly note the biology does not endorse a fully value-orphaned seed. The task is also appetitive-odour and human, against REE's gridworld objects.

**Confidence reasoning.** I put this at 0.78, supports. Source quality is high (clean human design, strong venue), mapping fidelity is high for the existence-of-identity-expectation premise, and transfer risk is moderate. The residual discount is the seed-versus-readout distinction, which no single imaging study can close.
