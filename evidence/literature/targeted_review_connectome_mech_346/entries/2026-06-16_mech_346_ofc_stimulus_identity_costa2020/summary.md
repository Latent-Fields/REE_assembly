# Primate OFC codes chosen-stimulus identity and stimulus-specific value (Costa & Averbeck, 2020)

*According to PubMed.* Costa & Averbeck, *The Journal of Neuroscience* 40(12):2553-2561. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2355-19.2020)

**What the paper did.** Three macaques performed a three-armed bandit in which novel options periodically replaced familiar ones, forcing genuine explore-exploit decisions. The authors recorded single units in orbitofrontal cortex and asked which decision variables OFC encodes: the identity of the chosen stimulus, the reward outcome, the chosen value, an exploration bonus, choice location, and the value of the current environment.

**Key findings relevant to the claim.** Two results matter for MECH-346. First, OFC *strongly* encoded the identity of the chosen stimulus and the reward outcome — these were the dominant signals, the variables that define states and state transitions in their model. Second, and more pointed, OFC value coding was *stimulus-specific*: value was bound to the identity of the option, not represented as identity-agnostic scalar. Chosen value and exploration bonus appeared at intermediate levels; location and environment value at low levels.

**How this maps to REE.** This is direct support for the what/where factorisation underlying MECH-346. The claim's move — seed `z_goal` from the most-wanted object's `z_object` rather than a raw resource value — presupposes that value is meaningfully attached to *which object* it belongs to. Costa & Averbeck show exactly that in primate OFC: the substrate does not hold a free-floating value, it holds value indexed by stimulus identity, and identity is the strongest thing it holds. A "most-wanted object" selection is then a biologically coherent operation over identity-specific value, rather than a category error.

**Limitations and caveats.** The mapping has two boundaries worth stating plainly. (1) Stimulus-specific value coding supports *binding* value to identity; it does not show the system performs an arg-max over identities to *seed* a single downstream goal latent — the selection step (`bank.most_wanted(per_axis_drive)`) is REE's, not the paper's. (2) There is an anatomical wrinkle the broader literature insists on (and which a sibling entry on medial-OFC value coding underscores): medial and lateral OFC dissociate value versus identity coding, so a single `z_object` seed conflates two codes that the brain keeps partly separate. Macaque bandit-task transfer to REE objects is the usual residual.

**Confidence reasoning.** 0.75, supports. Primate single-unit evidence is high quality and the stimulus-specificity result maps tightly onto the claim's premise. I discount for the arg-max selection step the paper does not test and for the medial/lateral subregion dissociation, which means "OFC codes identity" is true but coarser than the claim's single-seed picture.
