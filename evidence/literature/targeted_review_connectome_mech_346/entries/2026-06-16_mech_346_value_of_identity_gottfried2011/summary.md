# The value of identity: olfactory notes on OFC function (Gottfried & Zelano, 2011)

*According to PubMed.* Gottfried & Zelano, *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences* 1239:138-148. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1749-6632.2011.06268.x)

**What the paper did.** This is a focused review, drawing heavily on the authors' olfactory work, that pushes back against the then-dominant "OFC encodes value" orthodoxy. Its argument is structural rather than experimental: value-based models of OFC have good explanatory power but are typically *disconnected from the very stimuli* that give rise to the value. Without object identity, the authors argue, value becomes "orphaned from its source."

**Key findings relevant to the claim.** The review marshals evidence that OFC performs stimulus-specific predictive coding of *both* perceptual outcomes and expected values — that is, the predictive representation is indexed to the identity of the thing predicted. The takeaway is a position: a complete account of OFC must give object identity a "cooperative role," not relegate it to a substrate on which a value scalar floats free.

**How this maps to REE.** This entry is the *design-rationale* anchor for MECH-346 rather than a mechanistic test. The claim's whole motivation is the worry that a raw single `z_resource` value snapshot is a value orphaned from its source — exactly Gottfried & Zelano's complaint about value-only OFC models. Seeding `z_goal` from the most-wanted object's identity latent is the REE-side remedy to precisely the gap this review names. When a claim's literature confidence is zero, it is worth recording the conceptual lineage explicitly, because governance should be able to see that the design move was not arbitrary.

**Limitations and caveats.** I am deliberately conservative here. This is a review/position piece; it motivates the identity-over-bare-value stance but does not demonstrate the specific seed-substitution mechanism, and its evidence base is largely olfactory, with generality to non-olfactory object goals asserted rather than shown. It should weight the claim less than the two empirical entries (Howard & Kahnt; Costa & Averbeck) it sits beside, and I would not want it counted as independent mechanistic confirmation.

**Confidence reasoning.** 0.66, supports. The discount from the empirical entries is intentional: high relevance to the design rationale, but review-level rather than a direct test, so source quality and mapping fidelity are both held in the moderate band.
