# Behrens et al. (2018) -- the lower level of ARC-102, well characterised

ARC-102 asserts that REE has two abstraction levels that must not be conflated: a substrate level
(theta-packaged units, cognitive-map traversal, type-encoder -- computed-over and sub-symbolic) and
a symbolic level (recombination over named, grounded primitives). A distinction of that form is only
worth defending if both relata are real. This review is what makes the lower one real.

Behrens and colleagues take the place and grid codes of the hippocampal-entorhinal system -- first
characterised for physical space -- and argue that they are a special case of a general mechanism
for learning and reusing relational structure. The claim is not that everything is spatial; it is
that spatial navigation happened to be where a domain-general relational code was first visible.
They marshal the neural evidence, give a computational account of how such codes could be learned
and generalised, and note that artificial systems built on the same principles recover the same
map-like representations. This is abstraction with no symbols in it: nothing is named, nothing is
recombined by rule, and the generalisation falls out of the geometry of a learned representation.

For ARC-102 the value is precise and limited. It establishes that "abstraction" already has a
well-defined, mechanistically specified, non-symbolic sense in the brain sciences. So ARC-102's
insistence on distinguishing levels is not terminological fussiness about one word doing two jobs --
the lower job is a real computational kind with its own literature. That is the precondition for the
claim, and it is met.

What the paper does not do -- and this is where I want to be careful, because the temptation is to
read a supportive review as more supportive than it is -- is give any reason to believe in a second,
categorically distinct level. If anything it pushes the other way. The paper's whole argument is
unification: one relational-map mechanism, spanning spatial and non-spatial, concrete and abstract.
Taken at face value, the parsimonious extension of Behrens et al. to symbolic reasoning is "the same
machinery, applied to discrete named inputs", not "a different level". That is a live competitor to
ARC-102 and, on this evidence alone, the better-supported one.

There is also a mapping gap that should be logged rather than smoothed over. ARC-102 names
theta-packaged units and a type-encoder as the substrate-level mechanisms. Nothing in this review
speaks to either. The correspondence between the hippocampal-entorhinal relational code and REE's
V4 object-reasoning substrate is an analogy that REE is asserting, not one this literature has
checked.

Confidence 0.70: high-quality synthesis, one arm of a two-arm claim, and an implicit argument
against the other arm. ARC-102's real evidential burden is not showing that substrate abstraction
exists -- that is settled here -- but showing that symbolic abstraction is not simply more of it.
