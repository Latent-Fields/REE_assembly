# How do you feel -- now? The anterior insula and human awareness (Craig, 2009)

*According to PubMed (PMID 19096369).* Craig, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 10(1):59-70, 2009. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn2555).

**Claims:** MECH-214 (goal referent must be E1-representable), MECH-215 (self-model prerequisite for agentive prediction) -- the L3 interoceptive-self strand of self_model_v4:SELF-8, and the entry that harvests the anterior insula completion-set partner.

## What the paper did

Craig's review consolidates a decade of his anatomical and imaging work into a single thesis: the anterior insular cortex (AIC) re-represents interoception -- the brain's read-out of the physiological condition of the body -- and this re-representation is the substrate of subjective feelings and, he argues, of awareness itself. He marshals the breadth of conditions in which the AIC is implicated (bowel distension, orgasm, cigarette craving, maternal love, decision-making, sudden insight) and the presence of the specialised von Economo neurons to argue that interoceptive re-representation is not one feeling system among many but the common basis of *all* felt states.

## Why it grounds the claim

MECH-214 says the referent of any goal must be representable in E1's latent schema space, and its central clinical prediction is that "wanting without clear object" -- anhedonia, motivational flatness -- can be an E1 *representation* poverty rather than a dopaminergic disorder: wanting fires on a satisfaction state the agent cannot actually represent. Craig's paper is the biological warrant for the half of that claim that concerns satisfaction states specifically. The satisfaction or homeostatic state a goal aims at (warmth, satiation, relief) is, in the body, an interoceptive state, and Craig establishes that there is a dedicated cortical machinery for re-representing exactly such states and turning them into felt feelings. So when REE says a goal's satisfaction referent must be E1-representable, the biology says: the relevant E1 channel for the primitive, homeostatic goals MECH-214 names is interoceptive, and it has a locus. That makes the MECH-214 interoceptive-goal channel biologically real rather than stipulated -- which is the point of grounding it before building it.

It touches MECH-215 more lightly: the interoceptive self is the *affective* half of the z_self subject. A self-model with only an exteroceptive body schema (where the body is, what it can do) but no interoceptive channel could still predict action capacity yet would have no representable satisfaction state -- a capacity-without-affect gap that MECH-215's "affective baseline" clause anticipates.

The **anterior insula** is harvested here as a completion-set partner (the interoceptive-self locus), proposal-first. It converges with the right-posterior-insula ownership signal that Tsakiris 2010 independently named, which is worth noting: two different strands of this pull point at insular cortex as the interoceptive anchor of selfhood.

## Limitations and caveats

This is a review of insula function in humans, not a test of the REE failure mode. It grounds the *existence* and *locus* of an interoceptive self-representation; it says nothing about REE's z_goal/z_self wiring and does not show that an agent lacking the channel exhibits the predicted wanting-without-satisfaction pathology. That demonstration is the V4 DR-14 experiment (the proxy/hedonic dissociating environment, node SELF-6), which this lit-pull does not run or relieve. I have tagged MECH-214 and MECH-215 only -- not SD-030 (this is interoception, not agency/efference) and not ARC-081.
