# Vlemincx et al. 2011 -- Sigh rate and respiratory variability during mental load and sustained attention

**Claim tested:** MECH-109. Direction: **supports** (respiratory side only). Confidence 0.35.

This is the paper MECH-109's `evidence_quality_note` already leans on (cited there as "Vlemincx 2010, PMID 20536901"; it was epub 2010 and appeared in the 2011 volume). Until now it existed only as prose inside the LIT-0094 synthesis and had no `record.json`, so the index treated MECH-109 as having no literature at all. This entry fixes that and records the calibration honestly.

The design is simple. Respiration was recorded across a baseline, a stressful mental arithmetic task, a non-stressful sustained attention task and recovery periods between them. Sustained attention reduced *total* variability in breathing rate, making the breath more regular and rigid; stressful arithmetic reduced *correlated* variability. Sighs increased during mental load and during recovery after the attention task.

The REE reading is that concentrated engagement compresses the respiratory clock and that sighs act as resets, which fits MECH-109's picture: under commitment the sweep clock is suppressed, and a sigh forces a single cycle. I think that reading is plausible, but I want to be clear it is ours. The paper measures breathing, not planning. It makes no claim that anything cognitive was reset, and the rise in sighs *during* the stressful task, not only afterwards, blurs the neat "suppression under load, release at offset" timing that the claim note describes.

The anxious breath-hold failure mode, which is the claim's most distinctive clinical prediction, isn't touched either; no one held their breath. Confidence 0.35. It supports the respiratory half of the story and leaves the planning half untested. The claim note's suggestion to "upgrade to provisional after one targeted sigh-latency experiment" looks right to me, and this paper is not a substitute for that experiment.
