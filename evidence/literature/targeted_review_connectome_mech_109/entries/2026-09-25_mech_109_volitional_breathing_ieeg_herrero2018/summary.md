# Herrero et al. 2018 -- Breathing above the brain stem: volitional control and attentional modulation

**Claim tested:** MECH-109. Direction: **supports** (structural premise). Confidence 0.48.

Herrero and colleagues recorded intracranial EEG in epilepsy patients and showed that cortical and limbic activity follows the breathing cycle across a widespread network. The tracking sits in the gamma-band envelope and is specific to grey matter, so it is neuronal and not a movement artefact. They then altered the breathing. When patients paced their breath deliberately, breath-iEEG coherence rose in a frontotemporal-insular network; when they simply attended to their breathing, it rose in anterior cingulate, premotor, insular and hippocampal cortex.

Of everything in the current literature neighbourhood, this comes closest to the manipulation MECH-109 names. The claim is that breathing is the one control-plane oscillator with a top-down, voluntary handle. Herrero shows that exercising that handle changes how strongly the respiratory rhythm organises cortical activity. If, as MECH-108 proposes, the plan-sweep clock rides on breath-locked limbic and hippocampal excitability, then a voluntary route that modulates breath-cortex coupling is exactly the kind of lever the claim requires.

It is still a premise and not a test. No planning variable was measured, so we don't know whether stronger coherence under volitional pacing changes anything computational, let alone plan abandonment specifically. Volition and attention are also tangled: deliberately pacing the breath means attending to it, and the two conditions recruited overlapping networks. A sceptic could fairly say the "voluntary handle" might be an interoceptive-attention effect that happens to use the breath. The samples are small and clinical.

Confidence 0.48: good evidence that the handle exists at the cortical level, no evidence yet about what it does to planning. The obvious next step is to add a planning DV to this paradigm (sweep-like hippocampal replay proxies, or behavioural abandonment latency) across spontaneous, paced and breath-hold conditions.
