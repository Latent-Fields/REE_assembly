# Replay content is biased and generative, not a faithful re-run (Pfeiffer 2018)

According to PubMed, this review ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.22824)) interrogates what hippocampal replay actually *contains*, and the answer matters for whether Q-055's erosion premise even holds. Early accounts treated replay as off-line reactivation of recent experience. Pfeiffer marshals the evidence that the content is considerably more interesting: replay over-represents rewarded locations rather than tracking traversal frequency, it frequently runs *forward* to guide upcoming behaviour rather than rehearsing the past, and a substantial fraction reflects recombined or pre-existing network assemblies -- including sequences the animal never actually experienced.

The relevance to Q-055 is that it undercuts the simplest version of the diversity-erosion worry. That worry assumes replay strengthens whatever trajectory class was most heavily traversed, mechanically deepening the dominant attractor. But if REE's SD-017 replay inherits this generative, recombinative character, then replay is not obliged to concentrate probability on already-dominant classes -- it can re-seed, recombine, and prospectively sample, which is precisely Q-055's counter-mechanism (b) that "sleep replay itself is designed to sample diversity." On this reading sleep could even *increase* trajectory-class diversity.

I have kept this at evidence_direction `mixed`, confidence 0.55, because the same facts cut both ways. Reward-biasing of replay is generative relative to traversal frequency, but it is *concentrating* relative to reward -- it would funnel behaviour toward high-reward classes, which is itself an erosion pathway for diversity that is merely reward-orthogonal. And this is a qualitative review of rodent place-cell content with no behavioural-diversity metric attached. Its value is that it forbids the lazy conclusion: we cannot assume replay is a faithful reinforcer of the dominant trace, so the erosion premise is not free. It reframes Q-055 -- the empirical fork is what *biases* REE's spontaneous replay content -- rather than answering it.
