# Cavanagh, Frank et al. 2011 — Subthalamic nucleus stimulation reverses mediofrontal influence over decision threshold

Cavanagh, Wiecki, Cohen, Figueroa, Samanta, Sherman & Frank (2011, [Nat Neurosci 14:1462–1467](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2925)) is the targeted single-entry addition recommended by the SD-034 claim-synthesis decomposition (`claim_synthesis_SD-034-closure_2026-06-19.md`, Section 5.2 Child B) as the magnitude anchor for **MECH-446 (de-commit-authority magnitude)**. It is added on top of the existing Mayr & Keele 2000 backward-inhibition entry, which grounds the *strength* of a post-completion refractory; this paper grounds the part Mayr & Keele do not — that the brake is **conflict-graded and causally real**, not a fixed binary gate.

The study combines human scalp EEG with subthalamic-nucleus (STN) deep-brain stimulation in Parkinson's disease patients performing a decision-conflict task. The headline result has two halves. First, under high decision conflict, mediofrontal theta increases and the decision threshold rises proportionally — the agent demands more evidence before committing, scaled to how much conflict is present (a graded "hold your horses" signal, not an all-or-none stop). Second, STN-DBS **reverses** this mediofrontal influence over the threshold: stimulating the STN decouples mediofrontal theta from the threshold adjustment. That second half is the causal demonstration — the conflict-graded hold is implemented through the mediofrontal→STN hyperdirect pathway, and manipulating the STN changes the magnitude of the brake.

For MECH-446 the relevant import is the **gradedness with a control variable**. MECH-446 asserts that the closure-coupled de-commit lowers post-closure MECH-090 latch occupancy with authority sufficient to overcome the natural-commit occupancy it competes against, via a *committed-run-scaled* refractory — a longer committed run should trigger a proportionally longer de-commit. The biological signature Cavanagh & Frank establish is exactly a BG brake whose strength tracks a control variable and is causally manipulable, rather than a fixed refractory window. This is the sharpest available anchor for the "magnitude scales with re-commitment pressure" component of MECH-446, complementing Mayr & Keele 2000 (which establishes that a post-completion refractory exists and has measurable strength, but does not address scaling).

Two transfer distinctions matter and are recorded as failure signatures. First, the STN brake here scales with trial-by-trial decision **conflict** and modulates a **pre-commit decision threshold** (how much evidence to gather before committing), whereas MECH-446 specifies a **post-completion** de-commit whose magnitude scales with **committed-run length**. The shared structure — a graded BG brake whose strength tracks a control variable — is real and well-evidenced; the *identity* of the control variable (conflict vs prior-commitment-duration) is an inference, and the committed-run-length scaling remains the experimental question for the gated 460h re-queue, not something this paper settles. Second, the substrate is human EEG + clinical DBS, a transfer step from REE's artificial closure-plane; the localisation (mediofrontal → STN) is consistent with REE's MECH-090/MECH-260 basal-ganglia-analog locus, but the closure-operator's OFC/ACC completion-detection side is not what this paper measures.

Mapping fidelity 0.70 — directly supports the graded/conflict-scaled magnitude that is MECH-446's distinctive claim, discounted for the conflict-vs-committed-run-length and pre-threshold-vs-post-refractory distinctions.
