# Polack, Friedman & Golshani (2013), *Cellular mechanisms of brain state-dependent gain modulation in visual cortex* -- ARC-044

## What the paper does

Mice on a treadmill, head-fixed, watching drifting gratings, with a pipette in a layer 2/3 or layer 4 cell. The observation being explained is one that had been reported repeatedly by then and not mechanistically accounted for: when the animal runs, visual cortical neurons fire more to the same stimulus, and yet they remain just as orientation-selective as they were at rest. That combination -- more output, same discrimination -- is the signature of gain rather than of a change in what the neuron is computing. The contribution is to go inside the cell and say what produces it. Blocking noradrenergic transmission abolished the tonic depolarisation that accompanies locomotion; blocking cholinergic transmission did something else, disturbing the membrane-potential distribution during immobility. Two neuromodulators, two separable jobs, one behavioural state change.

## Why it bears on ARC-044

ARC-044 asserts a global arousal/precision signal carried by LC-NE that sets the overall sensitivity of processing, and it cites the gain-control literature for the further point that gain modulation controls signal-to-noise ratio and not merely magnitude. This paper is about as direct a piece of support for that specific proposition as an intracellular study can be. The arousal state changes, noradrenaline is causally necessary for the change, the effect is a gain effect in the technical sense (rates up, tuning preserved), and the mechanism is a shift in the membrane-potential operating point -- which is exactly why the effect is not a simple output multiplier and why it has consequences for variability as well as amplitude.

It also tells us something REE should build to rather than around. If the substrate of arousal gain is a tonic depolarisation of the evaluating unit, then the right place to apply an arousal term in REE is upstream -- at the channel's threshold or input offset -- not as a scaling applied to whatever the channel emitted. Those two implementations are indistinguishable in mean firing rate and quite different in their effect on the variance of the channel's output, and ARC-044's own interest in signal-to-noise is precisely an interest in the variance.

## What it does not say

Almost all of ARC-044. The claim is a two-layer architecture -- a global arousal gain modulated by a context-specific gain from vmPFC/OFC -- and this preparation contains only the first layer. There is no context manipulation, no prefrontal contribution, and consequently no evidence whatsoever about how the layers compose, which is the question ARC-044 actually stakes itself on. It would be easy, and wrong, to file this as a supporting paper for the unification thesis. It supports one of the four mechanisms ARC-044 wants to unify (MECH-026) having a real substrate, and it supports the choice of noradrenaline as the carrier. It is silent on unification.

There is a smaller complication too. The cholinergic result is a third factor that ARC-044's four-mechanism list has no slot for, and it is not a minor one -- it was separately necessary, for a different feature of the state change. A claim that says four REE mechanisms are one biological mechanism has to reckon with a biology in which even the single global layer turns out to be at least two interacting systems.

## Confidence

I have put this at 0.75 and called it supporting rather than mixed, because within its narrow scope it does support what ARC-044 says, and the dissociation it reports is a complication for the claim rather than a contradiction of it. The number is held down mainly by transfer risk. Mouse primary visual cortex under locomotion is the canonical arousal-gain preparation, but REE's evaluation channels are not sensory feature detectors and its arousal is hazard-linked rather than movement-linked. Whether the cellular story generalises across that gap is an assumption I am making, not something the paper establishes, and it is the assumption I would want tested first if this claim ever became load-bearing.
