# Jepma & Nieuwenhuis (2011) -- arousal tracks which choice class is made, not just how sharply

According to PubMed, this study measured pupil diameter while participants played a gambling task with a slowly drifting payoff structure, using a reinforcement-learning model to classify each choice as exploitative or exploratory. Three findings: exploratory choices were preceded by larger baseline pupil diameter; individual differences in baseline diameter predicted individual tendency to explore; and pupil changes around the exploit-to-explore transition tracked changes in task utility. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn.2010.21548)

I have logged this as *mixed* rather than as support, because on its face it is the more awkward of the two Jepma papers for MECH-463. The claim says a global scalar cannot produce behavioural differentiation. Here a global scalar index predicts precisely which class of behaviour occurs. If one stopped reading there, it looks like a counterexample.

I do not think it is one, but the escape route is narrow and worth stating plainly rather than assuming. Under a softmax, raising temperature flattens the distribution without touching the score ordering; more probability mass lands off the argmax; an observer with an RL model fitted to the choices scores those draws as "exploratory". So the entire correlation is reproducible with the ordering never once violated. MECH-463 survives -- but only under the reading where its argmax-invariance claim is about the *score vector* and explicitly not about the *sampled action*. That is the same knife-edge the Aston-Jones review sat on, and it is now the second paper to land on it. Two independent literatures pushing on the same seam is a signal that MECH-463's statement of scope needs to be tight in the substrate, not just in the prose.

The evidential weight is limited in two ways I want on the record. The measurement chain is long: pupil indexes LC activity (a contested identification in its own right), LC activity indexes gain, gain is the analogue of REE's temperature. Each link leaks. And the design is correlational, so the arrow may run backwards -- a control state that drives both the choice and the arousal would produce this exact pattern with the scalar as a passenger rather than a driver.

The most interesting thing about this paper is its relationship to its own authors' reboxetine study. That study moved the noradrenergic scalar causally and got nothing; this one observes it passively and gets a clean relationship. Same group, same theoretical target, opposite implications. When the correlational and causal arms of a literature disagree like that, the honest conclusion is that the field has not established that this scalar *causes* strategy change -- and that is a reason to weight this entry down, which is why I have put it at 0.5, below the null it apparently contradicts. A causal null with verified drug action tells you more about mechanism than a correlation does.
