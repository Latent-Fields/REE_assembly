# Jepma et al. (2010) -- a global noradrenergic scalar, moved on its own, changed nothing

According to PubMed, this is the direct human causal test that adaptive gain theory had been missing. Fifty-two healthy volunteers received reboxetine (a selective noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor), citalopram (an SSRI active comparator), or placebo, double-blind, parallel-groups. AGT predicts that elevating tonic NE should push participants toward disengagement and exploration. Two tasks were built to detect exactly that -- a diminishing-utility task and a gambling task with a drifting payoff structure. The result was a null: no group difference in task disengagement, no group difference in exploratory choice, despite the drugs demonstrably acting on central and autonomic markers. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2010.00170)

This is the most useful paper I found for MECH-463, and it is worth being precise about why. MECH-463's core assertion is that a channel applying one scalar uniformly across all K candidates cannot change which candidate wins. The cleanest possible test of that is to grab such a channel, move it in isolation, and see whether behaviour differentiates. That is what this study did, pharmacologically, in humans, with a placebo control -- and behaviour did not differentiate. The drug reached the brain and the behaviour did not care. That is the signature MECH-463 predicts.

I want to resist the temptation to bank it too hard, though, because it is a null, and nulls are promiscuous. Absence of a strategy shift is equally consistent with MECH-359's reading (the scalar is simply too weak to steer), with a dosing failure, and with the tasks not being sensitive to the kind of exploration NE governs -- the authors themselves flag that the LC-NE system might regulate exploration that *exceeds* the current task context, which their within-task paradigms could not see. So the honest statement is: this constrains the space, it does not close it.

And it emphatically does not do the one thing that would be most valuable here. MECH-463 and MECH-359 were registered as distinct precisely because they differ in polarity -- MECH-359 says affect fails to convert, MECH-463 says affect actively entrenches the incumbent and changes the commit gate. Both predict this null. The measurement that would separate them is the commit gate itself: does raising the scalar change *whether* and *how sharply* commitment fires, holding the winner fixed? This paradigm never looks there. So I have tagged the entry to both claims, which is the accurate thing to do, while noting that it raises the floor under both rather than discriminating between them.

Confidence 0.66. The design is genuinely good -- better than most psychopharmacology, with an active comparator and verified drug action, which is what rules out the boring "the drug did nothing" explanation. The cap is inferential, not methodological: this is a null that is *consistent with* MECH-463 rather than one that *implies* it. Against a claim whose own registered falsifier is an arousal-conditioned variance decomposition, that is useful supporting context and not a substitute.
