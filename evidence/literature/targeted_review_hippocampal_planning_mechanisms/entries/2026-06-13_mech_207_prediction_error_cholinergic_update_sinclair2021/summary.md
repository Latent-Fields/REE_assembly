# Prediction error, cholinergic gating, and memory updating (Sinclair et al. 2021) — MECH-207

**Claim:** MECH-207 — acetylcholine acts as a permissive write-gate on the surprise buffer: prediction errors trigger hippocampal representation destabilisation and offline memory updating *only when basal-forebrain cholinergic activation co-occurs*.

## What the paper did

This is the primary the earlier (Hasselmo) entry flagged as the load-bearing follow-up. Sinclair, Manalili, Brunec, Adcock, and Barense ran a human fMRI study in which participants watched familiar narrative videos that were interrupted *immediately before their expected endings* — a clean way to elicit mnemonic prediction errors (surprise that reality contradicts a confident expectation). Three findings matter here. First, prediction errors **reversed** the usual relationship between hippocampal activation and memory: greater hippocampal activation predicted memory *preservation* after expected endings, but memory *updating* after surprising ones. Second, hippocampal activation *patterns* stabilised as each episode unfolded (sustained representations), and prediction errors **disrupted** these sustained patterns — with the degree of disruption predicting how much the memory was updated. Third, and decisively for MECH-207: the relationship between hippocampal activation and subsequent memory **depended on concurrent basal-forebrain activation**, which the authors interpret as cholinergic modulation regulating when memories become malleable.

## Why it matters for REE — and how it resolves the mixed flag

MECH-207 makes a conjunctive claim: prediction error destabilises the hippocampal representation *and* this becomes offline updating *only with* basal-forebrain cholinergic co-activation. Sinclair et al. supply both arms in one study. The PE-driven disruption of sustained hippocampal patterns is the "surprise buffer destabilisation"; the dependence on concurrent basal-forebrain activation is the "permissive write-gate." This is about as direct a grounding as a non-invasive human study can give.

Crucially, it **dissolves the apparent contradiction** that left the Hasselmo 1999 entry marked *mixed*. Hasselmo's classic model places *consolidation* — the stabilisation of existing traces — at *low* acetylcholine. Sinclair's result is about *updating* — making traces malleable to incorporate new information — and ties that to cholinergic (basal-forebrain) co-activation at the moment of prediction error. These are different operations on different memories, not opposite claims about the same one: low-ACh consolidation stabilises what is already stored, while PE-coincident cholinergic activation opens a specific, surprised representation for revision. MECH-207 is precisely the *updating/write* side, and that is what Sinclair grounds. With this entry the claim moves from a single mixed record to a supports-plus-frame pair (Sinclair supports the write-gate directionality; Hasselmo frames the broader encode/consolidate switch).

## Caveats and confidence

The honest limit is the same indirectness that caps all human imaging here: "cholinergic" is operationalised as basal-forebrain BOLD activation and hippocampus–basal-forebrain functional connectivity — a *proxy* for acetylcholine, not a direct measurement or a cholinergic manipulation. So the permissive-gate conjunction is established *correlationally*; a causal test (e.g. cholinergic agonist/antagonist crossed with prediction error) would be the next strengthening step, and is worth noting as the residual debt for anyone building the gate. With that caveat the mapping fidelity is high (the PE × basal-forebrain conjunction maps almost one-to-one onto MECH-207) and the source is a strong human PNAS study. Confidence 0.78 (supports). This closes the directionality question that held HPL-9 at *partial*.
