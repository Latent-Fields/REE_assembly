# Louie, Khaw & Glimcher (2013) — Normalization is a general neural mechanism for context-dependent decision making

**Source:** Louie, K., Khaw, M. W., Glimcher, P. W. (2013). *Proc Natl Acad Sci USA* 110(15):6139-6144. [DOI 10.1073/pnas.1217854110](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1217854110) · PMID 23530203. (According to PubMed.)

**Claim under test:** MECH-439 — the cluster-autopsy **Reading-2** lever (rank-preserving renormalization of the primary score F against the competing field), intended grounding for **MECH-448**.

## What the paper does

This is the paper that carried divisive normalization out of the sensory cortex and into **value-based choice** — which is why, of the four normalization entries in this directory, it is the closest domain match to REE's E3 selection problem. From the abstract: *"In sensory systems, divisive normalization seems to be a canonical neural computation… Recent electrophysiological studies have extended these insights beyond the sensory domain, demonstrating an analogous algorithm for the value signals that guide decision making, but the effects of normalization on choice behavior are unknown."* The authors close that gap. They show that **relative value coding** — each option's value rescaled by its proportional contribution within the choice context, i.e. a divisive normalization over the option set — predicts specific, measurable choice phenomena, and they confirm them in *both monkey and human* multi-option choice. The headline behavioural result: *"choice models using normalization generate significant (and classically irrational) choice phenomena driven by either the value or number of alternative options,"* and *distractor options significantly alter choices between target options.*

The structural content REE cares about: an option's selection signal is the option's value **divided by the summed value of the whole field**. Add a high-value distractor and the target's *normalized* value drops, shifting (and often degrading) the choice. That is divisive normalization operating on exactly the kind of signal E3 commits on.

## Why it is the strongest bridge for Reading-2

The cluster autopsy argues the F-monopoly is a selection-variance problem and the principled lever is to renormalize F against the competing field rather than widen a near-ties shortlist. Two things this paper supplies that the sensory-domain anchor (Carandini & Heeger) cannot:

1. **Same-domain evidence.** The normalization here is over *value/choice* signals, not sensory contrast. Importing "divide the primary score by the field" into REE's E3 value selector is therefore a short, same-domain transfer — the lowest transfer risk in the pull. The autopsy's Reading-2 is not borrowing a sensory trick; it is reusing the documented value-coding computation.

2. **The mechanism that makes a rank-preserving renormalization actually lift diversity.** This is the crux. Pure divisive normalization is order-preserving, so on its own it keeps the highest-value option highest — it would not, by itself, hand the commit to a non-top action. The diversity in Louie et al comes from **range-compression interacting with stochastic choice**: normalization shrinks the differences between option values, and once those differences are small relative to choice noise, the chooser commits to non-best options more often. That is the concrete bridge from "compress F's lead" to "committed-action diversity rises," and REE's Reading-2 lever inherits it.

## The load-bearing divergences (treated as findings, per biology-before-formal-definitions)

**Divergence 1 — the lift is stochastic-commit-conditional.** The behavioural diversity is produced by compressed value differences *interacting with a stochastic choice stage*; for fixed context the deterministic value ordering is preserved. So this evidence backs a *softmax-style* F-renormalization paired with a stochastic commit, and is **inert under a hard deterministic argmax**. REE's E3 must possess (or be given) a temperature/stochastic commit stage for the normalization lever to convert to committed-action diversity at all. This is the same precondition the Carandini & Heeger anchor surfaces, here confirmed in the value domain — which raises it from a theoretical caveat to a build requirement.

**Divergence 2 — pooled-symmetric normalization can corrupt the commit.** The normalization divides the target by the **summed value of all options**, symmetrically. That same pooling is what produces the "classically irrational" context effects: a high-value distractor lowers the *target's* normalized value and degrades the choice. So a faithful whole-field F-renormalization is double-edged — it can lift diversity *and* lower the effective value of the very candidate REE wants to commit, depending on which scores enter the normalization pool. REE's intended lever singles out F (asymmetric); the biology pools the field (symmetric). The honest consequence: MECH-448's rung-2 build must (a) decide the normalization-pool composition deliberately, because that choice decides whether authority transfers to the modulatory field or the commit just gets noisier, and (b) bound the accuracy cost that pooled normalization is documented to carry.

And, as with the anchor: REE's *stronger* stated lever — "F removed from the final argmin, used as an eligibility envelope only" — is rank-altering and is **not** what this paper demonstrates. Louie et al grounds the *weaker*, rank-preserving, whole-vector renormalization-with-stochastic-commit. The asymmetric/argmin-removing version still leans on the separately-filed quality-diversity justification, not on this entry.
