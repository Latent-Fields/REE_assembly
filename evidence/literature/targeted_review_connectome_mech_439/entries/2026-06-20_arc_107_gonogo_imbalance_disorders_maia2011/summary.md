# Maia & Frank 2011 — Go/No-Go imbalance as the disorder map for the MECH-449 psychiatric-failure-mode column

**Claim grounding:** ARC-107 (BG selector constitution), MECH-449 (Go/No-Go eligibility constitution), MECH-448 (F→eligibility demotion) · ARC-106 component 5 (Go/No-Go imbalance disorders) — the mandated psychiatric-failure-mode column
**Source:** Maia TV, Frank MJ. *Nature Neuroscience* 14(2):154–162 (2011). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2723) · PMID 21270784.
*According to PubMed.*

## What the paper does

This is the review that launched the modern computational-psychiatry programme. Maia and Frank take the reinforcement-learning models of dopamine and cortico-basal-ganglia-thalamo-cortical (CBGTC) circuits — the same Go/No-Go (D1/D2) machinery that the rest of this review grounds — and show that *disturbances* of specific model parameters reproduce the signatures of specific disorders. Parkinson's disease: dopamine depletion impairs **Go** learning and produces bradykinesia, while dopaminergic medication can over-tilt toward **Go** and produce impulse-control disorders. Tourette's and OCD: cortico-striatal Go/No-Go and inhibitory-control pathology. ADHD and addiction: Go/No-Go learning asymmetries and altered dopaminergic reinforcement. Schizophrenia: aberrant prediction-error signalling. The unifying move is that one machinery, mis-set in different ways, yields a *family* of disorders distinguished by **which parameter is off**.

## Why it is the right grounding for the psychiatric column

ARC-106 makes the psychiatric-failure-mode column a *requirement*, not an afterthought (framework §7): a component is only psychiatrically interpretable if its breakage maps to a recognisable disorder, and that interpretability is "earned, not assumed." Maia & Frank 2011 is the canonical source that supplies this column for the ARC-107 Go/No-Go constitution. It grounds, with a clinical literature behind each, the exact dyad the design note already names:

- **No-Go over-pressure / under-Go** → perseveration, bradykinesia, anhedonic action-collapse. This is the *current* REE failure read clinically: nothing but F converts; the committed repertoire narrows. In MECH-448's column this is "envelope too tight → bradykinesia/avolition." Maia & Frank source it to the Parkinsonian / hypodopaminergic pole.
- **Go over-pressure / under-No-Go** → impulsivity, premature commitment, the manic/hyperkinetic pole. In MECH-448's column this is "envelope too wide / F removed without bounded No-Go → disinhibition, impulsivity." Maia & Frank source it to the hyperdopaminergic / medication-induced-impulsivity pole.
- **OCD-spectrum loss of inhibitory braking** → the braking-loss failure mode ARC-107 names directly, grounded here as cortico-striatal Go/No-Go pathology.

This is what converts a flat REE result into a model with clinical content. As ARC-106 §7 puts it: ungrounded, "committed-action entropy stays flat" is just a failed experiment; grounded against this disorder map, it is a candidate computational model of psychomotor narrowing.

## The discipline this entry demands

The honest caveat is that this is a **model-of-a-model** step, and ARC-106 flags it explicitly: clinical interpretability is *earned*. Maia & Frank ground the clinical mapping (Go/No-Go imbalance ↔ disorder); they say nothing about REE. A REE selector failure earns a disorder label only if it reproduces the *specific signature* — perseveration *with intact value learning*, say, or impulsive commitment *specifically under conflict* — not merely a shared word. Two risks follow. First, the human/animal-disorder → REE-selector jump is a genuine domain transfer. Second, because the same machinery underlies several disorders, a single mis-set REE lever could superficially resemble more than one of them; the falsifier must *localise* which failure each lever actually produces rather than reach for whichever label is handy. ARC-106's own guardrail applies: do not force a disorder label where the analogy is weak — a row may honestly carry "no clean analog."
