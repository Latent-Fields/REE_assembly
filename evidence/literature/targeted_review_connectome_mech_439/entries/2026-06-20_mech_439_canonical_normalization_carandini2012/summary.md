# Carandini & Heeger (2012) — Normalization as a canonical neural computation

**Source:** Carandini, M., Heeger, D. J. (2012). *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 13(1):51-62. [DOI 10.1038/nrn3136](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn3136) · PMID 22108672. (According to PubMed / Nature.)

**Claim under test:** MECH-439 — specifically the cluster-autopsy **Reading-2** lever (a rank-preserving F→eligibility demotion / continuous renormalization of the primary score F against the competing field), the intended grounding for the residual-ceiling child **MECH-448**.

## What the paper does

This is the review that named divisive normalization a *canonical* computation. A neuron's response is modelled as its excitatory drive divided by a common factor that includes the summed activity of a **normalization pool** — schematically `R_j = D_j^n / (σ^n + Σ_k D_k^n)`. The review's central argument is empirical breadth: the same divisive operation, first developed to explain contrast responses in primary visual cortex, recurs in olfaction, in auditory cortex, in MT, in multisensory integration, in the modulatory effects of **attention**, and — the domain that matters here — in the **encoding of value**. Its presence from invertebrates to mammals is the basis for calling it canonical: a standard operation the brain reuses across problems rather than a region-specific trick.

Three structural properties of the canonical computation are load-bearing for REE and I state them explicitly because the whole entry turns on them: divisive normalization is (1) a **ratio** (divisive gain control, not subtraction), (2) **pooled and symmetric** — every unit is divided by the *same* denominator built from the whole field, and (3) **order-preserving and range-compressing** — it rescales the numerators monotonically, so it shrinks the gaps between options without changing which option is largest.

## Why this is the anchor the autopsy asked for

The cluster autopsy's Reading-2 says the F-monopoly is fundamentally a *selection-variance* problem: F owns ~88–89% of E3 committed-selection variance (V3-EXQ-571), and the principled fix is to **renormalize F against the field** rather than to widen a discrete shortlist at near-ties. Carandini & Heeger is precisely the paper that makes "renormalize the dominant channel against the field" a *documented canonical operation* rather than an ad hoc REE move. Two features of the canonical computation support the lever directly:

- **It is continuous and global.** The divisive ratio acts on every channel at every step, scaled by the pooled field. This is exactly why the autopsy prefers it over `k=f(F-gap)`: a top-k shortlist only does anything when the top-F gap is already small, whereas normalization compresses the F-vs-rest margin *everywhere*, including in the regime where F dominates outright.
- **It is order-preserving, so it is genuinely *rank-preserving*.** It does not delete or reorder candidates; it keeps F's information intact while stopping F from setting the scale. That is the property MECH-448's lever wants — demote F's *authority*, not its *eligibility*.

So far this reads as clean support. It is not, and the reason is the part the user asked to be treated as load-bearing rather than buried as a caveat.

## The load-bearing asymmetry (pooled-symmetric divisive norm vs single-score F-demotion)

Per the project's *biology-before-formal-definitions* discipline, the divergence between the canonical computation and REE's intended lever is the finding, not a footnote. There are two faces to it.

**Face 1 — order-preservation means normalization alone does not demote F.** Because the computation is order-preserving on the numerators, if F's drive exceeds every competitor's, F's *normalized* response still exceeds every competitor's. Canonical divisive normalization compresses F's lead; it does not strip F's rank. So applied faithfully to REE's E3 score vector, it would leave F the deterministic argmax and lift *nothing*. Where does the behavioural diversity in the value literature actually come from, then? From **range-compression under a downstream stochastic commit**: normalization shrinks the value differences so that, under a fixed decision noise or softmax temperature, the probability of committing a non-F-best action rises (this is the Louie/Glimcher relative-value-coding mechanism, filed as the sibling entry). The implication for REE is concrete and testable: the normalization lever only converts to committed diversity *if E3 has a downstream stochastic / temperature selection stage*. Under a hard deterministic argmax with no temperature, divisive normalization is inert. That precondition is a deliverable for the rung-2 build, surfaced by this anchor.

**Face 2 — pooled-symmetric vs single-score-targeted.** The canonical computation divides *every* option, F included, by the *same* field-built denominator. REE's proposed lever singles out F. These are not the same operation. A *faithful* divisive-norm lever must renormalize the whole score vector, which compresses F's lead **and** the modulatory channels' leads proportionally — so the modulatory field gains relative leverage only if the modulatory scores are *excluded from* or *down-weighted in* the normalization pool. In other words, *which scores enter the denominator* decides whether normalization actually transfers authority to the modulatory field or merely compresses everything uniformly. That is load-bearing design, not a tuning knob. And REE's *stronger* stated lever — "F removed from the final argmin, used as a graded eligibility envelope only" — is **rank-altering at the commit level** (a non-F criterion picks among F-eligible candidates). That is no longer the canonical computation; it cannot borrow this paper's biological canon, and it needs the separate quality-diversity / MAP-Elites justification already filed for Child 3.

The honest net: Carandini & Heeger legitimises a *rank-preserving, continuous, pooled* F-renormalization paired with a *stochastic commit* — a weaker, biologically-faithful reading of Reading-2 — and simultaneously warns that the asymmetric, argmin-removing version of the lever is a different and non-canonical build.

## How this should read against the 689a fork

689a tests the *near-ties shortlist*. This anchor speaks to the *other* arm of the fork (the renormalization lever) and sharpens what a rung-2 build must be. If 689a shows the shortlist fails or lifts only at near-ties, the autopsy routes to F-renormalization; Carandini & Heeger says the principled form of that renormalization is a *whole-vector pooled divisive normalization with a downstream stochastic commit*, not an asymmetric F handicap — and that the build must (a) confirm E3 has a temperature/stochastic commit stage and (b) decide the normalization-pool composition so the modulatory field actually gains leverage. Those two design constraints are the concrete carry-forward into MECH-448's rung-2 experiment.

## Confidence

**Mixed, confidence 0.62.** Source quality is very high — the canonical, defining review of divisive normalization across species and modalities, explicitly listing value coding among its domains. Mapping fidelity is moderate: the divisive-ratio / pooled-denominator framework maps tightly onto "renormalize F against the field" and the continuous-global property directly grounds the autopsy's preference for renormalization over a near-ties shortlist — but the order-preserving and pooled-symmetric properties mean the canonical computation supports only a *weaker* (stochastic-commit-conditional, whole-vector) version of the lever, while REE's asymmetric "F out of the argmin" exceeds it. Transfer risk is moderate: the canonical-computation claim transfers in principle and the review itself lists value coding, but the conversion to committed diversity is conditional on a downstream stochastic commit stage that REE's E3 selector must be shown to possess.
