# Mink 1996 — focused selection and surround inhibition: the BG select by releasing inhibition, not by argmax

**Claim grounding:** ARC-107 (BG selector constitution), MECH-449 (Go/No-Go eligibility constitution), MECH-448 (F→eligibility demotion) · ARC-106 components 1 (Go/No-Go) and 3 (pallidal permission gate)
**Source:** Mink JW. *Progress in Neurobiology* 50(4):381–425 (1996). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0301-0082(96)00042-1) · PMID 9004351.
*According to PubMed.*

## What the paper argues

Mink's review is the canonical statement of the "focused selection" model of basal-ganglia function. Its central, deliberately counterintuitive claim is that **the basal ganglia do not generate movement**. Movement is generated by cortical and cerebellar mechanisms; what the basal ganglia contribute is *selection*. They do this with a two-sided operation: a broad **surround inhibition** that clamps down on competing motor programs which would otherwise interfere with the intended one, and a **focal release** of inhibition from the desired program so that it — and only it — can proceed. The clinical half of the argument is that when this fails, you do not get "no movement" but the wrong movements: inability to inhibit competitors yields slowing, abnormal postures, and involuntary muscle activity (the parkinsonian/dystonic/hyperkinetic spectrum).

## Why it matters for the ARC-107 constitution

The design note (§1) reframes REE's conversion ceiling as a *selection-structure* fault: a signal forms, becomes measurable, reaches E3 authority — and committed action still doesn't change, because the selector is `candidates → F-score dominance → committed argmin`. Mink names precisely the architecture that is missing. The selected action in his model is **the one whose inhibition is released**, not the one with the highest input value. That is the argmin-vs-permission distinction at the heart of ARC-107: a pallidal-like *permission-to-commit gate* rather than a bare scalar comparison. The surround inhibition is the biological original of MECH-449's **bounded No-Go pressure** (suppress competing/irrelevant/unsafe channels); the focal release is the original of the **pallidal permission gate** over an eligible set, within which MECH-448 lets a non-F channel arbitrate. So Mink grounds two of the constitution's components at once, and grounds the framing sentence of the whole proposal: *strength is necessary but not sufficient — an action also needs lawful access*, and access here is literally the release of inhibition.

## The failure modes it predicts — in both directions

This entry is unusually useful for the **psychiatric-failure-mode column** because Mink's model fails in two opposite directions, and ARC-107 can inherit both. If the focal-release signal is itself driven by the dominant scalar F, then the "center" is always F's argmax and the surround always suppresses the non-F channels — the diversity ceiling simply reappears one layer down, in the gating step. That is the residual-ceiling risk MECH-448 targets, relocated to the disinhibition stage, and it is the design note's warning that a Go/No-Go layer keyed to F changes nothing. In the other direction, because the model is fundamentally about *preventing unwanted movement*, a naive import risks **over-suppression** — too much No-Go, the surround swallowing the center — which is the bradykinesia / repertoire-narrowing / over-braking failure (the same signature Mink reads off basal-ganglia degeneration). MECH-448's psychiatric column already names exactly this dyad: envelope too wide → disinhibition/impulsivity; envelope too tight → bradykinesia/avolition. Mink is the grounding for that both-directions reading.

## Limitations

Two honest discounts. First, this is a **conceptual review**, not a single decisive experiment — its authority comes from integrating physiology, lesion data, and anatomy, but it is an argument, so I cap it below the causal optogenetic and electrophysiological entries. Second, the strict **center-surround anatomy** has been partially contested in the decades since, even where the focused-selection *function* has held up. I therefore import the function (focal-go plus surround-no-go, selection-by-release) and explicitly not the literal wiring — consistent with ARC-106's function-not-homology guardrail (G1). The currency mismatch is also real: Mink's units are motor programs with literal tonic GABAergic inhibition, whereas REE's are abstract committed-action classes with no tonic-inhibition substrate, so "release inhibition" becomes "admit to the eligible set / grant commit permission," a functional translation.

## Confidence

**0.70 (supports).** Strong, canonical grounding for the selection *structure* ARC-107 imports — selection by release, bounded surround suppression, permission rather than argmax — discounted for being review-level rather than a single experiment and for the functional (not mechanical) translation of tonic inhibition into eligibility/permission.
