# Kravitz et al. 2010 -- optogenetic control of parkinsonian motor behaviour

**What they did.** Using D1-Cre and D2-Cre mice with channelrhodopsin in dorsomedial striatum, Kravitz and colleagues drove each striatal output population on its own. Exciting indirect-pathway (D2) neurons produced a parkinsonian animal: more freezing, bradykinesia, fewer locomotor initiations. Exciting direct-pathway (D1) neurons did the opposite. In 6-OHDA dopamine-depleted mice, direct-pathway activation fully rescued freezing, bradykinesia and initiation deficits.

**Why it matters for ARC-109.** The classical model had never been causally tested. This paper does test it, and finds that the two populations are dissociable and push behaviour in opposite directions. For ARC-109 that is the population-level precondition for the claim's second leg. If Go and No-Go really are two populations, a lesion biased toward the No-Go side should yield an akinesia-like signature: fewer initiations, longer commitment runs, perseveration. A matched reduction of a single additive scalar should not produce that signature. Kravitz shows the biology has exactly this kind of dissociable, directional phenotype to aim at.

**Where the mapping strains.** Two gaps. First, optogenetic drive of a population is not the same as a change in dopamine gain on it, and ARC-109's lesion is a d2_da_gain sweep. Treating the phenotype as a prediction for that knob assumes the two produce the same effective shift in No-Go output. Plausible, not shown. Second, the readout is gross motor behaviour, not selection among competing candidates, so the paper says nothing about leg (i), conflict versus indifference. There's also a practical point the audit already made: REE has no registered disorder-analog instrument for leg (ii) yet. The biology gives us a phenotype, and we have no probe to see it with.

**Confidence.** 0.68. Strong causal methods and clean dissociation, moderated by manipulation and readout mismatch. Of these entries, this is the best reason to build the leg (ii) instrument (initiation rate, commit-run length, perseveration) before running the lesion sweep.
