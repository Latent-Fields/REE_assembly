# Collins & Frank 2014 -- Opponent Actor Learning (OpAL)

**What they did.** Collins and Frank took the standard actor-critic architecture and replaced its single actor with two opponent actors, G and N, standing in for the direct (D1) and indirect (D2) striatal populations. Each learns its own action weights from the same critic prediction error, with opposite nonlinearity: G comes to specialise in discriminating good options, N in discriminating bad ones. Dopamine enters twice. It shapes the learning, and separately it sets how strongly each actor's weights count at the moment of choice (beta_G versus beta_N). They then showed that this single framework reproduces dopamine's effects on probabilistic reinforcement learning, effort-based choice and motor skill learning, and, importantly, the *interaction* between the learning and incentive effects, which models handling one effect at a time do not.

**Why it matters for ARC-109.** ARC-109 claims that REE's current Go/No-Go rendering, one additive scoring sign under MECH-449, throws away information, and that Go and No-Go should be two populations with opposite dopamine sensitivity at selection time. OpAL is as close to that claim as the literature gets. The choice rule weighs G and N separately, and dopamine tilts the weighting. So an option carrying strong G and strong N (a genuine approach-avoidance conflict) behaves differently from one carrying weak G and weak N (indifference) as soon as dopamine moves off its balance point. That is the conflict-versus-indifference distinction in ARC-109's leg (i).

**Caveats I have to own.** OpAL wins a model comparison. It does not run the ablation ARC-109's own falsifier requires, an additive-scalar control that cannot reproduce the split's choices. And there is a convergence here that should give us pause rather than comfort: at balanced dopamine, OpAL's opponent contribution collapses toward a difference score. That is exactly REE's own finding that the split is structurally nil at da == 0 or zero straddle. The literature and our substrate agree that the split only earns its keep when dopamine is actually moving. So the claim's value depends on REE carrying a live tonic-DA proxy, which is an empirical question about our organism, not about the brain.

**Confidence.** 0.74. High mapping fidelity (the split *is* OpAL's architecture), a strong venue, moderate transfer risk. It supports the claim that the split is a principled, dopamine-dependent representation. It does not yet support the claim that REE's behaviour needs it.
