# Cui et al. 2013 -- concurrent activation of direct and indirect pathways

**What they did.** Cui and colleagues used Cre-dependent GCaMP3 and fibre photometry to record direct-pathway (D1) and indirect-pathway (A2A/D2) striatal neurons separately in freely moving mice doing an operant task. The classical model predicts reciprocal activity, Go up and NoGo down when the animal moves. Instead, both populations rose transiently together when the animal initiated an action, and stayed quiet when it was inactive. Co-activation in one hemisphere preceded, and predicted, contraversive movement within about half a second.

**Why it cuts both ways for ARC-109.** On one side it helps the claim. If Go and No-Go are often high *together*, their joint state carries information, and summing them into one signed score (REE's current MECH-449 rendering) throws that away. That is precisely ARC-109's representational-loss argument: conflict (both high) must not look like indifference (both low). On the other side, it complicates the claim's wording. ARC-109 describes the split in strongly opponent terms, and this paper is one of the main reasons the field stopped believing in simple push-pull. A more current reading is that the direct pathway selects the wanted action while the indirect pathway suppresses competitors, a centre-surround picture. That is still two populations, but a different computation than "opposite sign on the same candidate."

**Caveats.** Photometry averages over many cells, so the paper can't say whether co-active D1 and D2 neurons code the same action or rival ones. Later cellular-resolution imaging has refined the picture, and I haven't pulled it here. I'm recording this as `mixed` rather than stretching it into support.

**Confidence.** 0.55. The practical upshot for REE: when the leg (i) conflict-versus-indifference test is designed, "No-Go" should be allowed to act on competitors rather than only on the same candidate. Otherwise we may be testing a model of the basal ganglia the biology has already moved past.
