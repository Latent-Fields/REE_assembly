# Koechlin, Ody & Kouneiher 2003 -- The architecture of cognitive control in the human prefrontal cortex

This is the empirical paper standing behind the "3-4 levels" figure that MECH-321's title attributes to Koechlin & Summerfield 2007. It is worth going back to it directly, because the 2007 paper is a review and the number originates here. Using event-related fMRI with a parametric manipulation of how much stimulus, contextual, and episodic information a response depends on, Koechlin et al. resolve a cascade of three nested control levels along the lateral prefrontal axis: premotor cortex selecting responses given a stimulus, caudal LPFC selecting which stimulus-response mapping applies given the present perceptual context, and rostral LPFC selecting according to the temporal episode in which the stimulus occurs. The 2007 successor adds branching control at frontopolar cortex, which is where the fourth level comes from.

So the magnitude is real, and it is not a soft or hand-waved number -- it is three cleanly dissociated fMRI loci, extended to four by a later, separately-motivated result. If the question is "does biological hierarchical action control terminate at a small integer number of levels?", this paper says yes, and gives the reason: the cascade runs out of cortex. There is no region rostral to frontopolar cortex to host a fifth level. That is a genuinely interesting constraint and a defensible prior for setting a decomposition depth cap in the low single digits.

The difficulty is that MECH-321's `depth_cap` does not control the same quantity. Koechlin's levels are qualitatively heterogeneous -- each one processes a different kind of context-conditional information, and they are anatomically fixed. MECH-321's depth counts how many times a single tiling operator recursively re-applies to its own output on the rollout stream. Those are different constructs that happen to share a number. An architecture could perfectly well instantiate Koechlin's four-level cascade while allowing its decomposition operator to recurse twice, or ten times, at any one of those levels. The inference from one to the other is an argument by analogy of magnitude, not a measurement.

There is a second gap that the existing ARC-070 entries for this cluster already flag and which applies here unchanged: the cascade is measured during overt, externally cued task performance. MECH-321 acts on the imagined trajectory. Nothing in an anatomical terminus constrains how many times an agent may re-segment a simulated plan, and the constructive-episodic-simulation transfer that licenses the rest of the ARC-070 anchor cluster does not obviously carry a level count with it.

I have marked this `mixed` rather than `supports` precisely because it does both jobs at once: it supplies the only real empirical referent the 3-4 figure has, and it shows that the referent is at the wrong grain. Confidence 0.55, weighted toward mapping fidelity because this is an architectural-parameter question rather than an effect-size question -- the paper is excellent and the inference from it is the weak link. The practical recommendation is small and worth making: MECH-321's own `implementation_note` is already more honest than its title, recording the cap as mirroring ARC-071's `chunk_max_depth` for operator symmetry and registering an open Q-claim on whether the cap should be hard or soft. That framing is the correct one, and the title should be brought into line with it rather than the other way round.
