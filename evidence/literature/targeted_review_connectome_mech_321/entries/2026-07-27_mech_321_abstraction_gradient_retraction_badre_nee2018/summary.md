# Badre & Nee 2018 -- Frontal Cortex and the Hierarchical Control of Behavior

MECH-321's title attributes its depth cap of 3-4 levels to two sources, and this is David Badre's own re-appraisal of the first of them nine years on. The conclusion is blunt: "Our review of the evidence supports neither a unitary model of lateral frontal function nor a unidimensional abstraction gradient." What replaces it is separate frontal networks interacting via local and global hierarchical structure -- hierarchy survives, but the single ordered axis does not.

That matters for the parameter question in a specific way. A "depth cap of N levels" is only well-defined if there is a thing to count levels along. Badre & D'Esposito 2009 was cited for exactly that: a rostro-caudal gradient scaled by abstractness, with an ordered series of levels running from response through feature, dimension, and context to episode. If the gradient is not unidimensional, the series is not a series, and there is no number of positions along it to cap at 3-4. The citation is grounding a numeric parameter on a construct that its own source now disputes.

It is worth being precise about what this does and does not overturn. ARC-070's R3 verdict -- that decomposition is multi-level rather than single-level -- is untouched, and the existing ARC-070 entry for Badre & D'Esposito 2009 reads that paper correctly on exactly that point. Hierarchical structure in frontal cortex is not in question here. What is in question is whether the 2009 review ever supported a level COUNT, and reading the existing ARC-070 entry closely, it did not even claim to: that entry's own `ree_translation` says "practical implementation may bound the recursion depth at 2-3 levels for tractability, but the architectural commitment is recursive in principle". The tractability bound was the entry author's suggestion, correctly labelled. Somewhere between that entry and MECH-321's title, a tractability suggestion acquired a citation.

The honest reading is therefore that MECH-321's cap has no literature grounding from this quarter and never did, and that this is a mild governance failure of attribution rather than a scientific error. The cap of 3 may well be right. Nothing here says it is wrong; a review arguing against a strong form of the gradient claim does not establish that a different number is correct, and the paper concerns overt cognitive control, inheriting the same rollout-versus-execution transfer risk as everything else in this anchor cluster. But it must be argued for on tractability and symmetry grounds -- which is what MECH-321's `implementation_note` already does -- rather than by citation.

Marked `weakens` at confidence 0.72. Not higher, because withdrawing a justification is a weaker act than falsifying a parameter, and this is a narrative review rather than a decisive experiment. Not lower, because self-correction by the original author on the exact construct at issue is about as directly relevant as a source can be. There is a downstream implication worth flagging for the substrate: if frontal control is several interacting networks at different scales rather than one recursive axis, then "decomposition depth" may not be the right control parameter at all, and the biologically-faithful analog would be a small set of decomposition operators at distinct scales rather than one operator recursing N times. That is a design question rather than a parameter question, and it is bigger than this entry can settle.
