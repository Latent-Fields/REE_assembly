# Bays & Husain 2008 -- Flexible resource model: the productive counterweight to fixed slots

**Claims:** SD-046, SD-027, SD-028, MECH-254
**Direction:** mixed (supports the precision-gate premise, challenges discrete slots)
**Confidence:** 0.58

## What the paper did

Bays & Husain challenged the dominant "fixed number of items" account of visual working memory. Using a feature-recall precision paradigm, they showed that visual WM capacity is **not** a fixed slot count but a **limited resource shared dynamically across all items** in a scene. The resource shifts flexibly between objects, biased by selective attention and by the targets of upcoming eye movements, and the *proportion* of resource allocated to each item determines the **precision** with which it is remembered -- a relation governed by a simple power law. More items means each is remembered less precisely; there is no hard cliff at four.

## Findings relevant to the claims

This entry is deliberately in tension with the Cowan entry, and that tension is the point -- the node should carry the genuine, unresolved slot-vs-resource debate rather than pretend the capacity literature is settled.

- **SD-027 / SD-028 / MECH-254 (capacity-limited precision-weighted selection + attentional template).** Here Bays & Husain are straight support, and strong. MECH-254's selection weight is literally `precision_i * (...)`, and SD-028 asserts the attentional template is a *precision-space* object. Bays & Husain show precision *is* the currency of capacity-limited selection in biological vision, that it is a shared resource allocated by attention, and that allocation is biased toward the acted-on / saccade target. That is the access-gate premise, measured.
- **SD-046 (multi-slot GoalState).** Here the direction flips to a challenge. If SD-046 commits to N discrete equal-capacity slots, Bays & Husain predict that is the wrong idealisation: adding a third or fourth active goal should not grant it a full slot -- it should *dilute the precision of all goals*, with the marginal goal represented poorly. The biological prediction is graceful degradation, not a hard cutoff, and the acted-on goal should draw disproportionate precision. A fixed-slot SD-046 would mis-predict this.

The combined Cowan + Bays&Husain reading is the most useful thing this layer produces: the capacity bound is real and small (Cowan), but whether it is *discrete slots* or a *shared precision resource* is unsettled in the human literature itself (hybrid "slots + averaging" models exist). SD-046's discrete-slot framing is therefore a design *choice* that the biology problematises -- which is exactly the kind of fork the V4 roadmap exists to surface.

## Limitations and caveats

Measured on visual WM for simple features (orientation, colour), not on goals or trajectories -- so the bearing on SD-046 is by analogy, and the transfer risk is high (0.42). The slot-vs-resource question is genuinely open in the source literature; this paper is the canonical resource-model statement but it did not end the debate. Its honest contribution is to ground the precision/resource framing of the *access gate* strongly while *destabilising* the discrete-slot framing of the *goal state* -- hence the mixed evidence_direction rather than a clean supports.
