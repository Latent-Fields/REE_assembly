# Reimer, McGinley, Liu, Rodenkirch, Wang, McCormick & Tolias (2016) -- Pupil tracks both noradrenergic and cholinergic activity

**What they did.** In awake head-fixed mice they imaged the activity of noradrenergic and cholinergic axons in cortex with two-photon calcium imaging, while recording pupil size, during quiet waking and during locomotion.

**What they found.** Rapid pupil changes were highly correlated with activity in both transmitter systems, and the two split by timescale. Fast dilations were tightly tied to phasic activity in noradrenergic axons. Longer-lasting dilations, such as those during locomotion, came with sustained activity in cholinergic axons.

**Why it is here.** Most of the human evidence for MECH-084, including two papers in this pull, uses pupil as a stand-in for NA. This paper decides how far that stand-in can be trusted, and the answer is double-edged, so I have recorded the direction as mixed. On the supporting side, fast pupil events do track phasic NA, which is the channel MECH-084 says produces the snap. The phasic-NA / sustained-ACh split also lines up neatly with the claim's own division of labour, where NA orients now and ACh determines whether a lasting trace is laid down (MECH-083). On the weakening side, pupil is not an NA-specific measure. Any pupil-based effect that has not been resolved by timescale could be cholinergic, and pupil alone can never supply MECH-084's matched-arousal control.

**Limits.** This is a readout paper in mice. It says nothing about sampling allocation, orienting or top-down prediction, and the coupling between human pupil and LC is looser than the axon-level coupling measured here.

**Confidence.** 0.5, mixed. What it mainly contributes is a discount factor on the pupil entries, together with a physiological correlate for the NA/ACh timescale split.
