# Vazey, Moorman & Aston-Jones (2018) -- Phasic LC activity regulates cortical encoding of salience

**What they did.** They used Cre-dependent optogenetics to drive LC-NE neurons in rats in two patterns, phasic bursts and tonic firing, and recorded single units and field potentials in cortical targets. They then paired weak, non-salient sensory stimuli with phasic LC activation and compared the responses to those evoked by salient stimuli, which trigger phasic LC firing on their own.

**What they found.** Phasic activation produced a distinct cortical event-related potential. Tonic activation did not. Salient stimuli drove strong excitatory cortical responses within that ERP window. Weak stimuli produced small responses, but when a weak stimulus was time-locked to a phasic LC pulse, its response rose to the salient-stimulus level. The mechanism was a facilitation of long-latency signals in the target regions.

**Mapping to MECH-084.** This is the paper in the pull that comes closest to the manipulation the falsifier actually asks for: a phasic NA pulse against a tonic pattern, in the same animal, with a cue-locked DV. The phasic pulse transiently increases the weight of the incoming bottom-up signal. Tonic drive does not reproduce the effect. That is the "snap" component of the claim, and it is evidence that temporal profile matters and not only mean level.

**What it does not show.** There is no top-down prediction in the paradigm for the pulse to override, so it cannot tell "NA shifts weight from E2 constraint toward E1 input" apart from "NA turns up gain on whatever is arriving." MECH-084 is the first of these. There is also no orienting behaviour or reorientation latency, and the phasic/tonic comparison differs in stimulation pattern without being matched for total NE released. The recording conditions and species add transfer risk.

**Confidence.** 0.6. Good causal evidence for phasic specificity. Only indirect evidence for the allocation-ratio framing.
