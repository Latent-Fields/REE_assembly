# Communication through Coherence (Fries 2015) — coherence as a binding/routing substrate

**Paper.** Pascal Fries, *Rhythms for Cognition: Communication through Coherence*, Neuron 88(1):220–235, 2015. The mature statement of the CTC hypothesis Fries first proposed in 2005. The argument is mechanistically simple and empirically far-reaching: a rhythmically active neuronal group is not uniformly receptive — its rhythm carves out recurring windows of high and low excitability. Two groups can exchange information effectively only when the sending group's output arrives during the receiving group's open window — that is, only when the two are *coherent*. Coherence is therefore not an epiphenomenon of activity; it is a dynamically reconfigurable switchboard that selects which populations are, at any moment, in communication. Attention, selective routing, and (in the binding literature this directory tracks) the dynamic grouping of features into objects all fall out of the same principle.

**Why it sits in this directory.** REE's object-representation thread keeps bumping into one question: is there a *separable* coherence factor that does work the integrated prediction error E cannot already do? The V3-EXQ-641a coherence-ablation autopsy named CTC explicitly as the "closest mechanism" to what its coherence term was reaching for, alongside REE's own MECH-089 (theta–gamma nesting packaging E1 for E3) and MECH-270 (ephaptic coupling). So this paper is the biological anchor for the *class* of mechanism REE is gesturing at — and ARC-006's claim that entities are *bindable* structures needs exactly this kind of "how does binding happen dynamically" grounding, which it did not previously have.

**What it supports, and what it pointedly does not.** Read carefully, Fries cuts both ways for REE, and that is the honest value of including it. It supports the class strongly: coherence-gated communication and binding is real, central, and well-replicated in cortex. But CTC is precise about a precondition — coherence does work *only when the underlying groups carry genuinely co-varying information and share an excitability rhythm*. That precondition is exactly what 641a found missing: under a fair contrast-matched control, REE's cross-stream coherence term was functionally redundant with E, because `world_states`↔`states` carry the *symbol* of coherence without the bound *content*. CTC predicts that result. A coherence read laid over streams that are not genuinely bound will register coherence that gates nothing — decorative, not causal. So the paper grounds the target architecture (a bound-representation substrate over which coherence could legitimately gate selection) while simultaneously explaining why the current V3 substrate cannot yet express it.

**Mapping caveat (transfer risk).** CTC is inter-areal cortical communication measured in spike–LFP and LFP–LFP coherence across gamma and beta bands in macaque and human. REE's coherence read is over engineered latent streams inside a selection rule — there is no spiking population, no shared excitability rhythm, no inter-areal axonal delay structure. The transfer is to the *principle* (coherence selects and binds what is jointly read), not to the implementation. I have therefore tagged this to MECH-044 (relational binding/comparison) and ARC-006 (bindable structures) at class level, and held confidence to 0.72: source quality is near-ceiling, but mapping fidelity is deliberately moderate and transfer risk is elevated by precisely the gap 641a made concrete. This is grounding for *where the substrate should go*, not evidence that it has arrived.
