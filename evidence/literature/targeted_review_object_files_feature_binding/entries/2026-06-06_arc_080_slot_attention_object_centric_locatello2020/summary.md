# Slot Attention (Locatello et al. 2020) — the ML existence proof for a token-keyed object-file

**Paper.** Francesco Locatello et al., *Object-Centric Learning with Slot Attention*, NeurIPS 2020 (arXiv:2006.15055). The contribution is a small architectural module that sits between perceptual features (e.g. a CNN's output) and a *set* of representational vectors the authors call slots. The slots start as random samples from a shared distribution and are refined over a few rounds of attention — but with a crucial twist: the attention is normalised *over slots* rather than over input locations, which forces slots to *compete* for the input. The competition makes each slot specialise to one object. The slots are exchangeable (permutation-symmetric) and generalise to scenes with more objects than were seen in training. On unsupervised object discovery (CLEVR, Tetrominoes, multi-dSprites) it matched or beat the prior state of the art while being faster and lighter.

**Why it belongs in this directory, and why now.** The whole object-representation thread turns on one design fork the memo names explicitly: is an "object" a *type* (a category tag, which is all REE's live SD-015→049→057 lineage currently has), a *spatial anchor* (the ghost-goal bank), or a *token instance* (this object, tracked as an individual)? ARC-080 commits to token-bound identity. The obvious question a skeptic asks is: is a token-instance object representation even learnable, or is it a philosopher's wish? Slot Attention answers that cleanly — yes, end-to-end, unsupervised, and cheaply. It is the natural computational realization of the "generalise z_object from a type-tag store to a token-keyed object-file" step the memo defers to V4/late-V3. So this entry grounds the *buildability* of ARC-080's core primitive, where the cognitive-psychology entries (Kahneman, Treisman, Xu&Carey) grounded its *reality* in minds.

**What it grounds, and the two seams.** It supports the identity/binding half of ARC-080 and bears on MECH-045 (object-file buffers) — slots *are* object-file-like buffers, learned rather than hand-designed. But two seams keep confidence at 0.7. First, vanilla Slot Attention is *single-frame*: it groups features into objects within an image but has no machinery for tracking a token *through occlusion over time*. Permanence — ARC-080 Pillar 1 — needs a recurrent extension (the SAVi line of follow-on work), so this paper grounds binding/identity but not persistence. Second, and more REE-specific: slots specialise because they compete to *reconstruct the perceptual input*. REE's `z_object` is deliberately detached from reconstruction and coupled instead to SD-049/SD-057 drive and goal value. The binding pressure that makes slot attention work has no current REE analog — so importing slot attention would mean either supplying a reconstruction/contrastive objective over REE's latent streams or finding a value-driven substitute for the competitive pressure. That is a real open design question, not a detail.

**Mapping caveat (transfer risk).** This is image/CNN unsupervised object discovery; REE works over engineered latent streams with a value-coupled, reconstruction-free object code. The transfer is to the *principle and buildability* (token-instance slots are learnable; competition is a viable binding mechanism distinct from temporal synchrony), not to a drop-in module. I have tagged it to ARC-080 and MECH-045 and held confidence to 0.7: a strong, reproduced existence proof (high source quality), with transfer risk raised by the reconstruction-vs-value mismatch and the single-frame-vs-permanence gap. Useful note for the eventual substrate decision: REE now has *two* candidate binding mechanisms in the literature record — coherence/synchrony (Fries, Singer&Gray) and competitive slot attention (this) — and they are not the same bet.
