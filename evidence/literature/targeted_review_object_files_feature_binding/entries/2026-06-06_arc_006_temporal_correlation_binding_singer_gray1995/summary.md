# The temporal correlation hypothesis (Singer & Gray 1995) — binding by synchrony, and its discontents

**Paper.** Wolf Singer & Charles M. Gray, *Visual feature integration and the temporal correlation hypothesis*, Annual Review of Neuroscience 18:555–586, 1995. This is the canonical synthesis of the binding-by-synchrony idea. Its starting point is the binding problem in its sharpest form: a visual scene activates feature detectors scattered across cortex, and the brain must somehow mark *which* features belong to *which* object without a dedicated grandmother cell for every possible conjunction. Singer and Gray's answer is temporal: neurons coding features of the same object fire in synchrony (prominently in the gamma band), and that synchrony — the *relative timing* of firing, not just the identity of the active cells — is the relational tag that says "these belong together." It is a flexible, combinatorially cheap grouping code, computed on the fly.

**Why it belongs here.** This is the historical taproot of the entire binding-by-coherence family the Fries 2015 CTC entry generalises, and of REE's own MECH-089 (theta–gamma nesting) and MECH-270 (ephaptic coupling). For ARC-006 — "entities are sparse, persistent, *bindable* structures" — it supplies the missing mechanistic content: a concrete proposal for what *bindable* could mean physically. Before this pull, ARC-006's binding claim rested on cognitive-psychology object-file work (Kahneman/Treisman) and had no neural account of the binding operation itself. Singer & Gray is that account.

**Why the direction is "mixed," not "supports."** Intellectual honesty requires flagging that this hypothesis, for all its influence, has never settled. The most serious challenge (Shadlen & Movshon 1999, and a long subsequent literature) is that observed synchrony may be a *by-product* of recurrent cortical connectivity rather than the binding *code* — and that, crucially, a synchrony code does nothing unless some downstream reader is actually built to exploit coincidence. Absent such a reader, the correlation is epiphenomenal. That critique should sound familiar: it is precisely the failure mode the V3-EXQ-641a autopsy found in REE, where a coherence term over `world_states`↔`states` turned out functionally redundant with prediction error under a fair control — the *symbol* of binding without a reader that the binding changes. So this paper is double-edged in the same way Fries is: it establishes that relational/temporal binding is a real and central candidate mechanism (good for ARC-006's existence), while its own decades of controversy warn against asserting that a coherence factor is *non-reducible* just because it can be measured.

**Mapping caveat (transfer risk).** The evidence base is cat and monkey visual cortex spiking; REE has no spiking populations and no shared excitability rhythm, and its `z_world` is engineered pre-split, so its features need not co-vary in time at all. The pull grounds the *existence and plausibility* of a temporal/relational binding mechanism for ARC-006 and MECH-044 — not the claim that REE's current substrate realizes it. Confidence is held to 0.66: a field-defining Annual Review (high source quality) whose mapping fidelity is genuinely capped by the unresolved question of whether synchrony binds or merely accompanies binding.
