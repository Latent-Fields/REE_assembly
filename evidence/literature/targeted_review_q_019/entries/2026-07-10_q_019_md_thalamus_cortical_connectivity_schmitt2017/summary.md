# Mediodorsal thalamus sustains PFC rule representations (Schmitt, Halassa et al. 2017)

**Claim tested:** Q-019 -- three-gate vs single-gate BG architecture (substrate leg)
**Direction:** supports (three-gate; thought-loop substrate) -- confidence 0.68

## What the paper did

Schmitt and colleagues asked what the thalamus *contributes* to thalamocortical interaction beyond relaying signals. In mice performing a cross-modal attention task that requires holding a rule ("attend to sound" vs "attend to light"), they recorded prefrontal cortex (PFC) and found rule-specific neural sequences. Optogenetically manipulating the **mediodorsal (MD) thalamus** and PFC dissected the causal roles: MD input *amplified local PFC connectivity*, letting rule-specific sequences emerge and persist, but MD did **not** carry the categorical rule information itself. Enhancing MD excitability sharpened rule specificity and improved behaviour; broadly enhancing PFC excitability *degraded* both. The authors frame this as a principle new to the field -- thalamic control of *functional cortical connectivity*, dissociable from information relay.

## Why it matters for Q-019

Q-019's thought loop (loop 2) is explicitly located in the associative/theta-associated cortico-BG-thalamic circuit, with the mediodorsal thalamus named as the structure that keeps thought from "bleeding back" into sensorium and where the E3 complex operates on affectively-weighted trajectories. The claim needs the MD thalamus to be a genuine *gate on thought-level representations*, not a passive relay -- otherwise the thought loop is not a separable gating locus and the architecture collapses toward single-gate. Schmitt et al. supply exactly the missing substrate evidence: MD actively *sustains and stabilises* the currently held rule/representation by tuning cortical connectivity. A maintained, protected thought representation is what a thought-loop gate is for. This is the strongest available causal grounding for the thalamic side of the three-gate architecture, complementing the sensorium-loop TRN evidence already in this review.

## Mapping and its limits

The honest gap: Q-019 attributes to MD a *specific* function -- suppressing bleed-back of the free-running thought loop into sensorium -- whereas Schmitt demonstrates a *maintenance/amplification* role. These are compatible (sustaining a rule representation is functionally adjacent to protecting it from sensory intrusion), but the paper does not test the suppression claim directly, so the fidelity is partial. The task is a two-rule attentional selection in head-fixed mice; mapping that onto the REE E3 associative loop with its hippocampal trajectory map is a loose, structural analogy rather than a tight behavioural correspondence. Standard mouse-to-substrate transfer risk applies.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality is very high (0.92): Nature, bidirectional optogenetic causation, prefrontal electrophysiology, and a within-study dissociation of "control of connectivity" from "relay." Mapping fidelity is moderate (0.62): it firmly grounds MD-as-thought-loop-gate at the substrate level but evidences the maintenance function rather than the exact bleed-back-suppression Q-019 names. Transfer risk is moderate-to-high (0.4). Net 0.68 -- a strong, load-bearing addition to the thought-loop leg, with the caveat that it establishes the *existence* of a dedicated MD gating function, not the precise gating semantics the claim specifies.
