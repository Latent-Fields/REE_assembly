# A third dissociable striatal territory: ventrolateral neostriatum (Lelos, Harrison & Dunnett 2011)

**Claim tested:** Q-019 -- three-gate vs single-gate BG architecture (substrate leg)
**Direction:** supports (three-gate; functional parcellation) -- confidence 0.60

## What the paper did

By 2011 the striatum was already known to be functionally split into a dorsomedial territory (DMS, necessary for goal-directed action) and a dorsolateral territory (DLS, necessary for habit) -- the Yin/Balleine double dissociation. Lelos, Harrison and Dunnett asked whether a *third* subregion, the ventrolateral neostriatum (VLS), carried yet another distinct function. Using excitotoxic VLS lesions in rats, they ran three tests: instrumental goal-directed action (sensory-specific devaluation), Pavlovian approach with devaluation, and the development of habitual responding. VLS-lesioned rats formed and used instrumental action-outcome associations normally, and developed devaluation-insensitive habits normally -- but they were selectively impaired at using a *Pavlovian stimulus-outcome association* to adjust responding to the current incentive value of the reward. The VLS is therefore a third striatal territory with its own dissociable lesion signature.

## Why it matters for Q-019

Q-019 asks whether commit-gating needs *several separable gating loops* or a *single gate scoring several criteria*. The decisive empirical shape is dissociability: three-gate predicts that removing one gate degrades only its axis. The DMS/DLS pair already shows two dissociable territories; this paper extends the picture to *three*, showing the striatum is a set of non-redundant, functionally parcellated territories rather than one convergent selector. That parcellation principle -- distinct gating loci, each selectively lesion-able -- is the structural fact the three-gate reading rests on, and it directly weakens the single-gate alternative (which would predict graded global deficits from any subregional lesion, not clean selective ones).

## Mapping and its limits

This is where honesty is required. The three striatal territories Lelos et al. distinguish -- goal-directed (DMS) / habit (DLS) / Pavlovian-incentive (VLS) -- are **not** the three loops Q-019 posits (sensorium-attention / thought-trajectory / motor-commitment). The paper supports *three-gate-in-principle* (the striatum gates via multiple dissociable territories) but does not provide a territory-for-territory match to the REE loops. So its evidential contribution is the parcellation/dissociability claim, layered onto the sensorium (TRN) and thought (MD thalamus) evidence in this review, rather than a direct instantiation of any one loop. As with all the lesion evidence here, the gate is inferred from a lesion-behaviour dissociation rather than measured as a gating variable, and rodent-to-REE-substrate transfer applies.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality is good (0.78): a careful triple-condition lesion dissociation from an established lab, though in a lower-impact venue than the Nature and EJN entries. Mapping fidelity is moderate-low (0.58): it robustly supports functional parcellation of striatal gating but on a control-mode axis (goal-directed/habit/Pavlovian) that differs from Q-019's loop axis. Transfer risk is moderate (0.4). Net 0.60 -- a genuine but supporting-cast contribution: it corroborates the *three-way dissociability* the architecture needs, while being explicit that the three territories it names are not the three REE loops.
