# DLS lesion disrupts habit but spares outcome expectancy (Yin, Knowlton & Balleine 2004)

**Claim tested:** Q-019 -- three-gate vs single-gate BG architecture (substrate leg: does commit-gating need separable gating loops, or does one gate evaluating several criteria suffice?)
**Direction:** supports (three-gate) -- confidence 0.72

## What the paper did

Yin, Knowlton and Balleine trained rats to lever-press for sucrose under interval schedules, which reliably generate *habits* -- responding that has become insensitive to the current value of the outcome. They then devalued the sucrose by pairing it with lithium-chloride-induced nausea and tested, in extinction, whether animals still pressed. Sham-operated controls kept pressing despite devaluation (the hallmark of habit). Rats with excitotoxic lesions of the **dorsolateral striatum (DLS)** did *not*: their responding dropped after devaluation, i.e. control had reverted to the goal-directed, outcome-sensitive system. A second experiment added a **dorsomedial striatum (DMS)** lesion group and showed the effect was specific -- only DLS lesions restored outcome sensitivity; DMS lesions did not disrupt habit. This is a clean double dissociation of two anatomically distinct striatal territories serving two distinct modes of instrumental control.

## Why it matters for Q-019

Q-019's testable core is a *dissociable lesion signature*: three-gate is confirmed if lesioning one gate degrades **only** its behavioural axis while the others survive; single-gate is confirmed if the gates collapse to one effective gate with no dissociable signature. This paper is the canonical demonstration that the striatum is not one convergent selector but a set of territories whose lesions produce *selective* -- not global -- deficits. Removing the DLS does not abolish action selection; it strips the *automated* (habitual) mode and hands control back to the deliberate (goal-directed) mode. That is precisely the shape the three-gate hypothesis predicts, and it directly instantiates Q-019's **action-commitment loop (loop 3)** and its *automaticity gradient*: over-trained behaviour migrates into a distinct sensorimotor territory, and when that territory is removed the migration reverses.

## Mapping and its limits

The mapping is faithful at the level of *principle* -- separable territories, dissociable lesions, an automaticity-linked handoff between control modes -- but it is not a one-to-one image of Q-019's full triad. The DMS/DLS axis is a *two-territory* goal-directed-vs-habit distinction; Q-019 posits a *three-way* sensorium / thought / motor-commitment architecture. So this evidence load-bears on the action-commitment leg and on dissociability-in-principle, not on the sensorium or thought gates (those are served in this review by the TRN and mediodorsal-thalamus entries respectively). The usual rodent-to-substrate transfer risk also applies: a lever-press habit is a narrow behavioural proxy for the REE commit boundary, and the "gate" here is inferred from a lesion-behaviour dissociation rather than measured as a gating variable.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality is high (0.9): a foundational, much-replicated causal lesion design with a within-study specificity control (DMS vs DLS). Mapping fidelity is moderate-to-high (0.7) because the result is the dissociable-lesion signature the claim itself nominates as decisive, discounted for the two- vs three-territory mismatch. Transfer risk is moderate (0.35). Net 0.72 -- solid support for the three-gate leg, with the honest caveat that it evidences dissociability and the action loop, not the whole three-gate scheme.
