# Freezing revisited: coordinated autonomic and central optimization of threat coping (Roelofs & Dayan 2022)

Claims: MECH-599. Direction: supports. Confidence: 0.50. PMID 35760906, DOI 10.1038/s41583-022-00608-2.

Roelofs and Dayan argue that freezing is not passive fear. It is a coordinated state, triggered when a threat is detected, that hides the animal, sharpens perception, and prepares a fight or flight response. Noradrenaline and acetylcholine coordinate it across brain and autonomic nervous system. They offer a computational framework for how those parts fit together in time.

This bears on the part of MECH-599 that V3-EXQ-1109 made load-bearing: the consumer contract. Defensive consumers should read predicted threat, not accumulated injury. In REE, MECH-279 currently gates freeze on raw z_harm_a multiplied by duration. The claim notes flag that as a tension with SD-020/MECH-258 and leave it to governance. This review sits on the threat-read side. It describes freezing as a response to *detected threat* with an information-gathering job, which fits SD-099-style orienting better than an injury-accumulation gate.

It does not settle the tension. MECH-279 is aimed at a catatonia-like phenotype, and a pathological freeze driven by accumulated suffering could be a deliberately different state from the adaptive freeze described here. The honest summary is that REE may need both, and should not let one variable drive both.

Confidence 0.50: a theoretical review that informs the consumer contract and says nothing about cue specificity.
