# Differential roles of human striatum and amygdala in associative learning (Li et al. 2011)

Claims: MECH-598. Direction: supports. Confidence: 0.66. PMID 21909088, DOI 10.1038/nn.2904.

Li and colleagues ran a human fear-conditioning reversal task with mild shocks and fitted a hybrid model in which each cue carries both a value and a Pearce-Hall associability. Amygdala activity tracked the associability term, and striatum tracked the prediction error. The two quantities dissociated across regions.

This is the paper the MECH-598 notes most need and do not cite. Unlike the rat CeA work it is aversive, and it uses reversal, which is the claim's own signature test: after the A/B swap, surprise should reopen learning about the cue. The amygdala tracking a per-cue learning-rate state under shock is close to the computational object the claim proposes.

The mapping still has gaps. The design has two cues, not a retrospective window over many candidate causes, and "associability" is a fitted per-cue scalar, not something shown to control learning causally. So it supports the per-cue, persistent, aversive associability core of MECH-598. It does not support the retrospective-eligibility-window part, which remains the claim's own addition.

Confidence 0.66, the highest for a MECH-598 entry, because it is the best match on valence and on the reversal signature.
