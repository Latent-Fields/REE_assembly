# Elucidating an Affective Pain Circuit that Creates a Threat Memory (Han et al. 2015)

Claims: MECH-599. Direction: supports. Confidence: 0.64. PMID 26186190, DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2015.05.057.

Han, Soleiman, Soden, Zweifel and Palmiter traced an affective pain pathway from CGRP neurons in the parabrachial nucleus to the central amygdala. Silencing these neurons blocked pain responses and stopped mice forming a threat memory. Stimulating them optogenetically, with no shock, produced defensive behaviour and a threat memory. CeA neurons carrying the CGRP receptor were needed for the memory to form.

For MECH-599 the useful point is that a dedicated affective-harm signal is enough to *create* a threat memory. The store is written by a teaching event, not by an accumulation of damage. That is the structure the claim proposes. The pathway carries the affective, motivational side of pain, which maps loosely onto SD-011's z_harm_a. The MECH-599 consumer contract puts that stream in the teacher role, not as the variable defensive consumers read. This paper is consistent with that allocation.

What it does not show is that the resulting memory is cue-specific or held in a discrete store, and cue specificity is MECH-599's distinctive commitment. It also shows the same input driving both acute pain responses and learning, so the separation between responding to harm and learning about its cause is not visible at this stage of the circuit.

Confidence 0.64. Strong causal biology; partial mapping.
