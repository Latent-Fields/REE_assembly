# The central nucleus of the amygdala and the construction of defensive modes across the threat-imminence continuum (Moscarello & Penzo 2022)

Claims: MECH-599. Direction: supports. Confidence: 0.52. PMID 35915178, DOI 10.1038/s41593-022-01130-5.

Moscarello and Penzo propose that the central amygdala builds defensive modes (freezing, flight, avoidance) through mutually inhibitory, winner-takes-all circuits, selecting the mode by how imminent the threat is. The selection is applied to threat associations the animal has already learned.

This paper is already in the corpus for MECH-489, where the relevant point is the selection architecture. For MECH-599 the relevant point is the input. The defensive system selects its mode from learned threat predictions and their imminence. It does not select from how much damage has already accumulated. That is MECH-599's consumer contract: SD-099 release, MECH-279 freeze entry and E3 harm scoring should read predicted threat.

It also suggests an ordering for REE. Imminence is a property of a prediction: how soon the predicted harm will arrive. An agent with no cue-specific threat predictor has nothing to compute imminence from. So MECH-599 is plausibly a prerequisite for any later imminence-graded defensive mode selection.

Confidence 0.52: a review, with the mapping limited to the consumer's input type.
