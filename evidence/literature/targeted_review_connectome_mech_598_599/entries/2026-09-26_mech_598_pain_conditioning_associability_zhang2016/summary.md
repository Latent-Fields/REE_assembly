# Dissociable Learning Processes Underlie Human Pain Conditioning (Zhang et al. 2016)

Claims: MECH-598. Direction: supports. Confidence: 0.62. PMID 26711494, DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2015.10.066.

Zhang, Mano, Ganesh, Robbins and Seymour gave people visual cues that predicted thermal pain, mostly to the left or the right arm. They modelled two kinds of conditioned response separately: a general preparatory response (skin conductance, facial expression) and a specific withdrawal of the arm about to be hurt. Each had its own learning process with its own associability. The preparatory system's associability and prediction error correlated with amygdala-striatal activity. The withdrawal system's associability correlated with the cerebellum on the same side.

For MECH-598 there are two points. First, this is associability in pain conditioning in humans, which is about as close to the claim's aversive trigger as the literature gets. Second, associability is not one number per cue. Each learning system carries its own. SD-011 already splits harm into an affective accumulated stream and a sensory-discriminative proximity stream. If those map, even loosely, onto preparatory and consummatory, then a V3 build may need an associability state per consumer rather than one shared vector. That is my mapping, not the authors' claim.

It also slightly weakens the amygdala-only subject label, since the specific system's associability sits in the cerebellum.

Confidence 0.62: strong aversive support, correlational method, and a single-cue design with no retrospective window.
