# Neural correlates of variations in event processing during learning in central nucleus of amygdala (Calu et al. 2010)

Claims: MECH-598. Direction: mixed. Confidence: 0.50. PMID 21145010, DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2010.11.019.

Calu and colleagues recorded central amygdala neurons while rats had rewards delivered or omitted unexpectedly. CeA firing rose when an expected reward failed to arrive, faded as the omission became expected, and tracked behavioural measures of attention that CeA lesions abolish. It did not rise to the cues, and it did not rise to unexpected rewards.

So CeA sends a specific signal, "the expected good thing did not happen", rather than a general surprise signal. Two things follow for MECH-598. First, the claim's wording that associability is driven by "the |PE| of outcomes it was present for" is more general than what the CeA data show; the biological update signal is event-class-specific. The claim already defines its trigger as a phasic adverse change, so this is a tightening of the notes rather than of the mechanism. Second, CeA does not hold the per-cue value. It detects the event, and the associability lives somewhere else, which agrees with the consolidation and DLS entries.

The valence question cuts both ways. Reward omission is plausibly an aversive event, "an unexpected negative change in valued self-state" in the claim's own words. Read that way, this is supporting evidence: an aversive surprise drives a CeA signal that raises attention to the cues present. Read strictly as appetitive, it is only an analogue.

Mixed, confidence 0.50. Good data; ambiguous mapping.
