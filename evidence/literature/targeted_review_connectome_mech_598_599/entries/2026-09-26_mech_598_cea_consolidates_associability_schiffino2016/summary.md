# Consolidation of altered associability information by amygdala central nucleus (Schiffino & Holland 2016)

Claims: MECH-598. Direction: supports. Confidence: 0.55. PMID 27427328, DOI 10.1016/j.nlm.2016.07.016.

Schiffino and Holland asked whether the associability boost from a surprising omission needs consolidating, like other memories. It does. Infusing a protein-synthesis inhibitor, or silencing the central amygdala, *after* a session with the surprising omission prevented the rats from later showing faster learning about the cue. Their earlier work showed CeA is not needed when that faster learning is expressed. The authors read this as CeA running something like a post-surprise replay that consolidates an associability memory stored elsewhere, probably in posterior parietal cortex.

For MECH-598 this supports the persistence clause, which is the part the claim says to drop if reversal is not faster. Associability looks like a stored thing that has to survive from one encounter to the next, not an online boost that decays with the eligibility window.

It also connects MECH-598 to machinery REE already has. A consolidation step after the event is what MECH-431 (tag-and-capture) and MECH-074b (replay tagging) are about. The claim notes separate MECH-598 from both, and that is defensible, since those decide *whether* a trace is written and not *how fast a cue is learned about later*. But this paper suggests biology uses the replay machinery to store the associability state itself. A V3 build with an instantly updated per-code associability vector would skip that step. That is fine for a first test, as long as it is recognised as a simplification.

Confidence 0.55. Appetitive, omission-driven, one lab, and the replay is inferred rather than observed.
