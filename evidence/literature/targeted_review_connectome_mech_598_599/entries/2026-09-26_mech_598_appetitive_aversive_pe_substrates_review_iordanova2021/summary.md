# Neural substrates of appetitive and aversive prediction error (Iordanova et al. 2021)

Claims: MECH-598. Direction: supports. Confidence: 0.50. PMID 33453307, DOI 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2020.10.029.

Iordanova, Yau, McDannald and Corbit review what causes and carries prediction error in the brain, for rewards and for threats. Their framing has two roles: prediction error changes associations, and it allocates attention to stimuli so they can be learned about. They trace aversive prediction error through the ventrolateral PAG, amygdala, locus coeruleus noradrenaline, dopamine, hippocampus and cortex.

For MECH-598 the review supports the premise that aversive surprise does two separate jobs. It does not help decide the specific question the claim raises, whether the attention role is a persistent per-cue learning-rate state. That is what the Pearce-Hall modelling papers (Li 2011, Zhang 2016) address.

One point is worth noting. Noradrenaline from locus coeruleus is a classic broadcast gain, close to what REE files under MECH-074a, which is MECH-598's matched control. The review therefore reminds us that biology has both the broadcast and the selective route. The claim is not that broadcast gain is absent, only that it is not sufficient.

Confidence 0.50. A good map of the territory, but not a test of the claim.
