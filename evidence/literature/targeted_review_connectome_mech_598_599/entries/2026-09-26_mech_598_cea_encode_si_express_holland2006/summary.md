# Different roles for amygdala central nucleus and substantia innominata in the surprise-induced enhancement of learning (Holland & Gallagher 2006)

Claims: MECH-598. Direction: supports. Confidence: 0.58. PMID 16597732, DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0390-06.2006.

Holland and Gallagher inactivated either the central amygdala or the cholinergic substantia innominata, either at the moment an expected event was omitted or later, when the rat learned about the cue. CeA was needed at the moment of surprise and not afterwards. The basal forebrain was needed afterwards and not at the moment of surprise. The surprise is registered by one system and used by another.

This is the most direct animal support in the set for the *persistent* half of MECH-598. The claim says the aversive event writes a learning-rate state onto candidate causes, and later encounters read it. If the whole thing were a one-shot boost at the time of the event, there would be no reason for the write and the read to have different substrates. The claim's own falsifier, a frozen-associability arm that should be slower at reversal, is testing exactly this separation.

Two caveats pull in different directions. First, the paradigm is appetitive and the surprise is an omission, so the aversive trigger MECH-598 needs is not tested. Second, the read-out side is cholinergic. That is a neuromodulatory gain on learning, which is close to what REE files under MECH-398 and treats as the *control* condition. So biology may implement associability as a per-cue tag plus a gain applied when the tag is read. That would not falsify MECH-598, but it does mean a V3 build that puts everything in the per-cue vector is a simplification.

Confidence 0.58: strong for the structure, silent on valence.
