# Dorsolateral striatum is critical for the expression of surprise-induced enhancements in cue associability (Asem et al. 2015)

Claims: MECH-598. Direction: mixed. Confidence: 0.45. PMID 26108257, DOI 10.1111/ejn.13001.

Asem, Schiffino and Holland found that the dorsolateral striatum is needed to *express* enhanced cue associability but not to *establish* it. They place the effect in the amygdala-nigra-striatum loop that also produces conditioned orienting responses to cues.

This is mixed for MECH-598. It supports the write-then-read structure seen in the Holland & Gallagher inactivation study. It weakens any literal reading of the claim's subject label, "amygdala.aversive_pe_cue_associability". Associability in the rat is a property of a circuit, with the amygdala at the front end.

The more useful point is for experimental design. If associability and learned orienting share circuitry, then in REE a MECH-598 arm could beat the broadcast arm simply because the agent orients to A more, and not because it learns about A faster. SD-099 already owns harm-driven orienting. The Q-113 test and any later MECH-598 test should log orienting and learning rate separately, so the two effects are not mistaken for each other.

Confidence 0.45: an appetitive rodent circuit paper whose value to REE is mostly as a design constraint.
