# Neural substrates for expectation-modulated fear learning in the amygdala and periaqueductal gray (Johansen et al. 2010)

Claims: MECH-598, MECH-599. Direction: supports. Confidence: 0.65. PMID 20601946, DOI 10.1038/nn.2594.

Johansen, Tarpley, LeDoux and Blair recorded from the lateral amygdala and periaqueductal gray while rats learned that a tone predicted shock. In both regions the response to the shock shrank as the shock became expected. Inactivating PAG reduced the amygdala's shock response and impaired learning. So PAG relays a teaching signal to the amygdala, and that signal is scaled by surprise.

This is the strongest single entry for MECH-599's "taught by aversive-PE events" clause. The biological threat store is not taught by how much harm happened. It is taught by how much more harm happened than expected. Its teacher also comes from outside the amygdala, which is a constraint for a V3 build: a ConditionedThreat store needs an upstream PE signal, not a raw z_harm feed. This agrees with SD-020 and MECH-258, and with the V3-EXQ-1109 diagnosis that a consumer reading raw accumulated harm is reading the wrong variable.

For MECH-598 the paper is weaker than the claim notes imply. What it shows is Rescorla-Wagner: expectation modulates the strength of the teaching signal. It does not show that surprise changes the learning rate of the cue. It establishes the trigger, not the associability.

Confidence 0.65. Excellent evidence, and an honest reading splits the mapping: strong for MECH-599, trigger-only for MECH-598.
