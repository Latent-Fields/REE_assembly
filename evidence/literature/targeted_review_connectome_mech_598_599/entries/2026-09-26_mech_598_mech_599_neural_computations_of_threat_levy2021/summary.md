# Neural Computations of Threat (Levy & Schiller 2021)

Claims: MECH-598, MECH-599. Direction: supports. Confidence: 0.55. PMID 33384214, DOI 10.1016/j.tics.2020.11.007.

Levy and Schiller review threat as a sequence of computations: the first encounter with danger, learning and storing the cues that predict it, updating those predictions, and choosing what to do. They argue these stages share neural and computational machinery and should be studied together.

For MECH-599 the relevant move is structural. The review treats learned cues that predict danger as their own stage, distinct from the first aversive encounter. That is the separation between injury and threat prediction the V3-EXQ-1109 result made necessary in REE. For MECH-598, the review covers the associability-based modelling of threat learning, including the authors' own reversal work, as part of the same pipeline.

There is a mild counterweight. The review's emphasis on shared mechanisms argues against building the threat store, the associability state and the defensive consumers as fully isolated modules. A V3 build should share representations (z_world codes) across them rather than duplicate them.

Confidence 0.55: a framing review, supportive but not a test.
