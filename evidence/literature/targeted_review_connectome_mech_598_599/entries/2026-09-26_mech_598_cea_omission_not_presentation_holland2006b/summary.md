# Enhanced conditioning produced by surprising increases in reinforcer value are unaffected by lesions of the amygdala central nucleus (Holland 2006)

Claims: MECH-598. Direction: mixed. Confidence: 0.50. PMID 16102981, DOI 10.1016/j.nlm.2005.07.001.

This is the disconfirming paper I went looking for, and it came from the same lab. Holland produced surprise the other way round: instead of omitting an expected outcome, they added an unexpected one (sucrose after the expected food). Learning about the accompanying cue was enhanced, and it was enhanced just as much in rats with central amygdala lesions.

The authors' conclusion is that the CeA surprise circuit is engaged preferentially by omissions. That matters for MECH-598 because its trigger, in the harm case, is mostly the other kind of surprise: harm arriving when it was not expected. The claim notes cite Holland & Schiffino 2016 for "CeA-dependent surprise-induced associability". This paper says that citation covers omission-type surprise, and possibly not the unexpected-delivery case the claim actually cares about.

I would not over-read it. The enhancement here could be ordinary Rescorla-Wagner unblocking, where a bigger-than-expected outcome teaches more, and not an associability change at all. If so, the paper bounds where CeA is needed but says nothing against aversive associability as such. In the human aversive work (Li et al. 2011; Zhang et al. 2016), amygdala activity tracks model-derived associability under shock and pain, which is the case the claim needs.

Recorded as mixed, confidence 0.50. The practical consequence is a wording fix rather than a threat to the claim: when MECH-598 is hardened, the substrate citation should be the human aversive-associability work, and the CeA omission literature should be presented as the appetitive analogue.
