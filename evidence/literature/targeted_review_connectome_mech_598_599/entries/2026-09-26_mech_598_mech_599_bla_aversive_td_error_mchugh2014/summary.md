# Aversive prediction error signals in the amygdala (McHugh et al. 2014)

Claims: MECH-598, MECH-599. Direction: supports. Confidence: 0.55. PMID 24990922, DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4465-13.2014.

McHugh and colleagues measured tissue oxygen and theta oscillations in the mouse basolateral amygdala during fear conditioning. As the tone came to predict the shock, the shock response shrank and the tone response grew. Unexpected shocks produced bigger responses than expected ones, and omitting an expected shock pushed the signal below baseline. The size of the shock response predicted how the animal behaved the next day. That is the shape of a temporal-difference prediction error.

I've tagged it to both claims for different reasons. For MECH-598 it confirms that the trigger exists: the amygdala carries an aversive prediction-error event that is larger when harm is unexpected. It also shows the signal is signed, which means it is the Rescorla-Wagner/TD half and not the unsigned associability drive the claim adds. For MECH-599 the relevant finding is the transfer of response from shock to tone, which is what forming a cue-specific threat prediction looks like.

It also bears on the V3-EXQ-1109 observation behind both claims. The amygdala signal is a prediction about harm that moves to the cue. It does not track how much damage has built up. That is the injury/threat separation MECH-599's consumer contract relies on.

Confidence 0.55. Indirect signals (hemodynamics, LFP), mouse, and for MECH-598 it covers only the trigger.
