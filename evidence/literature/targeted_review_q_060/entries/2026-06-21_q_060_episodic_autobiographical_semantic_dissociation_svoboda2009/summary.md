# A durable episodic-autobiographical vs semantic dissociation — but inside the MTL

According to PubMed, Svoboda and Levine (2009, *Journal of Neuroscience*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3452-08.2009)) used prospectively-collected, ecologically-valid stimuli — participants audio-recorded their own autobiographical events and personal semantic facts over 6–8 months — and then scanned them while they re-heard recordings for the first, second, or eighth time. Two findings bear on Q-060. First, episodic-autobiographical and semantic remembering produced *distinctly different* regional activation patterns, and these patterns held even after eight repetitions. Second, within the medial temporal lobe the dissociation was specific: the hippocampus was engaged by vivid episodic-autobiographical recollection in particular, whereas parahippocampal cortex served both autobiographical and semantic retrieval. The authors read this as support for multiple-trace theory — the hippocampus stays engaged for rich episodic recollection regardless of how many times the memory is reactivated.

This is the paper in the Q-060 set that most genuinely cuts both ways, which is why I have marked it `mixed`. A proponent of autobiographical-as-distinct-type can point to the robust, rehearsal-resistant dissociation and say: these are two different things with two different signatures. But the *locus* of the dissociation is the decisive detail for REE. The distinction does not live in two parallel stores; it lives inside the episodic/MTL machinery, as a hippocampal vividness-and-recollection signal riding on a shared parahippocampal substrate. That is exactly the shape ARC-085 predicts if autobiographical memory is episodic content carrying retained self/contextual tags — the tags drive the hippocampal recollection signature — rather than a semantic-like store of its own.

For REE the translation is that the autobiographical/semantic split is real and stable, but it is a *mode* of the single episodic substrate (ARC-007), not evidence for a second machinery. It also dovetails with MECH-121: the content that keeps engaging the hippocampus across repeated reactivation is precisely the content that resists draining into de-tagged semantic memory — i.e. autobiographical = episodic that won't fully semanticise.

I have assigned confidence 0.55. Source quality is high (a strong *J Neurosci* design with naturalistic stimuli), but mapping fidelity is deliberately discounted: because the same result is citable by both poles of Q-060, the paper constrains the architecture (the dissociation is an MTL-internal modulation) without by itself deciding tag-versus-type. The concrete failure signature is the shared parahippocampal engagement — a dedicated autobiographical type would not be expected to route through the same cortical stage as semantic retrieval.
