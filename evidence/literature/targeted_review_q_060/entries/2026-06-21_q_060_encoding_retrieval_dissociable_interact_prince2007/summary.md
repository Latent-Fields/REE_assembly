# Episodic and semantic processes are dissociable but interdependent

According to PubMed, Prince, Tsukiura and Cabeza (2007, *Psychological Science*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01864.x)) used a factorial fMRI design to pull apart episodic memory encoding (EE) and semantic memory retrieval (SR), two operations that normally confound each other because encoding an episode tends to trigger semantic retrieval and vice versa. Within the left temporal lobe they found a clean structural dissociation — the hippocampus tracked successful encoding while a posterior lateral region tracked successful semantic retrieval. But the prefrontal picture was one of *interaction*, not separation: a posterior LIPFC region served SR, a mid region served both, and an anterior region served EE only when SR was also high. In other words, the two memory operations recruit partly shared machinery and are interdependent in practice.

The relevance to Q-060 is structural rather than topical. This paper does not study autobiographical-versus-semantic content directly; it studies episodic-encoding-versus-semantic-retrieval. But its core lesson — dissociable components embedded in a shared, interacting substrate — is exactly the parsimony argument behind the ABM-1 decision. ARC-085's thesis is "one substrate, not two parallel machineries," and a finding that the very operations underlying episodic and semantic memory cannot be cleanly pried apart in prefrontal cortex strengthens the case against modelling autobiographical memory as an independent first-class store sitting beside semantic memory.

For REE the translation is supportive but indirect: it favours a single memory substrate with shared and partially-distinct components (where autobiographical is a tagged region of that substrate) over a taxonomy of independent stores. I have kept `evidence_direction: supports` but pinned confidence at 0.5, because the autobiographical dimension is inferred here rather than measured — the paper is one analytical step removed from the question Q-060 actually poses. The concrete failure signature worth recording is that anterior LIPFC engaged in episodic encoding *only* when semantic retrieval was also high; independent stores would not show that kind of conditional coupling.

This entry is the weakest of the three Q-060 papers on mapping fidelity and is included as corroborating, not load-bearing, evidence — it shows the general shape (shared interacting substrate) that the Renoult and Svoboda entries instantiate for the specific autobiographical case.
