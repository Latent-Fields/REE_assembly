# Personal semantics dedifferentiates with age — graded, not categorical

According to PubMed, Tanguay, Renoult and colleagues (2021, *Memory & Cognition*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-021-01225-7)) recorded ERPs while young (n=28) and older (n=26) adults judged personality traits for self-relevance (self-knowledge), judged the same traits' relevance to social groups (general semantic), and then performed a recognition test (episodic). In young adults the late positive component — the index of episodic recollection — cleanly separated all three conditions, replicating the group's prior claim that *self-knowledge has neural correlates dissociable from both general semantic and episodic memory*. The headline result is what happens in ageing: that three-way differentiation collapses, and the residual amount of differentiation tracks each older adult's composite episodic memory score.

This matters for Q-060 because the live question is no longer *whether* autobiographical content is distinguishable from semantic content — everyone agrees it is — but whether that distinction earns a **separate first-class memory type/store**, or is better modelled as a **self/affect/provenance tag on the existing episodic event token** (the ABM-1 / ARC-085 decision: a binding-and-indexing layer over the ARC-007 episodic store). Renoult's "personal semantics" framework, of which this paper is an empirical instance, sits the personal/autobiographical dimension on a *continuum* between pure episodic and pure semantic, and this study shows the boundary is graded and degradable rather than categorical. A regime whose distinctiveness fades smoothly with ageing — and in proportion to episodic integrity — looks like a tag whose strength varies, not like a structurally separate store that would either be intact or lesioned.

The mapping to REE is therefore supportive of the parsimonious reading: autobiographical memory as episodic content that *retains* its self/affect tags and resists full semanticisation (the MECH-121 drain), rather than as a parallel machinery. The honest caveat is that the paper's "personal semantics" is trait self-knowledge — the already-semanticised, de-tagged end of the spectrum — not autobiographical *event* memory, which is the richly-tagged end Q-060 actually cares about. So this constrains the taxonomy (the boundary is graded) without directly settling the event-memory case.

I have set `evidence_direction: supports` with confidence 0.6. The discount is almost entirely transfer risk: Q-060 is an architecture decision about whether a layer "earns its keep," and ERP component dissociations speak to that only by analogy. Source quality is good (the originating lab, a clean design, a peer-reviewed venue), and the failure signature is concrete — a first-class separate type should not dedifferentiate as a function of episodic memory score.
