# The self's pain code is not what carries the other's pain

If the previous entry is the case for shared representation, this is the study that tells you how much of it survives contact with multivariate methods. The answer is: the region-level overlap survives, the representational sharing does not.

Krishnan and colleagues trained patterns to predict somatic pain intensity and vicarious pain intensity separately. Both reached 100% accuracy on high-versus-low within their own condition. Then they cross-predicted -- somatic pattern applied to vicarious data, and vice versa -- and both fell to chance. Not reduced, not attenuated. Chance. Against 100% within-condition performance, that is about as clean a dissociation as this literature produces, and it is not the kind of null that underpowering explains away. They also recovered somatotopic information at 93% for both conditions, which sounds like sharing until you see where from: somatosensory circuits for somatic pain, mentalizing-related circuits for vicarious pain. Even the body-referenced part of vicarious pain is carried by different machinery, not by the same machinery re-indexed to another person.

For ARC-094 this cuts both ways, and I do not think the tension should be smoothed over.

It weakens the positive half. ARC-094 says fast empathy must emerge from binding existing motivational-affective streams across self -> object -> other. The most natural way to build that -- and, I suspect, the way a REE implementer would reach for first -- is to route other-directed affect through the self's own affective code, treating the other as an argument to a function the organism already runs on itself. For pain, that is precisely what the brain does not do. The self-pain code carries no information about the other's pain. An architecture that derives other-affect by re-pointing the self-affect stream is not what is instantiated here.

It supports the prohibition half, and rather strongly. If vicarious pain cannot be decoded from the somatic pain pattern, then there is no scalar sitting in first-person affect that indexes empathic response. An `empathy_score` computed off the self's affective state would be reading a channel that demonstrably does not contain the signal. The prohibition ARC-094 states turns out to be better motivated than the mechanism it proposes in its place.

What survives, and this is the constructive reading, is the no-module commitment itself. The vicarious pattern is not in an empathy organ. It is distributed, and it sits in mentalizing circuitry -- machinery with plenty of other work to do, engaged in theory-of-mind tasks that have nothing to do with pain. So the architecture is still binding of existing streams. It is just that the streams bound are not the ones the self-first reading assumes. ARC-094 may need its self -> object -> other progression restated: not "the self's affect stream extended outward", but "other-directed affect assembled from mentalizing and affective streams that each already exist for other purposes". That is a substantive amendment to the claim, not a gloss, and it is the main thing this pull turns up for governance.

One limitation that matters for the claim as scoped. ARC-094 is explicitly about *fast* empathy. Laboratory vicarious pain, induced by watching images or videos of others in pain with time to process them, plausibly recruits deliberate perspective-taking rather than the fast route. The design does not separate these, so the dissociation may hold for slow empathy and not settle the fast case at all. That is a real gap rather than a hedge -- the fast/slow distinction is load-bearing in ARC-094 and untested here.

Confidence 0.72. Methodologically the strongest source in this pull, and its bearing on the claim is clear even though it points in two directions at once.
