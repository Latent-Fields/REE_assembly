# Empathy runs on the organism's own affective machinery

This is the canonical meta-analytic result behind the shared-representation account of empathy, and it is the strongest single source for ARC-094's positive half. Lamm, Decety and Singer combined an image-based meta-analysis over nine independent fMRI datasets with a coordinate-based meta-analysis over thirty-two studies -- a stronger design than the coordinate-only ALE approach, because the image-based component uses the full statistical maps rather than reported peaks. The core finding: empathy for another's pain recruits bilateral anterior insula and medial/anterior cingulate cortex, and this network overlaps with the network engaged by pain experienced first-hand.

For ARC-094 the relevant reading is what is *absent*. There is no empathy region. There is no structure that comes online for other-directed affect and stays quiet otherwise. What happens instead is that machinery the organism already runs for its own aversive states is engaged under a different access route. That is the architectural content of ARC-094's positive clause -- fast empathy emerging from existing motivational-affective streams -- and it is what the meta-analysis shows at the level neuroimaging can show it.

The second finding is the one I find more persuasive, and it is often passed over. The wider recruited network depends on *how* the other's pain is conveyed. Picture-based paradigms, where you see an injured hand, pull in somatosensory and action-understanding areas. Abstract affective cues, where you are told someone is in pain, pull in mentalizing regions instead -- precuneus, ventromedial prefrontal cortex, superior temporal cortex, temporo-parietal junction. Same empathic outcome, different stream combinations, selected by input modality. A dedicated empathy module emitting a scalar would not do this; it would present the same signature regardless of route. Binding of whatever streams the situation makes available is exactly what would. So the route-dependence is a better argument against the module than the overlap is for the sharing.

Two limitations, and the first is serious enough that it needs its own entry in this pull. Overlap here is established at coordinate and blob resolution. Two conditions activating the same voxels is fully compatible with spatially interleaved but functionally distinct populations, and the inference from spatial overlap to shared representation is exactly the inference that multivariate methods were developed to test. Krishnan and colleagues subsequently ran that test and it came out against the strong reading; I have entered that paper alongside this one rather than letting this stand unqualified.

Second, anterior insula and anterior mid-cingulate are perhaps the two most promiscuously activated regions in the human neuroimaging literature. They respond to salience, effort, uncertainty, arousal, and error. Their appearance in empathy paradigms is therefore weakly diagnostic on its own -- it is consistent with genuine affective sharing and equally consistent with "this stimulus is important", which would not support empathy in the sense ARC-094 needs.

I should also be explicit about what this does not touch. ARC-094 specifies a self -> object -> other progression, and this literature has nothing to say about the object stage; that intermediate term has no correlate in the pain-empathy corpus at all. And the evidence is confined to pain, a single strongly conserved aversive state, which is the easiest case for a sharing account and probably not representative of empathy's harder instances.

Confidence 0.74. Strong source, directly on point for the "no dedicated module" half, weaker on the specific binding architecture, and superseded in its strong representational reading by later multivariate work.
