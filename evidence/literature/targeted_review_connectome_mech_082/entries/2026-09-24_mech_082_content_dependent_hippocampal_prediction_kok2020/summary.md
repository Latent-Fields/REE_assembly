# Kok, Rait & Turk-Browne (2020) -- Content-based dissociation of hippocampal involvement in prediction

**What they did.** This follow-up to Kok & Turk-Browne (2018) asked whether the hippocampus' predictive role is domain-general. In two high-resolution fMRI experiments, auditory cues predicted either abstract shapes (Experiment 1) or simple oriented gratings (Experiment 2). Inverted encoding models reconstructed what the hippocampus represented in each case.

**What they found.** The answer depended on the content. For complex shapes, the hippocampus again represented the *expected* shape and so could be a source of top-down prediction. For simple gratings, it represented only *unexpected* orientations, a signal that looks more like prediction error than prediction.

**Why this matters for MECH-082.** MECH-082 is written as though any hippocampal map distortion propagates down into E1 sampling. This paper suggests a narrower version: the hippocampus may push predictions into perception for compound, relational or object-level content, and act more like a surprise detector for primitive features. That fits the clinical picture reasonably well. Hypervigilance in PTSD attaches to meaningful configurations (a face, a doorway, a smell in a context), not raw orientation. It also has a practical consequence. A REE test of MECH-082 that applies distortion along low-level E1 dimensions could come back negative for reasons that have nothing to do with the core mechanism.

**Caveats.** An absence of predictive signal in the grating condition may partly reflect fMRI sensitivity. And REE's latent dimensions do not line up with "simple versus complex stimulus," so turning this into a design constraint takes a modelling decision of our own.

**Confidence (0.42), direction mixed.** It supports the mechanism in one regime and weakens its domain-general form in the other. Its main value is as a design constraint on any future falsifier.
