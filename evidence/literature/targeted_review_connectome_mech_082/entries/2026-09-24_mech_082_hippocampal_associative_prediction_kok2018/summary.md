# Kok & Turk-Browne (2018) -- Associative prediction of visual shape in the hippocampus

**What they did.** Participants learned that particular sounds predicted particular abstract shapes. Kok and Turk-Browne then recorded high-resolution fMRI and used inverted encoding models to reconstruct which shape each region was representing, in visual cortex and in the hippocampus.

**What they found.** The two regions came apart. Visual cortex represented whatever shape was actually shown. The hippocampus represented only the shape the cue *predicted*, even when that was not what was on screen. Across participants, stronger hippocampal predictions went with larger expectation-related facilitation in visual cortex.

**Mapping to MECH-082.** Two parts of the claim have a clear analogue here. First, a map-side prediction can be dissociated from current sensory evidence; the hippocampus holds the expected stimulus, not the seen one. Second, the strength of that prediction scales with a top-down change at the sensory level, and the sensory system is not retrained to produce it. If one wanted a biological picture of "the map's expectation leaking into perception," this is a good one.

**Limits.** The relationship is a correlation across participants, not a trial-level causal chain. What is measured in visual cortex is facilitation of representation, which is not obviously the same thing as a shift in where sampling is allocated. The predictions were accurate, so distortion is untested. And REE splits the pathway into a hippocampal map and a separate E2 forward model; this paper cannot tell those apart.

**Confidence (0.52).** Methodologically strong, structurally apt. The gap between the measured quantity and the claimed DV holds the mapping down.
