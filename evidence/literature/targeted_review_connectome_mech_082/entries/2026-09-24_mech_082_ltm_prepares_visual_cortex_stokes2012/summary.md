# Stokes et al. (2012) -- Long-term memory prepares neural activity for perception

**What they did.** Stokes, Atherton, Patai and Nobre asked how long-term memory reaches forward into perception. Participants learned scenes in which targets appeared at consistent locations. The team then measured behaviour (perceptual sensitivity at memory-predicted locations), EEG oscillations during the anticipatory interval, and fMRI preparatory activity in visual cortex.

**What they found.** Memory content sharpened sensitivity at predicted locations. Before the target appeared, alpha-band power fell over visual cortex in a spatially specific pattern, and extrastriate cortex showed spatiotopic preparatory modulation. The fMRI implicated an integrated network including the hippocampus and a dorsal frontoparietal circuit as the probable organiser of these preparatory states.

**Mapping to MECH-082.** This is the cleanest precedent I found for the claim's intermediate step. What a system remembers changes how its sensory front end is poised to sample, per location, before evidence arrives and without the sensory encoder relearning anything. The spatiotopic alpha change is close to what REE's what_would_answer calls a per-dimension E1 sampling-allocation readout. That matters because the audit notes existing E1 metrics do not record sampling allocation at all.

**Limits.** The hippocampus appears as one node of a network, not as a demonstrated proximal cause. The memories were valid; a distorted map was never tested. There is no feedback-channel ablation, which is the contrast the claim actually needs. Alpha desynchronization indexes gain and readiness; calling it "sampling allocation" is our translation.

**Confidence (0.58).** Good source and useful mapping for the propagation and DV halves. Silent on distortion and on the necessity of the feedback channel.
