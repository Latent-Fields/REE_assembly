# Hannula, Ryan, Tranel & Cohen (2007) -- Relational memory in eye movements, absent in hippocampal amnesia

**What they did.** Across four experiments, participants studied faces superimposed on scenes. At test they saw a scene with three equally familiar faces and had to pick the one originally paired with it, with eye tracking throughout. The authors varied whether a scene preview was shown first, letting an expectancy form, and whether an overt response was required. They also tested amnesic patients with hippocampal damage.

**What they found.** Controls looked disproportionately at the correct face. With a preview, the bias appeared within 500-750 ms, more than a second before any explicit response, and it held even when no response was required. Without a preview it arrived about a second later. In the amnesic patients the viewing effect was absent.

**Mapping to MECH-082.** Where the eyes go is the most literal biological version of "E1 sampling allocation." Here a relational prediction from the hippocampus biases sampling quickly and obligatorily, ahead of report, with no retraining of anything perceptual. Take the hippocampal source away and the bias disappears. Of the papers in this pull, this is the only one that gives a necessity test of the map-to-sampling leg.

**What remains open.** The ablation removes the *source*, the map, not the *channel*. MECH-082's falsifier asks for the E2-to-E1 feedback to be cut while the map stays intact, and for the map to be distorted rather than removed. Neither was done. The patients' damage is described at the level of the hippocampal system and the group is small. The expectancies were also true ones. So the question MECH-082 actually cares about, whether a wrong map produces the matching wrong sampling (as in hypervigilance), is not addressed.

**Confidence (0.60).** The highest in the pull because both the DV and the necessity test are close to the claim's shape. Still bounded by veridical memory and a small patient sample.
