# Summerfield et al. (2006) -- Orienting attention based on long-term memory experience

**What they did.** Summerfield, Nobre and colleagues built a task in which people first learned where small targets sat within photographs of naturalistic scenes, and then had to detect targets when the scenes reappeared. They compared this memory-guided orienting with a conventional task where a transient spatial cue told participants where to look. Event-related fMRI recorded which regions supported each kind of orienting.

**What they found.** Memory-guided attention worked fast, within surprisingly short intervals, and gave reliable, sizeable detection advantages. Its neural signature was an interaction between regions that retrieve spatial-context memory and the parietal-frontal orienting network. The hippocampus was engaged specifically by memory-guided orienting, not by cue-guided orienting, and its activity correlated with the size of the behavioural benefit.

**Mapping to MECH-082.** MECH-082 claims that changes in the hippocampal map propagate through E2 predictions to bias E1 perceptual sampling, without any E1 retraining. This paper supports the *propagation* part of that story: a hippocampus-dependent memory reshapes where the perceptual system samples, trial by trial, with the sensory encoder unchanged. Is the frontoparietal orienting network an E2-to-E1 sampling-control consumer? Perhaps. It is at least the best-documented biological candidate for that role.

**What it does not show.** The memories were accurate. The claim's weight rests on *distortion*: a wrong map producing wrong, residue-consistent sampling. Nothing here tests that. The hippocampal evidence is also correlational, so a reader who treats hippocampal retrieval as running alongside the orienting signal, not upstream of it, is not refuted. And the E2 stage is our interpretive gloss; the paper identifies a hippocampal-frontoparietal network, not a forward model.

**Confidence (0.55).** Strong source, moderate mapping. It establishes that the channel exists in humans and is fast. That is necessary for MECH-082 but well short of sufficient.
