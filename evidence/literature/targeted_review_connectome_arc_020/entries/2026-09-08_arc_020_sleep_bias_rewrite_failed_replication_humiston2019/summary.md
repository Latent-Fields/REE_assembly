# A falsifier withdrawn, not a claim supported

**Humiston & Wamsley (2019), _PLoS ONE_ 14(1):e0211416.**
Claim assessed: **ARC-020** -- "Offline consolidation is protected by typed authority/write boundaries."

## Why a null result is in this pull at all

ARC-020 says offline consolidation cannot write directly into privileged authority stores. If you
go looking in the human literature for the strongest thing pointing the other way, you arrive
quickly at Hu et al. (2015, _Science_): counter-stereotype training, then a 90-minute nap with
targeted memory reactivation cues, and a reported reduction in implicit racial and gender bias that
persisted a week. That is about as close as human work gets to "an offline process reached in and
changed something that looks like a policy rather than a picture."

Humiston & Wamsley tried to reproduce it with n=31, powered at 0.9 for the original effect. They
found nothing. Bias was, if anything, non-significantly *greater* for cued stimuli. Their 95%
confidence interval did not overlap the original's, which is the basis of their argument that this
is not simply a noisy estimate of the same underlying effect.

## What that does and does not do for ARC-020

It withdraws a falsifier. It does not supply support. I want to be exact about this because the
temptation to treat the two as equivalent is precisely how a claim's confidence inflates without
anyone deciding that it should.

A null replication is consistent with at least three worlds: the boundary exists; the boundary does
not exist but auditory cueing during a nap is simply a feeble way to cross it; or the original
finding was a false positive about something with no bearing on boundaries whatsoever. Nothing here
distinguishes them. And nothing here touches ARC-020's actual assertion -- that the boundary
discriminates by *type* -- because no condition ever contrasted an authority-affecting target
against a representational one.

## The objection that may dissolve the whole line

Here is the part I find most persuasive against my own reason for pulling the paper. Is implicit
bias, as measured by an IAT, an ARC-020 authority store at all?

REE's own `sleep.md` taxonomy has a "MAY update" list covering representational, residue and
precision material, against which POL/ID/CAPS are the protected exceptions. An implicitly-measured
associative structure -- a set of learned co-occurrence strengths, not accessible to report, not a
commitment the agent has made -- reads much more naturally as the former. If that is right, then Hu
et al. was never a falsifier of ARC-020; it was a demonstration that offline consolidation updates
exactly the kind of thing ARC-020 says it *should* be free to update, and this replication's
failure settles nothing about boundaries in either direction.

I cannot resolve that from here, because it turns on how REE draws the line between an associative
structure and a policy, which is a question for the claim's own architecture doc rather than for
the sleep literature. But governance should see the objection stated rather than have it surface
later as a reason the entry was overweighted.

## Confidence

0.2, with mapping fidelity at 0.15 -- the lowest in this pull -- and direction recorded as `mixed`
rather than `supports`, deliberately. The entry's function is to tell a future reader that the
best-known counter-evidence to ARC-020 does not replicate. That is worth having on file. It is not
a reason to move the claim.
