# The regime signature tracks recurrence, not the first episode — and medication confounds "normalisation"

According to PubMed, Li, Su, Yan and colleagues (2021, *Human Brain Mapping*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.25391)) treated 41 first-episode, drug-naive MDD patients with eight weeks of an SSRI or SNRI and compared resting-state functional connectivity before and after, alongside a cross-sectional comparison with 92 matched controls. Two results speak to Q-061. First, the study was framed against a prior big-data finding that abnormal intrinsic DMN connectivity is present in *recurrent* MDD but *not* in first-episode drug-naive patients — and consistent with that, the authors found no significant connectivity difference between their treatment-naive first-episode patients and controls. Second, antidepressants themselves drove widespread connectivity *reductions* across most networks, which the authors stress must be disentangled from MDD pathophysiology.

This entry is included specifically to keep the Q-061 file honest about the chronicity dimension (the RA-001 sub-question), which the Li (2012) and Mulders (2016) entries do not address. The staging signal here is the relevant one: if the depressive network regime's connectivity signature is a property of recurrence/chronicity rather than of the acute first episode, then early and late depressive regimes may not be the same object — which is precisely the premise behind RA-001's claim that the connectivity-severity relationship reverses sign past a chronicity threshold, and behind Q-061's sharp sub-question of whether early-effective repair routes still work late. A regime that only fully crystallises with recurrence is one where "reversal" plausibly means something different early versus late.

But the mapping caveats are heavy, hence `evidence_direction: mixed` and confidence 0.5. The first-episode-versus-recurrent contrast is *cross-sectional* and partly inherited from the cited big-data study, not a within-subject longitudinal test — so it cannot establish that a repair route that reconfigures an early regime *fails* on a late one; it can only show the signature is stage-associated. Worse for any naive reading of the other two papers, this study demonstrates that antidepressants reduce connectivity globally, meaning treatment-related "normalisation" can be a medication artefact rather than true regime repair. That is a direct methodological caution REE should carry: in MECH-367 terms, a regime vector moving toward baseline under an intervention is not automatically evidence that the underlying maladaptive regime has been repaired.

Taken together with the other two entries, the literature converges on a careful middle position for Q-061: the depressive network regime is **partially and conditionally reversible** (Li 2012; Mulders 2016), with a **chronicity/staging association** (this paper) that is consistent with — but does not prove — a hard duration-lock. Crucially, no entry in this set demonstrates therapeutic reversal of a specifically *chronic* (>24-month) regime, so the RA-001 threshold question stays genuinely open, exactly as the claim's `answer_state` status holds. The anti-overclaim guard in the Q-061 notes is well supported: the evidence does not license calling depression "merely a reversible software state."
