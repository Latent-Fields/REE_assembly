# Posterior DMN normalises, anterior DMN persists — partial, locus-specific reversibility

According to PubMed, Li, Liu, Friston and colleagues (2012, *Biological Psychiatry*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2012.11.007)) scanned 24 MDD patients and 29 controls at rest, then rescanned 18 patients after 12 weeks of antidepressant treatment, decomposing the default mode network with ICA into anterior and posterior subnetworks. Both subnetworks were hyperconnected in untreated depression. After treatment the *posterior* subnetwork abnormality normalised — but the *anterior* subnetwork abnormality persisted even in patients who had clinically recovered. The authors propose the persistent anterior abnormality as a biomarker of asymptomatic depression and a substrate for the field's notoriously high relapse rates.

This is the single most on-target paper for Q-061, because it refuses both of the clean answers the question is poised between. Depression's network regime is not monolithically reversible (the anterior subnetwork does not normalise even at symptomatic recovery), and it is not monolithically locked (the posterior subnetwork does normalise). It is *partially* reversible, and the reversibility is locus-specific: some sub-regimes reconfigure, one residual locus resists and quietly carries relapse risk forward.

The mapping to MECH-367 is direct. Q-061 asks whether REE's repair machinery — offline integration and MECH-124 replay rebalancing, decommitment from ruminative loops, re-access to social affordances, goal-stream re-coupling — can reconfigure a maladaptive depressive regime, or whether chronicity locks it. This paper's answer-by-analogy is "some of it, not all of it": the repair routes would be expected to reconfigure parts of the regime (cf. posterior normalisation) while a residual locked locus (cf. the persistent anterior subnetwork) resists and underpins relapse. That is a more textured target for REE than a binary reversible/irreversible flag, and it argues for modelling repair as operating differentially across the regime's components rather than on the regime as a single object.

The honest caveats keep confidence at 0.62. This is a 12-week study in patients of mixed illness duration, not specifically the >24-month chronic population that RA-001 is about — so it establishes partial reversibility but does **not** test the duration-threshold hypothesis (whether early-effective routes fail late). And human DMN connectivity is a coarse analogue of REE's MECH-367 regime vector. Still, of the Q-061 set this entry has the highest mapping fidelity, because the anterior/posterior dissociation almost literally instantiates "some repair routes work, one regime resists." The recorded failure signature — anterior hyperconnectivity persisting in asymptomatic, recovered patients — is also the cleanest available refutation of any "depression is merely a reversible software state" overclaim, which the Q-061 notes explicitly warn against.
