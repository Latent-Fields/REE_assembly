# Hahamy, Dubossarsky & Behrens 2023 — context-specific reactivation

Nature Neuroscience 26:1080–1089, DOI `10.1038/s41593-023-01331-6`.

In two naturalistic narrative datasets, representations of past events were reactivated in hippocampus and default-mode regions specifically at event boundaries. The reactivated events were not arbitrary recent memories: they were preferentially those relevant to the current narrative context.

For MECH-547 this is useful **adjacent support**. It demonstrates that current context can select which stored information becomes exposed/reinstated, which is the biological pattern the receiver-conditioned translation thought is trying to capture. It argues against a single fixed read surface that always exposes the same information.

The boundary is important. The paper does not learn or identify a mathematical `T(sender, receiver)` bridge, and it does not distinguish receiver-state conditioning from ordinary cue-conditioned retrieval. Therefore it cannot by itself support the stronger claim that an inter-system translator must inspect the receiver's state. That remains the job of the registered `T(A,B)` versus `T(A)` plus receiver-permutation assay.
