## What the paper did

Worbe, Palminteri, Savulich, Daw, Fernandez-Egea, Robbins and Voon tested the effect of acute dietary tryptophan depletion (TD) -- a standard method for transiently lowering central serotonin synthesis in healthy volunteers -- on model-based versus model-free decision-making. The study used a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover design: n=44 healthy volunteers (order-counterbalanced into two groups of 22) each completed the task battery once under TD and once under a balanced (placebo) amino-acid drink, tested ~5 hours post-ingestion once TD had biochemically taken effect (TD lowered the plasma tryptophan-to-large-neutral-amino-acid ratio; F(1,42)=41.6, p<0.0001). The core task was a two-stage sequential decision task (the Daw two-step task), in which first-stage choices lead probabilistically (70%/30%) to one of two second-stage states, each carrying its own probabilistic monetary outcome. The authors' key methodological extension was running the task in both a reward (monetary gain) version and, in the same subjects, a punishment (monetary loss) version -- allowing a within-subject test of whether serotonin's effect on model-based control depends on outcome valence.

## Key findings relevant to MECH-186

TD produced opposite-signed effects on model-based (goal-directed) choice depending on valence. In the reward condition, TD reduced the model-based weight (omega) relative to placebo -- goal-directed control of reward-seeking degraded toward habitual, model-free responding (F(1,39)=6.93, p=0.012). In the punishment condition, the effect reversed: TD produced a significant outcome-by-transition interaction (the behavioural signature of model-based control) that was present only in the TD condition, not placebo (F(1,21)=12.07, p=0.002) -- i.e. lowering serotonin promoted, rather than degraded, goal-directed avoidance of monetary loss. The authors interpret this valence asymmetry as most likely mediated by TD's effect on the average-reward representation that anchors the model-based/model-free arbitration, rather than a direct effect on planning capacity per se.

## Translation to REE

The valence-dependent double dissociation -- TD degrades goal-directed benefit pursuit while sparing or promoting goal-directed harm avoidance -- is the same qualitative pattern MECH-186 requires, now grounded in the paper actually being cited. In REE terms: depleting the tonic serotonergic floor is predicted to collapse VALENCE_WANTING (benefit terrain legibility) without collapsing VALENCE_HARM_DISCRIMINATIVE (harm terrain legibility). Worbe et al.'s reward-condition result is the human causal-manipulation analog of benefit-terrain collapse toward flat/habitual responding; their punishment-condition result -- goal-directed avoidance intact or sharpened under TD -- is consistent with harm-discriminative terrain not depending on the same floor. Because TD is a real pharmacological manipulation rather than an individual-differences correlation, this is stronger causal evidence for the asymmetry than a purely observational design would provide.

## Limitations and caveats

The paper's own mechanistic account for the asymmetry (altered average-reward representation under TD) is not the same claim as MECH-186's "floor maintains benefit terrain legibility" framing -- the mapping from "model-based reward pursuit degrades" to "VALENCE_WANTING terrain goes flat" is a reasonable functional homology, not a demonstrated identity, and this record's `mapping_caveat` reflects that. The reward-condition effect, while statistically significant, is modest (F(1,39)=6.93). TD is a single ~5-hour pharmacological manipulation in one session per condition, not the sustained chronic exposure that MECH-186's "benefit terrain collapse under chronic harm" scenario describes, so the "chronic" component of the claim remains untested by this source. The task measures discrete monetary reward/loss choice, not the residue-field dynamics REE models directly.
