# Zeng et al. 2024 -- the reasoning capacity is the attack surface

ARC-103's second sentence is the load-bearing one: *recombination and structure-mapping are exactly the machinery that produces persuasive harmful rationalisations*. That is an empirical bet about where danger comes from, and the lesion studies filed alongside this entry cannot test it -- they show what happens when harm sensing is removed, not that argumentative capacity is the thing that exploits its absence. This paper attacks the question from the other side.

Zeng et al. take a taxonomy of persuasion techniques from the social-science literature and use it to generate what they call persuasive adversarial prompts: fluent, interpretable, human-readable arguments for why the model should do the prohibited thing. These are not gibberish suffixes or gradient-optimised token strings. They are the kind of thing a capable arguer would say. Reported attack success rate is over 92% against Llama-2-7b-Chat, GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 across 10 trials, exceeding the algorithm-focused attacks that the field had been benchmarking against, and the authors note that existing defences do not cover this class.

The finding that matters architecturally is the one I put first in `failure_signatures`: robustness to one attack class did not transfer to the argumentative class. Models hardened against optimised adversarial suffixes still folded to a good argument. If a safety mechanism is implemented as a consideration *inside* the reasoning process -- one more thing to weigh -- then a sufficiently good argument is precisely the input that reweighs it. This is what ARC-103 is trying to forbid by construction, and it is why the claim is written as a negative architectural commitment rather than as a training objective.

Two honest limits, both in the record. First, RLHF refusal behaviour is not an embodied harm-sensing channel; it is closer to the kind of mechanism ARC-103 already treats as insufficient. So this paper confirms the failure mode ARC-103 anticipates, but it cannot confirm that ARC-103's remedy would hold -- nobody has built the embodied authority and attacked it. Second, attack-success-rate numbers are judge-dependent and have historically drifted between papers; I read the >92% as 'large and easily reproduced' rather than as a constant worth quoting precisely.

There is also a boundary question with ARC-104 worth flagging, since the two claims carry `possible_overlap_with` on each other. This study is input-side: persuasion arrives in the prompt. ARC-104 owns the language-INPUT update path, ARC-103 the inference OUTPUT. I have tagged it ARC-103 because the vector is argumentative recombination rather than token-level manipulation -- the attack works *by reasoning*, which is ARC-103's subject -- but a governance reviewer could reasonably argue it belongs to both, and the existing `targeted_review_arc_104` directory already holds Wei et al. 2023 and Qi et al. 2025 on the input-side failure modes.

Confidence 0.74. Direct, large, and aimed squarely at ARC-103's threat model; discounted for soft metrics, for testing the wrong safety mechanism, and for the ARC-103/ARC-104 boundary ambiguity.
