# Koenigs et al. 2007 -- lesioning harm sensing makes reasoning more willing to harm

Six patients with focal, bilateral ventromedial prefrontal damage were asked to judge moral dilemmas. The design's power is in its selectivity: the patients were not globally abnormal moral judges. On impersonal moral dilemmas, and on non-moral ones, they answered like everybody else. The abnormality appeared only in the high-conflict *personal* cases -- the ones where a clear aggregate-welfare argument is available but executing it requires an emotionally aversive act of direct harm. There, and only there, the patients endorsed the harmful act at an elevated rate. The authors' reading is that emotion is *necessary* for normal moral judgement in precisely the class of case where reasoning has a persuasive argument for harm.

That last phrase is why this paper belongs to ARC-103 rather than to a general moral-neuroscience file. The claim ARC-103 makes is not that reasoning is bad, nor that harm sensing is more accurate. It is architectural and negative: that symbolic, compositional, and analogical inference must be **structurally subordinate** to embodied harm sensing, because recombination is exactly the machinery that manufactures a good-sounding case for a harmful act. Koenigs et al. is the cleanest available biological instance of what happens when that subordination is removed by ablation rather than by design. The cost-benefit argument does not become *wrong* in these patients -- it becomes *unopposed*. Deliberation, left to itself, did not reconstruct harm avoidance from first principles.

The translation to REE has to be stated carefully, and the caveat in the record is not a formality. What was lesioned in these patients was an affective/somatic valuation channel, and what remained was general deliberative reasoning -- not a symbol-manipulating inference engine of the kind ARC-103 governs. So the paper supports the *motivation* for ARC-103's commitment (an inference system with no independent harm authority will endorse harm when the arithmetic favours it) more strongly than it supports the commitment's *content* (that a harm authority can be given effective veto over inference outputs). Nothing here demonstrates a working veto; it demonstrates the damage done by its absence. That is a real but one-directional kind of evidence, and I have priced it accordingly.

A second limit worth naming: n=6. Focal bilateral VMPFC damage is rare, and this is the canonical cohort rather than an independent replication. The effect has held up across the subsequent literature -- Moretto et al. 2010, filed alongside this entry, reproduces the behavioural direction in an independent Italian cohort and adds the psychophysiological mechanism -- which is the main reason I set source quality at 0.9 rather than lower despite the sample size.

Confidence 0.78. The evidence is causal, selective, and points in the direction ARC-103 asserts. It is held below the 0.8-1.0 band because the mapping from 'affect' to REE's 'embodied harm sensing' is an interpretive step, and because the claim's actual architectural content -- subordination by construction -- is not what this experiment tested.
