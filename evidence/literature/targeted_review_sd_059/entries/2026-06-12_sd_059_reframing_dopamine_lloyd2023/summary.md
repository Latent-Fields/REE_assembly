# Lloyd & Dayan 2023 — reframing punishment as approach-to-safety (MECH-358 approach bonus)

*According to PubMed.* Lloyd K, Dayan P. "Reframing dopamine: A controlled controller at the limbic-motor interface." *PLoS Computational Biology* 19(10):e1011569, 2023. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011569).

This is the computational counterpart to the two rodent/human circuit papers, and it speaks most directly to MECH-358's *approach bonus* — the part of the mechanism that does work once the credit table exists. Lloyd and Dayan are interested in how Pavlovian influences interfere with operant behaviour and how that interference is sometimes accompanied by accumbens dopamine release. Their proposal is that direct manipulation of the dopamine signal is itself an instrument of cognitive control: when instrumental and Pavlovian drives conflict, a controlled "reframing" mechanism recasts the prospect of possible punishment as an opportunity to *approach safety* (and, symmetrically, recasts possible reward loss). They operationalise this and fit it to rodent behaviour from two active-avoidance/omission datasets in which accumbens dopamine was recorded, and show the model reproduces key features of dopamine transients at the discriminative cues.

The mapping to MECH-358 is almost on the nose at the level of computational role. MECH-358's approach bonus is, under future threat, a per-candidate *negative* (favoured) E3 score-bias toward credited escape-affordance classes — formally `-clamp(approach_gain * threat_scale * combined_affordance[class], 0, bias_scale)`. "Recast punishment as an opportunity to approach safety" is the same operation stated in normative language: at the moment the threat cue appears, tilt selection toward the action that leads to safety. The discriminative-cue dopamine transient is the moment the threat-context gate opens and the credited class is favoured — which is also why MECH-358's threat-context gate (bonus is zero when safe) is load-bearing rather than cosmetic.

The caveat is implementation, and it matters for which REE claim this really evidences. Lloyd and Dayan model a *learned, dopaminergic, deliberately deployed* controller; MECH-358 is a non-trainable arithmetic regulator with explicit clamp, gate, and per-tick leak guards. So the support is at the level of "this computational role is sound and biologically grounded," not "this is how it is implemented." If anything, the paper is a closer match to MECH-375 (the trainable `Q_relief` critic that is the learned successor to MECH-358's fixed table) than to the fixed bridge itself — worth noting because it tells us the REE roadmap from fixed table to learned controller has a real neuroscience analogue. Confidence 0.70.
