# Boeke et al. 2017 — striatal credit for instrumental safety in humans (MECH-358 safety half)

*According to PubMed.* Boeke EA, Moscarello JM, LeDoux JE, Phelps EA, Hartley CA. "Active Avoidance: Neural Mechanisms and Attenuation of Pavlovian Conditioned Responding." *Journal of Neuroscience* 37(18):4808–4818, 2017. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3261-16.2017).

If Moscarello & LeDoux 2013 is the rodent suppression story, this is the human translation that gets at the *other* half — the part SD-059 actually adds. The design is a clean contrast: after threat conditioning, one group learns active avoidance (they can prevent the shock through an action) and a yoked group undergoes extinction with shock presentation matched to the active group but without instrumental control. The next day the authors measure threat responses (skin conductance) to the conditioned cues, then run a fresh round of threat conditioning with novel stimuli.

Two findings carry the mapping. First, the yoked-extinction group showed a return of conditioned responding, while the active-avoidance group did not — and the avoidance group also showed *attenuated* conditioning to novel stimuli the next day. Second, the groups differed in striatal BOLD responses to shock omission. The authors read this as the striatum supporting a shift from innate defensive responses toward adaptive behavioural responses when the animal has had control over the threat.

This is the closest direct evidence I found for MECH-358's safety half. The striatal response at shock omission is the response-produced safety credit that the bridge writes when a directed action terminates the threat (`threat_scale <= 0` → credit `safety_affordance[class]`). The persistence and the transfer to novel cues are the behavioural face of SD-059's central move: tag the credited first-action class as an escape affordance that is approachable under *future* threat. And the yoked-extinction null is, if anything, the cleanest validation of SD-059's logic — passive safety exposure, with the same shock statistics but no action-contingent credit, does not confer durable protection. That is precisely the failure SD-059 attributes to a global "avoidance works" scalar with no affordance-indexed action credit (the V3-EXQ-603h shape).

The caveat is that BOLD-at-omission indexes a credit-*like* signal at the circuit level; it does not isolate a per-first-action-class table or the threat-gated approach bonus, which are the specific computational commitments of MECH-358. So this is strong evidence for the *existence and necessity* of an action-contingent safety credit, and human evidence at that, but it under-determines the exact data structure. Confidence 0.74: human within-subject design lowers transfer risk, mapping is good for the safety half, and I held it below 0.8 because the credit is inferred rather than resolved to discrete action classes.
