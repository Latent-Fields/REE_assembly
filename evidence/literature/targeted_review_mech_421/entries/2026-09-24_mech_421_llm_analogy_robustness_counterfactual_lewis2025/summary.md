Lewis and Mitchell tested whether large language models do general analogical reasoning or lean on similarity to their training data. They built variants of letter-string analogies, digit matrices and story analogies that probe the same abstract structure but are unlikely to resemble pre-training text, and compared humans and GPT models on the originals and the variants. Human performance stayed high on the letter-string variants; the GPT models' performance fell sharply.

This speaks to the built-in versus emergent question because the strongest artificial evidence for emergence without explicit structure-mapping is the LLM result (Webb, Holyoak and Lu 2023, already in the corpus under Q-074). If that capacity collapses when the surface is made unfamiliar, it lacks the property MECH-421 defines analogy by: mapping relational structure regardless of surface features.

The reach is limited. It is about text-trained language models, and newer or reasoning-tuned models may behave differently. It does not show that emergence from a structured relational map would fail. What it offers REE is a test requirement: any Q-074 emergence arm has to transfer on surface-novel and counterfactual pairs, not only on familiar ones, or it can pass for the wrong reason.
