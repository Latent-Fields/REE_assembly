Webb, Fu, Bihl, Holyoak and Lu built visiPAM, which takes representations learned directly from naturalistic images and applies a similarity-based analogical mapping operation taken from cognitive theories of reasoning. With no training on the task, it outperformed a state-of-the-art deep network on an analogical mapping benchmark and matched the pattern of human performance on a new task that required mapping 3D objects across unrelated categories.

This bears on both MECH-421 and Q-074. It supports structure-mapping as the operation that delivers transfer across dissimilar inputs. For the build-vs-emerge question it points toward a hybrid: learned representations plus an explicit mapping step did better than a system that had to learn mapping end to end. It is worth noting because the same authors reported emergent analogy in GPT-3 (already in the corpus under Q-074); this paper shows them choosing an explicit mapper when they want reliable zero-shot mapping.

The limits are real. Part correspondence between objects is a much shallower relational structure than the claim's examples, the comparison is against a single deep-learning baseline, and nothing here involves cognitive-map traversal.

Confidence 0.5, direction mixed: supportive of structure-mapping as the right operation, and a point against expecting it to emerge without a mapping step.
