# Calcagno, Pennisi, Proietto Salanitri, Sorrenti, Palazzo, Spampinato & Bellitto (2026) -- Dream2Learn: Structured Generative Dreaming for Continual Learning

**Verification note:** peer-reviewed, published in the International Journal of Computer Vision, vol. 134, issue 8, article 388, doi 10.1007/s11263-026-02958-1. Verified 2026-09-03 via CrossRef (title, authors, journal, volume/issue/article number all matched) and cross-checked against the identical content on its arXiv preprint (2603.01935, posted 2 Mar 2026), which supplied the full abstract and a limitations discussion the publisher page did not surface without a login wall.

## What the paper did

Dream2Learn asks how a continual-learning classifier can generate its own useful training experience without simply reconstructing what it has already seen. Rather than the standard generative-replay move of trying to recreate past data, the authors pair a classifier with a frozen diffusion model and drive it, via soft prompt optimization, to produce "dreamed" samples: novel, semantically coherent classes that are consistent with what the classifier already knows but were never actually observed. The crucial design decision, and the one most relevant to REE, is what happens to these dreamed samples afterward: they are not added to any persistent memory buffer. Only the prompts that generated them are retained, in what the authors call a persistent dream inventory. The dreamed content is used once, to expand and reorganize the classifier's representation space, and then it is gone as a stored artifact -- the generated data are explicitly not used to replace memory. Tested on Mini-ImageNet, FG-ImageNet, and ImageNet-R, the method beats rehearsal-based continual-learning baselines and, notably, achieves positive forward transfer -- prior dreaming makes the network better at learning genuinely new future tasks, not just better at not forgetting old ones. The authors are candid about one imperfection: because the diffusion model doing the dreaming was pre-trained on a broad prior, some of what it generates as "novel" can actually leak information about classes the classifier has not seen yet -- measured at 3.93% of class replacements -- and they propose a concept-erasure mitigation rather than claiming the problem is fully solved.

## Findings relevant to MECH-094

MECH-094 is REE's functional requirement that simulation-mode content must not accumulate in the viability map as committed, real experience -- because when that distinction fails, the result is confabulation, simulated events encoded as if they had actually happened. Dream2Learn's architecture, arrived at for entirely different (ML engineering) reasons, embodies almost exactly this principle: dreamed samples are explicitly excluded from the persistent buffer; only prompts survive as committed artifacts, and the generated content itself is used transiently to reshape representation without ever being written as though it were accumulated real data. That is a genuinely independent instance of MECH-094's core architectural commitment, arrived at from a completely different direction. At the same time, the paper's whole point is to actively mine generated content as a productive, ongoing learning driver -- not merely to tolerate simulation as an occasional, tightly-gated exception the way MECH-094's own carve-out (MECH-322) does. Whether a cognitive agent should treat generated self-training this permissively is a question MECH-094 does not itself answer, and Dream2Learn's clean success in a classification setting does not resolve it for REE's very different concern (confabulation as a clinical/functional failure mode, not classification accuracy). The measured leakage rate is, if anything, the most directly relevant finding: even a system explicitly engineered to keep the simulated/real, novel/known boundary clean leaks across it at a small but non-zero, measured rate -- a concrete illustration, from an unrelated domain, of exactly the kind of boundary failure MECH-094 exists to prevent.

## REE mapping and limitations

The domain gap here is real and should not be minimised: an image classifier reorganizing its representation space via diffusion-generated samples has no analogue of REE's committed-experience/viability-map distinction, no clinical confabulation framing, and no default-mode-network write-profile machinery whose failure modes MECH-094 was built to describe. The correspondence that holds is at the level of a shared design principle -- keep generated content structurally useful without letting it masquerade as accumulated real experience -- not at the level of a shared mechanism or a shared failure mode. This is exactly why the entry is recorded as mixed rather than cleanly supporting: the architecture's separation-by-design is a genuine, convergent instance of MECH-094's principle, while the paper's broader use case (mining dreams as an active training driver) and its own leakage finding are each, in different ways, either orthogonal to or a mild caution against treating generated content as unproblematically safe.
