# Bisimulation metrics: value-relevant compression, formalized (Ferns, Panangaden & Precup 2011)

**Source.** Ferns, N., Panangaden, P. & Precup, D., *Bisimulation Metrics for Continuous Markov Decision Processes*, SIAM Journal on Computing 40(6):1662-1714, 2011.

**What the paper does.** This is the continuous-state extension of the authors' earlier finite-MDP bisimulation metric work, and is the canonical formal reference for the construction. A bisimulation (pseudo)metric assigns each pair of MDP states a distance built from two ingredients: the difference in their immediate rewards, and a Wasserstein (earth-mover) distance between their next-state transition distributions -- with the metric appearing recursively inside its own distance-between-distributions term, defined as the fixed point of a contraction mapping. The headline theoretical result is that this metric bounds the difference in optimal value between two states: states that are bisimulation-close are provably close in value, which licenses using the metric to aggregate or abstract a state space for tractable planning without materially damaging value-based decision-making.

**Why it matters for MECH-520.** MECH-520's own registration names bisimulation metrics as external precedent alongside value-equivalent models, and this is the standard citation for the construction. Bisimulation is, at its core, a REWARD/VALUE-relevant compression scheme: two states that differ only in ways irrelevant to reward and future value-relevant transition structure are collapsed together, by design. This lands in the same conceptual territory as MECH-520's diagnosis -- a representation objective built around reward/value alone treats non-value-relevant distinctions as noise to be discarded.

**Why this is marked mixed, in the same way as the value-equivalence entry.** The bisimulation literature treats this collapsing property as the point, not a hazard: it is offered as a principled way to make planning tractable by throwing away exactly the state detail that doesn't matter for decision-making. It is not presented, in its own literature, as a warning about representational degeneracy that needs a countervailing predictive constraint. MECH-520 needs the reader to see the SAME formal move (collapse states that don't differ in reward/value-relevant ways) as risky when applied without limit, which is not the bisimulation literature's own framing -- much as with value equivalence, this is a citation that supplies the right formal mechanism while arguing, in its home literature, for the opposite conclusion about whether that mechanism is desirable.
