# Predictive objectives admit collapse too -- it's the dynamics, not the objective, that saves you (Tang et al. 2023)

**Source.** Tang, Y., Guo, Z.D., Richemond, P.H., Avila Pires, B., Chandak, Y., Munos, R., Rowland, M., Gheshlaghi Azar, M., Le Lan, C., Lyle, C., Gyorgy, A., Thakoor, S., Dabney, W., Piot, B., Calandriello, D. & Valko, M., *Understanding Self-Predictive Learning for Reinforcement Learning*, ICML 2023, PMLR 202:33632-33656. [arXiv:2212.03319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03319).

**What the paper does.** Self-predictive representation learning trains an online encoder so that a predictor network, applied to the online encoding, can forecast a target network's encoding of a future observation or state (the BYOL/SimSiam family of self-supervised objectives, imported into RL representation learning). The paper asks a question that sounds almost paradoxical: this objective has, among its global optima, a fully collapsed solution -- map every input to the same constant vector, and prediction loss trivially hits zero, with no informational content preserved at all. Empirically, well-designed instances of self-predictive learning do NOT collapse to this degenerate solution. The paper's contribution is explaining why: through a combination of theoretical analysis (in simplified/linear settings) and empirical validation, the authors identify that collapse is avoided specifically because of the OPTIMIZATION DYNAMICS used in practice -- the predictor network is updated faster than the representation, and gradients flow into the representation as semi-gradients (effectively stop-gradient-like, not full backpropagation through the target) rather than as full gradients. Under these dynamics, the authors show the learning process implicitly performs something like a spectral decomposition of the underlying state-transition structure, landing far from the collapsed optimum even though that optimum is reachable in principle.

**Why it matters for MECH-520 -- and why the finding cuts both ways.** MECH-520 claims that requiring predictive sufficiency across a temporal spread supplies the constraint a pure value objective lacks, holding representation open against collapse. This paper is the most mechanistically direct test of that claim found in this pull, because it studies exactly the failure mode MECH-520 is worried about (representational collapse) under exactly the kind of remedy MECH-520 proposes (a predictive objective) -- and finds the remedy is not automatic. A predictive loss, taken purely as a functional form, admits the same collapsed global optimum a value-only objective does; what actually prevents collapse in the self-predictive family is specific optimization-dynamics machinery (predictor-speed asymmetry, semi-gradient flow) layered ON TOP of the predictive framing, not the predictive framing by itself. This is a genuine complication for a naive reading of MECH-520 -- "add a predictive term and collapse is solved" is not quite right, at least not in this closely analogous setting.

**The REE-specific echo worth naming.** MECH-520's own registration cites SD-070 as the strongest existing evidence the collapse hazard is real for REE specifically: z_world was empirically measured collapsing to a participation ratio of ~1.06 at world_dim=128, and the fix that worked was NOT prediction alone but a predictive/classification loss PLUS an added VICReg-style variance/covariance penalty. That is exactly the shape of finding this paper reports in a different but closely analogous setting: the predictive term alone was not enough; an additional dynamics-shaping ingredient was needed to actually deliver the anti-collapse property. Read together, SD-070 and this paper triangulate on the same refinement to MECH-520: the claim as written ("predictive obligation... holds perception open") may need to be understood as "predictive obligation, correctly optimized, holds perception open" -- with the optimization-dynamics half doing real, non-optional work.

**Mapping limits.** This is a self-predictive (BYOL-style, predict-your-own-future-latent-via-a-target-network) architecture, which differs structurally from MECH-520's framing of predictive obligation against explicit future observations or world-state across the ARC-004 temporal spread. Whether the SPECIFIC collapse-avoidance mechanisms identified here (predictor-speed asymmetry, semi-gradient updates) transfer to a differently-structured predictive-sufficiency constraint is not established by this paper and would need its own check.

**Confidence.** 0.65. Source quality is high (rigorous ICML paper combining formal analysis with empirical validation, strong author group). Mapping fidelity is the highest of the three MECH-520 entries in this pull -- this is genuinely on-topic, not merely analogous -- but the finding complicates rather than cleanly confirms MECH-520's claim as literally stated, which the confidence and mixed direction both reflect.

**Lit-only verdict, not governance.** This is the strongest and most direct evidence in this pull bearing on MECH-520, and it is a genuinely mixed result: it confirms predictive objectives CAN prevent the collapse a value-only objective would produce, while also showing that outcome is conditional on optimization dynamics rather than guaranteed by the objective's form alone. Any future governance pass on MECH-520 should treat "predictive obligation prevents collapse" as needing the qualifier "given the right training dynamics," not as unconditionally true -- and should note SD-070's own need for an added variance/covariance penalty as REE's own instance of exactly this qualifier.
