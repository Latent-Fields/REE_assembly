# Koenigs, Young, Adolphs et al. (2007) -- "Damage to the prefrontal cortex increases utilitarian moral judgements"

## What the paper did

This is a classic causal-lesion study of moral cognition. The authors tested six patients with focal, bilateral, adult-onset damage to ventromedial prefrontal cortex (VMPC) -- a brain region already implicated in general emotional and social processing -- on a battery of moral dilemma vignettes, alongside brain-damaged and neurologically normal comparison groups. The vignettes varied in whether they pitted aggregate welfare (saving a larger number of lives) against a highly emotionally aversive personal action (e.g. directly causing one person's death to save several others).

## Key findings relevant to ARC-121

VMPC patients produced an abnormally "utilitarian" pattern of judgment specifically on the high-conflict dilemmas -- they were more willing than controls to endorse the emotionally aversive action to maximize aggregate welfare. Critically, their judgments on OTHER classes of moral dilemma (lower-conflict cases) were normal. This selectivity is important: it is not a global collapse of moral reasoning, but a specific deficit tied to the dilemmas that require integrating an emotionally aversive personal cost against an aggregate benefit -- precisely the kind of case where, per the companion Shenhav & Greene (2010) finding in this same directory, VMPC is recruited as part of a domain-general valuation computation.

## Translation to REE

Where the fMRI evidence in this pull is correlational (VMPC activity co-occurs with moral judgment), this lesion study supplies the causal complement: damaging the shared valuation/emotion hub produces a predictable, selective deficit in a specific class of moral judgment. This is meaningful support for ARC-121's claim that harm/ethics evaluation genuinely DEPENDS ON shared circuitry rather than merely co-activating alongside it -- if harm/ethics evaluation instead had its own fully independent, dedicated substrate, damage to the general valuation hub would not be expected to disrupt it this way.

## Limitations and caveats

The authors' own interpretive framing centers on VMPC's role in generating emotions -- "particularly social emotions" -- rather than a generic domain-general VALUE signal per se. This is a related but not identical account to ARC-121's "shared epistemic-state object" framing: it is possible the mechanism here is specifically about emotional generation being disrupted, rather than a shared abstract valuation/belief state being disrupted, and this study cannot fully distinguish those readings. The selectivity of the deficit (normal judgment on other dilemma classes) is also a caution against reading this as showing that ALL harm/ethics evaluation routes through this one hub -- it appears to be specifically the emotionally-loaded, high-conflict cases.

## Confidence reasoning

Strong source quality: a classic, highly-cited causal lesion study that remains a standard reference in moral-cognition neuroscience. Mapping fidelity is moderate -- the paper's own emotion-centric framing is adjacent to but not identical with ARC-121's shared-epistemic-state claim, and the selective nature of the deficit limits generalization, which is why this is treated as a valuable causal complement to Shenhav & Greene (2010) rather than a standalone confirmation.
