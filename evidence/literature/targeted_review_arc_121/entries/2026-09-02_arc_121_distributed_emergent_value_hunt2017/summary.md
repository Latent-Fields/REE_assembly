# A distributed, hierarchical and recurrent framework for reward-based choice

Hunt & Hayden (2017, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*) argue against the standard decomposition of
reward-based choice into distinct component processes that are serial and functionally localised --
valuation here, comparison there, action selection downstream. In its place they propose that choice
emerges from repeated computations distributed across many regions, supported by mutual inhibition in
recurrent networks and by the hierarchical organisation of processing timescales across cortex. The
claim that bites hardest is their last: on this account, certain correlates of value are *emergent
rather than represented explicitly in the brain*.

This entry is marked `mixed`, and the classification is not a hedge -- the paper genuinely cuts both
ways for ARC-121, and forcing it to one pole would misrepresent it.

It supports ARC-121 against the modular alternative. Building valuation and commitment as an isolated,
functionally localised module with private state is exactly the arrangement this review argues biology
does not use. That is the arrangement ARC-121 says REE is moving away from, and it is close to the
claim's own stated falsifier -- continuing to add local, mechanism-private representations. On that
axis the paper is on ARC-121's side.

It weakens ARC-121's object-realism. If value correlates are emergent properties of distributed
recurrent dynamics rather than explicit representations, then an apparent "shared epistemic-state
object" may be an observer's summary statistic over distributed computation rather than a thing the
system maintains. ARC-121 would then be a true description of REE's *analysis vocabulary* and a false
one of REE's mechanism -- and the difference matters, because the claim is meant to license design
decisions.

The uncomfortable implication is worth stating explicitly, because it is the most useful thing this
entry contributes. ARC-121's assertion and ARC-121's stated falsifier both presuppose that
representations are explicit and localisable enough to be counted as shared or private. Hunt & Hayden
deny that presupposition for the value construct. A framing whose affirmation and whose negation share
a premise that the evidence may not support is not straightforwardly adjudicable -- which is a
testability problem for ARC-121 as currently written, and one that the claim's own note ("not directly
falsifiable by a single experiment") acknowledges only partially. If ARC-121 is to be sharpened, this
is the seam to sharpen it at: state what observable difference obtains between a materially shared
object and a distributed computation that admits a shared-object description.

Caveats. The scope is reward-based choice and the value construct specifically, not knowledge,
uncertainty or possibility. The emergence argument is an interpretive stance on existing recordings
rather than a decisive experiment, and it is presented as an Opinion article. And there is a real
disanalogy: whether an explicit shared object is *required* or merely convenient in an engineered
system is a separate question that a review of biological recordings cannot settle. An engineered
substrate may legitimately make explicit what biology leaves implicit.

Confidence 0.60 -- the two directions partly cancel in what they license REE to conclude, and an
Opinion piece arguing for an interpretive reframing is weaker evidence than primary data. Transfer
risk 0.45 is the highest in this pull, for the engineered-versus-biological disanalogy above.
