# Knowing how much you don't know: a neural organization of uncertainty estimates

Bach & Dolan (2012, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*) take a literature that had grown up around
divergent theoretical assumptions -- risk, ambiguity, volatility, expected and unexpected uncertainty,
estimation uncertainty about parameters versus uncertainty about state -- and integrate those concepts
into a single decision-making framework in order to ask how the brain actually encodes them. Their
conclusion is the one that bears on ARC-121: the available evidence indicates that distinct neural
encoding of uncertainty, including summary-statistic-type representations, occurs in *distinct neural
systems*.

This is the clearest direct counter-evidence in the pull, and it is scored highest among the
non-supporting entries because its target is precise rather than adjacent. ARC-121 names uncertainty
explicitly as a constituent of the shared epistemic-state object it says REE's mechanisms are
converging on. Bach & Dolan report that in the biological case this constituent is plural, not
singular: different functional kinds of uncertainty are carried by different systems, and the
distinctions are drawn along theoretically motivated lines rather than incidental anatomical ones.
A REE design that collapses risk, ambiguity, volatility and estimation uncertainty into one
undifferentiated uncertainty slot inside a shared object would therefore not be simplifying -- it
would be departing from a known organisation, and would lose distinctions the biology apparently
maintains for a reason.

The REE-relevant reading has two branches, and it is worth being explicit that they differ in how much
they cost ARC-121. The cheaper branch: the shared object survives but must be *typed and multi-slot*
rather than carrying a single uncertainty field -- a refinement, not a refutation. The more expensive
branch: uncertainty is the constituent at which the convergence framing breaks first, and its
plurality is a symptom of the general case rather than an exception. Nothing in this paper settles
which branch is right; noting that the question exists is the contribution.

The caveat that keeps this at 0.72 rather than higher is a genuine one and should not be treated as
politeness. Anatomically distinct encodings do not entail functionally private state. Several systems
could each compute a different uncertainty statistic and still write into one shared, jointly readable
object -- an arrangement fully consistent with ARC-121, and one the anatomical evidence as reported
cannot exclude. The review is also from 2012, which places it before much of the entorhinal and
hippocampal generalisation work that the supporting entries in this pull rest on; it is not the
field's last word on whether these representations are as separate as they then appeared. And its
scope is human and animal decision neuroscience, with nothing bearing directly on engineered
substrates.

Confidence 0.72. Mapping fidelity 0.8 is the second-highest here because the paper's subject matter is
a constituent ARC-121 names verbatim rather than a neighbouring construct. Source quality 0.85 for
venue and integrative scope, discounted for being a review and for its age relative to the supporting
literature.
