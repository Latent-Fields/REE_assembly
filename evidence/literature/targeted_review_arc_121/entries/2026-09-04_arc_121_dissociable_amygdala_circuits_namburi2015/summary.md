# Namburi, Beyeler, Yorozu et al. (2015) -- "A circuit mechanism for differentiating positive and negative associations"

## What the paper did

This paper is included deliberately as the counterweight this literature gap needed: rather than asking whether reward and threat evaluation share a representation, it asks the opposite question and measures a genuine dissociation. Using optogenetic circuit-specific manipulation combined with ex vivo electrophysiology, the authors identified two anatomically distinct populations of neurons within the basolateral amygdala (BLA), defined by their projection target: neurons projecting to the nucleus accumbens (NAc-projectors) and neurons projecting to the centromedial amygdala (CeM-projectors).

## Key findings relevant to ARC-121

These two populations are not just anatomically distinct -- they are functionally and synaptically opposed. Following fear or reward conditioning, NAc-projectors and CeM-projectors undergo OPPOSING synaptic plasticity. Causally, photostimulating NAc-projectors supports positive (reward-like) reinforcement, while photostimulating CeM-projectors supports negative (fear-like) reinforcement. Most tellingly, photoinhibiting the CeM (fear-coding) population produces a genuine double dissociation with a competitive edge: it both impairs fear conditioning AND enhances reward conditioning simultaneously -- exactly the signature of two circuits competing for the same behavioral output, not two circuits reading from one shared upstream state.

## Translation to REE

ARC-121 claims that harm/ethics evaluation, alongside prediction, replay, planning, and learning, converges on consuming ONE shared epistemic-state object rather than maintaining its own private computational stream. Namburi et al. supply exactly the kind of counter-evidence this gap's brief asked to be sought explicitly: at the amygdala's input stage, positive (appetitive) and negative (aversive/threat) evaluation are computed by anatomically SEPARATE, synaptically OPPOSING, and causally competitive circuits -- not by one shared representation differentially read out. This is a genuine complication for a strong reading of ARC-121.

## Limitations and caveats

Two things temper how far this should be read as a refutation. First, this dissociation is measured at the amygdala's INPUT stage -- upstream of the vmPFC/ventral-striatum "common currency" hub implicated in the supporting entries of this pull (Bartra 2013, Shenhav 2010, Koenigs 2007). A staged architecture is entirely plausible: dissociable circuits could compute positive vs. negative associations early, with a shared representation forming only later, at the point of integration into an actual decision (including a moral one) -- this paper cannot adjudicate that possibility, only establish that dissociation genuinely exists somewhere in the pathway. Second, this is rodent Pavlovian fear/reward conditioning, not human moral or ethical judgment -- the leap from "BLA circuit-level dissociation between simple fear and reward learning" to REE's E3/harm-goal-error construct and to explicit ethics evaluation is substantial.

## Confidence reasoning

High source quality: a landmark, widely-cited Nature paper with strong causal circuit dissection (not merely correlational recording). Mapping fidelity is moderate: it is precisely the right KIND of measurement for testing ARC-121's crux (dissociable vs. shared), but the anatomical stage and species are one and two steps removed, respectively, from the human vmPFC-integration and moral-judgment level the supporting entries in this same pull address -- which is why both directions are recorded honestly rather than one being allowed to dominate the record.
