# Thalamo-amygdalar safety transmission for remote fear extinction (Silva et al. 2021)

**Source:** Silva BA, Astori S, Burns AM, Heiser H, van den Heuvel L, Santoni G, Martinez-Reza MF, Sandi C, Graff J. *A thalamo-amygdalar circuit underlying the extinction of remote fear memories.* Nature Neuroscience 2021;24(7):964-974. [doi:10.1038/s41593-021-00856-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-021-00856-y) (PMID 34017129).

## What the paper did

Combining viral tracing, neuronal activity mapping, fiber photometry, chemogenetics and closed-loop optogenetics in mice, the authors traced the circuit that attenuates consolidated fear. Extinction of remote (30-day-old) fear memories -- but not recent (1-day-old) ones -- depends on nucleus reuniens (NRe, midline thalamus) inputs to the basolateral amygdala (BLA). NRe-to-BLA inputs activate during remote extinction and are potentiated as fear declines; both total NRe and monosynaptic NRe-to-BLA activity rise shortly before freezing cessation. Pathway-specific inhibition impairs and activation facilitates remote extinction. The authors conclude the NRe registers and transmits safety signals to the BLA -- the first functional description of the circuit underlying attenuation of consolidated fear.

## Why it bears on the claim (MECH-304)

MECH-304 posits a cue-specific conditioned-safety prediction -- "this stimulus actively predicts that suffering will be omitted" -- whose expression pathway gates the release of avoidance/fear, trained by relief-completion (MECH-302) events. Silva supplies causal, bidirectional evidence for exactly this *active, transmitted* safety signal: a discrete circuit whose output suppresses an established threat representation, with the safety signal rising just before fear release. This is the cleanest available demonstration that safety prediction is its own functional system that acts *on* the fear circuit, not a byproduct of low harm and not the reward-circuit offset reinforcement of relief. It strengthens the active/cue-specific pole (MECH-304) just as Corches 2019 strengthens the contextual/passive pole (MECH-303), and both sit apart from MECH-302 relief.

## Limitations and caveats

The anatomy is the main caveat. MECH-304 names a cortico-striatal encoding substrate with an IL-to-CeA expression pathway; Silva implicates a midline-**thalamic** relay (reuniens) to BLA. The finding therefore *supports the premise* of an active transmitted safety signal that gates fear release while *flagging that REE's named safety anatomy is incomplete* -- a thalamic relay is missing from MECH-303/304. There is also a time-since-encoding dependence (remote but not recent fear) that no current REE safety mechanism represents; this is a candidate enrichment, not a contradiction. The work is rodent (with human imaging corroboration cited), so the suffering-stream mapping is at the substrate-premise level.

## Confidence

0.80, supports. Very high source quality (causal, bidirectional, multi-method), moderate mapping fidelity (active-safety-transmission premise strongly supported; the specific thalamic anatomy differs from MECH-304's wording), low-moderate transfer risk. The net reflects strong support for the *existence and causal role* of active safety prediction, with an explicit flag to broaden the substrate.
