# Prefrontal ensembles of context-safety signals (Corches et al. 2019)

**Source:** Corches A, Hiroto A, Bailey TW, Speigel JH 3rd, Pastore J, Mayford M, Korzus E. *Differential fear conditioning generates prefrontal neural ensembles of safety signals.* Behavioural Brain Research 2019;360:169-184. [doi:10.1016/j.bbr.2018.11.042](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbr.2018.11.042) (PMID 30502356).

## What the paper did

Using activity-dependent neuronal ensemble tagging in mice, the authors compared prefrontal (mPFC) populations recruited by conditioned fear versus generalized fear versus successful fear discrimination. They report quantitatively distinct activation in response to conditioned and generalized fear, and modulation of ensembles tied to acquisition of context-safety contingencies. The prelimbic (PL) subdivision monitors context-danger associations to conditioned fear, whereas differential conditioning sparks *additional* ensembles in both PL and IL associated with the inhibition of generalized fear. Their interpretation: fear discrimination is driven by gradual updating and rebalancing of prefrontal memory representations, and the prefrontal cortex carries an explicit representation of context-safety, not merely the absence of a threat representation.

## Why it bears on the claim (MECH-303)

MECH-303 posits a context-bound *passive* safety representation -- "this environment predicts absence of suffering" -- with a prefrontal-hippocampal substrate (IL for expression gating). Corches grounds the prefrontal locus directly and, more importantly for the differentiation question, demonstrates that **safety is an active, separately-coded prefrontal representation** rather than a low value on a single harm scalar. The PL-codes-danger / IL-and-PL-code-safety-discrimination structure is the neural form of the relief-vs-safety dissociation: a *prospective inhibitory predictor* of threat-absence, anatomically and computationally distinct from the NAc/VTA offset-reinforcement event that grounds relief (MECH-302; Bergado Acosta 2017 in the sibling entry). It complements the prior pull's Kreutzmann 2020 (IL required for safety *expression*) and Meyer 2019 (vHipp-PL conditioned inhibition) by adding ensemble-resolution evidence that danger-monitoring and safety-discrimination are separable populations.

## Limitations and caveats

The paradigm is *differential* fear conditioning -- learning to discriminate a safe-but-similar cue from a dangerous one -- which is closer to cue/context conditioned inhibition than to MECH-303's stated pure passive-exposure-without-harm update rule. In that sense the paper sits between MECH-303 (contextual passive) and MECH-304 (cue-specific conditioned inhibition); it most strongly supports the shared premise that prefrontal safety is an active learned representation. It does not test the vHipp-PL contextual store MECH-303 names, nor the diffuse slow update dynamics. So it confirms the *substrate and the active-representation premise*, not MECH-303's specific learning rule.

## Confidence

0.74, supports. Good source quality (ensemble-resolution mapping with behavioural discrimination), moderate-high mapping fidelity to the prefrontal safety substrate, moderate transfer risk. The discount reflects the gap between discriminative-conditioning safety (what was tested) and passive-contextual safety (MECH-303 as written).
