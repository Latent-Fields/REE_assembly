# The lateral habenula and its input to the RMTg mediates outcome-specific conditioned inhibition (Laurent, Wong & Balleine, 2017)

The two recording studies in this directory tell us the aversive signal exists and is correctly signed. This one is the causal complement, and it is the entry that does most of the argumentative work for MECH-053's *architectural* claim.

Laurent and colleagues trained rats on instrumental choice with Pavlovian conditioned inhibitors -- cues signalling the *absence* of a particular outcome. In intact animals, an outcome-specific conditioned inhibitor biased choice away from the action delivering that outcome and toward actions earning other outcomes. They then removed the lateral habenula, and separately used pathway-selective ablation to remove only those LHb neurons projecting to the rostromedial tegmental nucleus. Both manipulations abolished the bias. The conclusion the authors draw is the one that matters here: the LHb is critical for **negative** predictors to affect choice, and not for positive ones.

That asymmetric dissociation is what MECH-053 needs and what no recording study can supply. MECH-053 does not merely claim that REE should register aversive prediction error; it claims that the aversive gate should be structurally *separate* from reward-like precision weighting, so that "negative prediction error is not absorbed into a single scalar objective". The strong test of a separation claim is ablation: knock out the putative aversive channel and the appetitive control should survive. It does. A single shared valuation channel with a sign bit would not behave this way -- lesioning it would degrade both directions.

Now the caveat, which is substantial enough that I have kept mapping fidelity at 0.74 rather than higher. The inhibition demonstrated here is **learned and outcome-specific**. The animal has acquired, over training, that a particular cue predicts the absence of a particular outcome, and choice shifts accordingly. MECH-053 describes something faster and more generic: a gate keyed on harm-PE *magnitude* that "can interrupt or delay commitment before deep evaluation consolidates a trajectory". Those are not obviously the same mechanism. It is entirely possible that LHb->RMTg supports a slow, specific, learned inhibitory pathway, and that the fast generic interrupt MECH-053 wants either lives elsewhere or does not exist. The shared substrate is suggestive, not dispositive. Recorded as a failure signature rather than buried in prose, because if REE implements an indiscriminate harm-PE veto and it behaves badly, this is the paper that predicted the mismatch.

The other limitation is methodological and standard: lesion and ablation establish necessity for a behavioural outcome but say nothing about timing or pipeline position. MECH-053's distinctive commitment is *where in the commitment sequence* the gate sits, and no lesion study can address that. Across all five entries in this pull the same gap recurs -- representation (Matsumoto 2009), behavioural sign (Lawson 2014), causal necessity and separation (this paper), and plasticity (Wang 2017) are all evidenced, while the pre-consolidation timing is evidenced by none of them. That convergence is itself informative: the timing limb is `complex (probe-gated)` and wants a REE experiment varying harm-PE arrival relative to trajectory consolidation, not further literature.

Confidence 0.80. Source quality 0.86 -- pathway-selective ablation, behavioural dissociation, both sexes used. Transfer risk 0.35, the highest in this pull, reflecting both the rodent-to-REE distance and the learned/outcome-specific character of the effect.
