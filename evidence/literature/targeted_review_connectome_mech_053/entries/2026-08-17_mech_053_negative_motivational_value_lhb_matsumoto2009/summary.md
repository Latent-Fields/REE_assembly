# Representation of negative motivational value in the primate lateral habenula (Matsumoto & Hikosaka, 2009)

This is the paper that made the lateral habenula a control-plane structure rather than a curiosity. Matsumoto and Hikosaka conditioned macaques in two deliberately separated Pavlovian contexts -- one where the available outcomes were rewards of differing size, one where the available outcomes were punishments (airpuff) of differing likelihood -- and recorded single units in LHb. The population was most strongly excited by whichever conditioned stimulus predicted the *most unpleasant event available in that context*. It was excited by punishment delivery itself and inhibited by reward delivery itself, with both effects sharpest when the outcome was less predictable.

The finding that matters for MECH-053 is not simply "LHb responds to bad things". It is that the same population is driven in opposite directions by aversive and appetitive events, and that its excitation tracks *predicted* negative value rather than delivered pain. That is the signature of a signed prediction-error channel with its own polarity, sitting upstream of the dopaminergic system rather than inside it. MECH-053 asserts exactly this architectural separation: a fast aversive PE gate that keeps aversive salience distinct from reward-like precision weighting, rather than letting negative prediction error be absorbed into one scalar objective. The primate data say that biology made the same separation.

Where the mapping gets thinner is on the *gating* half of the claim. MECH-053 says the gate "suppresses or vetoes commitment when negative-valence or harm prediction errors spike" and "biases control-plane readiness and veto thresholds so that aversive signals can interrupt or delay commitment before deep evaluation consolidates a trajectory". This paper is a recording study. It establishes that the signal is present, correctly signed, and predictive; it infers behavioural control from LHb's projections to dopaminergic and serotonergic targets rather than demonstrating it. I have kept `evidence_direction` at `supports` because the representational claim is genuinely load-bearing for MECH-053's channel-separation argument, but the confidence sits at 0.82 rather than higher precisely because the veto function is asserted here and shown elsewhere (see the Laurent 2017 and Lawson 2014 entries in this directory, which carry the causal and the behavioural-suppression halves respectively).

One structural caveat deserves to be recorded rather than smoothed over, because it is the kind of thing that bites when a mechanism is implemented. The LHb signal in this study is **context-relative**: neurons responded to the worst thing available *in the current context*, not to an absolute harm magnitude. A REE gate that fires on an absolute harm-PE threshold would therefore not be reproducing the primate mechanism -- it would be reproducing a caricature of it. Whether REE wants the context-relative version is a genuine design question, not a detail; a context-relative gate is scale-free and never saturates, but it also means the gate will fire in a benign context on the least-benign option available, which is either a feature (vigilance) or a pathology (needless commitment suppression) depending on what else is running.

Confidence decomposition: source quality is high (0.92) -- Nature Neuroscience, primate single-unit, and the result has been replicated and extended for fifteen years. Mapping fidelity is 0.72: faithful for channel separation, indirect for commitment gating. Transfer risk is 0.30 -- primate-to-REE is a comparatively safe direction for a control-plane architectural claim, and the residual risk is less about species than about the representational-to-causal step.
