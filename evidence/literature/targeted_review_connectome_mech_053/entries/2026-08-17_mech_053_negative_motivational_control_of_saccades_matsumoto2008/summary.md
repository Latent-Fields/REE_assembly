# Negative motivational control of saccadic eye movement by the lateral habenula (Matsumoto & Hikosaka, 2008)

This is the weakest source in the MECH-053 pull and I have included it deliberately, for one narrow reason that the other four cannot supply.

It is a four-page review in a *Progress in Brain Research* proceedings volume, summarising the Hikosaka lab's macaque oculomotor work. The claim it makes is that the lateral habenula transmits reward-related signals to dopamine neurons -- principally to *inhibit* them -- and that in doing so "the lateral habenula suppresses less rewarding saccadic eye movements". The interesting part is the endpoint. The causal chain described runs all the way from an LHb signal to whether a *particular saccade is executed or not*. Not to a mood, not to a global motivational tone, not to a learning rate: to the fate of one specific motor commitment.

MECH-053 asserts that its gate "biases control-plane readiness and veto thresholds so that aversive signals can interrupt or delay commitment". The granularity implied there is per-candidate-action. Every other entry in this directory establishes something about the *signal* -- that it exists (Matsumoto 2009), that it is causally necessary for negative predictors to shift choice (Laurent 2017), that it predicts behavioural suppression (Lawson 2014), that it is shaped by learning (Wang 2017). None of them establishes that an LHb-type signal can operate at the granularity of a single action-in-preparation. This one does, and that is why it is here.

Now the two reasons its confidence is 0.62 and its source quality 0.55. First, and this is the important one: **the suppression described is of LESS-REWARDING saccades**. The driving variable is an appetitive value comparison, not an aversive prediction error. If this entry were read as direct evidence that harm-PE vetoes actions, it would be performing exactly the conflation MECH-053 exists to forbid -- collapsing aversive gating into reward-magnitude gating and thereby into "a single scalar objective". I have recorded that explicitly as the first failure signature, because it is the misreading a future governance pass is most likely to make when it sees "habenula suppresses action" in an index row. What this paper licenses is the claim that the LHb->DA pathway *can* veto at action granularity. It does not license any claim about what triggers the veto.

Second, it is a short review of primary work reported elsewhere. It should not be weighted as an independent empirical observation, and if a later pull retrieves the underlying primary papers (Matsumoto & Hikosaka's 2007 *Nature* report and its successors) this entry should be superseded by them rather than counted alongside them.

So: a narrow, honestly-discounted entry, included because the action-granularity question is otherwise unevidenced in this pull, and flagged so that its narrowness survives contact with the index.
