# Learning shapes the aversion and reward responses of lateral habenula neurons (Wang et al., 2017)

This is the entry in the MECH-053 pull that argues with the claim, and it is the one I would want read most carefully.

Wang and colleagues used fiber photometry and electrophysiology in freely-behaving mice to ask how LHb neurons respond to genuinely aversive stimuli and to reward, and how conditioning reshapes those responses. Bitterness, pain and social attack by aggressors all intensively excited LHb neurons. Aversive conditioning then induced activation by aversion-predicting cues **within a few trials**. Sucrose reward, by contrast, *inhibited* LHb neurons -- and inducing inhibition by reward-*predicting* cues required prolonged conditioning and high reward probability.

Take the supporting half first, because it is the strongest evidence in this entire pull for a word in MECH-053 that the other four entries leave completely unevidenced: **fast**. MECH-053 asserts a "fast aversive prediction-error gate" that can act "before deep evaluation consolidates a trajectory". Every other paper here speaks to whether the aversive signal exists, is signed correctly, is causally necessary, or predicts behaviour -- none of them speaks to its speed relative to the appetitive system. This one does, directly and quantitatively: a few trials for aversive cue acquisition, prolonged conditioning plus high reward probability for the appetitive counterpart. That asymmetry is exactly what a system would look like if it privileged aversive signals for rapid interrupt while letting appetitive learning consolidate slowly. It is good evidence, and it arrived from a paper I selected for a different reason.

Now the complicating half. The same LHb population is **bidirectional** -- excited by aversive events, inhibited by reward. MECH-053 says the gate "keeps aversive salience distinct from reward-like precision weighting". What does *distinct* mean there? On an implementation reading, it means two channels: a harm-PE pathway that does not carry appetitive information. This paper is hard to reconcile with that -- the neurons carry both. On a representational reading, it means one signed channel whose aversive and appetitive limbs have different dynamics and feed different downstream consequences, and which is not collapsed into a scalar objective. This paper supports that reading well.

I want to be plain that this ambiguity is a defect in the **claim**, not in the paper. MECH-053's current wording does not adjudicate between the two readings, which means the same experimental result reads as `supports` or as `weakens` depending on which reading the assessor happened to arrive with. That is precisely the condition under which a literature entry generates the illusion of evidential movement without any actual constraint being added. I have therefore set `evidence_direction` to `mixed` rather than picking a side, capped mapping fidelity at 0.68 with the reason attributed to the claim rather than the source, and recorded the disambiguation as the mapping caveat.

The governance-actionable consequence is small and cheap: MECH-053 should say which separation it means. If it means functional asymmetry within a signed channel, this paper is straightforward support and the entry should be re-read as `supports` on a later pass. If it means two disjoint channels, this paper is a real constraint and REE would be building something the mammalian substrate does not do. Either answer is fine; the current silence is what costs. That disambiguation is `complicated (buildable)` -- it is a wording decision by whoever owns the control-plane architecture doc, not a probe.
