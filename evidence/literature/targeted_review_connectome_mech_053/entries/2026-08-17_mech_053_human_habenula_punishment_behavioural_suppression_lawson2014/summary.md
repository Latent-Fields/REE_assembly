# The habenula encodes negative motivational value associated with primary punishment in humans (Lawson et al., 2014)

Of everything pulled for MECH-053 this morning, this is the paper whose result maps most directly onto what the claim actually asserts. Lawson and colleagues used high-resolution fMRI targeted at the habenula while healthy adults learned, trial by trial, which cues predicted painful electric shocks and which predicted monetary reward. They fitted a computational reinforcement-learning model to extract the dynamically updating value of each cue, then asked whether habenula BOLD tracked those values -- and, crucially, whether it predicted what participants *did*.

Both halves came out. Habenula responses were positive to the changing values of shock-predicting cues, and those responses predicted **behavioural suppression of responding** to those cues. On the appetitive side the sign flipped: negative habenula responses to reward-cue value predicted behavioural **invigoration**. MECH-053 asserts a fast aversive prediction-error gate that "suppresses or vetoes commitment when negative-valence or harm prediction errors spike", and asserts that this be kept structurally distinct from reward-like precision weighting. This study delivers, in humans, both the suppression-of-action link and the signed bidirectionality that the separation implies. The Matsumoto & Hikosaka entry in this directory establishes that the signal exists and is correctly signed in primate LHb; this one establishes that in humans the signal predicts action being held back.

The honest caveats are two and they are both about what fMRI cannot do. First, resolution: the habenula is a very small structure, and even high-resolution acquisition cannot cleanly dissociate *lateral* from *medial* habenula. MECH-053 is named for the lateral habenula and inherits its anatomical specificity from the LHb literature; this paper cannot independently confirm that specificity, and a reader who takes it as doing so is over-reading it. Second, and more consequentially for REE: MECH-053's distinctive content is a matter of **timing**. The claim is not merely that aversive signals reduce action; it is that they "interrupt or delay commitment *before deep evaluation consolidates a trajectory*". That is a fast, pre-consolidation interrupt. BOLD has neither the temporal resolution nor, in this trial-structured design, the analytic leverage to say anything about it. The observed behavioural suppression is response-rate suppression accumulated over trials -- entirely compatible with a slow value-tracking signal that biases responding, which is a materially weaker mechanism than the one MECH-053 posits.

So what should this change? It should move MECH-053's *functional* limb -- aversive PE reduces committed action, and does so through a channel with opposite sign to the appetitive one -- from inference to evidence, in humans, with behaviour as the dependent variable. It should not be allowed to discharge the *architectural* limb about where in the commitment pipeline the gate sits. That limb remains, in the work-graph vocabulary, `complex (probe-gated)`: it needs a probe with timing resolution, which in REE's case means an experiment that varies when the harm-PE spike arrives relative to trajectory consolidation and checks whether early spikes veto while late ones merely bias. That is a buildable REE experiment, and it is a better use of effort than another literature pass.
