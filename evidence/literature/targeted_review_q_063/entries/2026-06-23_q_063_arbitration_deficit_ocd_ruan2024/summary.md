# Disorders of compulsivity: Deficits in arbitrating learning strategies

According to PubMed. Source: Ruan, Liu, Liu, Yang & Peng (2024), *Addiction Biology* 29(8):e13433. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/adb.13433)

## What the paper did

This study goes after a question that maps almost exactly onto Q-063: when compulsivity shows up as an imbalance between goal-directed and habitual control, is that because one *system* is impaired, or because the *arbitration mechanism* -- the meta-controller deciding which system is in charge at any given moment -- is impaired? Nineteen alcohol-use-disorder patients, thirty OCD patients, twenty major-depression patients, and matched controls performed a two-choice, three-stage Markov decision task, and their behaviour was fitted independently with model-based and model-free reinforcement-learning models. The dissociation is the payoff. Alcohol-use-disorder and OCD patients both showed *less* model-based strategy choice in conditions where model-based control was optimal -- a single-system reading. But only OCD patients showed *higher* behavioural-control system switching in conditions where model-free use was optimal -- they kept switching strategies when they should have settled, a signature of the arbitration mechanism itself misfiring. Major-depression patients showed neither abnormality. The authors conclude that dysfunction in arbitration control between the dual systems, not merely impairment of one system, underlies compulsivity.

## Why it speaks to Q-063

Q-063's hard part is the second limb: can REE tell a loop that persists because the goal is genuinely valued from a loop that persists because the *decommit-friction / threshold landscape is biased* (MECH-369)? This paper is the closest empirical analogue I found to that limb. It shows, in humans, that compulsive persistence can be driven by an abnormality in the *control/arbitration layer* that is dissociable from the value computation -- exactly the "field-state-driven stickiness" that Q-063 wants to separate from "value-driven persistence." The system-switching abnormality is a behavioural fingerprint of a control-landscape bias that is decoupled from the value of any particular goal. That is real-world support that REE's conjecture is not exotic: stickiness can live in the arbitration/threshold machinery rather than in z_goal.

The discriminant control group is what lifts this above a generic compulsivity result. Major-depression patients, who also ruminate and perseverate, did *not* show the arbitration deficit. That matters for Q-063 because it warns the diagnostic must do more than detect persistence -- it must distinguish *compulsive* stickiness (arbitration/decommit-friction bias) from other sources of a persistent loop. A diagnostic that fired on any repeated behaviour would fail the very discrimination this paper demonstrates is possible.

## The caveats I want on the record

The paper's "arbitration mechanism" is a model-based/model-free meta-controller, which is not literally REE's slow-modulator field biasing the decommit-friction landscape. The mapping is functional -- a control-layer bias that produces persistence independent of value -- rather than mechanistic; REE's MECH-369 is a specific implementational hypothesis this study neither confirms nor rules out. And the design is correlational across patient groups, not a causal manipulation: it shows the arbitration deficit *co-occurs* with compulsivity, not that biasing arbitration *causes* a trapped loop. Q-063's 2x2 (goal value high/low x modulator bias absent/present) is precisely the causal manipulation this literature cannot do in humans but a REE substrate could -- which is rather the point of the open question.
