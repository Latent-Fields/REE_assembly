# Model-Based Control in Dimensional Psychiatry

According to PubMed. Source: Voon, Reiter, Sebold & Groman (2017), *Biological Psychiatry* 82(6):391-400. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2017.04.006)

## What the paper did

This is a review, and a well-cited one, that lays out the two-systems account of decision-making and connects it to compulsivity across diagnoses. The core distinction: goal-directed (model-based) control is prospective and rests on a learned internal model, so it makes decisions by reference to the *current* value of the affective outcome; habit (model-free) control integrates previously reinforced learning and is, in the authors' words, "autonomous of the current outcome value" -- implicit, computationally cheaper, but inflexible. The review describes and compares the tasks that tap these constructs (sequential model-based/model-free tasks, outcome-devaluation and slips-of-action paradigms), extends the framing to Pavlovian processes, and argues that a shift toward model-free control -- whether through a model-based deficit or a failure to deploy it -- is a transdiagnostic mechanism underlying compulsive behaviours and a candidate therapeutic target.

## Why it speaks to Q-063

Q-063 asks how REE could distinguish a high-value, legitimately-repeated goal from a slow-modulator-trapped compulsive loop -- a loop that persists because the decommit-friction landscape is biased rather than because the goal is genuinely valued. The entire premise depends on value-driven persistence and stickiness being *separable* in the first place, and that is exactly what this literature establishes. The model-based / model-free dissociation is the field's mature operationalisation of precisely the contrast Q-063 needs: a valued repeat is outcome-sensitive (model-based, value tracks z_goal), whereas a habitual/compulsive loop is outcome-insensitive (model-free, persists when value would predict stopping). The single most useful import is the outcome-devaluation logic: the marker of stickiness is that behaviour continues after the goal has been devalued. That is the conceptual sibling of Q-063's proposed test -- z_goal salience high in the valued-repeat cell but at-or-below baseline in the trapped cell, with the loop persisting regardless.

So this paper supports the foundational assumption and hands Q-063 a vocabulary and a task family that are already validated in human and animal psychiatry. It tells us the question is well-posed: the dissociation REE wants to make is one that real behavioural neuroscience makes routinely.

## The caveats I want on the record

Two. First, this is a conceptual review, not a direct test of REE's specific mechanism. Q-063's hypothesis is narrower and more particular than "model-free dominance": it proposes that the stickiness is driven by a *slow-modulator bias on the decommit-friction / threshold landscape* (MECH-369), and it pairs the diagnostic with an offline-integration leg (MECH-272/273 sleep-cluster reintegration reducing loop dwell-time without erasing legitimate residue). This review speaks to none of that machinery directly; the model-free construct is broader than "modulator-trapped," and a behaviour can be model-free for reasons that have nothing to do with a modulator bias. Second, the review is emphatic that the shift is *transdiagnostic and graded*, not categorical -- which is a gentle warning against Q-063's 2x2 framing tempting us into a false dichotomy. Valued and trapped may be ends of a continuum, and a good diagnostic should report where on it a loop sits, not just which box.
