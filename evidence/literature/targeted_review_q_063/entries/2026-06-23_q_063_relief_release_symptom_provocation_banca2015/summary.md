# Imbalance in habitual versus goal-directed neural systems during symptom provocation in OCD

According to PubMed. Source: Banca, Voon, Vestergaard et al. (2015), *Brain* 138(Pt 3):798-811. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awu379)

## What the paper did

Most studies of habit-versus-goal imbalance in OCD use abstract learning tasks. This one is unusual and, for Q-063, unusually useful: it provokes the actual symptom and watches the compulsive cycle unfold in real time. Fifteen OCD patients and fifteen controls underwent an individually-tailored live symptom-provocation fMRI paradigm, with the provoking stimuli graded across neutral, intermediate, and strong intensities, and with synchronous video-recorded behavioural avoidance responses. Critically, the design let the participant choose to reject or terminate the provoking stimulus, so the authors could separately analyse four phases of the cycle: symptom provocation, the urge to avoid, rejection, and *relief*. Under strong provocation, patients showed a dichotomous pattern absent in controls and in milder conditions -- a deactivation of caudate-prefrontal (goal-directed) circuits accompanied by hyperactivation of subthalamic-nucleus and putaminal (habitual) regions. The putaminal hyperactivation during provocation *preceded* its own deactivation during the avoidance and relief events, and effective-connectivity analysis put the ventromedial-prefrontal/orbitofrontal cortex at the hub modulating the whole circuit.

## Why it speaks to Q-063

Two things make this the entry I most wanted in the Q-063 set, even though its confidence is the lowest of the three. First, the stickiness limb: it shows a compulsive loop proceeding *while the goal-directed (value) circuitry is deactivated*. That is a vivid neural instance of exactly the trap Q-063 is trying to diagnose -- persistence with the value system offline. It also delivers the most important failure signature for REE's own diagnostic: if you read only z_goal / value and you find it at baseline, you might conclude the loop is low-value, when in fact the value circuit is simply *not engaged* and the loop is being driven from the habitual/threshold side. Absent z_goal is not the same as low value, and this paper is the cleanest illustration of why a value-only readout would misclassify a trapped loop.

Second, and more specifically, the relief event. Q-063's proposed diagnostic leans on the SD-034 closure operator -- the idea that in a valued repeat the closure operator *fires and releases*, whereas in a trapped loop it fails to fire (or fires without releasing). Banca and colleagues model precisely a provocation -> urge -> avoidance -> *relief* sequence, and find the putaminal trajectory that carries it. That provocation-to-relief arc is the biological cousin of REE's commit -> dwell -> release trajectory. The constructive reading for Q-063 is that the *release/relief transition* is itself a measurable event, and its presence or absence is a candidate discriminator: a loop that never produces a relief/release signature is one held by decommit-friction rather than discharged by value.

## The caveats I want on the record

This is correlational neuroimaging during symptom provocation; it does not manipulate value and stickiness independently, so it cannot, on its own, make the 2x2 dissociation Q-063 ultimately needs. The "relief" it measures is the subjective and neural cessation of an aversive urge, which maps onto REE's closure-operator *release* only by structural analogy -- it is not the same variable, and I do not want the mapping over-sold. The sample is also small (fifteen patients), as live-provocation designs tend to be. So I treat this as supplying the *shape* of the diagnostic Q-063 should build -- separately model the release event, do not infer value from an offline value circuit -- rather than as evidence that the dissociation has been achieved.

## Confidence

I put this at 0.60, supporting. A respected venue (*Brain*) and a genuinely novel ecological design (source quality 0.70), but small, correlational, and mapping to the closure-operator release event by analogy rather than measurement (mapping fidelity 0.55, transfer risk 0.40). The lowest-confidence entry in the set, and deliberately so -- but the one that best tells REE how to instrument the release leg of the Q-063 diagnostic.
