# Universal Principles in the Repair of Communication Problems (Dingemanse et al., PLoS ONE 2015)

## What the paper did

Twelve languages, eight unrelated language families, varied typological profiles, and a large corpus of naturally occurring informal conversation coded by conversation-analytic methods. The target was *other-initiated repair*: the sequence in which the recipient of an unclear message signals that something has gone wrong, and the sender then repairs the original.

Two findings. First, frequency: other-initiated repair occurs on average about once every 1.4 minutes of conversation, and it does so in every language sampled. Second, and more striking, structure: unrelated languages share the same three functionally distinct types of repair initiator -- an open type ("huh?"), a restricted request ("who?"), and a restricted offer ("did you mean X?") -- and deploy them in the same kinds of contexts. This ran against a then-prevailing assumption of radical cultural variation in conversational practice.

## Why this matters for ARC-099

ARC-099's inventory has fourteen items and they are not equally well-motivated. Shared world model, object tokens, joint attention, partial observability -- these are easy to defend as preconditions. *Repair* is the one I would expect a sceptical reviewer to push on first, because there is an obvious alternative story: repair is what a language develops once it has enough structure to be misunderstood in interesting ways. On that story repair is a consequence of bootstrap, not a precondition of it, and its place on the list is a category error.

Dingemanse et al. make that story hard to hold. A mechanism firing once every 1.4 minutes is not an occasional corrective; it is a substantial fraction of the interactional work being done. And the cross-family invariance of the *three-way functional typology* -- not merely "all languages have some way to ask for clarification", but the same three distinguishable moves used in the same circumstances -- is the kind of convergence that usually indicates a structural constraint rather than a shared inheritance or an accident.

There is a second contribution to ARC-099 that I think is more immediately useful than the first. The claim currently lists "repair" as a bare token. It does not say what satisfying that condition would consist of, which means the V6-entry gate cannot actually check it. This paper supplies the missing specification: not "the channel supports correction", but a channel carrying at least three functionally distinct initiator types to which the sender's response differs. That is checkable. A REE channel with a single undifferentiated "I did not understand" signal would satisfy the letter of the current condition and fail this one, and I think this one is right.

## Limitations, and the one that actually bites

The direction of time. This is a corpus study of *mature languages in ongoing use*. It establishes beyond much doubt that repair is universal in the endpoint. ARC-099 asserts that repair is a *precondition of the bootstrap*. Those are different propositions, and nothing in this paper distinguishes them from the alternative that repair co-evolves with -- or emerges out of -- an already-functioning signal system.

This matters more than the usual endpoint-versus-origin caveat, because of what ARC-099 is *for*. It is a gate. If repair is in fact an emergent consequence of signalling rather than a precondition, then the gate blocks a V6 probe on a capacity the probe was supposed to generate. That is a false negative at the gate, and for a contract whose stated purpose is preventing vacuous probes, a false negative is the more expensive error: a vacuous probe wastes a run, a false negative at the gate can stall a whole tier indefinitely while looking like diligence.

The lesser caveat is substrate distance, and it is the largest in this pull. The corpus is human spoken conversation, with prosody, gaze, gesture and a turn-taking system doing enormous amounts of coordinating work. ARC-099's "low-cost signalling channel" has none of that. Whether the three-way initiator typology survives a channel that thin is an open empirical question, and it is not obvious to me that it does -- the open type in particular ("huh?") may depend on prosodic contour in ways a discrete symbol channel simply cannot carry.

## Confidence

0.71, `supports`. Source quality 0.86: a genuinely impressive cross-linguistic effort, open access, from the group that defined the area, with headline numbers that have held up since. Mapping fidelity 0.60 and transfer risk 0.42 -- the highest transfer risk here -- both driven by the same two gaps: endpoint-versus-precondition, and rich multimodal human conversation versus a minimal artificial channel. I have scored it `supports` rather than `mixed` because it does genuinely strengthen the case for repair's membership in the inventory and it sharpens the condition into something checkable; the timing gap weakens the inference but does not point the other way.
