# Four of nine primitives already have device-level physical affordances

ARC-089 commits REE to a primitive vocabulary that is at once software-expressible now and legible as a future physical-substrate affordance. This review is the best current evidence that the second half of that is not wishful. It is also, read carefully, a reasonably precise measure of how much of the vocabulary is currently legible -- which is less than half, and not the important half.

What the hardware actually does. Memristance is defined as instantaneous resistance depending on the time integral of applied voltage or current: the device's present response is a function of its own history, physically, with no state variable being updated by anything. That is history-dependent deformation as a material property. Threshold-switching memristors placed in parallel with a capacitor oscillate under constant input, with spike frequency scaling linearly with input current -- oscillatory propagation, natively. Their volatility gives leaky integration with autonomous return to rest, which is multi-timescale dynamics in the device rather than in a differential equation someone is solving. Their switching is intrinsically stochastic, arising from atomic migration, thermal noise and electron correlation -- stochastic transition, again as physics. VO2 devices have been shown to produce more than twenty distinct spiking behaviours including bursting, frequency adaptation and subthreshold oscillation.

The review also makes ARC-089's central distinction explicit in a way I did not expect from a device paper. It contrasts a memristive neuron that exhibits Hodgkin-Huxley dynamics physically, at sub-picojoule energies and under 30 ns latency, with a digital system computing the same model at roughly 1200 floating-point operations per timestep. That is instantiate versus execute, stated as an engineering fact with a number attached. It is the cleanest empirical anchor I have found for what ARC-089 means when it says a substrate should be evaluated on whether it can instantiate the dynamics rather than merely run the software.

Now the limit, which I think is the more important finding for governance. Count the primitives the review covers: persistent state, history-dependent deformation, multi-timescale field dynamics, oscillatory propagation, stochastic transition. Five, generously, of nine. Now count what it does not touch: residue as deformation of the future action landscape, commitment as a real boundary between simulated and released action, offline reintegration, and attractor transitions in any sense richer than device multistability. Those are the primitives that distinguish REE's vocabulary from a generic neuromorphic wishlist that any lab in this field would write down. The covered set is the set everyone already agrees on. So this paper supports the legibility of ARC-089's vocabulary precisely where the vocabulary is least distinctive, and is silent where it is most.

Two hardware caveats worth carrying forward. Device variability is the field's headline unsolved problem -- cycle-to-cycle resistance scatter from stochastic filament growth, and device-to-device variation in switching delay, threshold voltage and retention. This creates an awkwardness specific to ARC-089: stochastic transition is on REE's primitive list as a desideratum, and in this hardware it is indistinguishable from a manufacturing defect. A criterion that is also a fault mode cannot discriminate substrates without a further account of which stochasticity is the wanted kind. And endurance spans four orders of magnitude across material systems, from a few hundred cycles to over a trillion, so "memristive substrates can host these dynamics" is not a claim about memristors as a class but about particular materials.

Confidence 0.55. The evidence is real and directly on point for the physical-legibility half of the claim, but partial in coverage, silent on the distinctive primitives, and separated from cognifold-level dynamics by several organisational levels that nothing here shows are bridged.
