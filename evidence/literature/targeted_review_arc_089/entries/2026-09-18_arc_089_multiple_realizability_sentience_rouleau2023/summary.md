# Multiple realizability supports the premise and complicates the promise

Rouleau and Levin give ARC-089 its conceptual warrant and then hand it its sharpest objection, which is why this entry is marked mixed rather than supporting.

The warrant first. The paper sets out multiple realizability and substrate independence with unusual clarity -- a function is substrate-independent if it can be achieved without contingency on a specific structure, material or medium -- and argues the case across living systems and beyond, from non-neural biological computation to engineered media. ARC-089's premise is exactly this: that the cognifold can be specified in a vocabulary that does not presuppose silicon or neurons, and that such a specification is meaningful rather than empty. On that premise the paper is squarely supportive, and it does the useful work of showing the position is defensible in the mainstream neuroscience literature rather than only in philosophy of mind.

The objection is the measurement problem, and it lands on the part of ARC-089 that is doing the actual work. The claim is not merely that the cognifold is multiply realizable; it is that a future physical substrate should be evaluated by whether it can *instantiate* these dynamics rather than merely *execute* modular software. That is an instantiate-versus-simulate distinction. Rouleau and Levin argue there are no methods to directly measure mental acts or states -- only behavioural correlates support inference. If the only access is behavioural, then a substrate that simulates the behavioural signature of the primitives and one that instantiates them are, by construction, not distinguishable by the compass. ARC-089's whole point is to make that distinction operative.

How much should this worry us? Less than it first appears, and the honest answer requires splitting the primitive list. Several of the nine are *physically* specified and directly measurable in a device: persistent state, history-dependent deformation, multi-timescale dynamics, oscillatory propagation, stochastic transition. You can put a probe on a substrate and establish these. The measurement objection does not touch them. But the primitives that carry REE's distinctive content -- residue as deformation of the future action landscape, commitment as a real boundary between simulated and released action, offline reintegration -- are *functionally* specified, defined by their role in a cognitive economy rather than by a physical signature. Those inherit the problem in full. So the objection is real but partial, and it partitions ARC-089's vocabulary into a measurable half and a half that will need behavioural inference exactly as the paper describes.

Two further cautions from the paper worth carrying into any future use of this compass. It argues humans are poor at recognising intelligence at unfamiliar scales or in unfamiliar problem spaces -- which is a warning that a human-authored "substrate-neutral" vocabulary may quietly encode the authors' own substrate intuitions. And it states that the minimal conditions for realising cognitive function remain unknown, which sits awkwardly beside a fixed list of nine primitives presented as evaluation criteria. Neither is fatal. Both argue for holding the vocabulary revisable rather than treating it as settled, which is consistent with ARC-089's candidate status.

Confidence 0.58. A good source, genuinely bearing on the claim in both directions, but conceptual rather than empirical, and aimed at sentience rather than at dynamical organisation -- so part of its force dissipates on the way across.
