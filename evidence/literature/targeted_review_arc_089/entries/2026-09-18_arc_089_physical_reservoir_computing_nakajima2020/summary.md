# Physical reservoir computing as precedent for dynamical-affordance substrate evaluation

Nakajima's perspective paper is the clearest available statement of the methodological move ARC-089 wants to make. In physical reservoir computing you do not port an architecture onto a device; you take a physical system whose intrinsic dynamics are already rich -- a photonic cavity, an analog circuit, a soft silicone body, a bacterial culture -- perturb it with input, and read out from its high-dimensional state. The computation is not something the substrate *runs*. It is something the substrate *is doing*. Only the readout is trained. Candidate substrates are then admitted or rejected on a dynamical specification: does the system have a high-dimensional state you can measure, does it respond nonlinearly to input, does it have fading memory?

That is precisely ARC-089's proposed stance, arrived at independently and for engineering rather than architectural reasons. It matters for the claim because the obvious objection to a substrate-independent primitive vocabulary is that it is a category error -- that "can this material instantiate history-dependent deformation?" is not a well-formed engineering question. Physical reservoir computing is an existence proof that it is well-formed, and that a field can be organised around asking it.

So what exactly does this license? Only the form of the move, and I want to be careful not to let the paper carry more than that. RC's admission criteria are a list of three, and REE's is a list of nine. The gap is not a matter of degree. A photonic reservoir satisfies every RC criterion and has nothing resembling a commitment boundary between simulated and released action, nothing resembling offline reintegration, and no residue that deforms a future action landscape. If ARC-089's vocabulary were built on the RC template it would admit substrates that cannot host a cognifold at all -- which is the failure mode worth naming, because the claim's stated purpose is to be a compass for *evaluating* future substrates, and a compass that points everywhere is not one.

There is a sharper tension too, and it is worth flagging rather than smoothing over. Fading memory -- the requirement that the influence of past input decays -- is close to canonical in reservoir computing, and it runs against REE's persistence and residue primitives, where the entire point is that history does *not* wash out but survives as structural deformation of the future landscape. A substrate optimised to satisfy the RC criterion would be optimised against one of REE's. That is a genuine cross-current, and it suggests the primitive vocabulary ARC-089 is committing to keep must be written without borrowing RC's list, even though it borrows RC's method.

One further limitation, minor but honest: physical reservoir computing in practice still trains its readout in conventional digital hardware. The exemplar systems are hybrid. So even the field that best demonstrates "instantiate, don't execute" does not fully achieve it, which is a reasonable caution against treating the instantiate/execute distinction as cleanly binary when ARC-089 is eventually used to judge a real device.

Confidence 0.62. The source is strong and the methodological precedent is real and directly on point, but ARC-089 is an architectural commitment and the honest question is how much of REE's specific content this underwrites -- which is: the stance, yes; the vocabulary, not at all.
