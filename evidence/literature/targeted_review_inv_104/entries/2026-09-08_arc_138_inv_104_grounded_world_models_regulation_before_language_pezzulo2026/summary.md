# Pezzulo et al. 2026 -- grounding before language, argued rather than shown

arXiv 2607.13560, submitted 15 July 2026. One correction first: the title is "Grounded world models in biological organisms and future embodied AI", not "...future artificial intelligence" as the thought's bibliography and the ARC-138 stub both record. Worth fixing when either is next edited.

The content is as represented. Pezzulo, Cisek and colleagues argue that generative and embodied AI systems are built on passive predictive learning in which linguistic regularities form the scaffold and other modalities are attached to it, while biological intelligence runs the other way: world models acquired through interaction provide the semantic scaffold, and language attaches to that. They name four things missing from current embodied AI -- intrinsic dynamics as the foundation for learning, action as what aligns those dynamics with the world, autonomous open-ended experience over passive assimilation, and early predictive and control mechanisms scaffolding reasoning, planning, imagination and mind-reading. Allostatic control and emotion appear as integral rather than as decoration.

For ARC-138 this is welcome company: the same thesis, argued independently by a senior group. But two things need saying plainly. It is a perspective and a preprint, so it is an argument rather than evidence, and nothing in it measures a developmental ordering. And it defends the grounding-before-language half of ARC-138's chain, not the regulation-before-interaction step, which is the link that makes ARC-138 distinctive rather than a restatement of embodied-cognition doctrine. A citation of this paper as support for the full five-term ordering would overstate it.

For INV-104 the transfer is narrower still and I have scored it accordingly. If the semantic scaffold is built by interaction, then which distinctions a compression must preserve is set by the organism's own regulatory and action demands rather than by a generic information criterion. That is INV-104's organism-relevance premise, and it is consistent with this paper, but the paper does not test it. Supports at 0.55.
