# Relief learning is distinguished from safety learning by the nucleus accumbens

**Source:** Mohammadi, Bergado-Acosta & Fendt (2014), *Behavioural Brain Research*. [DOI: 10.1016/j.bbr.2014.06.053](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbr.2014.06.053)
**Claim tested:** Q-067 (relief/safety decomposition of the escape-affordance bridge)
**Evidence direction:** supports · **Confidence:** 0.74

According to PubMed, this study asked a question that is structurally identical to the one Q-067 poses for REE: when an organism learns about an aversive episode, is the credit it assigns to *the cue that ended the pain* (relief) the same thing as the credit it assigns to *a cue that reliably signals the pain will not come* (safety), or are these two different learning processes? The authors trained rats in either a relief paradigm (a cue presented at the offset of footshock, which later acquires appetitive-like properties) or a safety paradigm (a cue explicitly unpaired with shock), and temporarily inactivated the nucleus accumbens with the GABA-A agonist muscimol during the learning session. Accumbens inactivation abolished relief learning but left safety learning intact. The dissociation is the result: relief and safety are not two labels for one mechanism; they recruit at least partly non-overlapping circuitry.

This matters for Q-067 because the claim's whole premise — that when the fixed first-action-class escape bridge is insufficient, the missing structure can be *relief-only*, *safety-only*, or *both-required*, and that a trainable successor (MECH-375 Q_relief, the action/cessation-contingent escape-credit term; MECH-376 P_safety, the prospective threat-absence predictor) should train only the weak component first — only makes sense if relief and safety are genuinely separable functions. If they were a single underlying quantity, splitting them into two trainable heads would be a redundant re-parameterisation, and "train the missing half first" would be incoherent. Fendt's group shows the separation is real at the level of the substrate, not just the task description. That is the biological warrant REE needs to justify building Q_relief and P_safety as distinct successors rather than one escape-value channel.

Where the paper stops short is exactly where Q-067's discriminator lives. It establishes that the two learning types are dissociable; it does not rank them — it does not tell us which one is the rate-limiting piece when a directed-escape policy fails. Q-067's answer key ("relief-only missing" if directed escape fails where action-contingent z_harm drop credit is absent but threat-absence is already predicted; "safety-only missing" if commitment-release/approach-to-safety fails while relief credit fires; "both-required" if neither half alone closes) requires a manipulation that holds one component fixed and probes the other under a *committed action* readout. This study uses conditioned startle modulation in rats, an autonomic/reflexive index, not an instrumental escape that the agent commits to and executes. So the mapping is faithful at the "are they separable?" level and weaker at the "which is missing?" level.

I have set confidence at 0.74. The source quality is good — a clean causal inactivation with the correct internal control (safety learning preserved demonstrates the lesion was not a blanket learning deficit). Mapping fidelity is moderate-to-high for the separability premise but does not reach the decomposition discriminator. Transfer risk is the main discount: a rodent startle paradigm to a V3/V4 agent's committed-escape bridge is a real domain jump, and the action-contingency that defines REE's relief term is only indirectly engaged by a Pavlovian-style offset cue. The honest reading is that this paper licenses the *architecture* of the proposed decomposition (two separable successors) without yet adjudicating *which* successor REE should train first — that adjudication still needs the V3-EXQ-603i fixed-first-action-class escape-bridge experiment to complete and be read on its own terms.
