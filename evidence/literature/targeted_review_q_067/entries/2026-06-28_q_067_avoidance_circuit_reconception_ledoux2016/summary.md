# The birth, death and resurrection of avoidance

**Source:** LeDoux, Moscarello, Sears & Campese (2016), *Molecular Psychiatry*. [DOI: 10.1038/mp.2016.166](https://doi.org/10.1038/mp.2016.166)
**Claim tested:** Q-067 (relief/safety decomposition of the escape-affordance bridge)
**Evidence direction:** supports · **Confidence:** 0.62

According to PubMed, this review revisits the oldest theory of avoidance — Mowrer's two-factor account, in which a Pavlovian conditioned stimulus first acquires fear and then any behaviour that *reduces* that fear is instrumentally reinforced. The authors document why "fear reduction" became a liability: it is a vague, global, introspective construct that the neuroscience of the intervening decades does not actually support. Their proposed repair is the part that matters for Q-067. They argue the reinforcing event underlying instrumental avoidance "should be conceived in terms of cellular and molecular events in specific circuits rather than in terms of vague notions of fear reduction," and they separate defensive reactions (freezing), instrumental actions (avoidance) and habits into distinct, dissociable circuits operating non-consciously.

Q-067 is, read in these terms, a concrete instance of exactly the move LeDoux and colleagues recommend. REE's fixed escape-affordance bridge currently treats the thing it is optimising as a single lumped "harm/fear reduction" signal. The claim proposes to stop doing that — to decompose the reinforcer into an action-contingent relief-credit term (MECH-375 Q_relief) and a prospective threat-absence safety predictor (MECH-376 P_safety), and to attribute each to its own trainable channel rather than tuning one escape value. The review's central thesis — that the lumped reinforcer is conceptually broken and should be replaced by mechanism-specific components — is precisely the rationale for that decomposition. It is the theory-side warrant that complements the experiment-side warrant from Mohammadi et al. (2014, relief/safety are neuronally separable) and the model-side warrant from Zhang et al. (2018, the relief arm is a real RL term).

I weight this entry the most cautiously of the three, and I want to be explicit about why. It is a review, not an experiment: it argues for mechanism-specific decomposition in general but runs no contrast that isolates relief from safety, and it certainly does not tell us which component is rate-limiting when REE's bridge is insufficient — the actual discriminator Q-067 needs. Its primary taxonomy (reactions vs actions vs habits) is also not identical to REE's axis (relief vs safety); it is adjacent. So I am crediting it for licensing the *form* of the decomposition — "do not optimise one lumped reinforcer; split it by mechanism" — and not for adjudicating the split.

Confidence is 0.62. Source quality is good (authoritative authorship, high-visibility venue) but the evidence is conceptual, which caps how far it can move a governance decision. Mapping fidelity is moderate: the decomposition rationale transfers cleanly, the specific relief/safety partition only partially. Transfer risk is the highest of the three entries because we are importing a theoretical reframing across both species and the reactions/actions/habits-to-relief/safety boundary. The fair summary across all three Q-067 entries: the literature solidly supports *that* relief and safety are separable and *that* the relief arm is a learnable RL term, and a leading review supports *decomposing* the escape reinforcer rather than lumping it — but none of them resolves Q-067's "which half is missing first," which remains an experimental question for the V3-EXQ-603i bridge run.
