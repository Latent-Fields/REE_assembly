# Blischke & Malangré (2016) — chunk concatenation in a complex arm-movement sequence

This is a secondary, element-level reanalysis of an earlier dataset (Malangré et al. 2014) in
which participants learned a 15-element pegboard sequence via massed arm-movement practice, then
were retested after either a night's sleep or an equivalent daytime waking interval. The
reanalysis strips out each element's own difficulty (its "reference" movement time) to isolate
what the authors call chunk concatenation cost — the extra time a movement takes specifically
because it sits inside a longer sequence, as opposed to being performed alone. That
context-specific cost dropped after sleep but not after wake, meaning the sequence became more
fluent to execute as a whole, without claiming anything about new chunks being formed.

Its value to MECH-490 is mainly as a second, independent confirmation of the same
formation-versus-fluency split found in the Bottary companion entry, in a structurally different
motor task — continuous arm movement rather than discrete keypresses — which reduces the risk
that the dissociation is an artefact of one particular paradigm. It says nothing, however, about
sleep *stage*: there is no polysomnography here, so it cannot be read as supporting (or
undermining) the nREM2/spindle attribution from the Bottary entry. It is silent on the REM-vs-SWS
question, not corroborating.

Held at a lower confidence than the companion entry mainly because of study design: this is an
exploratory secondary analysis of a modest, already-collected sample (n=24, split into two
12-person groups), not a study purpose-built and powered to test the concatenation hypothesis,
and it carries the same structural transfer risk to Fishtank's online-replanned action sequences
as the companion entry — a rote, once-learned, externally fixed sequence is not the same object
as an agent's continuously regenerated plan. Read together, the two entries triangulate on the
same real, sleep-sensitive, formation-distinct fluency phenomenon; only the Bottary entry can
speak to which sleep stage drives it.
