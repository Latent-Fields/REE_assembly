# Repetitive negative thinking in daily life and functional connectivity among default mode, fronto-parietal, and salience networks (Lydon-Staley, Kuehner, Zamoscik, Huffziger, Kirsch & Bassett, 2019, *Translational Psychiatry*)

The source thought is careful to flag persistence and dissolution as "load-bearing parts of the mechanism," not an afterthought bolted onto coalition instantiation -- scenario 5 in its falsification programme is specifically about what happens when a correctly-recruited coalition is dissolved too early or held open too long, predicting "premature closure" in the first case and "rumination, perseveration, or control capture" in the second. That is a strong, specific, falsifiable prediction, and it is worth checking whether anything in the existing literature already looks like a natural test of it. This paper is the closest match I found.

The design combines two things that are individually unremarkable but powerful together: resting-state fMRI connectivity, and four days of ecological momentary assessment sampling mood and repetitive negative thinking ten times a day in participants' ordinary lives (not a lab task). The "impaired disengagement hypothesis" of rumination the authors test is essentially a claim that people who cannot functionally decouple the default mode network (self-referential, internally-directed processing) from the fronto-parietal control network, once sadness triggers self-referential thought, get stuck there -- exactly REE's "over-long engagement" failure mode. And that is what they find: participants with higher between-network connectivity of default mode and fronto-parietal systems show a stronger increase in repetitive negative thinking following momentary sadness. Separately, and I think this is the more interesting part for REE's architecture, flexibility of the salience network's connectivity pattern specifically is protective -- people whose salience network reconfigures more readily show less of the sadness-to-rumination escalation. That maps unusually cleanly onto the idea that a distinct system (salience network, plausibly analogous to whatever detects that a control demand has resolved or needs escalating) is doing the actual dissolution work, separate from the DMN/FPN coupling that constitutes the "stuck" coalition itself.

I want to be honest about what this design can and cannot establish. It is correlational and between-person -- these are individual differences in resting connectivity and daily-life mood dynamics, not an experiment where a specific control demand is triggered and its dissolution timing is manipulated. So it supports the general biological plausibility that persistence and dissolution can fail as a mechanism SEPARATE from whether the right coalition was recruited in the first place (which is exactly the distinction MECH-481's scenario 5 wants to draw), but it cannot demonstrate that this is caused by, or specific to, a mistyped or mistimed control-demand classification in REE's specific architectural sense. It is evidence that the failure mode is real and dissociable in humans, not evidence for REE's particular causal story about why it happens.

Confidence: 0.6. Reasonable source quality (solid multilevel design, real ecological validity, though modest sample size and a partly clinical subsample). Mapping fidelity is fairly direct for the specific persistence/dissolution concept, tempered by the correlational nature of the evidence relative to REE's event-triggered mechanism.
