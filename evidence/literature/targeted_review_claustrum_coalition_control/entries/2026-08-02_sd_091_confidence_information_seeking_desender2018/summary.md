# Subjective Confidence Predicts Information Seeking in Decision Making (Desender, Boldt & Yeung, 2018, *Psychological Science*)

Before asking whether metacognitive control is graph-valued (SD-091's specific claim) or whether it recruits typed coalitions (MECH-481's specific claim), it is worth having solid evidence for the much more basic premise both claims are built on top of: that metacognitive monitoring actually causes a change in subsequent processing, rather than merely accompanying one. This paper is, to my mind, the cleanest available demonstration of exactly that, which is why I've included it even though it is not about the claustrum, coalitions, or network topology at all.

The methodological trick is elegant. Confidence and accuracy are normally so tightly correlated that it is hard to isolate confidence's own causal contribution -- if you find that low-confidence trials are followed by more careful behaviour, is that because of confidence itself, or just because those trials were objectively harder? Desender, Boldt and Yeung solve this by manipulating the STRENGTH versus the VARIABILITY of perceptual evidence in a way that holds objective accuracy constant across conditions while still producing a reliable confidence difference: variable evidence produces lower confidence than strong evidence even when accuracy is matched. With that dissociation in hand, they then let participants choose to sample additional information before committing to a response, and confidence -- not accuracy -- predicts that choice. Participants sample more when confidence is lower, even holding difficulty fixed. This replicates in a preregistered follow-up with an independent, larger sample and harder task conditions.

This is exactly the kind of evidence the source thought's "Candidate claim" needs: "a confidence... estimate becomes metacognitively effective only when it can alter the composition or operating regime of subsequent cognition." Here, confidence alters behaviour (information-seeking) independent of the objective state of the world. That is a genuine causal demonstration, not just a correlational one, and it is worth being clear that this is squarely support for SD-091's most basic premise -- that metacognitive control must be enactive, not merely representational.

Where I want to be careful is in not stretching this further than it goes. The corrective action tested here is a single type: sample more of the same evidence stream before responding. There is no manipulation here of DIFFERENT kinds of uncertainty requiring DIFFERENT kinds of correction -- no analogue of "a doubtful perception requires resampling, a doubtful memory requires provenance reconstruction." In REE's own falsifier language (registered in MECH-481's `what_would_answer`), this design cannot distinguish Arm 2 (parametric-only: e.g., a threshold or sampling-rate parameter shifts with confidence) from Arm 4 (typed coalition: a qualitatively different subsystem gets recruited). It is entirely consistent with a purely parametric account of confidence-guided behaviour change, and I would not want it cited as evidence for the coalition/topology axis specifically -- only for the more general, and still important, premise that monitoring-without-enactment is an empirically real failure mode worth building against.

Confidence: 0.55. Source quality is excellent (preregistered replication, clean causal design, strong venue). Mapping fidelity capped at moderate because the study's single-response-type design is structurally silent on the specific typed/graph-valued question SD-091 and MECH-481 are actually trying to answer.
