# The Human Claustrum Activates Across Multiple Cognitive Control Tasks (Huang, Stewart, Liu, Phylactou, Mathur & Seminowicz, 2025, *European Journal of Neuroscience*)

This is the most recent and most directly relevant empirical test of the network-instantiation account, from the same lab lineage as the Madden et al. review above (Seminowicz and Mathur are co-authors on both). Rather than proposing a model, it asks a simple question of an existing dataset: does the claustrum, isolated with the group's now-standard signal-separation method, show up across a genuinely varied battery of cognitive-control tasks -- Stroop, AX-CPT, cued task-switching, and Sternberg working memory -- in 55 healthy participants? It does. Bilateral claustrum activation appears in certain conditions and trial phases of all four tasks, concentrated during periods of higher cognitive demand, and it coincides temporally with activation of the broader task-positive cortical network.

This is a reasonable thing to cite in support of SD-091's premise that whatever the claustrum is doing, it is not narrowly tied to one cognitive-control paradigm -- a precondition for treating it as evidence for a general-purpose topology-control mechanism rather than a quirky one-off finding attached to a single task. But it is worth being precise about what the paper does not show, because that gap is exactly where MECH-481 lives. The result as reported is that claustrum activation scales with cognitive demand across tasks. It is not a report of what CHANGES in the cortical network configuration between, say, a Stroop-conflict trial and a Sternberg working-memory-load trial. A pure "more claustrum signal when the task gets harder" account -- which in REE's own falsifier language is closer to Arm 2 (parametric-only: threshold or gain scaling) than Arm 4 (typed coalition, where the recruited SET of systems differs by demand type) -- would be entirely consistent with this data. Distinguishing those two accounts needs task-conditional connectivity or multivariate pattern analysis showing that the SET of coupled regions differs by task type, not just that claustrum BOLD signal rises with difficulty. That analysis is not what this paper reports.

So: good, current, directly on-topic evidence that the claustrum's role generalizes across cognitive-control task families (worth citing for SD-091), but it should not be over-read as confirming the typed-recruitment mechanism MECH-481 proposes. If anything, this paper is a useful pointer toward what MECH-481's own falsifier would need to look like empirically in a claustrum-specific replication: task-conditional connectivity graphs, not just task-conditional activation magnitude.

Confidence: 0.55. Solid methodology and sample size keep source_quality reasonably high, but mapping_fidelity is capped at moderate because the reported effect (demand-scaled activation) underdetermines the specific claim REE needs (demand-TYPE-differentiated network configuration).
