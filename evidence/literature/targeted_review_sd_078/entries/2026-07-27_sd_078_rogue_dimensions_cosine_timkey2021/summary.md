# Rogue dimensions dominate cosine similarity — and mean-subtraction alone does not always fix it

**Timkey & van Schijndel (2021), EMNLP.** [DOI 10.18653/v1/2021.emnlp-main.372](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2021.emnlp-main.372)

## What the paper did

The authors asked a question that sounds procedural and turns out to be structural: when we compute a cosine similarity between two contextual embeddings, what is actually doing the arithmetic? They decomposed cosine similarity dimension by dimension across every layer of BERT, RoBERTa, GPT-2 and XLNet (all d=768), plus static Word2Vec and GloVe for contrast, over 500k random token pairs drawn from 85k Wikipedia articles. For each dimension they computed its mean contribution to the expected cosine similarity between random pairs, then asked how much of the *variability* in cosine similarity survives when the top-contributing dimensions are removed. They then ran an ablation study — zeroing individual dimensions and measuring the KL divergence induced in the language-modelling distribution — to ask whether the dimensions that dominate similarity are the ones the model actually uses. Finally they compared three corrections (per-dimension standardization, Mu & Viswanath's all-but-the-top, and Spearman ranking) against four human word-similarity datasets.

## What they found

The headline number is stark. In XLNet layers 10 and 11, a *single* dimension contributes more than 99% of the expected cosine similarity between randomly sampled tokens. That dimension (i=667 in the final layer) has a mean activation of 180.0, while every other dimension averages −0.084 with a standard deviation of 0.77. It is not a feature; it is an offset with a feature's job title. Removing just the top one dimension leaves almost none of the variance in cosine similarity explained by the remaining 767-dimensional subspace — r² = 0.028 for XLNet-12 and 0.046 for BERT-11. The static models are not like this at all: Word2Vec and GloVe sit at anisotropy 0.130 and 0.104 and are dominated by no single dimension. So this is a property that *emerges* in contextual models, not a generic property of embedding spaces.

The second finding is the one I did not expect and think matters most for us. The dimensions that dominate cosine similarity are largely *not* the dimensions that drive model behaviour. Ablating the cosine-dominating dimensions in GPT-2 and XLNet changed the language-modelling distribution only vanishingly. The representation was doing its job the whole time; the measuring instrument was broken. After postprocessing, the later layers correlate with human similarity judgments as well as or better than the intermediate layers — which quietly overturns a fairly settled story in that literature about final layers "becoming task-specific."

## How this maps to SD-078

The geometry is the same geometry. REE's measured z_world sits at pairwise cosine min 0.9767, mean 0.9964 — squarely inside the range this paper reports for its worst layers (GPT-2 layer 12: 0.885; XLNet layer 11: 0.981). And the consequence is the same consequence: SD-078 measured that raw z_world yields exactly one minted rule, `max_pairwise_rule_dist` of 0.0000, and a single context bucket. The paper's r² ≈ 0.03 result is the external, independently-derived form of that observation — once the common mode is doing the work, what is left is not a weak signal, it is close to no signal. That is the load-bearing support here, and it is what licenses SD-078's strongest structural argument: that raising `mature_mint_block_threshold` cannot help, because there is no threshold in a 0.9426-floored cone that separates anything. The paper's third finding also cuts our way in an unexpected direction, and against an easy objection: one might have argued that the CandidateRuleField is fine because the underlying `z_world` still carries structure. That is exactly true and exactly beside the point — the paper shows a representation can be perfectly serviceable to its consumers while its cosine is uninformative.

## Where it does not support us, and what that costs

I have marked this `mixed` rather than `supports`, and the reason is in section 5.3 of the paper. The authors compared corrections, and mean-subtraction alone — which is precisely and only what SD-078 does — was the *weaker* one. It "yielded substantial gains in most models with the exception of the final layers of GPT-2 and XLNet," where the rogue dimensions have exceptionally high variance: subtracting the mean made those spaces more isotropic, but 1–3 dimensions still drove the variability of cosine similarity afterward. Standardization, which additionally divides each dimension by its standard deviation, was the most successful method overall. SD-078 has no divisive term.

This is not a live contradiction. V3-EXQ-806 measured the landed fix on the real `CandidateRuleField` and it worked — `on_dist_mean` 1.67 against 0.0, nine rules against one, C1/C2/C3 all at frac 1.0. Mean-subtraction was evidently sufficient for the common mode z_world actually has, which is consistent with the paper's own "substantial gains in most models." What the paper contributes is a named failure mode with a diagnostic signature: if the z_world common mode ever migrates from a high-*mean* dimension to a high-*variance* one, SD-078's centering will stop working and the symptom will look identical to the pre-fix state. That is worth holding as a watch condition rather than a refutation, and it is cheap to check — the residual's per-dimension variance profile after centering is already computable from what the contract fixtures collect.

There is a smaller caveat worth recording because SD-079's functional restatement gestures at it. The paper does endorse rank-based readouts (Spearman) as robust to rogue dimensions, but immediately qualifies it: "rogue dimensions will tend to occupy the top or bottom ranks." So "rank-based readouts are exempt" is, as SD-079 already suspects, too coarse — the paper's position is closer to "less afflicted, not immune."

## Confidence

0.78. The source is strong — EMNLP main track, four models across every layer, an internal replication of Ethayarajh (2019), public code — and the mapping is unusually direct for a cross-domain pull, because what transfers is a linear-algebraic consequence rather than anything about learned content. I held it below 0.8 on two grounds. The generative story for the rogue dimensions there (punctuation tokens, position 0) has no REE analogue, so we are borrowing the consequence without the cause and cannot assume the two common modes will behave alike under drift. And the paper estimates a *static* corpus mean over a fixed corpus, where SD-078 runs an online EMA over a non-stationary stream — a strictly harder problem it simply does not speak to. That second gap is where SD-079's alpha-tuning work (0.05, not 0.02, because z_goal drifts) is doing something the literature here does not cover.
