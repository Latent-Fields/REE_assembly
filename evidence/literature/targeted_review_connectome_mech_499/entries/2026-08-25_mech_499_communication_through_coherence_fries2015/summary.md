# Rhythms for Cognition: Communication through Coherence (Fries 2015)

Fries's synthesis review lays out the mature "communication through coherence" (CTC) hypothesis:
gamma-band (30-90 Hz) synchronization between a presynaptic population and its postsynaptic
target renders that specific input effectively transmitted, because a synchronized excitatory
volley escapes the following inhibitory window while an unsynchronized one does not. When
multiple presynaptic groups converge on one postsynaptic target -- as happens whenever several
candidate representations compete for downstream influence -- the one selected by attention shows
stronger, higher-frequency gamma synchronization and thereby wins entrainment of the postsynaptic
population, effectively shutting out the others. Slower alpha-beta rhythms carry the top-down
signal that arbitrates which bottom-up gamma channel gets the advantage, and a theta rhythm paces
the overall sampling cycle. The paper cites decades of the author's own primate visual-cortex work
(V1-V4, V1-V2) as the empirical base.

**Why this is here for MECH-499, and why it is only partial support.** MECH-499 proposes something
stronger than CTC: that ephaptic/oscillatory field-level coherence functions as a
content-aggregation mechanism, synthesising distributed oscillatory information (phase, relative
phase, synchrony, cross-frequency relationships) into a single temporally-coherent composite state
that itself constitutes the hippocampal proposer's actionable-now anchor. CTC is real, extensively
replicated evidence that oscillatory phase relationships have genuine functional and behavioral
consequences for neural communication -- without that baseline fact, MECH-499 would have no
physical mechanism to stand on at all. But CTC's actual mechanism is a **filter**, not a
**constructor**: it routes an already-formed representation through or blocks it, arbitrated by
which input is more tightly phase-locked to the receiving population's excitability cycle. The
content being selected pre-exists the synchronization event. MECH-499 needs the field itself to
*synthesise* a new composite out of many streams' relationships to each other -- a fundamentally
different computational role even though both use the same physical substrate family (oscillatory
phase relationships in cortex/hippocampus).

**Mapping to REE.** This is precisely the failure mode the thought-intake itself flagged and asked
this literature pull to check: does the ephaptic/coherence literature already anchoring
MECH-228/MECH-270 (existence of field effects, field-spike coupling, SWR synchrony) actually
transfer to a content-aggregation reading, or does it only support the narrower
confidence/eligibility-readout reading MECH-270 already claims? CTC sits, if anything, closer to
MECH-270's territory (which stream is "anchor-eligible" -- a selection/gating question) than to
MECH-499's (what composite state results from combining many streams). It is the clearest
theoretical statement in the literature that oscillatory relationships are functionally
consequential, so it belongs in MECH-499's evidence base as necessary scaffolding, but it should
not be read as confirming the stronger content-aggregation claim.

**Limitations for this mapping.** CTC describes cortical (mostly visual) gamma/alpha-beta/theta
interplay across two or three named areas in attention tasks -- not hippocampal ephaptic coupling
during theta/SWR, and not aggregation across the many simultaneously active streams (z_self,
z_world, z_harm_s, ...) MECH-499 is actually about. The extrapolation from "phase relationships
gate transmission between two areas" to "field coherence aggregates content across many streams
into one composite now-state" is a genuine conceptual leap this paper does not make or test.

**Confidence.** 0.55. Source quality is very high (foundational, heavily cited, well-replicated
theory), but mapping fidelity to MECH-499's specific content-aggregation claim is low-moderate --
the mechanism described is gating/routing, not synthesis. Direction recorded as `mixed` rather
than `supports`: it supports the general premise that oscillatory phase relationships are
functionally load-bearing, while its own specific mechanism argues against, not for, the field
being read as an information-CONSTRUCTING aggregate.
