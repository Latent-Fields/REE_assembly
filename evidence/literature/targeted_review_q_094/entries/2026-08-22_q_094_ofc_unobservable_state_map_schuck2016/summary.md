# Human OFC represents a cognitive map of state space -- Q-094

Schuck, Cai, Wilson and Niv designed a 16-state decision task in which some states are, by construction, indistinguishable from sensory input alone -- you cannot tell which state you are in by looking. Applying multivariate pattern classification to human fMRI, they showed those unobservable states can be decoded from orbitofrontal cortex. Two further results give the finding functional teeth: decoding accuracy related to task performance and to the occurrence of individual behavioural errors, and the representational similarity between consecutive states correlated with behavioural accuracy on the corresponding transitions.

For Q-094 this supplies something the splitter-cell entry in this directory cannot. Wood et al. show a representation differentiates by internal variable; they do not show the differentiation is *used*. Here, when the state representation degrades, behaviour degrades in the specific way you would predict if the representation were the thing guiding action. Q-094's resolution condition explicitly requires that discrimination "translate into behavioural competence," and this is the closest any of the three papers comes to that standard. The underlying intuition Q-094 is built on -- that context is the organism's inference about which latent *situation* it is in, not which *scene* it is looking at -- is well supported here.

So why have I marked this `mixed` rather than `supports`? Because of where the map lives. Schuck et al. put it in orbitofrontal cortex, a structure separate from the hippocampal place and context system that REE's ContextMemory is loosely analogised to. Taken as architecture, that is an argument for a **distinct task-state module** sitting alongside a scene-keyed context memory -- not for concatenating `z_self` into the context retrieval query. And that option, if it is right, leaves MECH-150's recorded decision standing while still honouring the inference that motivated Q-094. It is a third pole, and I think governance should have it in front of it before a two-arm `z_world` versus `[z_self, z_world]` comparison is designed, because such a comparison cannot discover it. Q-094 already gestures at related territory by listing ARC-041 among its related claims; this is the literature-side reason to take that seriously.

I want to be scrupulous that this reading is mine and not the authors'. Schuck et al. argue about what OFC does. They make no claim about what hippocampal context representation should contain, and the anatomical separation between OFC and hippocampus does not automatically imply functional separation in an engineered substrate that has no obligation to respect brain regions at all. The mixed direction rests on an architectural inference this entry adds, and it should be weighted as such.

Two further limits. The 16 task states are abstract, discrete and externally defined by the experimental structure. Q-094's internal variables -- bodily state, confidence, inferred social situation -- are continuous, self-generated and not enumerable, so decodability of the former does not license assuming decodability of the latter. And fMRI multivariate decoding establishes that information is present in the measured signal; it does not establish that any downstream circuit reads it out in that form, still less that it is used as a retrieval key. That is the same represented-state-versus-query gap that bounds every entry in this directory, and it is the reason Q-094 remains genuinely open after this pull rather than merely under-evidenced.

Confidence 0.66, with transfer risk (0.45) the highest of the three Q-094 entries.
