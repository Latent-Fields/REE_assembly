# Beta-band oscillations as a status-quo / maintenance signal (Engel & Fries 2010)

According to PubMed, Engel & Fries (2010), *Beta-band oscillations--signalling the status quo?* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conb.2010.02.015)), is the review that gave the field its working hypothesis for what beta-band activity is *for*. Drawing together top-down cognitive work, motor-system recordings, and movement-disorder pathophysiology, they argue for a single unifying idea: beta activity, and beta-band coupling between regions, tracks the **maintenance of the current sensorimotor or cognitive state**. Their sharpest formulation is the one that matters here -- beta is expressed more strongly when maintenance of the status quo is *intended or predicted* than when a change is expected.

That predictive clause is what makes the paper relevant to MECH-445. The REE claim posits a `use_closure_commit_beta_coupling` path in which a closure-plane commit -- closure having fired on a *real sequence completion* -- elevates and binds the MECH-090 bistable latch. A sequence completion is, in Engel & Fries' terms, precisely an event that predicts the just-reached state should now be held. So the biology offers a natural rationale for why a completion signal would route through beta coupling to stiffen a latch, rather than through the ordinary evidence-accumulation route. The claim's most distinctive assertion -- that this happens *even on seeds where the agent never naturally commits decisively* (where `running_variance` never drops below `commit_threshold`) -- is the engineered version of "maintenance is predicted, therefore beta rises," decoupled from whether the system arrived at the state by confident accumulation.

Where I want to stay honest is the gap between framework and demonstration. This is a hypothesis review, not a single experiment that dissociates a completion-triggered beta drive from ongoing evidence-based maintenance. Engel & Fries describe beta as the correlate of holding *whatever* state currently obtains; they do not isolate a closure/completion event as a separable cause of latch elevation. MECH-445 makes the stronger, causal, dissociation claim. The review is *consistent* with that mechanism and motivates its shape, but it cannot confirm the independence-from-natural-commit part -- that is exactly what the V3 experiments (the SD-034 de-commit-pipeline line, 460-series) have to show on the substrate.

There is also a built-in caution worth carrying forward. The same review notes that *pathologically enhanced* beta yields "abnormal persistence of the status quo and a deterioration of flexible behavioural and cognitive control." Read onto REE, that is a prediction about the failure mode of MECH-445's own mechanism: a closure->beta->latch path that elevates the latch too readily, or fails to release it, would manifest as perseveration -- decisive commitment that the de-commit stage then cannot act on cleanly. That dovetails with the de-commit-pipeline framing in which MECH-445 is Child A (S3): the closure drive and the de-commit release are two halves of one control loop, and the biology says an over-strong hold is the characteristic pathology.

I rate this **supports** at confidence 0.55. The framework support is strong and canonical (source quality 0.85), but mapping fidelity is moderate (0.55) -- the review establishes that beta indexes predicted maintenance, not that a completion event causally and independently elevates a commitment latch -- and transfer risk is real (0.45), since mapping biological beta coupling onto an engineered `closure_commit_beta_coupling` latch is an analogy rather than a mechanistic identity. For an architectural claim I weight mapping fidelity heavily, which caps the aggregate at moderate.
