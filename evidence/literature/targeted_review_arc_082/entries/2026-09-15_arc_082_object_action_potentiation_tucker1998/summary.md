# Tucker & Ellis 1998 -- seen objects potentiate the actions they afford (ARC-082)

**What they did.** Participants saw photographs of ordinary graspable objects (a mug, a frying pan) and simply judged whether each was upright or inverted. Action was irrelevant to the task. The question was whether the object's action-relevant configuration, such as which side the handle was on or which way it would have to be twisted, nonetheless shaped the speed of responses.

**What they found.** It did. Handle orientation sped responses with the compatible hand, both bimanually and unimanually. Objects that need a clockwise or anticlockwise wrist rotation to grasp sped the matching wrist rotation. The authors conclude that seeing an object automatically potentiates components of the actions it affords, and that intention works on motor representations already in play rather than creating them from scratch.

**Mapping to ARC-082.** ARC-082 wants affordances bound to the object itself, with the V4 endpoint an object->action axis on a token-instance object-file slot. Two features of this result matter. First, potentiation is automatic and prior to intention, which is the ordering a cue->action projection feeding E3 selection implies. Second, what gets potentiated depends on *this* object's current orientation, not only on what kind of object it is. That is a small but real point in favour of keying on the token rather than the type.

**Caveats.** There is a serious competing account. Several later studies argue handle-compatibility effects are mostly spatial stimulus-response compatibility: the handle is simply a protrusion on the left or right, and nothing about grasping is needed. If that account wins, this paper speaks to spatial coding, not object-bound affordance. The effects are also small, momentary and non-persistent. They show the binding is *available*, not that it is stored with the object.

**Confidence.** 0.55: the classic behavioural anchor for the idea, held down by the unresolved alternative explanation.
