# Chao & Martin 2000 -- tools recruit premotor and parietal action regions (ARC-082)

**What they did.** Participants in an fMRI scanner viewed and silently named pictures from several object categories, including tools. The prediction was specific: because tools are defined by how the hand uses them, tool pictures alone should engage regions that store motor properties.

**What they found.** Tools selectively activated left ventral premotor cortex and left posterior parietal cortex. The authors link these to canonical F5 neurons and anterior intraparietal neurons in the monkey, cells that respond when a graspable object is merely seen. Recognising a tool, it seems, brings along the representation of what you do with it.

**Mapping to ARC-082.** ARC-082 describes tools and affordances as object->action binding, a specialisation of the ARC-080 object representation. This is among the most cited neural observations motivating that framing: for manipulable objects, identity and use-action are not stored in separate places reached by a separate cue projection. They are co-activated as part of seeing the object.

**What it does not show.** The contrast is between categories: tools against animals, houses and faces. That is evidence for *type-keyed* action knowledge, "tools afford grasping and using," whereas ARC-082's V4 endpoint is *token-keyed*, "this object in this slot affords these actions now." The honest reading is that biology likely has both, a semantic type-level store and an online instance-level binding (compare Tucker & Ellis 1998), and ARC-082 names only the second. The study is also correlational and early. Co-activation is not proof that the premotor activity participates in recognition or selection.

**Confidence.** 0.48: a foundational observation for the claim's premise, with limited power and a type-versus-token mismatch recorded as a failure signature.
