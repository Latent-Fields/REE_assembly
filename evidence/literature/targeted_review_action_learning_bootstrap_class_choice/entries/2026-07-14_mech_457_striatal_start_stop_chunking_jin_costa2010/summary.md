# Start/stop signals emerge in nigrostriatal circuits during sequence learning

**Class surveyed:** OPTIONS / SKILLS | **Evidence direction:** supports | **Confidence:** 0.66

**Source:** Xin Jin, Rui M. Costa (2010). *Start/stop signals emerge in nigrostriatal circuits during sequence learning.* Nature 466:457-462 DOI: 10.1038/nature09263

Jin & Costa show that as mice learn a self-paced operant action sequence, neurons in dorsal striatum and their nigrostriatal dopaminergic afferents develop activity specifically signalling the initiation and termination of the learned sequence, and this start/stop bracketing emerges over the course of learning. This is the neural signature of action 'chunking' -- concatenating primitive actions into a single retrievable unit with explicit onset/offset markers.

For the class-choice it is a striking biological instantiation of exactly the options-framework primitives: the initiation set and termination condition. It provides convergent evidence that temporal abstraction / chunking is a real learned mechanism the brain uses to promote well-practised behaviour from deliberate to automatic -- a mechanism for consolidating exploration into competent, reusable sequences.

It also cheaply constrains the build: if REE adopts temporal abstraction, the biologically-faithful form is explicit learned start/stop bracketing (option termination) rather than fixed-length macro-actions. Adopting the bracketing idea is lightweight and satisfies REE's brain-like-construction constraint; it does not require DIAYN's full discriminator apparatus.

Confidence 0.66: strong, canonical biological grounding for the class, but observational neuroscience rather than a floor->competent RL benchmark. It reinforces that chunking/consolidation is a different functional axis (automatization) from novelty-seeking, and that a lightweight options probe is the brain-faithful cheap entry point.
