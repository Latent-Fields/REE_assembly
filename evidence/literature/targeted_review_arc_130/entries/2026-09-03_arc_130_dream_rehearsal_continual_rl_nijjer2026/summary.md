# Nijjer (2026) -- The World Model Remembers, the Actor Forgets

**Verification note:** unreviewed arXiv preprint (2607.19749, submitted 22 Jul 2026, cs.LG). Verified against the arXiv abs page on 2026-09-03: title (abbreviated to "RL" from the given citation's "Reinforcement Learning" is the same paper), single author, and abstract all matched.

## What the paper did

Nijjer asks a question the continual-RL literature had apparently assumed an answer to without measuring it directly: when a DreamerV3-family agent forgets catastrophically across a sequence of tasks -- even with an unbounded replay buffer that in principle preserves every earlier experience -- which component actually forgets? The answer, from pre-registered component-level probes (n=3 seeds throughout), is unambiguous: the world model retains essentially everything measurable about old tasks (reward discrimination at a retention ratio near 1.0, intact value estimates, intact termination structure), while the actor's behaviour collapses regardless. This is framed, correctly I think, as a channel problem rather than a memory problem. The demonstration is causal, not just correlational: with the world model frozen and identical imagined rollouts available, ordinary reinforcement learning in imagination fails to recover a lost skill in any of three seeds, while supervised self-imitation on the world model's own graded ("dreamed") trajectories recovers it in all three, using zero environment interaction. Interleaved into training, this graded dream rehearsal produces a task-label-free, parameter-constant continual learner that retains perfectly across four- and eight-task chains where plain replay retains nothing. The authors are honest that the remedy is fragile on its own terms -- the "dream-grading" step that scores generated trajectories before use is load-bearing, and they had to characterize and guard against two distinct scoring failure modes to keep the results clean.

## Findings relevant to ARC-130 and ARC-131

ARC-130 formalises exactly the kind of gap this paper stumbles into empirically: organism-level mechanism status is stage-qualified along a ladder running from existence and representation through endogenous recruitment, local operation, competitive authority, committed throughput, ecological consequence, and retention -- and authority demonstrated at one stage does not establish that a mechanism's influence reaches committed behaviour. Here, the world model's representation of the old task is intact by every measure the authors probe, and yet the actor's committed behaviour collapses anyway; the frozen-world-model intervention shows the blockage sits specifically in the pathway from representation to committed action, which is precisely the joint ARC-130 isolates. It is a clean, independently-derived instance of the ladder's central claim, in a substrate REE never designed. The connection to ARC-131 (installability as a composition-dependent competence, distinct from temporal retention) is present but looser -- what this paper actually demonstrates reads closer to a retention failure (a competence that worked once decays as later training interferes with it) than to ARC-131's own compositional axis (whether a competence appears at all once the rest of the organism is switched on). I have recorded it as secondary evidence on ARC-131 for that reason, not as a direct test.

## REE mapping and limitations

The remedy that works -- supervised self-imitation where the world model's own generated trajectories become literal training targets for the actor -- deserves a specific and deliberate caveat rather than uncomplicated endorsement. REE has its own, carefully-scoped answer to almost the same question in MECH-094: simulation-mode content must not by default accumulate in the viability map as committed experience, precisely because failures of that distinction produce confabulation. MECH-094's one sanctioned exception (MECH-322) is narrow and conditional -- sleep-phase only, gated on a high-positive value tag from prior real-executed episodes, with accelerated dissolution absent later corroboration. This paper's domain (an image/policy classifier with no notion of confabulation or committed-experience accumulation) has no reason to respect any such boundary, and its clean positive result should not be read as license to relax REE's own gating -- if anything, it is a useful existence proof that model-generated rehearsal CAN work well when done carefully, which is an argument for getting the gating conditions right, not for dropping them. Separately, n=3 seeds throughout is a genuine statistical-power limitation even though the authors report complete seed separation and are unusually transparent about pre-registration and refuted hypotheses.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality benefits from real methodological rigor (pre-registration, full reporting of refuted hypotheses, an explicit characterization of the remedy's own failure modes) but is capped for preprint status and the small seed count. Mapping fidelity is solid for ARC-130 (0.58) given how directly the representation/throughput dissociation lines up with the ladder, and correspondingly weaker for the ARC-131 connection, which is retention-flavoured rather than compositional. Transfer risk (0.55) reflects both the domain gap to REE's own architecture and, independently, the MECH-094 tension in the remedy mechanism. The aggregate, 0.52, is a real supporting data point for ARC-130's stage-qualified status framework, read with the explicit caveat that its remedy mechanism is not something REE should adopt uncritically.
