# Where the two senses of 'object' meet

Social referencing is the situation I went looking for when the object stage turned out to be intended in both a cognitive and an object-relations sense, because it is the one place where the two readings are doing the same work at the same moment.

An infant at the edge of a visual cliff cannot evaluate the drop on its own resources. So it looks at the parent. What it takes from the parent's face is not free-floating affect -- it is affect *about the drop*. The parent's expression becomes usable precisely because it has the object as its referent. Self, object and other are bound in a single act, and the object is what gives the other's internal state enough content for the self to act on.

That is the object stage doing something, rather than being a way-station on a route from self to other. And it is simultaneously the cognitive-developmental object (a physical feature of the world, the third term of triadic attention) and the object-relations situation (the infant's affect regulated through an internalised reading of the caregiver). The two senses are not alternatives here; they are the same event described at two levels.

Moller and colleagues give a reasonably powered experimental instance: 81 infants aged 10-15 months, randomised to face their father or their mother across the cliff. The headline findings are about parent gender rather than architecture -- mothers encouraged more intensely, but infants crossed equally fast and equally anxious; only paternal expressed anxiety predicted infant anxiety and avoidance, and only in temperamentally anxious infants; parental encouragement alone predicted nothing.

Which brings me to the honest limit on this entry, and it is the reason I did not score it higher. The triadic structure is the paradigm's *premise*. The visual cliff is built to operationalise social referencing; it cannot then be cited as evidence that social referencing has the structure it was designed to instantiate. A premise is not a result. What this paper legitimately shows is that the triad behaves lawfully and selectively -- which is worth something, but less than it first appears.

The selectivity is itself informative and slightly awkward for a simple reading. If the object stage were a general channel carrying another's affect to the self, encouragement should have transmitted as readily as anxiety, and it transmitted not at all. Only anxiety moved, only from fathers, and only into infants already disposed that way. Whatever crosses the triad is filtered at both ends.

One scope note: 10-15 months is well after the period in which the fast route is presumed to seed the cognitive one, so this speaks to the object stage in operation, not to its place in the ontogenetic ordering. For the ordering, the Meltzoff and Gergely and Watson entries in this directory are the relevant ones -- and they disagree with each other about direction.

Confidence 0.62.
