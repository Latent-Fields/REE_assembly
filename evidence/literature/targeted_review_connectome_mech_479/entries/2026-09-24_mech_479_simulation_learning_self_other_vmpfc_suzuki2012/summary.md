Suzuki and colleagues (Neuron 2012) asked how people learn another person's values well enough to predict their choices. Model-based fMRI found two learning signals. A simulated-other reward prediction error in ventromedial prefrontal cortex was identical in form for self and simulated other, which they read as direct recruitment of one's own valuation process. But that alone did not account for learning: an action prediction error in dorsomedial/dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, generated by observing the other's actual choices, was also needed.

For the same-machinery question, this is the canonical "shared core plus adjunct" result. The self valuation system is reused for others, and an additional other-specific tracking circuit is necessary.

It speaks to MECH-479 only indirectly. Predicting another's decisions is a precondition for planning over their state, not the planning itself, and there is no multi-step component. It is most useful for the design of a V4 other-agent model: reusing the self value model for another agent may be a reasonable start but, on this evidence, it should be corrected by a channel that tracks the other's observed behaviour.
