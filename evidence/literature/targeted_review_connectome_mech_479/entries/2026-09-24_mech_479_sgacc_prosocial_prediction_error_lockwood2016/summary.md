Lockwood, Apps, Valton, Viding and Roiser (PNAS 2016) scanned participants choosing between stimuli that probabilistically delivered rewards to themselves, to another person, or to no one. A standard reinforcement-learning model fit all three conditions, but learning rates were lower when benefiting the other person. Activity in a posterior part of subgenual ACC/basal forebrain encoded a prediction error selectively in the prosocial condition, and more empathic participants learned faster for others and showed a more prosocial-selective sgACC response.

For the same-machinery question this is a mixed result. The computation looks the same (a prediction-error-driven update), but at least one region carries it only when the beneficiary is someone else. That is evidence of other-specific circuitry layered on a shared algorithm.

For MECH-479 proper it does not reach far. The task is one-step and has no planning component, so it cannot say whether benefiting another over time needs the planned pathway. What it suggests for REE design is narrower: other-agent benefit may warrant its own value/error channel rather than reuse of the self channel.

Confidence 0.5, mixed.
