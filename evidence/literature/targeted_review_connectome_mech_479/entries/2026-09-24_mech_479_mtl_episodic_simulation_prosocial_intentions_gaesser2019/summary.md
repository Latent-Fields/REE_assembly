Gaesser, Hirschfeld-Kroen, Wasserman, Horn and Young (SCAN 2019) built on earlier behavioural work (Gaesser and Schacter, PNAS 2014) showing that vividly imagining or remembering helping someone raises willingness to help. In fMRI, activity in the medial temporal lobe subsystem and right temporoparietal junction during imagined and remembered helping predicted how much each condition raised prosocial intentions. In a second experiment, TMS to RTPJ did not remove the effect, which the authors take as evidence that the MTL subsystem primarily supports it.

This is the only finding in the pull that ties the hippocampal system to other-directed behaviour, which is the neural direction MECH-479's "hippocampally-planned pathway" implies. It makes the claim biologically plausible rather than unsupported.

It is weak for the specific claim. The outcome is a rating of intention, not an action sequence. MTL was never perturbed, so its role is inferred by elimination. And constructing a vivid scene of helping is not the same as planning actions by their predicted effect on another's accumulated harm; it may simply raise motivation. Nothing here shows that a greedy controller would fail where a planner succeeds.

Confidence 0.4, supports (plausibility only).
