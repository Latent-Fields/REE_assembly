Lockwood, Klein-Flugge, Abdurahman and Crockett (PNAS 2020) had participants learn, under fMRI, which choices in a sequential task avoided painful electric shocks either to themselves or to a stranger. They fitted hybrid models that weigh "model-free" cached action values against "model-based" values computed from a map of actions to outcomes. People put relatively more weight on model-free control when the shocks would go to the other person. Model-free prediction errors for other versus self were tracked in thalamus/caudate, model-free moral learning signals appeared in subgenual ACC at choice, and switching after harming the other was associated with stronger sgACC-dlPFC connectivity.

This is the closest existing test of the question the MECH-479 note says is owed: whether other-directed planning recruits the model-based system specifically. The answer from this study is the opposite of what a naive reading of the claim would predict. Other-directed harm avoidance shifted toward the cached, efficient system, not toward planning.

It does not reach MECH-479's actual structure. The task has a short horizon and the harm is a discrete outcome, not an accumulating state of another agent that must be steered over many steps. MECH-479 argues that a 1-step greedy policy cannot, by construction, navigate another agent's integrated harm; that is a computational claim, and human preference for model-free control in a short task does not falsify it. What the study does rule out is citing human neuroscience as if prosocial behaviour were characteristically model-based.

Confidence 0.55, direction weakens: it weakens the implied biological grounding and the "requires" wording for short-horizon prosociality, not the long-horizon structural argument.
