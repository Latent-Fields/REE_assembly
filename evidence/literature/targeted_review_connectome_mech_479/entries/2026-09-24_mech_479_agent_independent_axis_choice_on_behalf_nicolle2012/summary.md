Nicolle, Klein-Flugge, Hunt, Vlaev, Dolan and Behrens (Neuron 2012) scanned people making value-based choices while a partner with different preferences was present. The brain modelled the partner's values and choices even when irrelevant, using the same computations as for the subject's own choices, in a neighbouring medial prefrontal region. When subjects then chose on behalf of the partner, the regions swapped roles: the region that had carried the subject's executed-choice values now carried the values of choices executed for the partner, and the modelling region now modelled the subject.

This is the clearest support in this pull for a same-machinery view of other-directed choice. The system that executes a decision appears to be agent-independent, taking whichever beneficiary it is currently deciding for.

It does not reach MECH-479's planning claim. The choices are single-step, there is no trajectory over another agent's state, and hippocampus is not involved. For REE it supports implementing prosocial planning by pointing the existing planner and E3 commitment at the other agent's state, rather than building a separate prosocial controller, but it cannot tell us whether that planner has to be the hippocampal one.

Confidence 0.45, supports (for same machinery; neutral on planned vs greedy).
