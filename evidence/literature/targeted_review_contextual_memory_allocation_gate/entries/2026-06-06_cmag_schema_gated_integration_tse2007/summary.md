# Tse et al. 2007 -- Schemas and memory consolidation

**Claim under review:** CANDIDATE-contextual-memory-allocation-gate (V4/V5 intake, 2026-06-06)
**Direction:** supports (schema_fit as a gating variable; integrate-pole) | **Confidence:** 0.68

## What the paper did

Tse, Morris and colleagues trained rats on a hippocampus-dependent flavour-place paired-associate task in which a set of flavour cues mapped onto spatial locations in a single arena -- a learned associative *schema*. Once that schema had been built up gradually over weeks, new flavour-place pairs could be acquired in a *single trial*, and -- the striking result -- those new traces became hippocampus-independent within 48 hours, whereas without a relevant schema the same kind of memory remains hippocampus-dependent for much longer. Hippocampal lesions given 48 h after new-pair training had little effect, and learning manipulations showed the schema played a causal, not merely correlational, role in this rapid assimilation.

## Why it matters for the intake

This is the cleanest causal evidence for the intake's `schema_fit` gating variable. How well a new experience matches a pre-existing structure determines *how* the trace is handled: a good fit licenses fast assimilation into the existing framework. In REE this lines up with INV-039 (schema-primed rapid assimilation, rate gated by map stability) and supports treating schema fit as a legitimate input to the allocation gate -- high fit producing integration pressure toward an existing trace. Where de Sousa 2026 supplies the controller and Cai 2016 the temporal variable, Tse supplies the *content-similarity-to-prior-structure* variable, and shows it is causally potent rather than epiphenomenal.

## Limitations and caveats

The mapping is looser here than for the other anchors, which is why confidence sits at 0.68. The paper operationalizes schema benefit as consolidation *speed* and hippocampal-independence, not as the binary decision to allocate overlapping versus separate traces. Reading fast assimilation as the gate's "integrate" output is a reasonable but real inference about a related-not-identical operation. The benefit is also demonstrated for schema-*consistent* items; the paper says little about how schema *mismatch* triggers active separation, so the separate-pole of `schema_fit` stays under-specified by this source. And the schema construct is a specific learned associative structure in rats, narrower than the general schema-fit term the intake proposes.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality is high (Science, causal lesion design, the foundational schema-consolidation paper). Mapping fidelity is moderate: it backs schema_fit as a genuine variable and the integrate-pole of the gate, but it measures consolidation rate rather than the allocation choice itself. Transfer risk is moderate (rat associative schema to a general REE schema_fit term). Net 0.68 -- solid support for schema_fit belonging in the variable basket, weaker support for it driving a discrete separate-versus-integrate decision.
