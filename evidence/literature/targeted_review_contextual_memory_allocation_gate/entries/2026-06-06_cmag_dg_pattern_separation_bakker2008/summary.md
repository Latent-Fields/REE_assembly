# Bakker et al. 2008 -- Pattern separation in the human hippocampal CA3 and dentate gyrus

**Claim under review:** CANDIDATE-contextual-memory-allocation-gate (V4/V5 intake, 2026-06-06)
**Direction:** supports (the separate-pole effector; MECH-147 substrate) | **Confidence:** 0.78

## What the paper did

Bakker, Stark and colleagues used high-resolution fMRI to resolve activity in human medial-temporal-lobe subfields while participants incidentally encoded a stream of images that were novel, exact repeats, or *similar lures*. The logic is a repetition-suppression contrast: a region biased toward *pattern completion* treats a similar lure like a repeat (suppressed response), whereas a region biased toward *pattern separation* treats the lure like something new (recovered, novelty-like response). They found a strong, anatomically confined pattern-separation signature in the CA3/dentate gyrus, while CA1, the subiculum, and the entorhinal and parahippocampal cortices showed the opposite, completion-biased pattern. This is among the cleanest human demonstrations that the DG/CA3 performs separation as a dedicated, regionally specialized operation.

## Why it matters for the intake

REE currently lists pattern separation as *planned, V4* under MECH-147 (DG sparse, non-redundant encoding of similar z_world before rollout). Bakker 2008 is empirical, human-anatomical backing for that substrate: the separate-pole of any Contextual Memory Allocation Gate has a concrete biological effector, and that effector is a real, localized computation -- not a side effect of failed retrieval. This matters for the intake's verdict because it confirms that "separate" is an *active* operation distinct from "no match" (the intake's open question Q4). The DG does not merely fail to find a prior trace; it actively orthogonalizes similar inputs into non-overlapping codes. A REE allocation gate is therefore arbitrating over a genuine two-effector substrate -- separation (DG) versus completion/integration (CA3 auto-association, CA1/EC) -- rather than choosing between act and abstain.

## Limitations and caveats

The evidence is an fMRI repetition-suppression *signature*, not direct observation of sparse codes; the DG sparse-encoding mechanism is inferred at the population level, so the mapping to a discrete REE z_world orthogonalization is by analogy. More importantly for governance: this paper supports the *effector* (MECH-147), not the *policy*. It shows that separation happens and where, but says nothing about a control signal deciding when to separate versus integrate -- which is exactly the novel lever the Contextual Memory Allocation Gate proposes. And because CA1/EC actively perform completion, a REE design that builds separation without the complementary integration pole would mis-model the circuit; the two poles are co-constitutive.
