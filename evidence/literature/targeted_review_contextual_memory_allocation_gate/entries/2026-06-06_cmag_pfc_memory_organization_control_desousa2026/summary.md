# de Sousa et al. 2026 -- The prefrontal cortex controls memory organization in the hippocampus

**Claim under review:** CANDIDATE-contextual-memory-allocation-gate (V4/V5 intake, 2026-06-06)
**Direction:** supports (existence + top-down control of an allocation gate) | **Confidence:** 0.72

## What the paper did

de Sousa and colleagues studied how mice decide whether two memories formed at different times should be *linked* (encoded in overlapping dorsal-CA1 ensembles) or kept *separate* (encoded in fresh neurons). Using contextual-memory paradigms with calcium imaging of dCA1 ensembles and causal manipulation of the ventromedial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC) and its downstream pathway, they show that the vmPFC is recruited over time to control hippocampal memory integration according to the *contextual similarity* between experiences. The control signal travels vmPFC -> medial entorhinal cortex -> dorsal hippocampus, modulating entorhinal activity, dCA1 ensemble overlap, and -- as the final gatekeeper -- the activity of neurogliaform inhibitory cells in dorsal CA1 that regulate which neurons are recruited to store a new memory. When two environments are meaningfully different, the vmPFC drives the hippocampus to use fresh neurons (separation); when they are similar, the vmPFC disengages and the hippocampus encodes in overlapping neurons (linking). Critically, by manipulating the pathway the authors could force memories to merge that should not, or hold separate memories that would otherwise link -- the signature of a genuine control mechanism rather than a passive correlate.

## The temporal-distance result (directly relevant to intake variable b)

The vmPFC's role is *time-scale specific*. Experiences encoded within a few hours of each other merge automatically: silencing the vmPFC when mice visited two environments roughly five hours apart changed nothing -- the memories linked regardless. It is only when experiences are separated by days (the study contrasts ~5 hours with ~7 days) that the vmPFC is deliberately recruited to adjudicate linking versus separation. Contextual similarity can override temporal distance in the linking direction: very similar environments could still be linked even seven days apart. This is the cleanest biological evidence I have found that **temporal distance is a first-class gating variable**, and that it interacts non-trivially with contextual similarity rather than acting as an independent additive term.

## How it maps to REE

This is the proof-of-existence the intake asked for: memory organization is an *active, top-down-regulated control problem*, not a passive store operation. The biological axis maps cleanly onto REE's architecture -- vmPFC -> control plane (ARC-035 vmPFC; SD-033 PFC subdivisions); medial entorhinal cortex -> memory interface / cue-query layer (SD-016); dorsal CA1 relational ensembles -> the ARC-007 residue-field relational graph; and the neurogliaform gatekeeper -> REE's categorical write gate (MECH-094) and mode-conditioned write gating (MECH-261). The decisive point for governance is the one the intake's overlap pass already reached: **REE already owns the gates this paper identifies; what the paper adds is that a higher-order context model actively decides overlap-versus-separation, using contextual similarity and temporal distance.** That decision -- the allocation *policy* -- is the genuinely novel lever, and it is precisely what is absent from the registered claim set.

## Limitations and caveats

The paper establishes that the gate exists and is top-down controlled. It does not formalize a *decision algorithm*, does not weight a basket of inputs, and offers no reality-coherence or false-linking cost term -- so it cannot, on its own, justify the specific input list in the intake's illustrative `allocate_memory_trace` sketch. The work is entirely in mice, on contextual-fear-style memory linking; the mapping to a REE artificial-agent write policy is an architectural analogy, not a measured correspondence. The schizophrenia and bipolar framing is explicitly speculative translation from mouse circuit work, with no human or clinical data, and must stay quarantined as speculation (consistent with the project's psychosis/confabulation-distinction memory). Finally, the within-hours auto-merge result bounds the analogue: the gate is *selectively* recruited over a days timescale, not a universal write controller -- any REE version inherits that scope limit.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality is high (top venue, causal manipulation, multi-level evidence from behavior through ensemble imaging to a named interneuron). Mapping fidelity is high for the existence of a top-down allocation gate and for context-similarity-plus-temporal-distance as gating variables, but low for the decision-algorithm and cost-term aspects the paper never addresses. Transfer risk is moderate (rodent fear-context circuit to artificial-agent policy), elevated by the speculative clinical framing. Net 0.72: strong support for *that* an allocation gate exists and is governed top-down, weaker support for *how* REE should implement the policy.
