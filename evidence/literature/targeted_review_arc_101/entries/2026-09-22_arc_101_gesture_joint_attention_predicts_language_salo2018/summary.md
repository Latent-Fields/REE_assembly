# Salo, Rowe & Reeb-Sutherland (2018) -- gesture and joint attention each carry unique variance into later language

Fifty-three infants, measured at 12 months for gesture use during parent-infant free play and for
joint attention on the Early Social-Communication Scales, then measured again at 24 months on the
Mullen expressive and receptive language scales. Two findings, and the second is the interesting
one. First, at 12 months gesture and joint attention -- particularly responding to joint attention
rather than initiating it -- load on a partly shared underlying construct. Second, despite that
shared construct, each still uniquely predicts language at 24 months.

ARC-101's bet is that proto-language partially bootstraps from grounded proto-communication given
shared attention among its enabling conditions. If that bet is right, human ontogeny ought to show
the grounded proto-communicative channel and the shared-attention channel doing partly separable
work on the way into language. That is what this study reports. The unique-variance result is the
part that matters: if gesture were simply a readout of joint attention, the two would be
interchangeable predictors and the ecology would look like one undifferentiated readiness factor
rather than the several distinct conditions ARC-101 enumerates.

The honest reading has to stop well short of mechanism, and I have set the confidence low (0.55)
to say so. Prediction is not bootstrapping. An observational longitudinal design of this kind is
fully compatible with a common third cause -- general social motivation, caregiver input quality,
or simple maturation rate -- generating both the 12-month proto-communication measures and the
24-month language score, with the proto-communication doing no constructive work whatever. That
deflationary account predicts the same correlations, including the unique-variance pattern, if the
two 12-month measures index the common cause imperfectly and in different ways.

There is a second gap that matters more for REE than the confounding does. The outcome here is a
standardised vocabulary and language level in a child acquiring an existing language from
competent speakers. ARC-101 is about a population bootstrapping a system that does not yet exist.
A child learning English from English speakers is a transmission problem; ARC-101 is an origination
problem. Evidence that grounded proto-communication helps with the former is suggestive about the
latter but not the same claim, and the sample size (53) does not leave much room for the effect to
be pinned down precisely even within its own domain.

Confidence 0.55 -- consistency evidence from human ontogeny, not mechanistic support. It belongs in
the record because ARC-101's enabling-condition list should be answerable to developmental data,
and this is the developmental data that speaks most directly to the shared-attention and
grounded-gesture conditions. What it does not do is discriminate ARC-101 from the readiness-factor
alternative, and until the corpus contains something that does, the claim should stay at candidate.
