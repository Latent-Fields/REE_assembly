# Self-Other Mergence in the Frontal Cortex during Cooperation and Competition (Wittmann et al., 2016)

## What the paper did

Participants estimated their own and other people's abilities while the context varied between
cooperation and competition. Perigenual anterior cingulate cortex tracked the participant's own
performance. Dorsomedial frontal area 9 tracked the *other's* performance -- but not cleanly: it also
integrated contextual and self-related information. Behaviourally the same entanglement appeared in
both directions. Self-estimates were partly merged with the performance of others, and estimates of
others incorporated one's own performance. Whether standing next to a high performer raised or
lowered your estimate of yourself depended on whether you were cooperating with them or competing
against them. The authors' conclusion is unambiguous: area 9 carries interdependent representations
of self and other.

## Why I included a paper that cuts against the claim

The other two ARC-083 entries both come from the hippocampal other-agent-coding literature and both
lean supportive. A pull consisting only of those would misrepresent the state of the evidence, and
would leave the claim's weakest commitment untested. ARC-083's substantive content is not that other
agents are represented -- nobody disputes that -- but that each is represented in *its own*
token-keyed slot. Separability is the claim. This paper attacks separability directly, in humans, on
a variable that ARC-083 explicitly lists as a slot field.

The finding is worse for the claim than simple noise would be. If other-estimates were merely noisy,
a slot architecture would survive untouched. What is reported instead is *structured* contamination
whose direction flips with social context. A static per-agent slot has no way to express that. You
cannot specify `{z_self_j, z_harm_a_j, drive, commitment}` and recover a quantity whose relationship
to the estimator's own state reverses sign depending on whether the two agents are cooperating.

## What it does not show, and the distinction matters

It would be too quick to read this as a demotion trigger. The paper shows that the *content* of
other-estimates is contaminated. It does not show that the underlying representational slots are
shared. Those are genuinely different architectures, and they are behaviourally hard to tell apart:
separate slots joined by a strong, signed, context-dependent coupling term would produce exactly this
data while leaving ARC-083's allocation claim standing. So the honest reading is that this constrains
ARC-083 rather than refutes it -- and the constraint is constructive. It tells whoever eventually
builds PILLAR 4 that an explicit self-other coupling term is not optional.

I will note in passing, without tagging it, that the context-dependent sign reversal here rhymes
conspicuously with ARC-084's signed coupling edge, which was the other target of this same pull.
Cooperation and competition changing the sign of an inter-representational influence is close to
what ARC-084 asserts at the field level. I have deliberately not tagged this entry to ARC-084 --
an ability-estimation effect is not a cognifold field-coupling parameter, and tagging it there would
be the kind of loose mapping that makes an evidence base unreadable. But the resonance is worth a
sentence, because if both pillars are eventually built they will likely need the same mechanism.

## Confidence

0.62 -- slightly above the hippocampal entries. The reason is mapping fidelity rather than method:
this paper measures a competence estimate about another agent, which is literally one of the fields
ARC-083's slot specifies, where the hippocampal work offers only a spatial proxy. Capped where it is
by the content-versus-format ambiguity above, which is a real alternative explanation and not a
formality.
