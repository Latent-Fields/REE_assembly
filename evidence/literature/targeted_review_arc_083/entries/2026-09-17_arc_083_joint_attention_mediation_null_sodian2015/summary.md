# Declarative joint attention as a foundation of theory of mind (Sodian & Kristen-Antonow, 2015)

## The design, and why it is the one that counts

Eighty-three children followed from 12 months to 50 months. At the early wave: declarative pointing,
imperative pointing, mirror self-recognition, level-1 visual perspective taking. At the late wave: a
false-belief battery. Path analysis, with child gender and language ability controlled, and with
temperament and emotion recognition ruled out separately.

Every other paper in this directory is cross-sectional or a single-timepoint comparison. The chip
that commissioned this pull warned that object permanence, self-recognition and theory of mind all
improve with age, so correlations between them are close to guaranteed and evidence nothing about
necessity, and asked for evidence about ordering or necessity rather than co-occurrence. This is that
evidence. It is the only study here with the design to deliver it.

## The result

Declarative pointing at 12 months predicted false-belief understanding at 50 months. Imperative
pointing did not — which is a nice internal control, since the two are matched for motor form and
differ only in whether the child is *requesting* an object or *sharing attention* to it.

And the part that bears on DEV-NEED-021: mirror self-recognition did not mediate that path. Neither
did level-1 visual perspective taking. Both were correlated with joint attention. Neither carried
the route from early social competence to later theory of mind.

## What this does to ARC-083's prerequisite structure

DEV-NEED-021 says otherness inference REQUIRES object permanence plus self-stability, and ARC-083
restates it with `depends_on: [..., ARC-081, ...]` and the V5 plan's `readiness_gate` language making
the dependency operational. The self-stability half of that was measured here, in the right design,
and it did not do the work.

What did the work was **declarative pointing** — an act in which the infant and another agent
co-attend to a third thing, for no instrumental reason. That is a triadic self-other-object coupling,
and it has no node anywhere in ARC-083's prerequisite structure. If the developmental evidence is
taken at face value, the prerequisite REE should be carrying is not "a stable self" but "a channel
on which self, other and object can be bound into one shared reference". Architecturally that is a
much more interesting requirement, and a much cheaper one — it sits closer to the ARC-080 object-file
plus a communicative binding operation than to anything ARC-081 supplies.

It is also, I notice, roughly what the Bischof-Köhler entry independently arrived at from the other
direction. Her stated mechanism was *synchronic identification*: holding self and other simultaneously
in view. Declarative pointing is the behavioural signature of exactly that, one year earlier. Two
papers that disagree about whether self-recognition matters agree that the operative capacity is
relational rather than self-internal. When a supporting and a weakening source converge on the same
correction to a claim, that convergence is worth more than either verdict.

## The caveat I am least comfortable waving away

A mediation null is not a dissociation. It says self-recognition carried no variance on this path in
this sample; it does not say otherness inference can proceed without a stable self. With n=83 and a
38-month gap, a real but modest mediated effect could be invisible.

More seriously: the outcome is explicit false-belief understanding at 50 months. That is a long way
downstream of what ARC-083 actually needs, which is entity-level individuation — allocating a slot to
agent j and keeping it from contaminating agent k. It is quite coherent that self-stability gates the
*early* inference (is that thing an other?) while declarative joint attention predicts the *later*
metarepresentational elaboration (what does that other believe?). On that reading this result and
DEV-NEED-021 are not in conflict at all, because they are about different rungs of the same ladder.

I cannot rule that out, and I have not written the entry as though I could. It is why confidence sits
at 0.68 rather than higher, and why `mapping_fidelity` is 0.62 despite this being the best-designed
study in the pull. The gap between "false belief at 50 months" and "otherness inference" is the
single largest source of slack in this entry, and anyone reading it later should treat that gap as
open rather than as handled.

## Confidence

0.68 — the highest in this pull. The design is what earns it: longitudinal, with the age-and-language
control that the confound objection specifically demands, and with the self-representational variable
measured rather than assumed. Held below 0.8 by the null-versus-dissociation gap and by the distance
between the outcome measure and ARC-083's actual requirement.
