# Décalage in Infants' Search for Mothers Versus Toys (Slaughter & Boh, 2001)

## What the paper did

Infants between 7 and 14 months searched for a hidden target under curtained tables after an enforced
delay, with the delay lengthened each time they succeeded. The target was either their mother or a
large toy. Infants over 8 months sustained significantly longer delays when the hidden thing was
their mother. The authors conclude that mother permanence precedes object permanence.

Thirty-one infants were tested; seventeen completed the procedure.

## Why I went looking for this

The other four entries in this pull all address the self-stability half of DEV-NEED-021. The
object-permanence half — ARC-083's claim that otherness inference requires object persistence,
inherited as PILLAR 1 gating PILLAR 4 — had nothing at all. This is what I found, and it is thin.
Developmental psychology largely stopped asking the person-versus-object permanence question in the
1980s after it stalled on methodological grounds, so the literature is old, small, and unresolved.
I am recording that as the finding it is: the object-permanence half of DEV-NEED-021 is not merely
unevidenced in REE, it is close to unevidenced in the source literature.

## The result inverts the ordering REE assumes

ARC-083's reasoning for the prerequisite is stated in the V5 plan: otherness inference requires
object permanence "so an other persists as a trackable entity through occlusion". The implied
architecture is that a general persistence buffer comes first and other-agents are then a special
case of what it holds. PILLAR 1 before PILLAR 4.

What these infants did was the reverse. The socially significant entity persisted through longer
occlusions than the inanimate one, in the same children, on the same task, at the same session. If
agent-permanence were an application of a general object-file, it could not outrun it.

## The confound is also the finding

The obvious objection is that a mother is not an arbitrary other agent j. She is the most
motivationally loaded entity in the infant's world, and what the task measures is how long a
representation survives before search collapses. So this may be an effect of attachment salience on
memory persistence, not a category distinction between agents and objects. Jackson, Campos & Fischer
(1978, *Developmental Psychology* 14(1), 1-10) made essentially that argument against the whole
décalage literature — task demands, not the nature of the sought entity — and nothing here answers
it. Combined with 17 completing infants, that is why this entry carries the lowest confidence in the
pull at 0.50.

But I want to resist filing the confound as a reason to discount the entry, because for REE the
confound is the interesting part.

ARC-083's own `functional_restatement` says object permanence is "only PARTIALLY present (the SD-039
motivational ghost bank, not a true object-file persistence buffer)", and treats that as a shortfall
— PILLAR 1 is owed, and until it lands the permanence prerequisite is only half met. This paper
suggests the shortfall may be miscategorised. If what carries an *other* through occlusion in human
infants is motivational weighting rather than generic object-file machinery, then a motivational
ghost bank is not a degraded stand-in for the real thing. It may be the right primitive, and the
"true object-file persistence buffer" may be the wrong target for this particular dependency.

That is a build-relevant claim and it is cheap to act on: it would mean ARC-083's permanence
prerequisite can be satisfied by extending SD-039 to carry agent tokens, rather than waiting on
PILLAR 1 to be registered and built. I am not asserting it on the strength of seventeen infants. I
am flagging that the evidence REE has for the PILLAR-1 dependency currently points the other way,
and that the cheaper route has not been considered because the claim text pre-judged the ghost bank
as inadequate.

## Confidence

0.50, the lowest here, and the components explain the shape: source quality 0.55 (small, old,
contested), mapping fidelity 0.55 (it genuinely tests the ordering DEV-NEED-021 asserts, which is
rare in this pull, but confounds agency with attachment), transfer risk 0.60 (the highest, because
the confound and the mechanism REE would import are the same thing — motivational weighting — so I
cannot discount one without discounting the other).
