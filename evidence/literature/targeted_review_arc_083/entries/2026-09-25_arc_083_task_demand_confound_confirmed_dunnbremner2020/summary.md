# Investigating the social environment of the A-not-B search task (Dunn & Bremner, 2020)

Dunn and Bremner ran 48 nine-month-old infants through the classic Piagetian A-not-B search task -- the workhorse instrument behind most infant object-permanence findings, including the object arm of every person/object decalage comparison this project has on file -- and manipulated one thing only: where the experimenter looked while the infant was searching for the object hidden at the new location, B. In the standard condition the experimenter kept a neutral midline gaze; in the congruent condition they looked toward the correct B location; in the incongruent condition they looked toward the old, incorrect A location. Search accuracy on B trials tracked gaze congruence, and infants who searched incorrectly looked back at the experimenter more often afterward than infants who searched correctly -- the signature of a child treating another person's attention as an informative cue, not (or not only) consulting their own representation of where the object is.

Why this belongs in the ARC-083 file even though it never touches a mother-versus-toy comparison: ARC-083's notes record Jackson, Campos and Fischer's 1978 objection that the person/object decalage literature (Slaughter and Boh 2001 chief among the entries here) reflects task demands rather than a real conceptual gap between agent-permanence and object-permanence, and record that objection as unanswered by anything on file. This paper is the closest thing I could find, post-1978, to a direct empirical test of the mechanism Jackson et al. were worried about -- and it comes down on their side. If the *object* half of a decalage comparison is itself measurably contaminated by incidental social cues from the person doing the hiding, then a comparison against the *person* half (where the hider's own attention state is inherently informative in a way a toy's location is not) starts from an already-uneven footing. That does not prove the decalage is nothing but an artifact -- Slaughter and Boh's mother-versus-toy delay task is structurally different from A-not-B, so the specific confound size here does not transfer directly -- but it is a modern, well-controlled demonstration that the instrument family is sensitive to exactly the class of task-demand factors the 1978 objection named, which raises rather than lowers the bar the decalage literature needs to clear.

For REE: this does not move SD-039 or the person-permanence-as-motivational-weighting question forward on its own terms (see the companion Thomas et al. 2015 entry for that side). What it does is temper how much weight ARC-083's notes should put on the Slaughter and Boh 2001 result as architecture-shaping evidence -- the paper that inverted the assumed PILLAR-1-before-PILLAR-4 ordering rests on a task family this entry shows is gaze-sensitive, and nobody has yet run the gaze-controlled version of the mother-versus-toy paradigm specifically.

Limitations: this is object-only (no person-search arm), so it cannot itself produce a decalage estimate under gaze control -- that experiment, as far as this pull could determine, has not been run. It also never cites or engages Jackson, Campos and Fischer (1978) directly; the connection to that specific 1978 objection is this pull's inference from the shared task family and the shared task-demand mechanism, not the source paper's own framing. Confidence is held to 0.55 for that reason: strong on its own claim (task-demand contamination of A-not-B is real and demonstrated with modern rigor), moderate on the specific question this pull was asked -- whether the person/object decalage survives task-demand control.
