# Sutherland & Mather (2012) — arousal amplifies saliency selectively

**Claim tested:** MECH-074b (content-selective per-trace weight, not scalar gain).
**Direction:** supports · **Confidence:** 0.60

## What the paper did

A randomized behavioural experiment testing the empirical prediction of arousal-biased competition directly. Participants heard a negative-arousing or a neutral sound, then briefly viewed a letter array in which some letters were made high-salience through visual contrast, and reported as many letters as they could (partial report). The question: does arousal lift recall uniformly, or does it sharpen the existing salience bias?

## Key finding relevant to the claim

Across sound types, high-salience letters were recalled better than low-salience letters (baseline saliency effect). Crucially, on **arousing** trials **recall of high-salience letters increased while recall of low-salience letters did not change**. Arousal did not add a constant to everything; it selectively amplified the items that already had priority. This is the empirical shape MECH-074b's per-trace weight is meant to have — a saliency-dependent multiplier (high arousal_tag → larger weight; low tag → baseline) — and it is inconsistent with a scalar gain that would raise high- and low-salience recall together.

## Mapping to REE and its limits

The *direction* of the mapping is exactly right: selectivity, not uniformity. But two mismatches keep confidence at 0.60. First, this is **short-term / attentional** memory for letter arrays measured seconds after presentation, whereas MECH-074b is about **long-term hippocampal retrieval** with a weight that *grows* over minutes to a week. Nothing here speaks to a weight applied to consolidated traces or to the trace-age growth claim. Second, low-salience recall was *unchanged*, not *impaired* — so this experiment demonstrates selective enhancement but not the absolute peripheral suppression that the lesion data (Adolphs 2001) and ABC theory both invoke. That leaves open the same additive-vs-competitive form question flagged in the Mather & Sutherland (2011) entry.

## Bottom line

A clean, controlled confirmation of the *selective* (non-scalar) character of arousal's effect on memory, weakened as MECH-074b evidence chiefly by the memory-system and timescale gap. It corroborates the design constraint without testing the retrieval-locus or long-timescale predictions.
