# Roozendaal & McGaugh (2011) — memory modulation: the consolidation-vs-retrieval challenge

**Claim tested:** MECH-074b (BLA applies a *positive, content-selective per-trace weight at retrieval time* that grows with trace age).
**Direction:** mixed (supports the encoding-side story, weakens the retrieval-time locus) · **Confidence:** 0.62

This is the disconfirming/mixed source the pull is built to include, and it comes from the field's own originators, so it is worth taking seriously.

## What the paper says

The review is the canonical synthesis of amygdala memory modulation. Its central, best-replicated claim is that emotionally significant experiences enhance the **consolidation** of newly acquired memories, and that this is "integrated through noradrenergic activation of the basolateral amygdala that regulates memory consolidation via interactions with many other brain regions." So far this *supports* the REE amygdala story broadly — and specifically MECH-074a, the encoding-time arousal_tag write.

The sting for MECH-074b is the next sentence: "In contrast to their enhancing effects on consolidation, adrenal stress hormones **impair** memory retrieval and working memory. Such effects, as with memory consolidation, require noradrenergic activation of the basolateral amygdala." Two things follow.

## Why this weakens MECH-074b specifically

**Locus.** MECH-074b places the content-selective weight at *retrieval*, applied to already-stored hippocampal traces. Roozendaal & McGaugh place the amygdala's well-established, selective, memory-*enhancing* action at **consolidation**. On the mainstream account, the content-selective gating REE wants is an encoding/consolidation phenomenon, not a retrieval-time one. MECH-074b's own functional_restatement even hangs its non-transience on "BLA contribution grows with trace age (20 min → 1 week)" — but a growing effect over that window is exactly what a *consolidation* process looks like, which is arguably evidence that the effect REE is modelling belongs at MECH-074a's encoding tag plus consolidation, not at a separate retrieval weight.

**Sign.** MECH-074b's rule is w_i = 1 + α·arousal_tag_i, α ∈ [0.3, 1.0] — a weight ≥ 1, i.e. a *positive* retrieval enhancement. The documented amygdala action *at retrieval* (via glucocorticoids) is *impairing*. A retrieval-time BLA signal that is net-negative is the opposite sign to what MECH-074b posits.

## Why it is "mixed," not a clean refutation

The retrieval impairment they describe is the acute glucocorticoid/stress-*at*-retrieval effect. That is not identical to the slow, standing, trace-age-growing content-selective weight MECH-074b imagines; the review does not test such a mechanism, so it constrains rather than excludes it. It is entirely possible that a distinct, non-stress retrieval-bias exists — but the burden is now on the follow-up EXQ to show a *positive, content-selective retrieval-time* effect that is separable from consolidation, because the default expectation from this literature is that (a) the selective benefit lives at consolidation and (b) acute amygdala engagement at retrieval hurts rather than helps.

## Implication for the claim's confidence

MECH-074b's *content-selectivity* (vector-not-scalar) is well supported by the other three entries. Its *retrieval-time positive-weight locus* is the weakly-grounded, partly-contradicted part. The honest reading is: build/keep the selective mechanism, but treat the retrieval-vs-consolidation locus as an open empirical question, and design the follow-up EXQ so it can dissociate a genuine retrieval-time bias from an encoding/consolidation effect — otherwise a PASS could be consolidation masquerading as retrieval.
