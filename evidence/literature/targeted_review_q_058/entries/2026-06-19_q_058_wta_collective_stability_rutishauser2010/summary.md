# Collective Stability of Networks of Winner-Take-All Circuits (Rutishauser, Douglas & Slotine 2010)

According to PubMed ([DOI 10.1162/NECO_a_00091](https://doi.org/10.1162/NECO_a_00091)), this *Neural Computation* paper provides the formal backbone for thinking about Q-058's first two sub-questions. The authors take the soft winner-take-all (WTA) circuit — recurrent excitation plus shared inhibitory feedback, the connectivity motif seen in the superficial layers of cortex — and ask how to reason about stability when many such competitive modules are densely distributed and interconnected. Using nonlinear contraction theory (verified numerically), they derive the parameter ranges in which a single WTA module is stable, and, importantly, show that stability of small modules can be composed: arbitrarily aggregated networks of well-characterised modules remain stable within derived bounds. They also show that the same machinery, pushed into a high-gain regime, becomes multistable and supports state-dependent persistent activity.

For Q-058 this is directly on target. The claim's first sub-question asks which inter-field couplings genuinely need an *explicit sign* versus what the existing local softmax/WTA (MECH-090/ARC-030/MECH-254) already buys. Rutishauser et al. answer the constructive half cleanly: a soft WTA built on shared inhibition *already* delivers selective amplification, signal restoration, and decision-making — the very functions REE wants from competition — without any explicit signed inter-field edge. That is a strong prior that REE should default to local softmax/WTA and reserve explicit signed couplings for cases where the local motif provably cannot produce the needed behaviour.

The second sub-question — the safety differences between competition modes — is where the paper's warning matters, and it is the reason I recorded two failure signatures. First, there is an irreducible trade-off: the feedback gain that makes a WTA circuit good at decisive selection is the same gain that threatens stability, so an REE design that simply *strengthens* competitive coupling to force conversion (e.g. to break an F-dominance or a non-conversion ceiling) can push the network into multistable or runaway persistent states. Second, composing many competitive fields is not free: while the authors give conditions under which aggregation is safe, those conditions are bounds — coupling competitive fields together can destabilise the whole even when each field is individually well-behaved. Both points argue that "add a stronger competitive coupling" is a stability expenditure, not a free lever.

I have set evidence_direction to **supports** with confidence 0.68. Mapping fidelity is high because REE's inter-field couplings genuinely are WTA/softmax-like, so the theory transfers at the computational level with low transfer risk. I held confidence below 0.8 honestly: this is stability theory and simulation on abstract modules, not a biological measurement, and it is silent on two things Q-058 explicitly asks about — decommitment and residue-based (harm-stream, SD-010/SD-011) veto as distinct competition modes, and how competitive coupling should differ across waking action, simulation (MECH-094) and offline integration. Those remain open and will need separate evidence.
