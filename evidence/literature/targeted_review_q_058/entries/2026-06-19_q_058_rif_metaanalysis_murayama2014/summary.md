# Forgetting as a Consequence of Retrieval: A Meta-Analytic Review of RIF (Murayama et al. 2014)

According to PubMed ([DOI 10.1037/a0037505](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037505)), this *Psychological Bulletin* paper is the first major meta-analysis of retrieval-induced forgetting (RIF), and its purpose is precisely the adjudication Q-058 needs: does the evidence favour an *inhibitory* account (an active mechanism that reduces the accessibility of competing non-target items) or *non-inhibitory* accounts (strength-based competition, blocking)? The headline result is that the literature *largely* supports inhibition — but with genuinely challenging evidence on the other side, and with the conclusion varying substantially according to how RIF is operationalised and measured (for instance, whether cue-independence tests are used).

This is the calibration entry for Q-058's inhibition-versus-competition question. Read alongside the Anderson, Bjork & Bjork (2000) anchor, it does two things. It confirms, at meta-analytic scale, that inhibition is a real and distinct mechanism — so REE is right to treat an explicit signed/inhibitory coupling as a live design option beyond the local softmax/WTA it already has (MECH-090/ARC-030/MECH-254). But it also resists the over-reading. The distinction is graded and measurement-dependent, not absolute; a non-trivial share of competitive-interaction findings are explicable by strength-based competition or blocking without invoking active inhibition at all.

The practical implication for REE — and the reason I logged both failure signatures — is that "which inter-field couplings genuinely need an explicit sign" should be treated as a *per-coupling empirical question*, settled by a discriminating test, rather than a blanket architectural rule. An REE decision to always install an explicit inhibitory edge would over-read this evidence; so would a decision to model all competition as local softmax, since that would miss the inhibitory cases the meta-analysis does support. The meta-analysis essentially hands REE a methodology hint: the same way RIF researchers use cue-independence to separate inhibition from blocking, REE should design discriminating experiments that distinguish a coupling needing a signed edge from one adequately served by softmax.

I have set evidence_direction to **mixed** with confidence 0.66, and I want to be explicit that "mixed" is the faithful reading, not a hedge — the meta-analysis genuinely lands on partial, measurement-contingent support for inhibition. Source quality is high (a quantitative meta-analysis in a top review venue), which underwrites the confidence, but mapping fidelity is moderate because this is human behavioural inference about laboratory paradigms, and it is silent on the parts of Q-058 concerning decommitment, residue-based (harm-stream) veto, and the waking/simulation/offline distinction. Those remain genuinely open after this pull.
