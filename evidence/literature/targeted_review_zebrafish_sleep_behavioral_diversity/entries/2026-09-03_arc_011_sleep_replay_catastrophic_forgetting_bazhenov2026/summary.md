# Bazhenov, Delanois & Krishnan (2026) -- Not Just After One: Sleep-Inspired Replay Prevents Catastrophic Forgetting After Sequential Tasks

**Verification note:** unreviewed arXiv preprint (2606.08447, submitted 7 Jun 2026, likely cs.NE). Verified against the arXiv abs page on 2026-09-03: title, three-author list, and abstract matched the citation as given.

## What the paper did

The standard playbook in continual learning is to protect old memories during or immediately after each new task -- some form of regularisation or rehearsal applied continuously. Bazhenov, Delanois, and Krishnan test a different, more biologically-inspired schedule: let a network train sequentially on several new tasks first, with no protection at all, and only then apply a single unsupervised sleep-like replay phase. The finding is that this deferred replay partially restores performance across all the previously learned tasks at once -- it does not need to happen after every task to do useful work. The paper is honest about the boundary of the effect, too: task-specific knowledge resists interference from the very next task reasonably well on its own, but that resistance is not unlimited -- knowledge gradually decays as still more tasks accumulate without an intervening replay phase. So the picture is a deferred consolidation window with real protective value, not a mechanism that makes forgetting simply stop happening.

## Findings relevant to ARC-011 and MECH-092

ARC-011 is REE's architectural commitment that offline integration (sleep) is required for stability, with its own operationalisation naming representation drift, policy/diversity erosion, and residue-field runaway as what offline integration is supposed to prevent. This paper is a computational existence proof of the same causal shape at a coarser grain and in a different substrate: omit offline replay across a sequence of tasks, and you get exactly the instability ARC-011 predicts (catastrophic interference); insert one replay phase, even deferred, and stability across the accumulated tasks is substantially restored. That is a genuine, if indirect, existence-level confirmation that removing offline integration produces the predicted failure and that offline integration is a real causal lever against it -- useful, given that ARC-011 itself notes it has essentially no experimental evidence of its own (0 genuine_exp entries; its extensive sleep experimental corpus all attaches to child mechanism claims instead). For MECH-092 (quiescent E3 heartbeat cycles trigger hippocampal SWR-equivalent replay for viability-map consolidation), the functional shape is directly analogous -- an offline replay phase consolidating prior structure against the interference of new experience -- though the mechanism that triggers replay differs importantly, as noted below.

## REE mapping and limitations

Two caveats matter more than the headline result. First, the restoration is explicitly partial, and the paper's own decay observation -- protection erodes as more tasks accumulate without further replay -- means this should be read as evidence for a deferred consolidation window with a real capacity boundary, not for the stronger claim that any single offline pass guarantees stability going forward. It supports ARC-011's qualitative claim (offline integration is required, its absence produces instability) without licensing an inference about how often or how completely that integration needs to happen. Second, and specifically relevant to MECH-092: replay here is a scheduled, externally-triggered training-phase intervention applied across discrete labelled tasks, not an intrinsic process the network enters on its own during quiescence. MECH-092's specific claim is that quiescent E3 heartbeat cycles themselves trigger the replay -- a paradigm where replay timing is a training-schedule parameter chosen by the experimenter does not test that triggering mechanism, even though it tests the functional consequence (consolidation benefit) reasonably well.
