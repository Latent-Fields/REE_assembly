# Falasconi, Kanodia & Arber 2025 — Dynamic basal ganglia output signals license and suppress forelimb movements

The question this entry answers is whether the *effector* for a maintenance-time motor-program release exists, and at what level. Falasconi and colleagues recorded single units in the substantia nigra pars reticulata — the principal output nucleus of the basal ganglia — while mice executed a skilled forelimb lever-press task, and added optogenetic perturbation to make the claim causal. What they find is that SNr output is not a tonic permission signal that simply gates whether movement is allowed; it is "highly granular and dynamic," tiling the execution of the task at the population level, with individual neurons showing movement-specific firing pauses *and* increases. Sixty percent of modulated units were bidirectionally modulated. The same neuron pauses to release one movement and increases firing to suppress that same movement when a different movement runs. And the pauses are causally necessary: optogenetic activation of SNr neurons sharply reduced executed lever presses and increased their latency.

For the MECH-090 routing this establishes the layer at which the missing release lives. The maintenance and cessation of an ongoing committed motor program is a continuous, granular, bidirectional basal-ganglia *output* function, operating in real time at the motor-program level — which is exactly where REE's beta gate sits in the control stack. This matters for the B2-versus-B3b fork. The goal-disengagement literature (Wrosch, Klinger, Brandstätter), already surveyed and instantiated as MECH-340 on the ghost-goal bank, describes a *slow, appraisal-driven, goal-level* abandonment — a days-to-minutes motivational construct about whether to give up on a goal. Falasconi shows that motor-program release is a *separate and faster substrate*: a moment-to-moment basal-ganglia-output computation about whether to keep executing the specific program now running. These are two different levels, and the 592f gap is at the lower one. That argues against treating the latch release as a mere readout of goal-level disengagement (option ii / B2) and for a distinct motor-latch substrate (B3b).

The caveat that holds confidence to 0.74 is that Falasconi study the *effector*, not the *trigger*. They show how basal-ganglia output releases and suppresses ongoing movement; they do not show that *degraded action-value or competence* is the upstream signal that commands the suppression. That linkage — that the commanding signal can be continued internal evidence or decisiveness rather than an external stop cue — is supplied by the Resulaj changes-of-mind entry and the Cavanagh STN-threshold entry. Read alone, Falasconi proves only that a maintenance-time motor-program release exists and is continuous and distinct from goal disengagement. Read with the other two, the picture closes: a real, distinct, motor-latch-level release effector (Falasconi), driven by continued internal decisiveness (Resulaj), regulated by the STN/beta circuit that also gates admission (Cavanagh).

One sign-convention warning worth recording, because it would mislead a naive implementer: in this paper a firing *pause* releases a movement and a firing *increase* suppresses it, movement-specifically. The abort of a committed program is therefore the targeted increase-firing suppression on that movement's channel, not a global brake. A REE release coupling modelled on a single non-specific "stop everything" signal would be the wrong abstraction; the biology is a targeted, channel-specific suppression.
