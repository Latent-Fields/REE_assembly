# Cavanagh, Frank et al. 2011 — STN stimulation reverses mediofrontal influence over decision threshold

If Resulaj tells us that biological commitment can be revised after it is launched, Cavanagh and Frank tell us *which circuit* does the regulating, and the answer is the one that matters most for MECH-090: the subthalamic nucleus — the basal-ganglia substrate that the beta gate models. They recorded scalp EEG from Parkinson's patients making high- versus low-conflict reinforcement-learning choices, fit a hierarchical drift-diffusion model, and found that trial-to-trial medial prefrontal theta scaled the decision threshold up in proportion to conflict (P = 0.01). Then they did the thing that makes this a causal entry rather than a correlational one: they turned STN deep-brain stimulation on. With DBS on, the mPFC-theta-to-threshold relationship reversed (P = 0.045), responses sped up, and choices became impulsive. Disrupt the STN and you remove the brake.

This is the entry that kills option (iii) — the reading that admission-only is the biologically correct commitment for the beta-gate latch and that the four existing release pathways are the complete set. Cavanagh and Frank show that the STN is not a one-shot admission gate that fires once and then sits inert while a commitment plays out. It is a continuously-modulated, bidirectional regulator that dynamically raises and lowers the commitment threshold as a function of ongoing conflict and decisiveness — Frank's "hold your horses." Crucially, when you take that dynamic regulation away, the system does not become *more* committed; it becomes *impulsively under-committed*. The normal state of the circuit is to keep adjusting how strongly the current commitment is held. An architecture that models the beta gate as admission-only has implemented a lesioned STN.

For the routing decision this is decisive in two directions. First, it argues that the maintenance-time release REE is missing belongs *at the R-c / beta-gate level itself*, not bolted on as a separate downstream module — because biologically the admission gate and the maintenance regulator are the same circuit. That is autopsy option (a) resurfacing, and the biology here specifically supports siting it there. Second, it reinforces the graded-not-flag reading: the STN modulates a continuous threshold over the deliberation window, it does not throw a categorical switch. A degraded-decisiveness release coupling that respects this biology would scale the hold/release tendency with ongoing `score_margin` and `nav_competence`, not trip on a counter.

The honest caveat is the one I have weighted into a 0.72 mapping fidelity. Cavanagh measures the threshold during pre-response deliberation — it gates how *long* the system waits before committing, which is closest to commitment *entry timing*. It does not directly record the release of an already-initiated motor program; that is the work Resulaj and the Falasconi BG-output entry do. What Cavanagh establishes, and establishes causally, is that the relevant circuit is dynamic and bidirectional rather than latch-only, and that it is the STN — REE's beta-gate substrate. The three entries compose: the gate is dynamic (Cavanagh), an already-launched action can be reversed by continued evidence (Resulaj), and basal-ganglia output bidirectionally licenses and suppresses ongoing motor programs in real time (Falasconi).
