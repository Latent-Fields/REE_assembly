# Wessel, Diesburg, Chalkley & Greenlee 2022 — A causal role for the human STN in non-selective cortico-motor inhibition

This entry is in the pull to keep the verdict honest, and it earns its place by ruling something out. The most obvious candidate answer to "what aborts a committed motor program?" is the classic stop-signal circuit — the prefrontal-to-STN-to-GPi-to-thalamus hyperdirect brake that the whole action-stopping literature is built around. Wessel and colleagues give the cleanest causal demonstration of that brake in humans: during a verbal stop-signal task they measured TMS-evoked cortico-spinal excitability and found that when a response was successfully stopped, excitability was suppressed *non-selectively* — the suppression spread beyond the stopped effector to even task-irrelevant motor representations. And it was causal: the effect disappeared when the STN was disrupted by deep-brain stimulation. The STN mediates a fast, global cortico-motor brake.

So the effector is real, it is causal, and it sits at the same basal-ganglia level as REE's beta gate. But two features of the canonical stop-signal brake are precisely *wrong* for the 592f gap, and that is the point of including this entry. First, the trigger. The stop-signal brake fires in response to an *external* cue or a surprising event — it is reactive to something in the world, not to an internal assessment that the agent's own action-value or competence has degraded. REE already has the analog of the external/surprise-triggered brake: that is MECH-091, the urgency-interrupt, which fires on a `z_harm_a` escalation spike. The 592f gap is explicitly the *other* trigger — an internally-driven, decisiveness-failure release in the absence of any threat spike. The canonical stop-signal pathway does not model that.

Second, the scope. The stop-signal brake is non-selective: it globally suppresses motor output, including channels that have nothing to do with the action being stopped. That is appropriate for an emergency "stop everything" reaction to a surprising signal. It is the wrong shape for what REE needs, which is a *targeted* release of the one committed program whose readiness has degraded, leaving other action channels intact. The Falasconi entry's finding that basal-ganglia output suppression is movement-specific is the complementary positive evidence here: the release REE wants is the targeted, channel-specific kind, not the global brake.

The honest read, then, is that this paper supports the *existence and identity* of the STN brake effector while weakening the hypothesis that the canonical stop-signal mechanism is the *answer* to the 592f question — which is why I have logged it mixed rather than supports. The relevant mode of the STN is not the reactive non-selective stop; it is the proactive, conflict-graded "hold your horses" mode, a distinct function of the same nucleus, and that mode is covered by the Cavanagh entry. The composite verdict the four entries support is therefore quite specific: REE's missing release is an *internally-driven, decisiveness-graded, targeted* maintenance-time release sited at the R-c/beta-gate level (Cavanagh, Resulaj), routed through the same basal-ganglia-output effector family that the stop-signal brake also uses (Wessel, Falasconi) — but it is not the non-selective, externally-cued stop-signal reflex, and it must not be implemented as one.
