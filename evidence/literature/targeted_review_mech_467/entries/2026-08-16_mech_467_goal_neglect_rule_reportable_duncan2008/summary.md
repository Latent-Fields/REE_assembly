# Duncan et al. 2008 -- goal neglect and Spearman's g

## The phenomenon

"In goal neglect, a person ignores some task requirement though being able to describe it." That sentence
is the paper's own, and it is close to a verbatim statement of MECH-467's leg 3. Someone who can tell you
the rule, correctly, on demand, and who then does not do it.

The experiments test what drives it. The answer is slightly counter-intuitive and it is the useful part:
neglect is not driven by moment-to-moment load. Increasing the real-time demands of one task component
does *not* promote neglect of another. What drives it is the complexity of the whole body of knowledge
specified in the instructions -- the size of the task model that has to be built before performance
begins. Duncan's proposal is that as that model grows, its components compete for representation, and
vulnerable ones are lost. The individual-differences arm ties the whole thing to fluid intelligence.

## What this does for MECH-467

It converts the claim's central assertion from conjecture into documented fact. MECH-467 says a system
can pass rule-corruption tests while failing behavioural capture. Goal neglect is fifty years of humans
doing exactly that, with the rule-integrity test being the most direct one available -- ask them, and
they tell you.

That matters because MECH-467's leg 3 is the leg "rule-state protection cannot catch by construction",
and a claim of that form invites the objection that the failure mode is a bookkeeping artefact. It is not.
It is a phenomenon with a name, a paradigm, and a correlation with g.

## Where it cuts against the claim, which is the more interesting half

Duncan's *mechanism* is not the mechanism MECH-467 has in mind. He does not say the rule survives intact
while action goes astray. He says task components are lost from the operative task model under
representational competition -- and what remains describable is a different thing from what remains
operative.

Put that next to MECH-467's PASS criterion: rule drift at floor while wrong-target selection is elevated.
If REE reads rule drift off a stored rule vector -- the analogue of asking the participant -- then Duncan's
account predicts you can get that exact signature from plain rule corruption in the selection path. Leg 2
wearing leg 3's clothes. The dissociation would be a property of where the probe was placed, not of the
substrate.

So the actionable finding for the successor battery is: instrument rule state *at the point of action
selection*, not at a storage site, or the PASS is uninterpretable. Given that V3-EXQ-874 already returned
a 0/0 denominator on leg (c) and the claim is owed a redesigned battery anyway, this is a cheap thing to
build in now rather than discover afterwards.

A second, smaller design note falls out of the load result. If neglect is driven by task-model complexity
rather than by real-time demand, then a battery that cranks perceptual distraction while keeping the rule
set small may simply never elicit behavioural capture -- and return a floor that reflects the design, not
the substrate. MECH-467's non-degeneracy guard watches for an unregistered distractor; it does not watch
for an under-complex rule set.

## Confidence

0.72. Strong evidence that the phenomenon exists and is robust, weaker evidence that MECH-467 has carved
it at the right joint -- and the paper is, if anything, more valuable for the second thing than the first.
