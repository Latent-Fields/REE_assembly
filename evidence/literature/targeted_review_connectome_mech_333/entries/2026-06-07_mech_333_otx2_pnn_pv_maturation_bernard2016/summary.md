# Parvalbumin maturation and perineuronal nets gate critical-period onset and closure (Bernard & Prochiantz 2016)

According to PubMed. Source: Bernard & Prochiantz, *Neural Plasticity* (2016), [DOI 10.1155/2016/7931693](https://doi.org/10.1155/2016/7931693) (PMC4736602).

## What the paper did

This is a review that consolidates the canonical mechanism MECH-333 invokes by analogy. The ability of the environment to shape cortical function is highest during postnatal critical periods. In the visual cortex, critical-period onset is triggered by the maturation of parvalbumin (PV) inhibitory interneurons, which gradually become surrounded by perineuronal nets (PNNs) — a glycosaminoglycan-rich extracellular matrix. The review focuses on how the homeoprotein Otx2, transferred specifically into PV interneurons, regulates both the *onset* and the *closure* of the critical period for binocular vision, and how the Otx2–PNN interaction continues to regulate plasticity into adulthood.

## Key findings relevant to the claim

The synthesis makes three points that bear on MECH-333. First, the *onset* of the plasticity window is gated on the maturation of an inhibitory element (PV interneurons) — before that maturation, the window does not open. Second, the *closure* of the window is enforced by an active structural brake (PNN consolidation around mature PV cells), and disrupting that brake can reopen plasticity. Third, a single molecular regulator (Otx2) controls both ends of the window — opening and closing are two thresholds on the same maturation machinery, not independent processes.

## How this maps to REE (MECH-333)

MECH-333 explicitly frames its plasticity window as "the REE analog of E/I balance shift and GABA maturation that opens biological critical periods," with the diversity/inhibitory circuit held at parity until winner-take-all capture is averted. This review supplies the canonical mechanistic backbone for that framing: inhibitory (PV) maturation as the onset trigger, and structural consolidation as the closure brake that later enforces stabilization. The "same machinery opens and closes" nuance is useful for REE — it predicts that whatever element gates the V3 plasticity window open should also be implicated in closing it, rather than the window simply decaying. And the closure-as-active-brake point aligns with MECH-333's "before winner-take-all capture" language: the biological system does not merely let plasticity fade, it actively clamps the circuit once the diversity phase has done its work.

## Limitations and confidence

This is a review, not a causal test, so it carries less independent weight than the two empirical entries it sits alongside. Much of its content — Otx2 transfer, PNN biochemistry — is molecular detail with no REE analog; only the higher-level "inhibitory maturation opens, structural consolidation closes" logic transfers. And the usual visual-cortex-to-REE transfer distance applies. Its value is as the consensus mechanistic frame that Priya et al. 2019 tests causally (GABA transmission necessary to open) and that Huang et al. 2015 partially contests (silent-synapse maturation governs duration largely E/I-independently). Read together, the three entries triangulate MECH-333's mechanism rather than rubber-stamp it. I score this **supports at 0.62**: source quality 0.7 (review), mapping fidelity 0.6, transfer risk 0.4.
