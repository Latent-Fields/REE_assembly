# Silent-synapse maturation, not E/I balance, governs critical-period duration (Huang et al. 2015)

According to PubMed. Source: Huang, Stodieck, Goetze, Cui, Wong, Wenzel, Hosang, Dong, Löwel & Schlüter, *PNAS* (2015), [DOI 10.1073/pnas.1506488112](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1506488112) (PMC4475980).

## What the paper did — and why it is logged as a counterpoint

This entry is included deliberately as the disconfirming-mechanism voice for MECH-333. MECH-333 grounds its plasticity window in "an E/I balance shift and GABA maturation that opens biological critical periods." The prevailing view the authors set out to test is exactly that — that the balance between excitation and inhibition determines critical-period onset and closure. Against this, Huang et al. show that maturation of *silent* glutamatergic synapses (NMDA-receptor-only synapses lacking functional AMPA receptors) onto principal neurons is sufficient to govern the *duration* of the ocular-dominance critical period, and that this is largely independent of the inhibitory tone.

## Key findings relevant to the claim

PSD-95 (postsynaptic density protein-95) was absolutely required for experience-dependent maturation of silent synapses. Removing PSD-95 before critical-period onset produced *lifelong* juvenile ocular-dominance plasticity — the window never closed. Removing PSD-95 in adult visual cortex, after normal closure, *reinstated* silent synapses and *reopened* juvenile-like plasticity. Crucially, this silent-synapse-based plasticity was largely independent of inhibitory tone, and the developmental maturation of inhibition was itself PSD-95-independent. Glutamatergic transmission onto parvalbumin interneurons was unaltered in the knockouts. The authors generalize: once silent synapses are consolidated in *any* neural circuit, that circuit's experience-dependent optimization — its critical period — ends.

## How this maps to REE (MECH-333)

Two messages, pulling in opposite directions. Supporting MECH-333's *structural premise*: there genuinely is a bounded, tunable plasticity window whose open/closed state is mechanistically controllable and even reversible — exactly the kind of object MECH-333 wants. Against MECH-333's *specific mechanism*: the controlling variable here is postsynaptic glutamatergic (silent-synapse / PSD-95) maturation, not inhibitory tone, and ocular-dominance plasticity was explicitly found to be largely E/I-independent. For REE this suggests the "window-open" state might be better modeled as a property of the *excitatory / scoring* pathway's own maturation — the consolidation of its representations — rather than (or in addition to) a separate inhibition/diversity-weight knob. That is a live alternative to MECH-333's framing worth holding open.

## Reconciling with Priya et al. 2019

There is an apparent tension with the companion entry (Priya et al. 2019), where GABAergic transmission was *necessary* to open a window. The cleanest reconciliation: inhibitory maturation gates the *onset* of the window, while glutamatergic silent-synapse maturation governs its *duration* and closure. Under that reading both mechanisms are real and operate at different phases — which would mean MECH-333's inhibition-centric framing captures the opening but under-describes the closing. That is a testable refinement rather than a refutation.

## Limitations and confidence

The result is from mouse visual cortex with the usual transfer distance to REE's policy/scoring crystallization. The mixed direction is a statement about MECH-333's *mechanism*, not its *existence*: it corroborates the bounded-tunable-window premise while challenging the E/I-specific gate. I score this **mixed at 0.6**: high source quality (0.85, strong venue, clean genetic dissociations), mapping fidelity 0.55. Logged as mixed for governance honesty — this is precisely the kind of disconfirming-mechanism signal MECH-333 should weigh before committing to an inhibition-only window model.
