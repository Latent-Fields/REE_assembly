# GABAergic transmission is necessary to open a critical period (Priya et al. 2019)

According to PubMed. Source: Priya, Rakela, Kaneko, Spatazza, Larimer, Hoseini, Hasenstaub, Alvarez-Buylla & Stryker, *Journal of Neuroscience* (2019), [DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1253-18.2019](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1253-18.2019) (PMC6445995).

## What the paper did

MECH-333 explicitly names its biological warrant: it is "the REE analog of E/I balance shift and GABA maturation that opens biological critical periods." This paper tests, causally, whether GABAergic transmission really is the trigger that opens such a window. The background is the Hensch-lineage finding that maturation of GABAergic inhibition gates the onset of the critical period for ocular-dominance plasticity (ODP), and the Alvarez-Buylla/Stryker finding that transplanting embryonic MGE-derived interneuron precursors into postnatal cortex can induce a *second* critical period whose timing is set by the transplanted cells' maturation. The mechanism of that induced plasticity was unknown. Here the authors transplanted MGE interneurons from either wild-type donors or donors lacking the vesicular GABA transporter (VGAT) — which cannot load GABA into synaptic vesicles — and asked whether the GABA-less cells could still open a plasticity window.

## Key findings relevant to the claim

VGAT-null transplanted interneurons dispersed, differentiated into somatostatin- and parvalbumin-expressing interneurons, and acquired mature electrophysiological properties — they integrated into the host circuit much like control cells (though with somewhat more complex morphology). But they *failed to induce* ocular-dominance plasticity, whereas control transplants did. The window-opening is therefore gated specifically on GABAergic *transmission*, not merely on the presence of inhibitory neurons. And, consistent with the broader transplantation literature, the timing of the induced plasticity is dictated by the maturation state of the transplanted GABAergic cells.

## How this maps to REE (MECH-333)

MECH-333 posits a time-bounded plasticity-open window in which the dominant deterministic pathway's effective update magnitude is suppressed (or the diversity circuit's weight amplified to parity) before winner-take-all capture, justified by analogy to GABA-maturation opening biological critical periods. Priya et al. provide direct causal support for the biological side of that analogy: functional inhibitory transmission is *necessary and sufficient-in-context* to open a bounded plasticity window, and that window is yoked to a developmental clock. The "presence is not enough — it must be transmitting" result is a useful design constraint: in V3 terms, instantiating a diversity/inhibitory element is insufficient unless it is functionally exerting its signal during the window. This dovetails with the crystallization-arm debugging history (V3-EXQ-610c/d, INV-074/MECH-333/334), where the failure mode was precisely that a gated element existed but was never actually exercised (the policy was never trained, expansion never stepped) — a structural echo of "the interneuron is there but cannot release GABA."

## Limitations and confidence

The paper cleanly evidences the *opening* of a plasticity window by inhibitory maturation; it does not directly evidence REE's specific framing of "suppressing the dominant scoring pathway's update magnitude" or "amplifying a diversity circuit to parity." That part of MECH-333 is inferred from the analogy rather than measured here. There is also the usual substantial transfer from mouse visual-cortex ocular dominance to REE policy/scoring crystallization. Still, this is a clean genetic loss-of-function dissociation in a strong venue, and it grounds MECH-333's central choice — inhibitory/GABA maturation as the gate that opens a bounded plasticity phase. I score this **supports at 0.72**: source quality 0.85, mapping fidelity 0.6, transfer risk 0.4.
