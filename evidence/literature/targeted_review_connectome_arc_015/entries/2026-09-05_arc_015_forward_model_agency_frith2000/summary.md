# Frith, Blakemore & Wolpert (2000) -- what breaks when self-impact attribution fails

## What the paper argues

This is the canonical statement of the comparator account of agency. The proposal is that the
primary cognitive deficit behind delusions of control is a failure in the mechanism that derives
the *predicted* consequences of an action from a forward model built on the intended motor
commands. The paper works through the behavioural consequences -- most importantly a loss of
central error correction -- and the physiological ones: delusions of control are associated with
over-activity in parietal cortex, which the authors attribute to a failure to attenuate responses
to sensations of limb movement that could have been anticipated from the intended movement. Their
proposed cause is long-range cortico-cortical disconnection preventing frontal inhibitory signals
from reaching sensory areas.

## Why it bears on ARC-015 as strongly as it does

ARC-015 does not say self-impact attribution is helpful. It says it is *required*. Modal claims of
that kind are hard to evidence, because the usual experimental move -- show the system works better
with the mechanism -- establishes usefulness, not necessity. Clinical evidence is the exception,
and this is what makes the schizophrenia literature unusually load-bearing here. We are looking at
a system in which the attribution machinery has actually failed, and what we see is not a graceful
degradation. We see a coherent syndrome: self-caused sensation processed as externally caused,
central error correction lost, and the phenomenology of one's own actions belonging to someone
else.

The second finding is the one I keep returning to. The failure removes central error correction --
the ability to correct an action against one's own predicted outcome rather than against an
externally observed one. That is a *control* deficit, not merely an experiential one. Self-impact
attribution is therefore doing work in the loop, not annotating it afterwards. For REE this is the
substrate reading of the MECH-095 comparator and of the channel separation in the MECH-060/061/
066/067 cluster: the predicted-consequence channel and the observed-consequence channel have to
stay distinguishable all the way to the point of comparison, because the comparison is what the
system runs on.

The third detail is easy to skip and worth keeping. When the mechanism fails, parietal cortex goes
*over*-active. The architecture without an attribution channel does not fall silent -- it
over-reports, treating everything as news. That is the signature to look for in an ablated E-stack,
and it is not the signature one would naively predict.

## Limits I want on the record

The paper's domain is sensorimotor self-attribution on a short horizon: limb movement, self-tickle,
the immediate sensory return of one's own body. ARC-015 asserts something wider -- responsibility
*flow*, attribution over extended causal chains and over consequences that are not sensory returns
at all. The step from efference-copy attenuation to responsibility flow is an extrapolation this
paper does not make and does not underwrite. The specific disconnection story has also aged less
well than the comparator framing it supports; later work has complicated the mechanism without
displacing the architecture.

There is also an epistemic reading of ARC-015 recorded in its own notes -- that this machinery
prevents self-caused, action-solicited evidence from being counted as independent confirmation of
the prior that produced the action. This paper is the sensorimotor half of that story. The formal
half is in the Perdomo et al. (2020) entry in this directory.

## Confidence

0.8. Canonical source, near-literal architectural mapping, and clinical failure evidence that
speaks to the necessity claim rather than merely a utility claim. Held below higher values because
of the horizon gap between sensory attenuation and responsibility flow, and because a 2000 review's
mechanistic specifics have moved on even where its framing has not.
