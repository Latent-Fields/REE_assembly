# Voss, Chambon, Wenke, Kuhn & Haggard (2017) -- attribution reads the selector, and can fail alone

## What the paper did

The authors introduce what they call a *prospective* component of agency. Sense of agency is
usually studied retrospectively -- did the outcome match what I predicted? Here, an action is
primed subliminally, and the question is whether the fluency with which the action was selected
influences the reported sense of agency over the outcome that follows. It does. Priming speeded
responses and it raised reported agency, and that agency effect was associated with altered
connectivity between frontal areas and the angular gyrus.

Then the patient comparison. In schizophrenia, the priming effect on *response times* was intact,
and the frontal action-selection effects were intact. What was missing was everything downstream of
that: no priming effect on reported agency, no priming-related angular gyrus activation, and no
priming-related change in fronto-parietal connectivity. The authors conclude that angular gyrus
activity constitutes the experience of agency in part by *processing* action-selection signals
generated in the frontal lobes, and that the altered action awareness in schizophrenia reflects
impaired communication between those areas.

## Why this is the entry I would keep if I could keep only one

Because it dissociates rather than correlates. The standing difficulty with evidence for ARC-015 is
that attribution machinery and action machinery normally co-occur, so most findings are compatible
with attribution being a downstream commentary on action rather than a separate channel with its
own failure mode. This study pulls them apart in the cleanest available way: the selection signal
is generated normally, and the attribution built on it is absent. Same input, missing consumer.

For REE that is direct evidence for two things ARC-015 commits to. First, channel separation --
the MECH-060/061/066/067 cluster's insistence that self-caused and externally-caused streams stay
distinguishable is not bookkeeping, it is a real architectural boundary that can be severed on its
own. Second, that the comparator (MECH-095) should be reading *selection-stage* information and not
only outcome information. The agency signal here is prospective: it derives from how the action was
chosen, before its consequences are known. A comparator wired only to outcomes could not produce
this effect at all.

The failure signature is worth stating flatly, because it is the one that would fool an evaluator:
a system can act correctly and attribute wrongly. Behavioural competence is not evidence that the
attribution channel is intact. If we ablate the attribution path in E3 and grade on task
performance, we will conclude nothing was lost.

## What I am not claiming

The dependent variable is a subjective agency rating. E3 has no phenomenology and files no reports,
so the experiential half of this result does not transfer at all. What transfers is the structural
half -- attribution consumes selection signals, and the consumer can fail while the producer is
fine. That is a weaker claim than the paper makes and it is the one I am relying on.

The usual caveats apply otherwise: a single patient-fMRI study, sample sizes to match, subliminal
priming with correspondingly modest effect sizes, and selection fluency is one cue to agency among
several rather than the mechanism entire.

## Confidence

0.74. The dissociation design carries most of the weight; the subjective-report dependent variable
and single-study status hold it where it is.
