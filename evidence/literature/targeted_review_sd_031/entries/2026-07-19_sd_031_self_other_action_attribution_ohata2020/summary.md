# Ohata et al. (2020) — biological warrant for giving z_world its own comparator

According to PubMed, this study ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhaa028)) asked a question with an unusually close structural resemblance to SD-031's design bet. The authors combined a paradigm in which self-agency *evolves gradually* with sensorimotor experience — rather than being switched on and off — with multivoxel pattern analysis, and then did the thing that makes the result useful: instead of stopping at "which regions carry agency information," they asked which regions *prefer* attribution information over sensorimotor information.

The answer is a dissociation. Self-other attribution was decodable from sensorimotor, posterior parietal, anterior insular and higher visual cortices. But right supramarginal gyrus — a portion of inferior parietal lobe — was the most sensitive to self-other attribution, while bilateral precentral gyri and left IPL dominantly reflected sensorimotor information. Attribution and the motor signal that produced the action are carried with different regional weightings.

That dissociation is the load-bearing finding for SD-031, and it is worth being precise about why. SD-031 is not primarily a claim that agency attribution exists; it is a claim about *topology* — that the world-consequence stream deserves its own comparator, separate from the harm comparator (SD-029) and the self comparator (SD-030), rather than having attribution read off the motor stream. A preference dissociation between an attribution-preferring parietal site and sensorimotor-preferring precentral sites is close to the biological form of that architectural argument. It also puts a name to the "parietal outcome-prediction circuits" that SD-031's functional_restatement already gestures at.

Three caveats, and the middle one is the substantive one. First, MVPA decodability establishes that attribution information is *present* in a region's BOLD patterns. It does not establish that the brain computes it as an explicit `observed - predicted` subtraction, which is the specific functional form SD-031 asserts. Plenty of codes are decodable without being computed that way. Second, the external consequence in this paradigm is the visually-observed motion of an object under partial participant control — a thin slice of what REE's z_world is meant to carry. SD-031's own examples ("I moved the block," "the door opened because of me") span a much wider range of non-harm world-state consequences, so this is a visuomotor special case rather than the general causal-footprint stream. Third, and easy to elide: their partition is self versus *another agent*, whereas SD-031's validation contrast is agent-caused versus *externally*-caused, where "external" in REE includes non-agentive world dynamics. Related partitions, not the same one.

The practical value is in how this constrains SD-031's pending validation rather than in whether it "confirms" anything. SD-031's `evidence_quality_note` already specifies the gate — world_dim >= 128, with ARC-065 behavioural diversity active so agent-caused and externally-caused events are balanced. Ohata et al. add a design lesson to that gate: attribution information was *distributed*, and it took a preference analysis to separate it from sensorimotor information. A validation that reads a single residual magnitude and finds a gap risks recording as "attribution" something that is partly the motor signal. The 2026-06-06 activation smoke (self-caused residual ~2.0 vs externally-caused ~22.6 at world_dim=128) is a magnitude contrast of exactly that vulnerable kind. Adding a discriminative readout that separates attribution-specific from action-specific variance — rather than only reporting the residual gap — would make the eventual result substantially harder to explain away, and this paper is the reason to build that in before the run rather than after.
