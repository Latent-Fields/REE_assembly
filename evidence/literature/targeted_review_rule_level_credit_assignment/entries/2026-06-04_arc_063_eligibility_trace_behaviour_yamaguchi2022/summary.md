# Yamaguchi et al. 2022 -- The eligibility trace has a behavioural, plasticity-gated correlate

**Claim touched:** ARC-063 element (iv). Companion to the Brzosko slice result.

## What the paper did
Using head-fixed Pavlovian conditioning with accurate measurement of effective reward timing, and optogenetic stimulation of nucleus-accumbens presynaptic activity, the authors showed that the reward-sensitive period for a brief tone is within ~1s of the tone and depends on plasticity-related signalling in the NAc -- in conformity with the synaptic eligibility trace measured in slice work. They frame this as support for using the synaptic eligibility trace to build biologically plausible reinforcement-learning models.

## Why it matters for ARC-063
This is the behavioural bridge for the credit mechanism. Brzosko shows the synaptic eligibility trace exists; Yamaguchi shows it has a measurable behavioural footprint -- a reward-sensitive window, plasticity-gated, in a reward hub. ARC-063's evidence-trace records presuppose that an outcome can be bound back to the event/rule that earned it within some creditable window; this paper confirms such a window is real and biologically implemented, not just a modelling convenience.

## The honest caveat
The window here is ~1s, tied to a sensorimotor cue in the NAc. ARC-063 needs credit assigned to *rules* over horizons that organise extended behaviour, which is both a longer timescale and a different (rule-level) target. So this grounds that an eligibility window *exists and is plasticity-gated*, while leaving the rule-level and long-horizon form as REE design hypothesis -- the same residual gap noted for the Brzosko entry.

## Confidence
0.64 -- supports. A solid behavioural-optogenetic confirmation that strengthens the mechanism's biological reality; discounted for the timescale and rule-target gaps.
