# Opendak, Meyer, Callaghan et al. (2025) — Ontogeny of the reward circuit

**Claim under test:** MECH-329 — the *mesolimbic dopamine* wanting system seeds z_goal anchors before the liking system is calibrated.
**Role in this pull:** the developmental reward-circuit review, and the honest stress-test of MECH-329's neuromodulatory specificity.

## What the paper argues

This is a nine-author review (Sullivan, Tottenham, Callaghan, Bath, Lee and colleagues) arguing that you cannot understand a reward circuit by reading infant data through the lens of adult circuitry. Reward processing has to be framed within *developmental transitions*: at each age, a distinct, age-specific network supports the adaptive behaviour that age requires, and these networks are not simply immature versions of the adult one.

Their sharpest illustration concerns the perinatal period. Dopaminergic projections "exhibit late functional maturation," and are "likely not functional in supporting learning in the perinatal infant" — even though classical reward conditioning is robustly demonstrable in that window. What carries early reward learning instead is norepinephrine: "copious amounts of norepinephrine (NE) from the locus coeruleus (LC) flood the olfactory bulb (OB) when odors are paired with cues mimicking maternal care." Their methodological warning is the line worth pinning to the wall: "behavioral similarity does not imply circuit continuity."

## Why it matters for MECH-329 — both directions

**Supports the ordering.** MECH-329's temporal core is that functional approach/reward behaviour operates early, while a mature, calibrated evaluation comes later. This review is built on exactly that premise — reward learning is operational in infancy through age-specific circuitry, before the adult circuit is in place. The "wanting operational early, calibrated evaluation lags" framing is consistent with the developmental-transitions picture.

**Stresses the mechanism — and this is the more important half.** MECH-329 does not just claim early reward; it names *mesolimbic dopamine* as the seeding signal. This review directly complicates that for the earliest window: mesolimbic DA matures late and is "likely not functional" perinatally, with the first reward learning running on locus-coeruleus norepinephrine. So if MECH-329's seeding is meant to begin in the earliest accidental-contact window, attributing it specifically to mesolimbic dopamine is over-committed. The ordering survives; the neuromodulatory label should be held loosely — or generalised to "an early approach/incentive neuromodulator" — for the first phase.

It also sharpens the caveat already flagged on the Behne and Corbetta entries. Both infer an early approach/goal mechanism from infant *behaviour*. "Behavioral similarity does not imply circuit continuity" is precisely the warning that early appetitive behaviour can look adult-like while being driven by different circuitry — so the behavioural anchors ground the *ordering* but should not be over-read as confirming the adult-style *mesolimbic mechanism*.

## Limitations

The strongest mechanistic ontogeny evidence (olfactory-bulb NE, perinatal odour conditioning) is rodent, so mapping it onto the human-infant wanting system MECH-329 describes carries both a species and a developmental-window transfer. As a review it sets a framework rather than delivering one decisive ordering experiment.

## Confidence

0.55, direction **mixed**. High source quality and high mapping relevance — it is squarely about the ontogeny of the reward circuit MECH-329 invokes. Held at 0.55 by the mixed direction (supports ordering, weakens mesolimbic specificity) and the rodent-perinatal-to-human transfer in its strongest claims. The governance takeaway: keep MECH-329's *ordering*, but treat its *mesolimbic-dopamine* attribution for the earliest seeding window as provisional.
