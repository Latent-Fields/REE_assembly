# Corbetta (2021) — Perception, action, and intrinsic motivation in infant reaching

**Claim under test:** MECH-329 — the wanting system seeds z_goal anchors via *accidental* benefit contacts under approach drive, before the liking system is calibrated.
**Role in this pull:** the closest verifiable analogue to MECH-329's actual seeding mechanism.

## What the paper says

Corbetta synthesises the perception-action literature on how infants learn to reach. Her central claim is that goal-directed reaching is not pre-wired and does not start from a represented preference. It emerges, around 3–5 months, from a *casual contingent event*: "when the hand happens to contact a target." That accidental contact "provides new behavioral value," "reinforces the motor action, and intrinsically motivates infants to attempt to reproduce the behavior." Subsequent "repeated cycles of perception and action lead to the exploration of a range of motor responses," through which the reach is gradually refined.

The originating event is an accident; what converts it into a repeated, goal-directed behaviour is an intrinsic motivation/approach signal, not a calibrated evaluation of the outcome's hedonic worth.

## Why it matters for MECH-329

This is MECH-329's seeding story told in the human motor-development literature. MECH-329 says the first layer of the goal hierarchy is "structured by exploration-drive and accidental outcomes rather than by any calibrated preference function," with z_goal anchors written from "the earliest accidental benefit contacts." Corbetta's account matches that structure point for point: (1) the originating event is *accidental* (hand-on-target by chance); (2) what latches onto it is an *intrinsic approach/motivation* signal, not a mature preference; (3) repetition and refinement follow, building structured goal-directed behaviour out of the accident.

It also fills a timing gap left by the Behne et al. (2005) entry. Behne dates *third-person* goal-attribution understanding to ~9 months; Corbetta places the *first-person* accidental-contact-plus-motivation seeding earlier, at 3–5 months — closer to the Phase-0/early-Phase-1 window MECH-329 cares about.

## Limitations and the honest mapping caveat

Two caveats keep this at supporting-but-not-decisive. First, Corbetta's "intrinsic motivation" is a behavioural/motor-development construct; she does not identify it with mesolimbic-dopamine incentive salience. So the entry supports the *accidental-contact + approach-drive structure* of MECH-329 — but not the narrower claim that the seeding signal is specifically *mesolimbic dopamine*. That neuromodulatory-identity question is exactly where the Opendak et al. (2025) entry pushes back (arguing the mesolimbic DA system is not yet functional in the perinatal infant, with early reward learning running on norepinephrine instead). Second, this is a conceptual review, not a controlled test of the ordering; it asserts a developmental sequence consistent with wanting-before-liking rather than measuring it head-to-head.

## Confidence

0.58. Good source quality; moderate-to-good mapping fidelity (the accidental-contact-plus-intrinsic-motivation structure maps tightly; the neuromodulatory identity is unpinned); moderate transfer risk (review-level synthesis). Together with Behne 2005 it grounds the *developmental ordering* and the *accidental-seeding* structure; the mesolimbic-specific framing remains the part to hold loosely.
