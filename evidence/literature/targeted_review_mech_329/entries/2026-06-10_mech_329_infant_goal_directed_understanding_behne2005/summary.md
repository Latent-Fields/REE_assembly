# Behne, Carpenter, Call & Tomasello (2005) — Unwilling versus unable

**Claim under test:** MECH-329 — the wanting system (mesolimbic dopamine / approach motivation) seeds z_goal anchors via accidental benefit contacts *before* the liking system (hedonic evaluation) is calibrated.
**Role in this pull:** the verifiable replacement for the unverifiable "Keren-Portnoy & Tomasello 2021" anchor.

## What the paper did

Behne and colleagues handed toys to infants at 6, 9, 12, and 18 months. On some trials the transfer failed because the adult was *unwilling* (she teased the infant, or played with the toy herself); on others it failed because she was *unable* (she accidentally dropped it, or her hand was blocked). The outcome for the infant — no toy — was identical; only the adult's underlying intention differed. The measure was the infant's reaction: impatience, reaching, looking away, turning from the interaction.

From 9 months onward, infants reacted with reliably more impatience when the adult was unwilling than when she was unable. Six-month-olds showed no such differentiation. The authors read this as the first behavioural signature of infants understanding *goal-directed action* — they are not tracking the surface outcome but the actor's intention — and date that onset to around 9 months.

## Why it matters for MECH-329

MECH-329's load-bearing and thinly-evidenced half is a *developmental-ordering* claim: that approach/goal structure is operational early, before a mature hedonic preference function has been calibrated by repeated evaluation. The original claim cited "Keren-Portnoy & Tomasello 2021" for "infant proto-intentional reach precedes preference discrimination." That citation could not be independently verified — Marilyn Keren-Portnoy publishes in infant speech and phonological development, not reward or intentionality — and the MECH-189 biology-before-formal-definitions pass flagged it.

Behne et al. 2005 is the right anchor in its place. It is peer-reviewed, canonical, well-replicated, and comes from exactly the research lineage (Behne/Carpenter/Call/Tomasello) that the bogus citation gestured at. It establishes the temporal premise MECH-329 needs: goal-directed, intention-attributing structure is present and behaviourally measurable in the first year of life — long before the slow, repeated-exposure work of calibrating a stable preference function is complete.

## Limitations and the honest mapping caveat

The fidelity gap is that this study measures *third-person* goal attribution — how infants read an adult's intentional action — not the *first-person* approach drive that MECH-329 says writes the infant's own goal anchors. I am using early goal-directed *competence* as a proxy for early approach-*motivation* operability. The two plausibly co-develop, but the paper does not license the stronger mechanistic reading on its own, and if they dissociate developmentally the anchor weakens. There is also a window mismatch: "around 9 months" is a first-year milestone, later than the perinatal/Phase-0 "earliest accidental benefit contacts" framing in MECH-329's strongest wording. For the mesolimbic-specific and perinatal-timing parts of the claim, see the Corbetta (2021) entry (accidental-contact reaching mechanism) and the Opendak et al. (2025) entry (which complicates the mesolimbic-dopamine specificity in early infancy).

## Confidence

0.6. High source quality; moderate mapping fidelity (competence-as-proxy-for-motivation); moderate transfer risk (third-person-vs-first-person and timing-window gaps). This is the developmental-ordering anchor's solid lower bound — it makes "goal structure is early" verifiable — not a confirmation of the full mesolimbic-wanting-first mechanism.
