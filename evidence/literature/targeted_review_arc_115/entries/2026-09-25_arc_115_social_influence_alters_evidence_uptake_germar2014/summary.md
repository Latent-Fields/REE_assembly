# Germar et al. (2014) -- Social influence and perceptual decision making: a diffusion model analysis

**What they did.** Asch-style majority influence, taken apart with a drift-diffusion model. Participants made speeded perceptual colour judgments while seeing what a majority had supposedly chosen. The diffusion model separates two ways the majority could act. It could shift the *decision criterion* (a bias in where you set the bar), or it could change the *drift rate* (how the incoming sensory evidence itself is taken up).

**What they found.** Across two experiments, social information changed the drift rate and left the criterion alone. The others' opinion did not just tip the final report. It changed how the stimulus evidence was accumulated. The same group later replicated and extended this with ERPs (larger early N1 amplitudes and biased lateralised readiness potentials even on non-conforming trials; Germar et al. 2016) and with psychophysical shifts in the point of subjective equality (Germar et al. 2023).

**What it means for ARC-115, and why it cuts both ways.** ARC-115 says socially supplied agreement must not enter the accumulator that internal evidence feeds, "else being told something becomes indistinguishable from having found it." Germar's data show that in humans it does enter it. Social input lands at the evidence-uptake stage, the one stage a separate social channel would keep it out of. As a claim about biology, then, ARC-115 is weakened: brains do not keep the channels apart in the way the claim demands. As a claim about what collapse costs, it is strongly supported. The measured consequence is people seeing the stimulus differently because others said so, which is ARC-115's warning in miniature. A psychiatrist will think at once of folie a deux and of how a confident informant reshapes a patient's account of their own experience.

**What this means for the claim.** ARC-115 should be stated explicitly as a normative design constraint for REE (and as a candidate architectural account of conformity and suggestibility), not as "brains separate these channels". The dissociation falsifier stays the right test. This paper is effectively the human data for the collapsed control arm.

**Confidence.** 0.5, mixed, with moderate mapping: choice rather than confidence readouts, and perceptual rather than propositional content.
