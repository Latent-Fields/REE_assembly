# Toelch & Dolan (2015) -- Informational and normative influences in conformity from a neurocomputational perspective

**What it is.** A Trends in Cognitive Sciences review that rebuilds the old social-psychology distinction between *informational* and *normative* influence in decision-model terms. Informational influence treats others' choices as evidence about the world, and it helps you be right. Normative influence pushes you toward what gets you accepted, whether or not it is right. The authors place both inside drift-diffusion and reinforcement-learning models and reread Asch and Sherif through them. They then survey the neural literature and conclude it has largely *failed to segregate* the two.

**Why it matters for ARC-115.** ARC-115 is a non-collapse claim. A scalar confidence that mixes internally derived evidence with socially supplied agreement loses information you later need. Toelch and Dolan make the same complaint about the field's treatment of social influence: two distinct drivers get measured as one, and the science suffers for it. It also sharpens the design implication. The social-agreement channel ARC-115 asks for should probably not be a single scalar either. It should keep "they have information I lack" apart from "agreeing with them is socially safe". Only the first has any business being weighed against internal evidence. The second is the sycophancy route that EXT-001 names.

**The important concession.** The review shows that a purely *informational*, rational agent is expected to conform surprisingly often. So some movement in internal confidence after social agreement is not, by itself, evidence of a collapsed architecture. ARC-115's falsifier is correctly built around dissociation of readout profiles rather than around whether social input moves confidence at all.

**Confidence.** 0.5, supportive but theoretical. It notes that clean empirical segregation is still missing, and its cut (informational vs normative) only partly overlaps ARC-115's (internal vs social).
