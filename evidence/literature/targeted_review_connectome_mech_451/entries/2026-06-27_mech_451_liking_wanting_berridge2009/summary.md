# Dissecting reward: 'liking', 'wanting', and learning (Berridge, Robinson & Aldridge, 2009)

**Claim under test:** MECH-451 — the intermediate channel-granularity falsifier. This canonical review grounds the "hedonic liking" channel as distinct from the dopaminergic/vigour channel rather than collapsing both into one value signal.

## What the paper did

This is the canonical synthesis of the position that reward is not a unitary quantity but decomposes into three dissociable components: "liking" (the hedonic impact of a reward), "wanting" (incentive salience, the motivational pull toward it), and learning (the predictive associations that accrue). Each has partly separable neural substrates, established across a body of rodent pharmacology and lesion work with some human corroboration.

## Key finding relevant to the claim

The load-bearing dissociation is between liking and wanting. Dopamine manipulation drives "wanting" but *not* "liking" — stimulating dopamine in hedonic hotspots fails to enhance the hedonic reaction — and "liking" itself is generated by a restricted set of opioid/GABA hotspots separate from the broader dopaminergic incentive-salience system. For MECH-451 this is direct evidence that hedonic liking is a *separately instantiated* channel, doubly dissociable from the motivational/dopaminergic dimension. It supports treating "liking" as one of the finer channels rather than folding it into a single compressed value or into the vigour/dopaminergic channel (which Niv et al. ground separately in this same pull).

## Mapping to REE

The translation requires honesty about altitude. This review establishes *neurochemical and anatomical* dissociation — these are different channels in the brain. It is silent on whether a learner can *separately learn* them, and silent on whether finer granularity *improves conversion* of control influence into committed action. Mapping a hedonic-hotspot dissociation onto a learned gating layer's separable input is a substantial abstraction, which is why mapping_fidelity is the lowest in the MECH-451 set (0.5).

## Caveats and confidence

Two caveats double as concrete predictions for how the MECH-451 falsifier might fail. First, "liking" is shown to be *hard to move* — dopamine and most manipulations fail to enhance it, and it lives in small, fragile hotspots. A liking channel may therefore be low-variance and weakly action-coupled, exactly the profile of a channel a gating learner would down-weight toward zero, reproducing rather than relieving a conversion ceiling. Second, the components normally co-occur and interact; clean separability is a laboratory dissociation, not the default operating regime, so a learner may rarely encounter the states that make the liking channel informative. Logged **supports** (the channel-distinctness premise is well-evidenced) at confidence 0.5, predominantly rodent, with both caveats recorded as failure signatures.
