# Thomson (1985), *The Trolley Problem* -- the causal structure of harm matters

## What the source argues

Judith Jarvis Thomson's *The Trolley Problem* sharpened a puzzle inherited from Philippa Foot. Compare two cases with the *same* outcome -- one person dies, five are saved. In **Bystander at the Switch**, a bystander diverts a runaway trolley onto a side track where it kills one workman instead of the five ahead; most people judge this permissible. In **Transplant** (and in the later **Footbridge/push** variant), a surgeon could kill one healthy person to distribute organs and save five patients; almost everyone judges this impermissible. If only outcomes mattered, the cases would be on a par. They are not.

Thomson's diagnosis turns on two structural distinctions. The **Doctrine of Doing and Allowing** holds that doing harm is harder to justify than merely allowing it. More decisively, the **means/side-effect** distinction: in Bystander the one workman's death is a *foreseen side-effect* of diverting the threat, whereas in Transplant and Footbridge the victim's death is *used as a means* -- the plan requires it. Killing someone as a means to save others treats them as a mere instrument in a way that a like-lethal side-effect does not. The paper is a rigorous demonstration that *how* a harm is causally produced, not just how much harm results, carries moral weight.

## Why it bears on MECH-129

MECH-129's fourfold discriminant structure includes "(3) whether the obstruction is incidental or constitutive." This is not a natural-language flourish; it is a claim that the *causal role* of another agent's goal-failure in my action changes what kind of harm-to-agency (if any) I have done. Thomson's means/side-effect distinction is that discriminant in its most carefully argued form. A **constitutive** obstruction -- where the other agent's goal-failure is a means to, or the very point of, my action -- is the interpersonal analogue of using the trolley victim as a means. An **incidental** obstruction -- where their goal-failure is a foreseen by-product of my pursuing my own independent goal -- is the analogue of the side-effect death in Bystander. Thomson gives REE the philosophical warrant that this incidental/constitutive cut is real and *outcome-independent*: two actions can impose the identical goal-cost on another agent and yet be different harms. That is exactly why MECH-129 argues obstruction must be represented *with structure* (a typed relational signal) rather than folded into a scalar cost.

## Limits of the mapping, honestly

This entry is deliberately held at 0.58, the lowest in the pull, because the transfer is by *analogy of structure*, not shared subject matter. Every trolley case is about harm-to-**agent** -- death, the paradigm bodily cost. So Thomson is not direct evidence for the harm-to-agency *category*; it is evidence for one *discriminant* (incidental vs constitutive) that MECH-129 imposes on obstruction, and the discriminant happens to have been worked out on lethal-harm cases.

Two further cautions. First, the means/side-effect and doing/allowing distinctions presuppose **intent-attribution and plan structure**: to know whether an obstruction was a means or a side-effect you must model the *obstructing agent's own plan* and the causal role the other's failure plays in it. A goal-interference signal computed purely over trajectory geometry -- did my path raise the cost of yours -- cannot recover this. So Thomson simultaneously establishes that discriminant 3 is real *and* implies it is **not computable from the obstruction magnitude alone**; REE needs a self-model of plan structure to type an obstruction as constitutive. Second, the trolley intuitions are contested even by their author: Thomson herself in 2008 ("Turning the Trolley") repudiated the permissibility verdict on Bystander. A substrate that hard-codes discriminant 3 as a crisp binary is inheriting a distinction whose boundary cases the field has not settled. The direction is still **supporting** -- the causal structure of harm is load-bearing, which is MECH-129's point -- but the support is for the discriminant's *reality*, tempered by the difficulty of *operationalising* it.
