# Turner, Hadfield-Menell & Tadepalli (2020), *Conservative Agency via Attainable Utility Preservation* -- harm-to-agency, formalised

## What the source does

Reward functions are easy to misspecify, and an agent optimising a misspecified reward can make *irreversible* changes before a designer notices and corrects it. **Attainable Utility Preservation (AUP)** addresses this by adding, alongside the primary reward, a penalty on **impact**, where impact is defined as the change an action causes to the agent's **attainable utility** for a set of *auxiliary* reward functions -- roughly, how much the action alters the agent's ability to still achieve a *range* of goals from where it now stands. The striking empirical result is that even when the auxiliary reward functions are **randomly generated** -- carrying no information about the true objective -- penalising changes to their attainable utility suffices to make the agent conservative: on the AI-safety gridworld side-effect benchmarks it avoids irreversible damage (breaking vases, boxing itself in) while still completing its task. Preserving attainable utility preserves *option value*, and option value is what a later correction needs.

## Why it bears on MECH-129

Of everything in this pull, AUP maps onto MECH-129 at the finest grain, because it is at REE's own level of description -- a *computable signal*, not a normative principle. MECH-129 asserts that harm-to-agency is a **distinct signal type requiring a distinct architecture**, computed not over the agent's own cost stream but over the obstruction of goal-pursuit. AUP makes the missing quantity precise: **agency, formalised, is attainable utility** -- the ability to still reach a range of goals -- and **harm-to-agency is a reduction in attainable utility**. AUP then demonstrates three things MECH-129 needs to be true: that this quantity is (i) *computable*, (ii) *meaningfully separate* from the primary task reward, and (iii) *effective* at suppressing exactly the irreversible, goal-foreclosing changes that agency-harm consists in. This is the formal shell of the second harm signal MECH-129 proposes adding to E3 -- a signal over reachability and option sets, sitting beside, not inside, the harm-to-agent cost channel.

It is worth noting the co-author overlap with the existing goal-inference cluster in this directory: Dylan Hadfield-Menell is also the senior author of Cooperative Inverse Reinforcement Learning, the framework the Baker (2009) inverse-planning entry connects to. The two halves fit: CIRL/inverse-planning supply *how you infer another agent's goal*; AUP supplies *how you measure the reduction in their ability to reach it*. Together they sketch the substrate MECH-129 requires -- other-agent goal modelling feeding an attainable-utility impact signal.

## Limits of the mapping, honestly

Held at 0.72 -- the highest confidence in this pull, but with a specific structural caveat that stops it going higher. As **published**, AUP measures the agent's impact on **its own** attainable utility over auxiliary rewards. That is a self-referential *reversibility / low-impact* proxy: keep your own options open so you can be corrected. Harm-to-agency in MECH-129's sense is about **another agent's** ability to reach **their** goals. Turning AUP into a harm-to-agency signal therefore means **re-pointing the measure at another agent's attainable utility**, which requires that agent's (inferred) reward set. AUP hands REE the signal *form* -- an impact measure over attainable utility -- but not the **other-agent goal model** that MECH-129 lists as requirement (a); compose the two and you have a harm-to-agency signal, use AUP alone and you have penalised the *wrong agent's* optionality loss.

Two further cautions. AUP's measure is **sensitive to the choice of auxiliary reward set and baseline** (stepwise vs inaction comparison). Turner et al. show random auxiliaries suffice for generic *low-impact*, but they do **not** establish that any auxiliary set faithfully tracks a *specific* other agent's goal -- so an other-directed harm-to-agency signal built on AUP inherits an unresolved auxiliary/baseline-selection problem, the same counterfactual-baseline issue flagged in the Feinberg entry in this directory. And validation is on **small gridworlds**; the scaling and the faithful construction of other-agent auxiliaries are open. The direction is firmly **supporting** -- AUP proves a distinct, computable agency-impact signal is buildable and behaviourally effective -- while marking exactly the gap (self -> other re-pointing) that MECH-129's own requirement (a) is written to fill.
