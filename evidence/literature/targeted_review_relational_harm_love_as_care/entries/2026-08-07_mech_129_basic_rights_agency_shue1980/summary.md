# Shue (1980), *Basic Rights* -- agency as a foundational protectable interest

## What the source argues

Henry Shue's *Basic Rights* set out to defeat a then-standard move in political philosophy: that "negative" rights (to be left alone -- security) are real and cheap while "positive" rights (to provision -- subsistence) are aspirational and dispensable. Shue argues, first, that some rights are **basic** in a precise sense: enjoying them is a *necessary condition* for enjoying any other right at all. If your physical security is not protected, your right to free speech is worthless, because anyone can silence you by force; if your subsistence is not met, likewise. Security and subsistence are therefore the two paradigm basic rights -- not the most valuable rights, but the ones without which no other right can be exercised.

Second, and more influentially, Shue dissolves the negative/positive dichotomy. **Every** basic right, he argues, generates a *threefold* set of correlative duties: duties to **avoid depriving** people of the right, duties to **protect** them from deprivation by others, and duties to **aid** those who have been deprived. Even the "negative" right to security implies positive duties to build police, courts, and protective institutions. A single interest thus fans out into structurally different obligations, and no one of them alone honours the right.

## Why it bears on MECH-129

MECH-129 requires "agency" to be the sort of thing whose *obstruction* is a distinct and weighty category of harm, not merely one more entry in a cost ledger. Shue's notion of a **basic right** is exactly a protectable interest that is foundational *because it conditions the pursuit of everything else*. The standing capacity to pursue one's goals -- agency -- is basic in precisely Shue's sense: obstruct it systematically and every particular goal becomes unreachable. That grounds the normative weight MECH-129 places on harm-to-agency as a category apart from harm-to-agent.

Shue's tripartite-duty analysis speaks even more directly to MECH-129's *architectural* thesis. The claim is not merely that harm-to-agency exists but that it "requires distinct architectures." Shue shows, in the human case, that a single interest (here, agency) generates obligations that *cannot be discharged by one mechanism*: not-obstructing, preventing others' obstruction, and helping the already-obstructed are structurally different tasks. A REE substrate that only penalises the agent's *own* obstructive trajectories implements at most Shue's first duty (avoid depriving) and remains blind to the other two. So Shue independently motivates MECH-129's insistence that harm-to-agency is architecturally richer than a re-used aversive cost signal.

## Limits of the mapping, honestly

Held at 0.6. Shue's project lives in the political philosophy of human rights and the correlative duties of institutions and persons; the distance to a per-trajectory goal-interference signal in an agent substrate is several inferential steps, and I have not pretended otherwise. Two specific mismatches keep the fidelity moderate.

First, Shue's basic rights are **standing capacities** -- enduring security and subsistence -- whereas MECH-129's harm-to-agency is frequently about a *specific in-flight goal*. These coincide only when the obstruction threatens the *enduring* capacity for goal-pursuit, not a momentary or competitive aim. An unweighted obstruction signal that treats every blocked micro-goal as a basic-right deprivation over-counts: much everyday goal-interference is trivial or consented-to competition, which Shue's framework would never dignify as a basic-rights violation. This is a genuine calibration warning for any substrate that reads "obstruction detected" as "harm-to-agency."

Second, Shue's **protect and aid** duties have no natural counterpart in a purely aversive avoidance signal. If harm-to-agency is modelled only as "penalise my own obstructive acts," the substrate structurally cannot represent duties to *prevent* a third agent's obstruction or to *assist* an already-obstructed agent -- duties Shue argues are exactly as basic as non-obstruction. So Shue both *supports* MECH-129 (agency is a distinct, foundational, architecturally-demanding interest) and *raises the bar* on what a complete harm-to-agency architecture would have to include. The direction of evidence is **supporting**, with the caveat that Shue implies MECH-129's own sketch (a goal-interference signal integrated into E3) is a first duty of three.
