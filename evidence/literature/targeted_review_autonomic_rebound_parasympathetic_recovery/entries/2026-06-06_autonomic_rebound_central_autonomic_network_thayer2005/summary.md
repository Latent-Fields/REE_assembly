# Central autonomic network: an endogenous parasympathetic-recovery driver (Thayer & Brosschot 2005)

According to PubMed. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psyneuen.2005.04.014) -- *Psychoneuroendocrinology* 30(10):1050-8 (review).

**What the paper argues.** Using the neurovisceral-integration model, Thayer & Brosschot describe
a central autonomic network (CAN) -- overlapping prefrontal executive, affective, and attentional
circuitry -- that regulates autonomic balance through a reciprocal inhibitory cortico-subcortical
circuit. Prefrontal activity actively maintains parasympathetic (vagal) tone and restrains
defensive/sympathetic circuits; when the prefrontal cortex is taken "offline," that inhibition is
withdrawn and a relative sympathetic dominance is released. Low heart-rate variability indexes this
prefrontal-parasympathetic restraint failing, and chronic failure is treated as pathogenic.

**Why it matters for the candidate claim.** The single most important question for registering an
endogenous-rebound claim separately from social soothing (MECH-355) is: *is there an internal,
central driver of recovery, or is recovery only ever externally cued?* This review is the canonical
statement that the driver is **endogenous and central** -- a prefrontal-vagal restraint system that
operates without any external stimulus or conspecific. That is exactly what the candidate needs:
its trigger can be the agent's own offset-detection (the `z_harm_a` derivative going negative), and
its eventual driver can sit alongside REE's prefrontal-analog control (ARC-016 precision, SD-033a
lateral-PFC), with no social input required.

**How it maps to REE.** It locates the recovery driver, not the update rule. It tells us the REE
rebound mechanism belongs to the *endogenous control* family (distinct from MECH-355 soothing,
whose driver is a conspecific, and from MECH-303/304 safety, a learned predictor over a horizon).
The minimal V3 form does not need to instantiate the full CAN -- only the offset-triggered boost to
the MECH-219 `recovery_rate` / SD-032e `drive_bias` leak -- but the framework says a more elaborate
version would route through REE's PFC-analog precision control.

**Limitations and caveats.** This is a conceptual review, not a measurement of offset-triggered
rebound; it warrants the *existence and central-endogenous location* of an active
parasympathetic-recovery driver, not any specific update rule. The prefrontal-vagal anatomy maps
only analogically onto REE's precision/PFC-analog control. Two failure signatures are worth
carrying forward: (i) when the prefrontal driver is offline (e.g. under high load / committed
states) the recovery process is withdrawn -- so the REE analog cannot be assumed always-on; and
(ii) chronically failing recovery (sustained low tone) is the disease picture, so the REE analog
should be able to *fail* (perseverative non-recovery) as a modelled mode, not only succeed.

**Confidence.** 0.72 -- strong as framing for the endogenous-central-driver premise, discounted
because it is a review rather than a direct test and because the anatomy-to-REE mapping is
analogical.
