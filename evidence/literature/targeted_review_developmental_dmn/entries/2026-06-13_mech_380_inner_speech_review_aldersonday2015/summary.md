# Inner Speech: Development, Cognitive Functions, Phenomenology, and Neurobiology (Alderson-Day & Fernyhough, 2015)

*Psychological Bulletin* 141(5):931-65. [DOI 10.1037/bul0000021](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000021). According to PubMed (PMID 26011789).

## What the paper did

This is the field's integrative review of inner speech -- covert speech, verbal thinking -- pulling together the developmental, cognitive, phenomenological, and neurobiological strands that had grown up largely in isolation. The organising spine is Vygotskian: external social speech is appropriated by the child as *private* (egocentric, out-loud, self-directed) speech that regulates the child's own behaviour during difficult tasks, and this private speech then *condenses* over development into covert inner speech that keeps an abbreviated, frequently dialogic structure. The authors end by proposing a multicomponent model that distinguishes condensed from expanded inner speech and dialogic from monologic forms.

## Findings relevant to the claims

Two things matter for our grounding. First, the developmental mechanism: the paper documents that overt self-narration is not a by-product but a *functional* phase -- private speech use predicts task performance and rises under difficulty, and it does not simply disappear but goes underground. This is the externalise-then-internalise route stated as an empirical regularity, not a metaphor. Second, the neurobiology: inner speech production recruits a core centred on the **left inferior frontal gyrus** (Broca's area), with superior temporal gyrus involvement (especially for the monitoring/auditory-quality aspects), plus supplementary motor and parietal contributions. So there is a dedicated production circuit whose output can run either overtly or covertly -- exactly the architecture MECH-380 posits for a self-narration control surface that begins external and compresses inward.

## How it translates to REE

For **MECH-380** (private speech is the external phase of a cognitive-control system; the Vygotskian ladder is the route by which that external surface becomes internal), this replaces the textbook anchor with mechanism evidence: a real production core, a documented internalisation trajectory, and a functional (not merely expressive) role in self-regulation. For **MECH-381** (the externalise->internalise *compression* ladder, distinct from the within-play type progression), the condensed-vs-expanded inner-speech distinction is the concrete instance of compression -- the same regulatory content carried first in expanded external form, later in abbreviated covert form. The biological warrant we wanted is here: a control signal can become internal without losing its control function.

## Limitations and caveats

The honest boundary is large and worth stating plainly. REE has no linguistic modality, so the "narration" in MECH-380/MECH-384 is a structured trace, not natural-language inner speech -- the review grounds the *developmental architecture* (externalise then internalise; a production core that goes covert) but not the use of literal language. More substantively, the review shows inner speech *supports* executive function; it does not show the external phase is *necessary* as a control surface. The private-speech-to-performance link is developmental and individual-difference evidence, not a demonstration that cognition must be externalised before it can be internalised. And inner speech is variable -- some adults report very little of it, and forms differ -- so the ladder is a central tendency, not an obligatory sequence. Both points are recorded as failure signatures: they bound how far this paper licenses MECH-381's strong "the externalise->internalise compression IS the developmental mechanism" wording.
