# Neural Correlates of Inner Speech and Auditory Verbal Hallucinations (Jones & Fernyhough, 2007)

*Clinical Psychology Review* 27(2):140-54. [DOI 10.1016/j.cpr.2006.10.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2006.10.001). According to PubMed (PMID 17123676).

## What the paper did

Jones and Fernyhough review the neuroimaging and neurophysiology of inner speech and ask why the literature looks inconsistent. Their answer is that earlier studies rested on "unexamined assumptions about the form and developmental significance of inner speech." They re-read the evidence through a Vygotskian lens: inner speech is internalised social dialogue, and it exists along a continuum from **expanded** forms (full, sentence-like, retaining the turn-taking structure of external conversation) to **condensed** forms (abbreviated, semantic, fully covert). They then apply this to auditory verbal hallucinations (AVHs), arguing that AVHs arise from abnormalities in the transition between condensed and expanded *dialogic* inner speech -- which would explain why hallucinated voices are experienced as another person's, with acoustic properties and a tendency toward commands.

## Findings relevant to the claims

The load-bearing finding for us is the **condensed/expanded distinction and the transition between them**. This is the most explicit mechanistic statement available of MECH-381's externalise->internalise *compression* axis: the same regulatory content is carried first in an expanded, external-like form and later in a condensed, covert form, and the movement between the two *is* the compression. The neural side supports MECH-380: tasks with low verbal-self-monitoring demand (reciting in inner speech) and high demand (auditory verbal imagery, which requires monitoring the self-generated voice) dissociate, with high-monitoring tasks engaging middle and superior temporal gyrus -- evidence that the external/monitored phase is a distinct functional operation, not a passive echo. The dialogic framing is a bonus that touches MECH-382: inner speech can carry more than one perspective, which is the substrate a first/third-person reframe would act on.

## How it translates to REE

This entry is the *mechanism* companion to the Alderson-Day & Fernyhough (2015) review in the same directory. Where that review establishes the developmental architecture and the production core, this paper supplies the compression operation MECH-381 names -- condensation -- and reads the neural dissociations as evidence that self-monitoring of a self-generated control signal is a real, separable computation. That is precisely the operation REE's proposed self-narration surface would perform.

It also surfaces a failure mode REE has not modelled and should note: if the condensed<->expanded transition (or the self-generation tag) breaks, the self-produced control signal is experienced as *external/other*. That is a frame-confusion instance (cf. INV-061) and a provenance-tag failure (cf. MECH-094 tag loss, MECH-430 provenance vector). The compression ladder is therefore not automatically safe -- a point worth carrying into any V4 design of the surface.

## Limitations and caveats

The paper's engine is a clinical model of hallucination, not a developmental-control account of a healthy agent, so the transfer to REE is two steps removed (clinical -> non-clinical, linguistic -> non-linguistic). The condensed/expanded framework is a theoretical *integration* of mixed and partly contested neuroimaging findings, not a settled result. Confidence is accordingly moderate (0.62): the mechanism it offers is the right shape for MECH-381, but it arrives wrapped in an AVH model and rests on correlational data. Literature confidence only; no experimental confidence is implied or changed.
