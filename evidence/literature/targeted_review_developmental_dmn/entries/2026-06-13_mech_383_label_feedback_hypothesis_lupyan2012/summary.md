# Linguistically Modulated Perception and Cognition: The Label-Feedback Hypothesis (Lupyan, 2012)

*Frontiers in Psychology* 3:54. [DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00054](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00054). According to PubMed (PMID 22408629).

## What the paper did

This is the theoretical statement of the mechanism MECH-383 imports by name -- the label-feedback hypothesis -- and it is the "top-down precision-control partner" the grounding task asked me to harvest. Rather than a single experiment, Lupyan synthesises a body of work showing that labeling exerts rapid, pervasive effects on processes usually treated as non-verbal: categorisation, visual discrimination, and even the bare detection of whether a stimulus is present. He then poses the puzzle that organises the field: label effects are simultaneously *deep* (they reach basic visual processing) and *fragile* (verbal interference can abolish them). His resolution is to drop the verbal/non-verbal representational divide and treat language as something that **modulates ongoing perceptual and cognitive processing online, in a flexible and task-dependent way**.

## Findings relevant to the claim

The value here is the mechanism, not a datum. "Labels modulate ongoing perception top-down, flexibly, by task" is exactly the framework MECH-383 needs to assert that a self-generated label is a *control input to perceptual precision* rather than a post-perceptual report. Where the companion Lupyan & Swingley (2012) entry gives the causal behavioural effect, this paper gives the account of *what kind of thing* that effect is -- a feedback signal into perception -- which is what licenses placing labels on REE's precision-routing plane.

## How it translates to REE

MECH-383 says self-generated labels ride on the ARC-005 precision-routing control plane and the distributed precision cluster (MECH-251/MECH-261), and explicitly disclaims a separate attention module -- consistent with the project's attention=distributed-precision-selection map. Lupyan's framework is the warrant for exactly that placement: labels are not a new faculty, they are a top-down modulator of the perceptual processing that is already happening. The task-dependence is also a useful design hint -- a label's effect should depend on the current task/goal context, not be a fixed re-weighting.

## Limitations and caveats

Two caveats bound the grounding. First, Lupyan's strong claim -- abandon the verbal/non-verbal distinction entirely -- is *more* than MECH-383 asserts. REE only needs labels to be one tunable control input alongside affect, goal, and uncertainty signals; it does not need the radical representational thesis, and should not inherit it. Second, the deep-yet-fragile finding means the channel is load-sensitive: verbal interference knocks the effect out, so a label-as-control input could not be treated as a reliable always-on precision signal in REE either (recorded as a failure signature). And as a position paper rather than an experiment, it grounds the *framework* for MECH-383 rather than adding a decisive new datum beyond the primary studies it cites.
