# Gottlieb, Oudeyer, Lopes & Baranes (2013) — the epistemic-deficit information-seeking drive, across humans and learning agents

**Claim tested:** Q-089 — does epistemic-deficit-driven information-seeking explain the cold-start competence split, as opposed to environmental difficulty, initialisation, premature death, or curriculum timing?

**Direction:** supports (theoretical grounding) · **Confidence:** 0.55

## What the paper did

This Trends in Cognitive Sciences review synthesises computational and neural accounts of information seeking, curiosity, and attention. Its central move is to define curiosity as an *epistemic* motivational mechanism — a drive to explore for the primary sake of gaining information, as distinct from information sought in service of an external goal such as food. It reviews computational formalisations of that drive (novelty, surprise, learning-progress, and information-gain models) and neural evidence from active sampling and eye movements, and argues these converge: intrinsic information seeking is an isolable drive that self-organises learning and, over development, produces ordered behavioural stages. Critically, the review draws its evidence not only from humans and animals but from artificial and robotic intrinsically-motivated learning agents. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2013.09.001)

## Why it speaks to Q-089

Of the supporting sources, this is the closest theoretical parent to REE's actual mechanism. MECH-482's epistemic-deficit accumulator is, in this review's vocabulary, an information-gap / uncertainty-reduction signal; MECH-483's orient/survey regime is the behavioural expression of curiosity-driven sampling. The review supplies three things Q-089 needs: (1) that an epistemic information-seeking drive is *real and isolable* from extrinsic reward; (2) that it *self-organises learning and development* — i.e. it is causally upstream of competence acquisition, not merely correlated with it; and (3) that this holds in *artificial learning agents*, not only biological ones, which is a materially lower transfer-risk bridge to REE seeds than the human decision-task studies can offer.

## The mapping, and where it breaks

Two caveats keep this from being decisive, and both are disconfirming-flavoured. First, the review is explicit that the intrinsic drive is *multiply realisable*: novelty, surprise, learning-progress, and information-gain are distinct computational drivers that all produce information seeking. So "epistemic deficit" as REE names it is under-determined — a REE seed's split could be driven by, say, a learning-progress signal rather than an uncertainty-deficit signal, and this review would not tell them apart. Mapping REE's specific accumulator onto "curiosity" is a genus→species inference. Second, the review notes curiosity can be *maladaptive* — an agent can be drawn to information that is learnable but useless — which directly undercuts the assumed monotonic "seek information early → acquire transferable competence" link at the heart of Q-089's confirming signature. Strong early orienting could, on this account, just as well mark a seed that burns its cold-start budget on uninformative novelty. And as a framework review it tests nothing empirically: it cannot adjudicate epistemic-deficit dynamics against the initialisation / difficulty / premature-death / curriculum confounds.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality high (0.8 — authoritative, heavily cited synthesis). Mapping fidelity is the highest of the supporting set (0.65) because it articulates the epistemic-deficit drive directly and in learning agents. Transfer risk is the lowest of the human/biological-leaning sources (0.4) because the framework explicitly spans artificial learners. But it remains a framework, not a test, and it actively surfaces the multiple-realisability and maladaptive-curiosity problems — so it is capped at 0.55: it strengthens the case that Q-089's mechanism is coherent and worth substrate-testing, while itself naming two of the reasons the test could come back mixed.
