# Astrocyte-gated multi-timescale plasticity for online continual learning (Dong & He, 2026)

According to PubMed, published in *Frontiers in Neuroscience*, [DOI 10.3389/fnins.2025.1768235](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2025.1768235).

**What the paper did.** Motivated by the tripartite synapse — where astrocytes regulate synaptic efficacy on slow timescales — the authors add an astrocyte-mediated gate to an online learning rule for deep spiking networks. A *slow astrocytic variable* integrates neuronal activity over time and uses that running estimate to modulate plasticity: it *suppresses weight updates when the regime is stable* and *enables them when the input distribution shifts*. Combined with eligibility traces and a broadcast teaching signal, this Astrocyte-Gated Multi-Timescale Plasticity (AGMP) matches offline backprop-through-time accuracy at constant memory cost and, in class-incremental continual learning (e.g. Split CIFAR-100), substantially mitigates catastrophic forgetting *without replay buffers*, beating other online learning rules.

**Why it bears on Q-062.** Q-062 is the necessity-falsifier for MECH-368, an *explicit event-level write-eligible state*. Its sub-question (2) is the sharp one: is "write-eligible" a *held intermediate state with its own entry and exit*, or merely a salience tag re-read at update time? The other two entries in this directory (Masse, Zenke) lean toward "no separate state needed — consolidation-priority does the work." This paper is the deliberate counterweight. The astrocytic slow variable is exactly a *held, regime-tracking gating state*: it has its own dynamics, it enters a suppressing regime when things are stable and a permitting regime when they shift, and it is that state — not a static importance prior — that decides whether plasticity is admitted. And it earns its keep: it improves continual learning over rules that lack it. That is evidence that a held write-eligibility state *can add value beyond* instantaneous channel gating plus a static consolidation prior — i.e. that MECH-368 might earn a keep rather than being redundant with MECH-261 + MECH-094 + MECH-285.

**The mapping, honestly bounded.** I score this **mixed**, and I want to be precise about why it is not a clean "weakens the null." First, the astrocytic gate is a *continuous plasticity-gain modulator* layered on eligibility traces, not a discrete event-level admission gate; it is a good analogue of "a held state governs write-eligibility" but not a literal instance of MECH-368. Second, it shows a held gating state helps in a *generic* deep SNN; it does not factor its benefit against REE's specific stack, so it cannot show that REE's existing combination is *specifically* insufficient. It establishes that the held-state design is a real, useful point in the space — which is enough to stop Q-062 being answered "no keep" on the strength of Masse and Zenke alone.

**Confidence.** I set this at 0.55. Source quality is mid (peer-reviewed Frontiers, but a recent single-group neuromorphic benchmark study), mapping fidelity is moderate because a gain modulator is not a discrete admission state, and transfer risk from SNN benchmarks to REE's online write path is substantial. Its value here is balance: with Masse (gating needs a consolidation partner; partner is parameter-stabilization) and Zenke (consolidation-priority alone substantially suffices) both tilting toward MECH-368-not-needed, this entry shows the held-state hypothesis is alive. Q-062 stays genuinely open — which is the honest state for a v4-parked question whose decisive evidence requires a model-update substrate REE has not yet built.
