# Context-dependent gating + synaptic stabilization (Masse, Grant & Freedman, 2018)

According to PubMed, published in *PNAS*, [DOI 10.1073/pnas.1803839115](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1803839115).

**What the paper did.** This is a clean factorial study of how to stop an artificial network forgetting old tasks as it learns new ones. The authors compare three things: weight *stabilization* (penalising changes to parameters deemed important for earlier tasks, à la EWC and synaptic intelligence); *context-dependent gating* (XdG — a context signal makes only a sparse, mostly non-overlapping subset of units active for any one task); and the two combined. The decisive result for our purposes is a dissociation: XdG *alone* does not support continual learning, and stabilization alone is limited, but together they hold accuracy near 95% across large numbers of sequentially presented tasks — and this holds for both feedforward and recurrent architectures, under both supervised and reinforcement learning. The authors frame this explicitly as "use multiple, complementary methods, akin to what is believed to occur in the brain."

**Why it bears on Q-062.** Q-062 is the falsifier for MECH-368: is an *explicit event-level write-eligible state* actually needed, or does REE's existing combination — MECH-261 mode-conditioned channel gating + MECH-094 provenance gating + MECH-285 consolidation-priority — already suffice to prevent undifferentiated global model updates? This paper maps almost directly onto that question, because XdG *is* a channel/mode gate and weight stabilization *is* a consolidation-priority mechanism. Its central finding cuts two ways, which is why I score it **mixed** rather than supports or weakens:

- It *weakens* the simplest "gating is enough" null. Channel/mode gating on its own (the MECH-261 analogue) demonstrably fails to prevent catastrophic interference; a complementary consolidation mechanism is required. So Q-062's answer is not "mode gating alone suffices."
- But it *supports the MECH-368-not-needed direction*. The complement that rescues gating here is *parameter-level stabilization* — a consolidation-priority / importance prior, i.e. the MECH-285 + MECH-094 part of REE's existing stack — and **not** an explicit per-event held "write-eligible" state. The paper achieves robust continual learning with gating + consolidation and never invokes anything like MECH-368. On its evidence, the existing combination is sufficient and the extra explicit state earns no keep.

**The mapping, honestly bounded.** The transfer risk is real and I have not hidden it. The paper's "context" is a *known, clean task cue* supplied to the network; Q-062 concerns admission gating on REE's online world-model/policy path where context is implicit and non-stationary and there is no oracle task label. So "XdG alone fails" transfers as "mode gating needs a consolidation partner," but the paper cannot, by itself, settle whether that partner must be a *held event-level state* (MECH-368) versus a *parameter-importance prior* (MECH-285). It biases the answer toward "no separate state needed," without proving it.

**Confidence.** I set this at 0.70. Source quality is high (PNAS, a careful dissociation replicated across architectures and learning paradigms), but mapping fidelity is held to 0.62 because the paper addresses parameter stabilization rather than an event-level write-eligible state, and the known-task-cue setting differs from REE's implicit-context path. This is the central literature anchor for Q-062: it rules out the naive "gating suffices" reading while pointing toward consolidation-priority — not an explicit admission state — as the load-bearing complement.
