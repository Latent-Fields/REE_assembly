# Atlas et al. (2016) -- Instructed knowledge shapes aversive learning in striatum and OFC, but not the amygdala

**What they did.** People were fear-conditioned to a cue paired with mild shock, and then the contingency was reversed, more than once. Half of them were told what the contingencies were and when they reversed; the other half had to learn everything from the shocks themselves. The authors fitted learning models to skin conductance and to BOLD, and asked which systems updated when the rule was *told* rather than *experienced*.

**What they found.** It splits. The amygdala learned from reinforcement in exactly the same way in both groups -- being told about a reversal did not move it; only the shocks did. Striatum and orbitofrontal cortex, by contrast, updated immediately on instruction, and did so in step with prefrontal responses to the instructions. And skin conductance in the instructed group flipped almost completely the moment the instruction was given, before a single shock under the new rule.

**How it maps to MECH-014.** The claim's second design constraint is that signals cannot mask embodied harm channels in the receiver. The amygdala finding is good news for that: there is a threat-learning pathway that keeps learning from what actually happens to the body, whatever it has been told. In REE terms, the harm stream's update rule should be closed to incoming signals. But the skin conductance result is awkward for the claim's wording. Arousal is about as embodied as a harm response gets, and a signal overrode it wholesale. So which is it? I think the honest reading is that "cannot mask" is too strong as written. Signals may and do legitimately shift expectation and arousal -- that is part of what makes a warning useful -- and what must be protected is narrower: the experiential learning update that registers harm when it actually arrives.

**What it cannot tell us.** Verbal instruction is language, not a minimal signal, and it sits closer to MECH-011's territory; a pre-linguistic channel may override much less. More importantly, the instructions were truthful. The masking failure MECH-014 most needs to exclude is a *false* all-clear -- a signal that suppresses harm registration when harm is present -- and this study did not test that. The design would extend naturally to it.

**Confidence: 0.60, mixed.** The most direct evidence on the non-masking constraint, supporting a protected harm-learning stream while showing that the claim, read broadly, is false.
