# Behrens et al. (2008) -- Associative learning of social value

**What they did.** Twenty-four people chose between two coloured options in a scanner. On each trial a confederate recommended one of them, and the confederate's reliability wandered unpredictably, as did the underlying reward schedule -- with the two volatilities manipulated independently. That design is what makes the paper useful here: it lets you ask whether a receiver treats another agent's signal as a separate source with its own learned reliability, or just folds it into its own experience.

**What they found.** The receiver keeps them apart. Reward volatility was tracked in the anterior cingulate sulcus; the volatility of the adviser's reliability was tracked in the neighbouring anterior cingulate gyrus. Both sources drove behaviour to a comparable degree, and the two learned values met only at the point of choice, in ventromedial prefrontal cortex. How much a given person leaned on advice versus their own history was predicted by the size of the volatility signal in the corresponding cingulate region. The authors' larger point is that none of this required a special social-learning faculty -- the computations were the same associative ones used for reward.

**How it maps to MECH-014.** The claim's first design constraint is that signals are integrated via trust weighting, meaning reliability inference. This is about as close to a direct demonstration of that as the human literature offers. It also sharpens the claim in two ways it does not currently state. First, reliability should be volatility-sensitive: an agent should learn faster about a sender whose trustworthiness is changing. Second, the signal should enter as its own weighted term at selection, not rewrite the receiver's own value estimates. And it bears indirectly on the cheapness constraint -- the receiver did well by learning *whether* the advice predicts outcomes, without any demonstrated need to model *why* the adviser gave it.

**What it cannot tell us.** The signal is a one-bit recommendation in a money game. Nothing about it is a harm alert, and nothing aversive is at stake, so it is silent on the second constraint (signals must not mask the receiver's embodied harm channels). The cheapness reading rests on what the task did not require rather than on a test; Diaconescu et al. (2014) show that when an adviser's incentives shift, people *do* reach for a model of the adviser's intentions. And REE has no second agent, so this is a design constraint for a future substrate, not a test of REE.

**Confidence: 0.66.** Strong source, direct fit to one of three constraints, silent on the other two's hardest parts.
