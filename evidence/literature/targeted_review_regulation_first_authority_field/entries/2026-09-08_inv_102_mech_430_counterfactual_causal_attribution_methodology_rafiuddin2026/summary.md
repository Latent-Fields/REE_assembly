# Rafiuddin & Sen 2026 -- C3T, a method borrowed with its limits stated

arXiv 2609.02131, submitted 2 September 2026. The citation is exact and the paper is what it was described as. C3T works on rumour-focused conversation threads and asks why sentiment changes: it treats discourse moves such as denial or correction, evidence, and toxicity as candidate interventions, builds on a causally annotated dataset layer providing sentiment labels, parent-child shift indicators, intervention tags and causal-source annotations, and learns sparse ancestor attribution to say which earlier message drove a given reply. Counterfactual queries are supported by forcing intervention embeddings on or off. Reported results: denials, corrections and evidence reduce downstream negativity, toxicity increases it, and attribution is more reliable than open-weight LLM baselines.

The thought that cites this says its findings do not transfer to embodied agents, and the chip instructs that it be banked as a methodological anchor only. Both are right, and I have set the direction to unknown and the confidence to 0.3 so that it cannot accumulate as support for INV-102 or MECH-430.

What is genuinely useful is the shape. INV-102 demands a distinct other-agent-caused hypothesis competing with world-caused and self-caused; MECH-430 demands that provenance carry source identity as a dimension. C3T is a worked instance of estimating which other agent's prior act caused an observed change, with competing candidate causes and a counterfactual test.

What the borrowing must not hide is the cost. C3T is handed a tree and an annotated vocabulary of intervention types; an embodied agent has neither and must infer both, which is most of the difficulty. And the counterfactual is a toggle on a learned embedding, so what it measures is the model's own sensitivity rather than the causal structure of the world. A REE falsifier built the same way would be vulnerable to exactly the confound INV-102 needs to avoid. Worth reading for the design; not evidence.
