# Muller et al. 2026 -- traveling waves, and a thumb on the scale for Q-103

Neuron 114(17):3156-3174, DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2026.06.019, PMID 42480536, published 2 September 2026 with an epub of 21 July. The thought's citation is exact.

The review gathers what is now a substantial literature: traveling waves are seen throughout the awake brain, they influence excitability and behaviour, and they arise both intrinsically and in response to sensory and behavioural events. For MECH-534 that is the biological instance of the authority field's premise -- inter-region coupling that has its own fast dynamics rather than being a fixed gate that content passes through.

The part that matters more is what it implies for Q-103, and I have marked the entry mixed to make the split visible. Q-103 asks whether the trajectory of the coupling state itself carries task-relevant relational structure, or whether it only sets eligibility and gain for content held elsewhere. This review leans toward the first. Waves "introduce spatiotemporal dependencies across sensory maps" that feedforward and feedback models do not capture, and the authors describe them implementing spatiotemporal computations, including predicting upcoming sensory input by embedding recent sensory history. History embedded in the wave is structure held in the field, not merely a gain applied to structure held elsewhere.

Two restraints. The review says these computational roles are potential, not established; documenting that waves exist and correlate with excitability and behaviour is not the same as showing the wave dynamics do the computing, and MECH-534's static-gate falsifier exists precisely because REE should not inherit the stronger reading for free. And Q-103's fork concerns REE's own L-space. Evidence for the representational branch in cortex does not settle it for REE, whose claim notes already adopt the brake that biological likeness must not outrank functional necessity. Mixed at 0.58.
