# Immobility reactions under threat: A contribution to human defensive cascade and PTSD

Volchan, Rocha-Rego, Bastos, Oliveira, Franklin, Gleiser, Berger, Souza, Oliveira, David, Erthal, Pereira & Figueira (2017), *Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews* 76(Pt A):29-38. DOI 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2017.01.025. PMID 28131873.

## What the paper does

Most of the defensive-behaviour literature that REE draws on is rodent. This review is human, and its method is unusual enough to mention: stabilometry -- measuring postural sway and its suppression while people are exposed to violence-related threat cues. Standing still turns out to be measurable in a way that distinguishes kinds of standing still.

The authors' central taxonomic point is that immobility under threat is not one thing. They distinguish three types within a defensive cascade. Attentive immobility is the oriented, reversible, monitoring variety. Tonic immobility is something else entirely -- a peritraumatic response, profound and unresponsive, which they report is strongly associated with the severity of subsequent post-traumatic stress disorder.

That last association is what gives the taxonomy its weight. These are not descriptive shades of the same phenomenon; one of them predicts clinical outcome and the others do not.

## Why this bears on MECH-489

MECH-489 is registered as a *phasic* arrest, and `claims.yaml` goes to some length to distinguish it from MECH-279's chronic, accumulated-suffering freeze -- the comment there is precise about it, that MECH-279 responds to harm being experienced while MECH-489 responds to something unexpected being noticed, before it is identified as harm at all. The V3-EXQ-910a autopsy examined that boundary independently and concluded it was scientifically sound and should not be redrawn.

This paper supports the boundary, and does so from a different direction than anything else in this directory. The rodent circuit work (Moscarello & Penzo, in this same directory) supports it from anatomy -- different points on the imminence continuum, different circuit configurations. This supports it from human phenomenology and clinical outcome: attentive immobility and tonic immobility are separated by more than duration, and the separation matters enough to predict who develops PTSD. Two independent lines converging on a boundary that was originally drawn on internal-consistency grounds is worth recording.

The second thing it gives is the cascade structure itself. MECH-489 asserts a sequence -- arrest, then orient, then act -- and one might reasonably ask whether that sequencing is a real organising principle or an artefact of how the design brief was written. This literature says the cascade is real and recognised. That is a modest contribution but not a nothing: it means the claim's overall shape is borrowed from something, not invented.

## Limitations, and they bound this entry tightly

The support here is *taxonomic*, not mechanistic. The three immobility types are distinguished largely by posture, reversibility and autonomic profile -- descriptively -- rather than by dissociating the mechanisms that produce them. So this is evidence that MECH-489 and MECH-279 are pointing at genuinely different things, and not evidence about how either of them works.

Nothing here speaks to MECH-489's trigger channel, its release rule, or its valence-gated decision. Those are the three sub-components the experimental evidence has actually been contesting, and this entry is silent on all of them. It should not be cited in connection with any of them.

The clinical anchor is also a mapping problem rather than a mapping strength. The outcome variable driving much of the authors' argument is PTSD severity, which has no REE analogue whatsoever. The population includes trauma-exposed individuals and the threat cues are violence-related; the emotional and biographical loading of that context is not something an agent in a reef ecology has. And the stabilometry evidence base is comparatively small, resting substantially on this group's own programme of work.

## Confidence

0.68. Source quality 0.78 -- a well-regarded review venue and a coherent method, but reviewing a smaller primary literature than the rodent looming work and drawing heavily on the authors' own studies. Mapping fidelity 0.68, which reflects a narrow but clean correspondence: the phasic/tonic distinction maps onto the MECH-489/MECH-279 boundary and onto nothing else. Transfer risk 0.28, the lowest in this directory, because this is human data -- with the caveat that the clinical outcome anchoring the argument does not transfer at all.
