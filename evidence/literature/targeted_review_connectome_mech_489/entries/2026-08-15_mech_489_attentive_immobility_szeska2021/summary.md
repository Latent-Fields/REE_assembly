# Attentive immobility in the face of inevitable distal threat

Szeska, Richter, Wendt, Weymar & Hamm (2021), *Psychophysiology* 58(6):e13812. DOI 10.1111/psyp.13812. PMID 33759212.

## What the paper did

The authors studied what happens in a human being who is facing a threat they cannot escape and cannot yet act on -- inevitable, distal, signalled by a conditioned cue. The state that emerges is called attentive immobility: the person stops moving and monitors the source of danger. The interesting methodological move is that they indexed it two ways at once, using measures that come apart mechanistically. Startle probes indexed defensive activation; conditioned cues potentiated the startle reflex, which is the signature of defensive hypervigilance. Cardiac measures indexed orienting; fear-conditioned participants showed stronger bradycardia -- cardiac deceleration -- than controls. And the two moved together: learned fear bradycardia was positively correlated with conditioned startle potentiation.

That correlation is the part that carries the argument. Either measure alone would be equivocal. Together they describe a single state in which the organism is motorically still and sensorily wide open.

## Why this bears on MECH-489

MECH-489 asserts a chain: phasic freeze-arrest, then orient/identify, then an epistemic-sufficiency override, then valence-gated approach, withdraw or resume. The word "then" is doing a lot of work in that sentence, and this paper suggests it may be doing the wrong work.

In the biology, freeze and orient are not two stages in sequence. They are two descriptions of one state. The immobility *is* the orienting posture. An organism in attentive immobility has stopped moving precisely so that it can monitor, and its defensive sensitivity is elevated rather than suspended during that period. Stillness here is not the absence of behaviour; it is a particular kind of engaged behaviour.

I record this as `supports`, and I want to be exact about what it supports. It supports MECH-489 *as a claim*. The claim's own design intent -- that arrest exists in order to enable identification, that freeze without orienting has no epistemic exit -- is a fair reading of what this literature describes. What the paper does not support is the arrest as currently built. The confirmed autopsy on V3-EXQ-910a code-traced the arrest state and found no sensory-sampling change, no active localisation, no belief update; the trigger location is captured once as a passive snapshot and never refined. On this paper's account, that is the motor half of attentive immobility with the attentive half missing.

This distinction is the substantive thing this pull can contribute. The question the autopsies left open is whether MECH-489's failures are a substrate defect or a mis-posed hypothesis. On the trigger channel, the Wang 2021 entry in this same directory suggests the hypothesis was posed with the wrong disjunct emphasised. On *this* leg, the answer runs the other way: the hypothesis looks biologically well-posed, and the gap is in the implementation. Those are different verdicts for different sub-components of the same claim, which is itself worth noticing -- the autopsies already flagged that MECH-489 bundles two sub-mechanisms that resolve in opposite directions.

## Limitations

The paradigm is conditioned, distal, *inevitable* threat, signalled by a cue the participant has learned. MECH-489's trigger is an unanticipated, unidentified sudden onset. Those sit at different points on the imminence continuum, and moving between them is an assumption I am making, not something the paper licenses.

The more important limitation is about what "attentive" means here. The study establishes a hypervigilant physiological state. It does not establish that specific information about the threat is acquired, that an identification occurs, or -- crucially for MECH-489's epistemic-sufficiency override -- that acquired information is what terminates the state. Startle potentiation and bradycardia tell you the organism is oriented and primed. They do not tell you it is learning anything. Anyone tempted to cite this as evidence for an epistemically-driven release mechanism should resist; it supports the attentive character of the arrest and stops there. The release question is taken up separately in the Liu 2025 entry, and even there the answer is partial.
