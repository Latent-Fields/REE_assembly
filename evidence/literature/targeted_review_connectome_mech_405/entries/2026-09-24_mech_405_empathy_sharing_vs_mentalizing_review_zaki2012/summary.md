Zaki and Ochsner reviewed about a decade of empathy neuroscience and organised it around two sub-processes: sharing another's internal state, and explicitly considering that state (mentalizing). They mapped these onto largely separable neural systems, and they criticised the field for artificial stimuli and for rarely linking brain responses to what people actually do socially.

For MECH-405 the review is a scoping tool. The claim's mechanism, running another's state through one's own motivational-affective streams, matches the experience-sharing sub-process well. The review is equally clear that empathy as a whole also depends on a separate mentalizing system, which computes the other's state rather than just labelling it.

That bears on how the claim is phrased. The other-model in the claim is described as a slot that binds the self stream to another agent. The literature summarised here implies that the other-model does real inferential work of its own, and that this work can produce empathic understanding without strong sharing. 'Fast empathy' is best read as the sharing route, not empathy in general.

Confidence 0.5, mixed: it supports the existence of the route and argues against the claim being the whole story.
