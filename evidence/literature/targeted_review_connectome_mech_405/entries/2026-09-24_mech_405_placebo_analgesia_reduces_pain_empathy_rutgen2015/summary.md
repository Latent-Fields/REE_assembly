Rutgen and colleagues induced placebo analgesia in adults, lowering their own experience of painful shocks, and then measured how much empathy they reported for another person receiving pain. In an fMRI experiment with 102 participants, people who experienced placebo analgesia also reported less empathy for the other's pain, and anterior insula and midcingulate responses to the other's pain were reduced. A second study with 50 participants gave the opioid antagonist naltrexone, which blocked placebo analgesia and also restored empathy for pain to normal levels.

This matters for MECH-405 because it goes beyond the usual observation that self-pain and other-pain activate overlapping regions. It manipulates the self-pain system and shows that other-directed pain affect moves with it, through a shared neurochemical route. That is what the claim predicts if other-bound suffering is produced by passing another's state through the agent's own suffering stream. It also suggests a clean falsifier for an eventual REE implementation: attenuating the agent's own harm/suffering channel should attenuate other-bound suffering.

It does not reach the other half of the claim. Nothing here identifies an other-model binding slot, and the design cannot show that empathy consists only of the self stream plus binding; mentalizing and appraisal contributions are left untouched. The empathy effects were modest and a placebo could plausibly shift general negative affect or rating style, though the naltrexone reversal makes a purely expectational account harder.

Confidence 0.68, supports, scoped to the routing (shared-stream) component.
