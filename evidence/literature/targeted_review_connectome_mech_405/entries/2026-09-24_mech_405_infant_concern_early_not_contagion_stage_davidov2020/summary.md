Davidov and colleagues followed 165 infants from 3 to 18 months and coded their responses to others' distress. Concern for the distressed person was visible from 3 months, was moderately consistent across situations and ages, rose only a little with age and levelled off in the second year. Prosocial helping, by contrast, rose steeply in the second year. Early individual differences in concern predicted later prosocial behaviour. An earlier accelerated-longitudinal study from the same group (Roth-Hanania, Davidov and Zahn-Waxler 2011) reported the same pattern from 8 to 16 months and found self-distress reactions rare overall.

This is the developmental evidence the claim note asks for (EMP-7). A natural developmental story for MECH-405 would run: infants first experience others' distress as their own (contagion through their own suffering stream), and other-oriented concern appears only once a stable model of the other allows the affect to be bound to that person. These data do not show that sequence. Other-oriented concern is present almost from the start and self-distress is uncommon.

The finding is not decisive against the mechanism. A rudimentary other-model may be present early, and the fast routing could run on it. What the data do challenge is the claim's gating condition, that routing waits on a stable other-model, and any assumption that contagion precedes concern. Coding concern from infant faces and vocalisations is also inferential.

Confidence 0.5, mixed: compatible with an early fast routing mechanism, but it weakens the stable-other-model gate and the contagion-first developmental story.
