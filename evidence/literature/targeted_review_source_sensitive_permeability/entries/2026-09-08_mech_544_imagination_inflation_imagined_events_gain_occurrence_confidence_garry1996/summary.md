# Garry, Manning, Loftus & Sherman 1996 -- imagining it makes you likelier to think it happened

Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 3(2):208-214, DOI 10.3758/BF03212420. No PubMed record exists for this journal and year, which is normal for Psychonomic Society titles of that period rather than a sign of trouble; the DOI resolves and the effect has a large replication literature. Participants rated the likelihood that various childhood events had happened to them, imagined some of them, and then rated again. Imagining raised confidence that the event had occurred.

MECH-542 puts autobiographical occurrence -- this happened to me -- at the head of its protected column. Imagination inflation is that protection failing, in healthy adults, under nothing more demanding than being asked to imagine something once or twice. It is the most direct human evidence for MECH-544's assertion that permeability pathology is intrinsic rather than incidental, and it fixes the direction of the design tension the claim states. A system that never showed this effect would be a system that could not learn from imagination either; the claim says the useful setting is in between, and this is the empirical shape of the lower boundary.

One distinction matters for how MECH-544 words its signature. Two mechanisms produce this effect, and the literature separates them. Source misattribution says the details generated while imagining are later recovered and mistaken for details of an experience. Familiarity misattribution says imagining simply makes the event feel more fluent, and fluency is read as evidence of having happened. The second route involves no source tag at all. Since MECH-544 names source-tag decay specifically, an assay that scored every occurrence error as tag decay would be conflating the two, and would be unable to tell a representation-level failure from a metacognitive one.

Effect sizes are modest, the measure is rated confidence rather than any action taken on the belief, and the paradigm has known demand characteristics. Supports at 0.65.
