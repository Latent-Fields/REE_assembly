# McGraw & Warren 2010 -- benign violation, and what it says about cheap exploration

Psychological Science 21(8):1141-1149, DOI 10.1177/0956797610376073, PMID 20587696. The package cites benign-violation accounts of humour, and the citation is accurate. The theory holds that humour occurs when and only when a situation is appraised as a violation of how the world ought to be, is simultaneously appraised as benign, and both appraisals happen at once. Five experiments in the moral domain show that benign moral violations produce amusement alongside disgust, and identify three things that make a violation benign: an alternative norm under which the situation is acceptable, weak commitment to the violated norm, and psychological distance.

For ARC-141 the relevance is real but indirect, and I want to be exact about that. SOC-HUM-1 already owns humour as a low-commitment deniable probe that reopens gridlocked norm conflicts, and the intake correctly declined to mint a new humour claim. This entry is the external anchor for a mechanism REE already holds, filed against ARC-141 because that is the claim under review.

The substantive contribution is a correction to how "low commitment" should be understood. ARC-141 treats humour as a technology that makes costly regions of possibility space cheap to visit. Benign-violation theory locates the cheapness not in the utterance but in the receiver's appraisal: the norm has to be genuinely violated, or nothing has been explored, and it has to be held benign at the same time, or the exploration is refused and the probe reads as offence. So the deniability is contingent and can fail, and its failure is exactly what makes a humorous probe informative about where another agent's boundary sits. A model treating humour as intrinsically low-commitment would lose that.

The psychological-distance route makes a testable prediction worth keeping: the same content should be funny at distance and offensive close up. Supports at 0.55, scored for a component account in 2010-era vignette designs rather than for the claim itself.
