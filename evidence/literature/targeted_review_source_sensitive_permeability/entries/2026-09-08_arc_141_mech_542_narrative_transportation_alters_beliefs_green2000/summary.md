# Green & Brock 2000 -- absorbed readers change their beliefs, label notwithstanding

Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 79(5):701-721, DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.79.5.701. There is no PubMed record, which is expected for JPSP of that vintage rather than a problem; the DOI resolves and the paper is among the most cited in narrative persuasion. Green and Brock proposed transportation -- absorption into a story, made up of imagery, affect and attentional focus -- as the mechanism by which narratives change beliefs, and showed that more transported readers held more story-consistent beliefs, evaluated protagonists more favourably, and noticed fewer false notes in the text.

That verifies the package's Tier-2 item: narrative transportation does alter downstream attitudes and beliefs. For ARC-141 it also complicates the cheerful reading of culture as a low-cost precommit workspace. If exploring a candidate trajectory moves your beliefs whether or not you endorse its source, then the workspace is not free; search is cheaper than commitment, as the thought says, but it is not costless, and the residue is the mechanism MECH-544 is about.

The detail most useful to MECH-542 is one the package does not emphasise. In this work the fact-or-fiction labelling did not do the moderating; transportation did. That is the same asymmetry Hartung and colleagues later found at much larger scale, arrived at from the positive side: content crosses into belief largely regardless of the source label, while the degree of absorption governs how much. If a REE assay found source framing to be the dominant moderator of general belief update, it would be showing something the human literature does not.

And the false-notes result names a distinct pathology route. Transported readers scrutinise less. That is a suppression of critical evaluation, not a failure of source tagging, and MECH-544 should keep the two apart -- a system that lost its source tags but kept its scrutiny would fail differently from an absorbed human reader. Supports at 0.58, discounted because transportation is measured rather than manipulated in the belief analyses.
