# Tien et al. 2025 -- enactment engages more than imagining, on a thin measure

Perspectives on Medical Education 14(1):736-749, DOI 10.5334/pme.1482, PMID 41245769, PMC12617402. MECH-543's external status is recorded by the package as Tier 3, meaningful but less decisive, with the caution against concluding that the brain has separate fiction, drama and humour systems. Having searched for the contrast the claim needs, I think Tier 3 is generous rather than harsh.

This is the closest study I found. Sixty second-year medical students first imagined being a patient or a caregiver, then enacted both roles, writing reflections after each stage. Engagement was indexed by function-word frequency using LIWC, and reflections from the role-playing stage showed significantly higher engagement than from the imagination stage, most clearly in the caregiver role; higher engagement went with higher human-rated empathy and perspective-taking.

Directionally that is what MECH-543 says. But the claim's content is that enactment produces distinguishable update fingerprints -- specifically on other-model formation, policy availability and affective prediction -- and none of those is measured here. More engagement is equally consistent with enactment being a stronger dose of the same process, which is precisely the alternative the claim needs to exclude. The design also runs imagination first for everyone, so the enactment advantage is confounded with order and with the cumulative elaboration of having already thought about the role. And the dependent variable is the frequency of function words in a written reflection, which is a long way from the psychological process it indexes.

I have scored this mixed at 0.35 rather than inflating it. If MECH-543 is to earn more than speculative status it will need a contrast that counterbalances route order and measures the three specific fingerprints separately; that experiment does not appear to exist yet, in humans or anywhere else.
