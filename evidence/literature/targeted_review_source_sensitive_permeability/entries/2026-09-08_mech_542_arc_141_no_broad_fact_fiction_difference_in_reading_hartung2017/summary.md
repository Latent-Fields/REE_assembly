# Hartung et al. 2017 -- a large null on fact versus fiction, and why it helps

Frontiers in Psychology 8:1618, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01618, PMID 28983269, PMC5613255. Nearly two thousand participants read stories labelled as based on true events or as fiction, in first or third person, and were measured on immersion, appreciation, perspective-taking and memory. The finding is a null: "no evidence that knowing a story is fictional or based on true events influences reading behavior or experiential aspects of reading". The authors suggest that what shapes reading is the wider context -- whether you are reading a newspaper or a novel -- setting reading goals, rather than a truth label doing work by itself.

The chip asked me to check whether the replication picture is worse than the source package suggests, and to report it plainly if so, because that would strengthen rather than damage MECH-542's wording. It is, and it does. MECH-542 was deliberately not written as "fiction label switches cognition into another mode"; it was written as selective permeability, protecting some variables and permitting others. A large, well-powered null against the global switch removes the rival account and leaves the selective one as the interesting hypothesis. The claim's honesty about the literature is vindicated by the literature.

Two things stop this being straightforwardly good news. First, MECH-542's falsifier runs in both directions -- uniform update refutes it, and so does no update at all -- and this result sits nearer the second pole than a proponent would like. It supports the wording while raising the bar for eventual confirmation: the selective effect will have to be demonstrated with variable-resolved measures, because coarse experiential ones show nothing. This study could not have detected selective permeability if it were there, so it is a bound on the global effect, not evidence for the local one.

Second, the authors' own alternative deserves attention for MECH-544. If human source-sensitivity is carried by contextual goal-setting rather than by a tag attached to the representation, then source-tag decay may be measuring an object that does not exist in the form REE posits. That is worth holding as a live possibility rather than a quibble. For ARC-141, a study finding fiction processed like fact is a caution, not a support, and I have kept the tag to record that. Supports at 0.7.
