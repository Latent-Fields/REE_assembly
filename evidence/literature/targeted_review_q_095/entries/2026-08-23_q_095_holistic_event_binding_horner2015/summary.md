# Evidence for holistic episodic recollection via hippocampal pattern completion (Horner et al., 2015)

**Claim tested:** Q-095 — does explicit coordinated episodic organisation add capability
beyond REE's existing trajectory-generation account?
**Direction: supports**, confidence 0.73. This entry speaks to the single capability Q-095's
own notes identify as having no clean owner in REE: binding.

## What the paper did

Participants learned "events" assembled from multiple overlapping pairs of elements — a
person, a location, an object, and so on — and were then tested on all the pairwise
associations within each event. The design is the whole point, and it is worth stating
carefully, because it is what makes the result discriminating rather than merely consistent.

Two hypotheses make different predictions here. On a pairwise-associative account — which is
what a trajectory-linked memory naturally implements — cueing element A to retrieve element B
should reinstate B and not much else. On a holistic account, cueing A should complete the
whole bound event, reinstating C and D as well, even though C and D are entirely incidental
to the question being asked.

The incidental reinstatement is what they find. Element types are represented in distinct
neocortical regions; when a single pairwise association is retrieved, neocortical activity
corresponding to *all* the event's elements is reinstated, including the task-irrelevant ones;
and the degree to which a given participant shows that incidental reinstatement correlates
with their hippocampal activity. Hippocampal activity at encoding also predicts memory
performance across all within-event pairs, not just some.

## Why it matters for Q-095

Q-095's notes are explicit that episodic BINDING — objects, locations, actions, internal
states, goals, outcomes and temporal relations bound into one coherent event representation —
was deliberately not registered as its own claim, because the raw thought names it without
developing it and it is entangled with MECH-154 and MECH-288. It is recorded as an open gap
and flagged as the most likely source of a future claim from that thought.

This paper is the reason that flag is right. Of the six capabilities in the bundle, binding is
the one where the reinterpretation horn has the least to say. Stachenfeld's successor
representation covers the geometric members; it does not do this. TEM binds structure to
sensory content, which is closer, but its binding is a two-way conjunction rather than the
n-way event coherence tested here. Holistic completion of an n-element event, including
elements incidental to the query, is a capability that pairwise trajectory linkage does not
predict.

It also hands REE a test that costs nothing new to build. No new environment is required —
only episodes whose elements include some that are incidental to whatever query is issued,
and a measurement of whether retrieval reinstates them. That is a far cheaper discriminator
than the full 2x2, and it targets the one place where an ADDS-CAPABILITY verdict is most
likely to be earned.

## Limitations, honestly

Two, and they pull in the same direction — toward not over-reading it.

First, the events are constructed. They are arbitrary overlapping element pairs learned in a
lab session, not experience that a segmenter carved out of a continuous stream. Their
coherence was imposed by the design. So the paper demonstrates that the hippocampus *can*
bind a set of elements it has been told to treat as an event; it does not demonstrate that
the system discovers which elements belong together. For REE that distinction matters,
because MECH-288's segmenter is what would have to supply the boundary, and this paper takes
the boundary as given.

Second, the hippocampal attribution is correlational. This is fMRI, not lesion or stimulation,
so "via hippocampal pattern completion" is the authors' interpretation of covarying BOLD
signal. The behavioural and reinstatement dissociation — holistic versus pairwise — does not
depend on that attribution being right, and that dissociation is what this entry rests on.
The anatomical localisation is a bonus, not the load-bearing part.

Confidence sits at 0.73: a well-designed discriminating measurement in a good open-access
venue, mapping to REE at the computational level where transfer is cleanest, discounted for
the constructed-event artificiality and the correlational anatomy.
