# 20 Years of Aberrant Salience in Psychosis: What Have We Learned?

Corlett & Fraser (2025), *American Journal of Psychiatry* 182(9):819-829. DOI [10.1176/appi.ajp.20240556](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.20240556) · PMID 40134268 · PMC12949980

**Claim tested:** MECH-043 — *Dopamine-like modulation of precision-weighting for unsigned prediction errors.*
**Direction:** mixed (confidence 0.70)

## What the paper did

Twenty years on from Kapur's "psychosis as a state of aberrant salience" — the account that became, as the authors put it, the de facto way of talking about delusion formation in labs and clinics — Corlett and Fraser ask whether it has held up. Their verdict is that its specific predictions about the neural and behavioural loci of dopamine dysfunction are lacking when read against the subsequent evidence, and particularly against what the last two decades of precise circuit-level tools have revealed about what the dopamine system actually does. In its place they argue for the contemporary successors: accounts casting dopamine in belief formation, belief updating under uncertainty, and abductive inference to the best explanation, which they judge to fit better both the behaviour of patients with delusions and hallucinations and the known function of their dopamine systems.

## How this translates to REE

This review cuts both ways for MECH-043, which is why it is filed as *mixed* rather than as support, and both directions are useful.

It supports MECH-043's **form**. Falsifier (ii) states that the claim is violated if precision weighting collapses into a scalar reward objective. This paper is the field's own verdict on that question, delivered independently of anything REE has argued, and it goes MECH-043's way: the scalar-salience framing is judged to have aged badly, and the successor accounts the authors endorse — dopamine in belief updating under uncertainty — are structurally the same *kind* of quantity as an inverse-variance precision weight, not a reward scalar. For a claim whose whole content is that the relevant dopaminergic quantity is a precision on unsigned surprise rather than a value signal, that is meaningful corroboration of the shape of the hypothesis.

It undercuts MECH-043's **confidence about attribution**. The authors' central negative finding is that locus-level predictions about where dopamine dysfunction sits have repeatedly failed as measurement improved. MECH-043 makes exactly such a locus-level claim — a precision channel in the E3 selector, feeding the commitment gate and the dACC affective-PE weighting. Read together with the Iglesias et al. (2021) entry in this pull, which is the pharmacological instance of the same lesson, the message is consistent: the field's track record on saying *which* channel carries dopamine's computational role is poor.

The composite reading I would offer is that MECH-043 has the right shape of quantity and an under-warranted degree of specificity about which channel carries it. That is not a reason to demote the claim. It is a reason to run the isolation experiment its CONFIRMING condition already specifies, since the discriminating question is exactly the one the literature has failed to settle.

There is a further constraint worth naming. The circuit-level heterogeneity Corlett and Fraser invoke is an argument that the dopamine system does several computationally distinct things at once. REE currently compresses all of it into one scalar, `current_precision`. The neighbouring candidate claims ARC-108 and ARC-109 exist precisely to decompress it — learned cortico-striatal gating weights, and a D1/D2 population split with opposite dopamine sensitivity. This review is, in effect, an argument that those claims are pointing in the right direction, and that MECH-043's single-channel framing is a provisional simplification rather than a finished position.

## Limitations and caveats

Three boundaries. It is a review, contributing no new measurement — it is the field's judgement about an evidence base, and such judgements can shift without any new datum arriving. Its unit of analysis is delusion and hallucination *formation* in patients, not precision-weighting as a computational quantity; the endorsement of belief-updating-under-uncertainty accounts is a family-level endorsement, not a specific vote for inverse-variance weighting of unsigned prediction error, and reading it as the latter would over-claim. And because the heterogeneity argument targets exactly the simplification REE has made, this review is better used as a constraint on how strongly MECH-043 may be *stated* than as evidence for or against whether it is true.
