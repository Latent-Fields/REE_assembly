# Orexin at the junction of sleep, feeding and metabolic state (Saper, 2006)

According to PubMed, Saper's "Staying awake for dinner" (Progress in Brain Research, 2006; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0079-6123(06)53014-6)) takes the flip-flop switch one step beyond the 2005 statement and asks how energy state enters the equation. The argument is that sleep-wake regulation is inextricably linked to feeding and energy metabolism, and that the orexin/hypocretin neurons sit at exactly that junction: they stabilize the wake side of the switch *while simultaneously* driving wakefulness and foraging for food. Circadian timing is folded in through the subparaventricular zone and the dorsomedial hypothalamus, which co-regulate feeding and wake-sleep patterning. The orexin neuron, in this telling, is not a pure arousal device but a metabolic-state-sensitive arousal device.

This is the entry I selected specifically to support the *metabolic-demand* component of MECH-286, which the 2005 flip-flop review touches only lightly. MECH-286 states that high override -- "sustained drive + threat under metabolic demand" -- suppresses sleep-mode onset. The threat half is carried by the emotional-input language of the broader sleep literature; the *metabolic-demand* half is carried here. Saper 2006 gives the concrete mechanism by which energy/drive state biases the same switch: orexin couples wakefulness to foraging under energy demand. So when MECH-286 lets metabolic demand participate in the override that holds the system out of sleep, it is leaning on a real and well-characterised coupling, not inventing one.

The caveat worth recording is one of emphasis, and it cuts slightly against a naive reading of the claim. The review's metabolic story is mostly about *negative energy balance promoting wake and foraging* -- you stay awake to find dinner. MECH-286 frames metabolic *demand* (alongside drive and threat) as *suppressing sleep onset*. These are the same switch pushed in the same direction, so the direction of evidence is supportive. But the claim's particular packaging -- a single override that suppresses onset and, when it relents, recruits a sleep-replay priority readout -- is not what the review describes or tests. The metabolic input is biologically real; the override-and-recruitment architecture around it is REE's contribution, and the review neither confirms nor denies that layer.

As with the other MECH-286 entries, this is secondary literature: a brief authoritative review, not a causal dissociation. It does not isolate a metabolic-demand override on sleep onset from orexin's general wake-drive, so it cannot by itself adjudicate whether MECH-286's override is one signal or several.

I score this 0.74. It is an authoritative source by the model's originator and maps well onto the metabolic-demand arm of the claim, but it is short, secondary, and framed around foraging-wake-promotion rather than the threat-and-demand-gated onset suppression MECH-286 specifies -- so I keep both mapping fidelity and the aggregate in the mid-0.7s.
