# Symmetries in human brain language pathways correlate with verbal recall (Catani et al., 2007)

## Why a counterweight belongs in this review

Two entries so far both push the same way: the dorsal route is left-dominant, the affective route is
right-biased and ventral, and ARC-146's separation is well-founded. A review that stopped there would
be advocacy. The question the intake's gate was really protecting against is subtler than "are there
two streams" -- it is whether REE should treat strong lateralisation as the thing to build.

Catani and colleagues used diffusion tensor MRI to dissect the direct Broca-Wernicke connection in a
normal population. What they found is that lateralisation is not a constant. More than half of
subjects showed extreme leftward lateralisation, but 17.5% were bilaterally symmetrical, with the rest
spread between. And then the result that gives the paper its title: the *more symmetric* individuals
were better overall at remembering words using semantic association.

The authors state the implication plainly -- bilateral representation, not extreme lateralisation,
might ultimately be advantageous for specific cognitive functions.

## What this does to the claim

It supports ARC-146 in one respect and complicates it in another, which is why the direction is
recorded as mixed.

The support: the arcuate *is* the dorsal route, and it *is* the lateralised structure. Whatever is
carrying affective coupling is not this. The dorsal/ventral, lateralised/bilateral carving holds.

The complication: lateralisation degree is a population parameter with wide variance, and the extreme
configuration is not the high-performing one on the measure taken. ARC-146 is written as an
architectural commitment -- a fact about how the design must be. The biology is a distribution. If
REE hard-codes a strongly lateralised symbolic route on the grounds that this is how brains are, it
will have hard-coded the modal configuration rather than the good one, and it will have converted a
tunable parameter into a fixed architectural fact without noticing.

That is a genuinely useful thing for the registration pass to know, and it is exactly the sort of
finding a gate exists to surface.

## Where I would not lean on it

The cognitive advantage is for semantic-association word recall, which is a ventral-flavoured
function, while the anatomical measure is the dorsal arcuate. A symmetric dorsal route predicting
better ventral-ish performance is not straightforwardly interpretable. It could be a third variable --
general white-matter integrity would do it, and the authors themselves note females were more likely
to be symmetric, which is an obvious confound route into a cognitive measure.

The imaging is also 2007-era deterministic tensor tractography, which under-recovers crossing fibres.
That method systematically tends to *miss* tracts, and it would miss them most where fibre geometry is
complex, so the reported right-hemisphere absence in the strongly-lateralised majority may be
overstated. If anything this biases the paper toward finding more extreme lateralisation than exists,
which makes its symmetric-is-better finding more striking rather than less -- but it also means the
17.5% figure should not be quoted as a quantitative target.

## Confidence

0.70, direction mixed. Good venue and a genuinely important corrective, discounted for the
cross-stream interpretive gap, the confound routes, and the dating of the tractography.
