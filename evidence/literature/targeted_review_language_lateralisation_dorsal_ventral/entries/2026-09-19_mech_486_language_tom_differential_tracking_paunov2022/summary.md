# Differential tracking of linguistic vs mental-state content (Paunov et al., 2022)

## Why this sits beside the Shain entry rather than duplicating it

Shain and colleagues report a null: no ToM reasoning in the language network. Nulls are informative
but structurally limited -- absence of overlap is not the same as presence of two mechanisms. Paunov
and colleagues supply the positive half, and the pairing is what makes the case.

Their design is a 2x2 over naturalistic material. Stories and dialogues carry both language and
mental-state content. Silent animations and live-action films carry mental states with no language.
Expository text carries language with no mental-state content. Forty-three participants,
individual-subject functional localisers for both networks, inter-subject correlation as the tracking
measure.

The result is a double dissociation. The ToM network tracked mental-state-rich stimuli robustly
*regardless of whether the mental states arrived linguistically*, and tracked the +linguistic/-ToM
stimulus only weakly. The language network tracked linguistic stimuli more strongly than
non-linguistic ones, more strongly than the ToM network did, and -- the load-bearing detail -- tracked
reliably even when the linguistic material contained no mental-state content at all.

## What this gives MECH-486

MECH-486 claims representational *interference*: that discrete, reportable, sequential language
representations and graded, affective, resonant empathy representations exert conflicting
representational pressures. That claim has a precondition. It only makes sense if the two content
types are genuinely different kinds of thing handled by different machinery -- if mental-state
representation were simply a downstream product of linguistic representation, there would be no
conflicting pressures to speak of, only one pressure with two read-outs.

The ToM network's *modality indifference* is what closes that precondition. It tracks mental states
from a silent animation as readily as from a dialogue. The representation is therefore not parasitic
on the linguistic one. ARC-123's "distinct input histories, representational pressures and gating" now
has something under it.

## The gap, which is large and which I think is good news

A double dissociation shows two systems tracking different things *in a brain that has both*.
MECH-486 is a claim about what happens when they are forced to share one workspace at matched
capacity, and neither halves of that counterfactual -- forcing, or matched capacity -- exists in any
neuroimaging study. No amount of human fMRI will settle it.

REE can. That is a substrate experiment: build the merged and separated configurations at matched
capacity and measure whether both degrade. So the right reading of this entry is that it grounds
MECH-486's premise and leaves its consequent entirely open, which is exactly the division of labour
between literature and experiment that the corpus is supposed to maintain. What it must not do is
blur into "MECH-486 is supported" at registration time.

Two smaller cautions. The dissociation is graded rather than absolute -- the ToM network tracked the
language-only stimulus weakly rather than not at all -- so the systems are separable without being
disjoint. And naturalistic stimuli vary on many dimensions at once; some of the between-network
difference may reflect modality or narrative structure rather than the intended factors.

## Confidence

0.72. Good design, right method, and the necessary positive complement to the null. Discounted for
n=43, for the graded rather than absolute dissociation, and because what it evidences is MECH-486's
premise rather than MECH-486.
