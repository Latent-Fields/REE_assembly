# The cortical organization of speech processing (Hickok & Poeppel, 2007)

## Why the gate existed

The 2026-04-16 language_lateralisation intake registered its dissociation claim (now ARC-146) but
held back `affect.bilateral_right_biased_coupling` behind a literature gate, on the grounds that the
affect-coupling MECH should not be registered on a vague "language is left-lateralised" picture. That
picture is folk neuroscience, and building an architectural commitment on it would have been building
on a caricature.

Hickok and Poeppel are the correction. Their dual-stream model has two routes with different jobs: a
ventral stream that processes the speech signal for comprehension, and a dorsal stream that maps
acoustic speech onto frontal-lobe articulatory networks. Crucially for us, the two are *differently
lateralised*. The dorsal stream is strongly left-hemisphere dominant. The ventral stream is largely
bilateral.

## What this settles for ARC-146

ARC-146 commits REE to treating dorsal high-fidelity structured symbolic routing as architecturally
distinct from the affective-coupling system, joined by a ventral interface that binds affect to
symbol, and forbids implementing affect as a property of the symbolic route.

The lateralisation asymmetry is what makes that separation load-bearing rather than descriptive. If
both routes were left-dominant, "two streams" would be a convenient carving of one mechanism and REE
could reasonably implement them as one pathway with two read-outs. They are not. A route that is
strongly left-dominant and a route that is largely bilateral cannot be two aspects of the same
mechanism, because a single mechanism would not have two different lateralisation profiles. The
anatomy forces the architecture.

That is the specific thing the intake wanted checked, and it holds.

## Three limits, because the mapping is easier to overstate than to state

The ventral stream's bilaterality here is for *comprehension* -- getting meaning out of the acoustic
signal. ARC-146's ventral interface is an *affect-to-symbol binder*. Those are different functions
that happen to share a route label, and running them together would be exactly the kind of quiet
elision that makes a claim look better evidenced than it is. The affective content of the ventral
route is carried by the aprosodia entry in this review, not by this one.

Hickok and Poeppel also qualify their own bilaterality: there are "important computational
differences between the left- and right-hemisphere systems" within the ventral stream. Bilateral is
not symmetric. A REE implementation that modelled the ventral route as one undifferentiated bilateral
pathway would be simplifying past the paper's own caveat, and would lose whatever the right-hemisphere
ventral system is specifically for -- which, on the aprosodia evidence, is a great deal.

And the model is about *speech*. ARC-146 says "structured symbolic routing", which is broader.
Dual-stream anatomy for acoustic-to-articulation does not automatically extend to symbolic routing in
general, and the claim should not be read as though it does.

0.82, the highest in this review. Near-ceiling source, and the mapping is structural -- the paper

than something from which ARC-146 must be inferred.
