# No evidence of theory of mind reasoning in the human language network (Shain et al., 2023)

## The methodological point first, because it is why this entry is worth its confidence

Language and theory-of-mind networks sit close together in cortex. Group-averaged fMRI blurs across
individuals whose functional anatomy differs by a centimetre or two, and the predictable result is
apparent overlap between any two neighbouring networks. A great deal of the "language and social
cognition share machinery" literature is built on exactly that artefact.

Shain and colleagues use individual-participant functional localisation across 151 people, which is
the correction for it. And their analysis has a structure I find more persuasive than a flat null:
they *first* report that all core language regions respond more strongly to false-belief vignettes
than to control vignettes -- the merged-system prediction, confirmed -- and then show that the
difference is largely attributable to linguistic confounds between the vignette sets, and that nothing
comparable appears in a nonverbal ToM task. They argue themselves out of their own initial result.

## What it settles for ARC-123

ARC-123 commits REE to language and theory-of-mind/fast-empathy as developmentally separate specialist
systems over a shared self/world/action core -- distinct routing, input histories, representational
pressures and gating -- rather than one merged social-linguistic system that differentiates later.
Both ARC-123 and MECH-486 currently carry zero literature entries, so the architecture has been
asserted without biological grounding.

This grounds the separation, and grounds it in the strongest available form: the merged prediction was
tested, appeared to succeed, and did not survive control. For an architectural commitment that is the
difference between a design chosen and a design assumed.

## The three places the mapping is doing work the paper is not

It is a null on overlap. ARC-123's more interesting clause is "partially *homologous* but
developmentally separate" -- a shared core with distinct routing. A null result cannot evidence
homology; it can only fail to find merger. The half of ARC-123 that would actually constrain REE's
implementation is untouched here.

The exploratory finding cuts against an over-reading. Social processing *did* produce responses in the
language network's periphery -- right-hemisphere homotopes of core language areas, and bilateral
angular gyri -- although not selectively for ToM. That periphery is precisely where partial homology
would live, and it is where MECH-486's representational-interference claim would have to be tested.
Reading this paper as "fully separate systems" discards the one result that speaks to the shared part.

And the substitution at the centre of it: ToM here is explicit false-belief reasoning -- effortful,
propositional, and (in the verbal condition) linguistically framed. ARC-123 and MECH-486 are about
*fast empathy*: graded, affective, resonant. A dissociation between language and explicit mentalising
is real evidence, but it may be the easy case; fast empathy might plausibly be *more* entangled with
prosody and hence with the language system's periphery, not less. I would not want the registration
pass to treat this gap as closed.

## Confidence

0.79. Large sample, the right method, and an analysis honest enough to dismantle its own first result.
Capped for being a null, and for the explicit-ToM-for-fast-empathy substitution, which is the entry's
largest single risk.
