# Smith, Forgie & Pellis (1997) -- The postpubertal change in the playful defense of male rats depends upon neonatal exposure to gonadal hormones

*Entry for ARC-073, recorded as counter-evidence to the claim's exclusion clause ("not by scheduled duration").*

## What the paper did

Male rats were either neonatally castrated or left intact, and their defensive tactics during play fighting were observed before and after puberty. Intact males showed the normal age-related shift from juvenile-typical to adult-typical playful defense. Neonatally castrated males did not -- they failed to exhibit the age-related change at all. The authors conclude that the postpubertal change in playful defense is preprogrammed by gonadal hormone exposure in the perinatal period.

This sits in a broader line of work from the Pellis group reporting that changes in play frequency and in the use of defensive tactics arise largely independently of social experience.

## Why this is in an ARC-073 directory

ARC-073 is a claim with a negative in it. It asserts that the play-to-real transition is triggered by competence saturation or drive pressure, *and not by scheduled duration*. Positive clauses accumulate support easily; the exclusion clause is the part that can actually be wrong, and the rest of this directory is not equipped to test it. The Hayden and Kane entries show that threshold-crossing disengagement rules exist; they say nothing about whether a schedule is also operating. The Colas entry has no clock and no exit, so it cannot adjudicate either. The Baarendse entry shows the play window matters but is neutral on how it should close.

This paper is the one case I found where the question is asked directly of a developmental play transition, and the answer goes the wrong way for ARC-073. In neonatally castrated males, play proceeded and play experience accumulated, and the transition did not happen. Competence, whatever it was doing, was not sufficient. An organisational hormonal signal laid down weeks before the play period even began was necessary. In intact animals the transition arrives on a pubertal schedule.

## What I think this actually licenses

Not a retraction. The honest reading is narrower and, I would argue, more useful.

The saturation criterion and the maturational schedule are not competitors in the biology; they look like a conjunction. A gate opens on a developmental timetable, and within it something experience-dependent does the work -- which is close to how ARC-049 and the surrounding play cluster are already framed in REE, and how the plasticity-window material treats neuromodulatory gating generally. What this paper makes untenable is the strong reading of ARC-073's exclusion: that no clock is involved at all, and that competence saturation is the whole story.

The tractable amendment is to make the LP-saturation test a *necessary* condition operating inside a maturational window rather than a *replacement* for one -- an AND, not an OR. That keeps the claim falsifiable (an agent whose LP saturates inside the window should transition; one whose LP saturates outside it should not), and it removes a negative universal the animal literature does not support. I have flagged this to governance rather than acting on it here; amending a claim is not a lit-pull's business.

## Limitations, and they are substantial

The transition studied is a change in the *style* of playful defense within continuing play -- not an exit from play into non-play behaviour. It is not a direct test of the mode switch ARC-073 describes, and the extrapolation from "the tactical shift is hormonally scheduled" to "the mode exit is scheduled" is real and should be held loosely. The mechanism, perinatal androgen organisation of a sexually dimorphic behaviour, has no REE counterpart whatsoever. The study is from 1997, in a mid-tier venue, with the modest sample sizes typical of that era and literature, and I located no direct replication in this pull. The companion paper on the absence of the pubertal shift in females (Smith et al. 1998, PMID 9742409) is consistent with it but is not independent.
