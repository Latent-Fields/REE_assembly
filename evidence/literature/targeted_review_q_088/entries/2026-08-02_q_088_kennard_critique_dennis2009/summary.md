# Margaret Kennard: Not a 'Principle' of Brain Plasticity (Dennis, 2010)

**Claim tested:** Q-088 -- does develop-without dissociate from mature-state lesion, and what may a single decisive probe of that dissociation license?

## The argument

Maureen Dennis's historical and critical review dismantles the "Kennard Principle" -- the widely-repeated claim that there is a negative linear relation between age at brain injury and severity of deficit, so that younger is always better. Dennis shows the slogan is neither Kennard's own view nor a principle. Kennard's actual research sought to explain outcome after early lesions as a *joint* function of age, lesion staging, laterality, location, number of lesions and outcome domain -- and explicitly resisted a single-factor law. The monotone "principle" was retrofitted onto her name in the 1970s plasticity literature and has, Dennis argues, distorted research and practice ever since.

## Why I include it, and why the direction is "mixed"

This paper cuts two ways for Q-088, which is precisely why it is worth a record. On one side it *supports* the claim's core premise: there genuinely is a developmental-versus-mature difference in outcome (an age effect is real), and it is not captured by a simple rule -- which means a mature lesion cannot be trusted to predict the develop-without verdict. That is the PASS direction of Q-088: the two protocols dissociate and the cheap one does not stand in for the expensive one.

On the other side it *weakens* any temptation to over-read a single result. If outcome is multi-factorial and non-monotone, then a decisive probe on one mechanism -- say INV-055's infant stage -- tells you about *that* mechanism under *those* conditions, and licenses nothing about the develop-without protocol as a general standing requirement. Strikingly, this is exactly the scope discipline Q-088 already writes into itself: it is framed as "ONE decisive probe, not a standing requirement," with the explicit instruction "do not generalise the protocol before the probe reports." Dennis supplies the external, historical justification for that caution -- the field's cardinal error was precisely the generalisation of a contingent, multi-factorial phenomenon into a law.

## Caveats

This is human developmental-neuropsychology scholarship, and several of the "factors" Dennis foregrounds (hemispheric laterality, lesion number) are brain-specific and have no direct REE counterpart. So I do not transfer its content; I transfer its *posture*: expect a dissociation, do not expect a law, and scope any single probe's conclusion tightly, holding non-mechanism factors fixed across arms so an apparent dissociation cannot be a covariate in disguise.

## Confidence

0.60. It is a careful, well-cited critical review, and its bearing on Q-088 is almost entirely about *interpretation* -- how much a single probe result may claim -- rather than about the existence of the effect, which the other four entries carry. I mark it `mixed` deliberately: it strengthens the dissociation premise while restraining the generalisation, and both of those are correct readings of what a Q-088 probe should and should not be allowed to conclude.
