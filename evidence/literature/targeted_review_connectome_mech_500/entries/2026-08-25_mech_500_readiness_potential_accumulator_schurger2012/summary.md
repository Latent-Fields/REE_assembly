# An Accumulator Model for Spontaneous Neural Activity Prior to Self-Initiated Movement (Schurger, Sitt & Dehaene 2012)

This landmark PNAS paper reframes the readiness potential (RP) -- the slow, gradual buildup of
scalp EEG activity that has for decades been interpreted as the neural signature of an unfolding
decision to move, most famously in Libet's free-will experiments -- as the product of an ordinary
mechanism: a leaky stochastic accumulator integrating spontaneous, largely noise-like neural
fluctuations toward a fixed threshold that triggers movement. When the imperative to move is weak
(a self-paced task with no external trigger), it is these spontaneous fluctuations, not a
deliberate unfolding "decision," that primarily determine the precise moment of movement
initiation -- movement happens whenever the accumulating noise happens to cross threshold first.
The model reproduces both the characteristic RP waveform shape and the empirical distribution of
movement-onset timing, and the authors confirmed a striking further prediction: rapid responses to
an unexpected external interruption are preceded by the same slow negative deflection seen before
self-initiated movement, even when the subject had no prior intention to move at all -- exactly
what an accumulator crossing threshold from ambient noise would produce, and hard to explain if the
RP specifically reflected deliberate preparation.

**Why this matters for MECH-500.** MECH-500 proposes that "whether a candidate future has become
sufficiently coherent for commitment to be permitted at all" is a distinct constitutional question
from content authority, and that fast cross-system field coherence -- extending MECH-499's
proposed aggregation mechanism -- is a candidate substrate for that readiness axis, explicitly
DISTINCT from MECH-434's evidence/posterior-driven epistemic commitment timing. This paper is the
sharpest parsimony challenge available in the literature to that motivating premise. The RP is the
closest existing biological analogue to "is this the moment commitment should be permitted to
land," and Schurger et al. show it is well explained without positing any field-coherence or
cross-population-synchrony mechanism at all -- an ordinary accumulator, operating on the SAME
mechanism family (evidence/threshold accumulation) MECH-434 already covers, is sufficient. If the
best-characterised real case of "readiness timing" reduces to accumulation, that weakens the case
that REE's proposal-generation machinery specifically needs a SEPARATE, coherence-based readiness
mechanism rather than simply extending an accumulator-class mechanism to the hippocampal case too.

**What it does not rule out.** This paper is about single-region (motor cortex/SMA), self-paced
movement-ONSET timing for an already-selected action -- not about gating commitment to a
hippocampally-generated CANDIDATE FUTURE against a cross-system field-coherence signal, which is
what MECH-500 actually proposes. The accumulator model demonstrates sufficiency for the movement-RP
case; it does not demonstrate that no additional, faster, coherence-based readiness signal could
also operate upstream in the hippocampal-proposal pipeline MECH-500 targets. The two mechanisms
are not mutually exclusive in principle.

**Confidence and direction.** 0.68, `weakens`. Recorded as weakens on the same basis MECH-090's own
evidence base uses Hanes-Schall/Cisek/Roesch-Calu-Schoenbaum as weakens: accumulator/competition
models are the well-evidenced scientific default that a proposal for a distinct mechanism must
argue past, and this is the clearest single case where that default was tested directly against
the "readiness needs its own signature" intuition -- and the default won. Source quality is very
high (landmark, extensively replicated); mapping fidelity is moderate because the specific decision
tested (movement onset) and mechanism (single-region accumulator) differ from MECH-500's target
(hippocampal-future commitment gating via cross-system field coherence).
