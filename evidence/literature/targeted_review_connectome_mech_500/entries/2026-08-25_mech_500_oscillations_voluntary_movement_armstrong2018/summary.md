# Neural Oscillations and the Initiation of Voluntary Movement (Armstrong, Sale & Cunnington 2018)

This narrative review surveys the literature on how the brain determines the moment a voluntary
movement begins, centering on the observation that brain states indicative of a forthcoming
movement are measurable before a person becomes subjectively aware of deciding to act. The authors'
preferred account is that slow-frequency oscillatory or subthreshold potential fluctuations (in the
readiness-potential/contingent-negative-variation family) may set movement timing by pushing
ongoing brain activity above a threshold that triggers action initiation -- effectively, an
oscillation-flavored variant of a threshold-crossing account. They explicitly flag that whether
these oscillatory dynamics CAUSE movement initiation or merely CORRELATE with it remains unresolved,
and propose non-invasive brain stimulation as the way to test causality directly.

**Why this is here for MECH-500.** MECH-500's central structural move is separating "content
authority" (which candidate future wins) from "temporal/readiness authority" (whether a candidate
is coherent enough for commitment to be permitted at all), and proposing that the readiness axis is
NOT reducible to MECH-434's evidence/posterior-driven epistemic commitment timing. This review is
useful, if modest, context for that general distinction: it takes seriously the idea that oscillatory
brain dynamics, not simply accumulated evidence, can be the operative variable in commitment/action
timing, which keeps the conceptual door open for a readiness axis that isn't just MECH-434 wearing
a different name.

**Why it does not directly support MECH-500's specific mechanism.** The review's own favored
account is single-region, slow-potential threshold-crossing -- structurally the SAME shape of
mechanism (accumulate/build toward a fixed threshold) as the Schurger et al. 2012 accumulator model
pulled as this claim's other literature entry, which shows that shape of mechanism does not require
positing anything beyond ordinary noise accumulation. Nothing in this review proposes or tests
CROSS-SYSTEM field coherence -- coherence BETWEEN populations or streams, as opposed to a
single-region potential crossing a threshold -- as the readiness variable. That is the specific
mechanism MECH-500 needs literature on, and this review does not supply it. The authors' own
acknowledgment that causality is unresolved further limits how much weight this can carry.

**Mapping caveats.** This is about single-region readiness-potential/CNV dynamics for movement
initiation in general human/EEG literature, not hippocampal proposal-generation, not
cross-system field coherence, and not gating commitment to an already-generated candidate future
specifically. Read together with the Schurger 2012 entry, the honest picture from this pull is that
the existing oscillation/readiness literature is dominated by single-region, accumulator-compatible
accounts -- the specific cross-system-coherence mechanism MECH-500 proposes remains, as the
thought-intake itself anticipated, largely untested in the literature searched here.

**Confidence.** 0.45, `mixed`. Review-level source quality is moderate; mapping fidelity to
MECH-500's specific field-coherence mechanism is low, even though the general axis-distinction
(oscillation-based vs. pure-accumulation readiness) is a fair, if partial, match.
