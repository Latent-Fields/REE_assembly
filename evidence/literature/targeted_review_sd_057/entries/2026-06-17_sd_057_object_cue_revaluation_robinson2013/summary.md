# Robinson & Berridge (2013) — Instant transformation of learned repulsion into motivational "wanting"

According to PubMed ([DOI: 10.1016/j.cub.2013.01.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2013.01.016), *Current Biology*).

**What the paper did.** Rats were trained so that a discrete, briefly-inserted metal lever always predicted an intensely unpleasant Dead-Sea-salt taste. They learned active *repulsion* toward this lever-cue. The authors then re-presented the lever for the first time while the animals were in a novel state of sodium depletion — a state that makes salt suddenly valuable. The previously-repulsive lever was *instantly* transformed into an attractive motivational magnet: rats jumped on it and gnawed it, despite never having tasted intense salt as anything but disgusting. Fos imaging confirmed elevated mesocorticolimbic activation (VTA, nucleus accumbens, ventral pallidum, orbitofrontal cortex). The behaviour appeared at first re-encounter, without re-learning.

**Why it matters for SD-057.** Two of SD-057's load-bearing pillars are (i) that incentive value is bound to a specific *object identity* rather than smeared into a single overwritten goal attractor, and (ii) that this per-object incentive token is *revaluable* — its wanting amplitude is recomputed at recall (MECH-345). Robinson & Berridge demonstrate both in their starkest form. The incentive value here is attached to a *particular discrete object* (this lever), and that value is so revaluable that it can be flipped from active repulsion to avid attraction in a single step. This is the behavioural signature of exactly the data structure SD-057 builds: a per-object token carrying an incentive value that is read and re-scaled at the moment of encounter, not a fixed cached liking-value and not a single z_goal slot overwritten on every contact.

**The mapping and its limits.** The candour required here is about *what drives the revaluation*. In this experiment the recomputation is produced by a physiological state change (salt depletion), which means the demonstration entangles the two mechanisms REE deliberately separates: object-binding (SD-057) and drive-state modulation (MECH-436). It shows incentive value is object-specific and revaluable, but it cannot, on its own, prove that the object-binding architecture is doing independent work apart from a drive gain acting on a single value. That separation is precisely what REE's split between SD-057 and MECH-436 is trying to make testable, and this paper sits at the junction rather than cleanly on the SD-057 side. A second limit: the behaviour is directed at a *single* lever-cue. SD-057's most-wanted argmax step — selecting one object among several simultaneously-represented incentive tokens and seeding z_goal from it — involves cross-object *competition* that a single-object task does not exercise.

**Confidence reasoning.** I set this at 0.70. Source quality is solid (≈0.82): a well-designed behavioural study with Fos corroboration in a strong venue, though Fos is a coarse activation marker and the core result is behavioural. Mapping fidelity is the binding constraint (≈0.70): the object-specific, revaluable incentive attribution maps cleanly onto SD-057's per-object token, but the entanglement with drive state and the absence of cross-object competition mean it supports the *components* of SD-057 without isolating the architecture as a whole. Transfer risk is moderate (≈0.35): single-lever rat approach behaviour versus REE multi-object foraging. Net: genuine support for the object-bound, revaluable nature of incentive salience — read alongside Flagel et al. for the what/where separability — while leaving SD-057's specific most-wanted-argmax-seed mechanism as an untested engineering commitment.
