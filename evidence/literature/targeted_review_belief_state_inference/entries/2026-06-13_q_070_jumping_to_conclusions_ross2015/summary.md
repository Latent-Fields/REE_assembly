# Jumping to Conclusions About the Beads Task? (Ross et al., 2015)

**Claims grounded:** Q-070 (failure-register; the premature-collapse / over-hasty-inference mode). Failure-register strand. **Direction: mixed.**

The "jumping to conclusions" (JTC) bias -- delusional and delusion-prone people deciding on the basis of less evidence in the beads / draws-to-decision task -- is the cleanest behavioural operationalisation of INF-7's **premature-collapse** and **anti-epistemic-panic** modes. Ross and colleagues meta-analysed the literature and reached a deliberately two-sided conclusion: the association between delusional ideation and reduced data-gathering is *real and reliable*, but it is *modest in magnitude and partly confounded* (notably by general cognitive ability) rather than a large, specific reasoning deficit.

That two-sidedness is why I have logged this as **mixed** rather than supports, and it is the most useful thing the entry contributes to INF-7. It supports *including* premature-collapse in the register -- committing on too little evidence is a genuine, measurable inference failure, and "treat residual uncertainty as zero" maps onto MECH-126 uncertainty collapse. But it simultaneously *weakens* any clean claim that the register's entries are crisp, dissociable mechanisms. The clinical reality of "hypothesis collapse too early" is graded, noisy, and entangled with general capacity -- which is exactly why Q-070 is framed as an *open question* ("do these modes reduce to the existing taxonomy?") and not as an assertion of a new parallel one. The meta-analysis is a calibration warning against over-crisp taxonomy.

The transfer caveat is large and I want it on the record: the beads task is a narrow, abstract human probabilistic-reasoning paradigm with no shared task, species, or substrate with REE's commitment-timing and epistemic-action-pressure dynamics. This entry grounds the *existence and clinical relevance* of an over-hasty-commitment failure mode, plus a methodological caution, not a mechanism REE inherits. Confidence 0.62 -- high source quality (a meta-analysis) offset by low mapping fidelity and the qualifying effect-size finding.
