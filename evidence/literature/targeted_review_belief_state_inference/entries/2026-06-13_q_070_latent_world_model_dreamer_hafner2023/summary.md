# Mastering Diverse Domains through World Models -- DreamerV3 (Hafner et al., 2023)

**Claims grounded:** Q-070, ARC-091. Strand L4 (latent world models).

DreamerV3 learns a compact *latent* dynamics model from interaction and then trains its actor-critic almost entirely inside *imagined* trajectories rolled out in that latent space -- it improves behaviour by dreaming, not by acting. With a single fixed configuration it reaches strong performance and data-efficiency across 150+ domains spanning discrete/continuous control, visual and low-dimensional inputs, and 2D/3D worlds, and it was the first agent to collect diamonds in Minecraft from scratch without human data -- a long-horizon, sparse-reward, partial-information task of exactly the kind that defeats reactive agents.

This is the machine-learning existence-proof for a premise REE shares: intelligent action under partial information depends on *latent prediction plus imagined rollout*, not reactive observation-to-action mapping. It is the ML analogue of REE's E2 action-consequence prediction (MECH-033) and ARC-018 hippocampal rollouts feeding the inference layer. A model-free agent with no latent model fails the tasks Dreamer solves -- the computational face of "cannot infer a route from partial evidence."

But Dreamer is also, deliberately, an *architectural counter-example* on two axes REE cares about, and the summary should say so. It optimises a single scalar reward and a monolithic latent; REE separates harm, goal, residue, commitment, and ethical texture precisely to avoid reward-collapse, and it treats imagined trajectories as provenance-tagged, corrigible hypotheses (INV-078) rather than as training signal to be consumed. So Dreamer grounds the latent-model-and-imagination *principle* while standing as a cautionary example on stream separation and hypothesis provenance. Combined with its status as a (non-peer-reviewed) engineering benchmark result, that is why this entry carries the lowest confidence in the pull, 0.65, and the highest transfer-risk component.
