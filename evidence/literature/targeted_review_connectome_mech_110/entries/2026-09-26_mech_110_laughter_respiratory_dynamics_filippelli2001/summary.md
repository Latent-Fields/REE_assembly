# Filippelli et al. (2001) -- Respiratory dynamics during laughter

**Claim tested:** MECH-110

## What the paper did

Eleven healthy adults watched the funniest scenes from a Roberto Benigni film while chest-wall volume was tracked with three-dimensional optoelectronic plethysmography, split into upper thorax, lower thorax and abdomen; oesophageal and gastric pressures were recorded in seven of them. Every fit of laughter began suddenly with a run of repetitive expiratory efforts at 4.6 +/- 1.1 Hz. Fits lasted 3.7 +/- 2.2 s and drove lung volume about 1.55 L below functional residual capacity, with oesophageal pressures well beyond flow limitation, which means marked dynamic airway compression. The diaphragm actively limited how much of the abdominal pressure reached the thorax.

## Relevance to MECH-110

MECH-110's notes rest on a physiological premise: laughter is a train of forced exhalations at several per second, far above the resting ~0.2 Hz rhythm, and so an override of the background respiratory clock. This paper measures that premise directly and confirms it. The claim's '3-7 per second' is right, and so is the picture of laughter as a distinct respiratory mode rather than exaggerated ordinary breathing.

## Where it pushes back

The same numbers are awkward for the mechanism. At about 4.6 Hz each expiratory pulse lasts roughly 220 ms. The best timing data we have for resolving an incongruity (the P600 component in Shibata et al. 2017, on the materials of Chan et al. 2013 in this directory) put a single resolution at around 600 ms. If each pulse were its own threat-write, sweep and clear, the cycle would have to run faster than one resolution. The fits are also stereotyped: sudden onset and a monotonic fall in volume. That looks like a released motor programme, the sort of thing a brainstem pattern generator produces once triggered, not a sequence of separately gated decisions. A gentler reading keeps MECH-110 alive: one resolution releases the laughter programme, and the rhythm of the pulses belongs to the motor system, not to repeated cognitive cycling. But that reading gives up the claim's distinctive 'each exhalation is one tag-clear' mechanism.

## Confidence

Mixed, 0.35. The respiratory premise is supported. By a timescale argument, the per-pulse cycling mechanism is weakened, though not refuted, since no cognitive measure was taken. This is exactly the kind of paper that should shape the laughter-bout and exhalation-timing DV in the claim's `what_would_answer`.
