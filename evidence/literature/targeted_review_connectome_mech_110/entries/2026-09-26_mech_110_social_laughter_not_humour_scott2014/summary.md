# Scott, Lavan, Chen & McGettigan (2014) -- The social life of laughter

**Claim tested:** MECH-110

## What the paper argues

Scott and colleagues push back on the everyday assumption that laughter is the product of humour. Drawing on observational work (Provine; Vettin and Todt), they report that most laughter occurs in conversation, and within conversation mostly after ordinary statements and comments rather than jokes. People are about thirty times more likely to laugh when others are present, and laughter is highly contagious. They distinguish reactive, involuntary laughter from controlled, volitional laughter. The two differ acoustically and in their neural correlates (the hypothalamus is more engaged in reactive laughter; auditory versus prefrontal responses when hearing involuntary versus voluntary laughs), and chimpanzees show an analogous split between tickle-evoked and play laughter. Laughter is also linked to bonding and to damping physiological stress responses.

## Relevance to MECH-110

The claim's title makes a general statement: *laughter is* rapid repeated threat-activation and safe-resolution cycling. If most real-world laughter has no incongruity or resolution in front of it, the general form cannot stand, and incongruity plus resolution is not a necessary antecedent of laughter as such. What survives is a narrower claim that the cycling account applies to *evoked* laughter, the involuntary, humour- or tickle-driven kind. MECH-110's `what_would_answer` already asks whether 'laughter occurs independently of the proposed cycling'; this review says that in the ordinary social case it largely does.

## What it offers the claim

The social findings are not all bad news. Contagion, bonding and stress regulation fit comfortably with the claim's V5-scoped extension, in which group laughter is a shared 'nothing to fear' signal and contagion is entrainment across agents. The review does not, of course, test entrainment of any planning clock.

## Caveats and confidence

This is a short forum review summarising other people's observations. The absence of an overt joke does not strictly show the absence of a small social incongruity being resolved, so a determined defender could reinterpret conversational laughter as micro-resolution. I have scored the weakening at 0.4 and framed it as a scope problem: MECH-110 should probably be narrowed to evoked laughter, with volitional or social laughter treated as a separate channel.
