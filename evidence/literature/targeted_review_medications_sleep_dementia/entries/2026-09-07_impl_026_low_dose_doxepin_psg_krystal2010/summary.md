# Low-dose doxepin: a clean dissociation, measured on the wrong variables

IMPL-026's low-dose doxepin row does something clever. It takes a tricyclic antidepressant -- a class the same table condemns to the HIGH-RISK tier for anticholinergic Phase 4 suppression -- and rescues one member of it by dose. Below 10 mg, the argument runs, doxepin is a selective H1 antagonist with negligible muscarinic activity, so it buys sedation without paying the anticholinergic REM penalty. The row is rated "NEUTRAL to mildly IMPROVES Phase 2/3". If that dissociation is real it is a genuinely useful piece of clinical guidance, and it is exactly the kind of claim a classification table exists to make.

Krystal and colleagues tested it, and this is the methodologically strongest study in this pull: randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, twelve weeks, with laboratory polysomnography, in 240 elderly patients with chronic primary insomnia (doxepin 1 mg n = 77, 3 mg n = 82, placebo n = 81). The 3 mg dose beat placebo on wake time after sleep onset, total sleep time and sleep efficiency from Night 1, and the authors report those improvements "sustained at N85 for all variables, with significance maintained for WASO, TST, overall SE, and SE in the last quarter of the night". Subjective sleep onset latency and sleep quality improved across weeks 1 to 12.

The safety result is the one that matters most for IMPL-026, and it is an explicit negative: "there were no significant next-day residual effects; additionally, there were no reports of memory impairment, complex sleep behaviors, anticholinergic effects, weight gain, or increased appetite." That is the dose-dissociation hypothesis confirmed on its own terms. Sedation was obtained; the anticholinergic signature that makes the TCA row dangerous did not appear. For a table whose organising logic is "which agents get you sleep without costing you a pipeline phase", this is close to an ideal result.

And yet I have marked it `mixed`, because of what was not measured. The polysomnographic outcomes reported are continuity measures -- WASO, total sleep time, sleep efficiency. IMPL-026 does not claim low-dose doxepin improves sleep continuity; it claims it "enhances sleep continuity *and spindle density* (Phase 3 compatible)". Spindle density is not in these results. Nor is a sleep-stage breakdown that would let anyone check the Phase 2/3 assignment. So the table's own primary citation supports the row's *safety* clause decisively and leaves the row's *mechanistic* clause -- the part that earns it a Phase 3 label rather than a bare "well tolerated" -- entirely unevidenced. Reading the absence of anticholinergic harm as the presence of architecture preservation is precisely the inferential slide this corpus should be catching.

Two further boundaries worth recording. The trial tested 1 mg and 3 mg; IMPL-026 specifies the 3-6 mg Silenor range, so half the row's dose window is uncovered, and a muscarinic-sparing finding at 3 mg does not automatically hold at 6 mg. And the population was elderly primary insomnia, not elderly at elevated Alzheimer's risk, over twelve weeks rather than the multi-year horizon on which the rest of the table assigns its dementia-risk directions. Chiu et al. (2021) independently rank low-dose doxepin as the optimal pharmacotherapy for older adults on combined efficacy and safety, which corroborates the practical recommendation without touching the phase question either.

Confidence 0.68. The study is better than that number suggests; what the number reflects is the distance between what it measured and what IMPL-026 asserts.
