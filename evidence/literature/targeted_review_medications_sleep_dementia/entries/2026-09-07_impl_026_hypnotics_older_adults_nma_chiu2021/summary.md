# A comparative ranking of hypnotics, next to a comparative ranking of hypnotics -- and they measure different things

IMPL-026 is, structurally, a ranking. It sorts sleep-affecting medications into tiers by predicted effect on the consolidation pipeline. The obvious external check on a ranking is another ranking, built independently and by different means, so this network meta-analysis is the most methodologically apt comparison available even though it was not written with anything like REE in mind.

Chiu and colleagues synthesised 24 randomised controlled trials covering 5917 older adults. Eszopiclone and low-dose doxepin ranked optimal for prolonging objective and subjective total sleep time (26.69 and 28.19 minutes); zaleplon was most effective for sleep onset latency (-21.63 and -15.86 minutes); temazepam was best for wake after sleep onset (-25.29 and -22.25 minutes); low-dose doxepin performed best on sleep efficiency. On safety, triazolam carried the higher risk of overall adverse events (odds ratio 1.96, 95% confidence interval 1.03 to 3.74). The authors' overall verdict was that "low-dose doxepin seems to be the optimal pharmacotherapy" once efficacy and safety are considered together.

At one point the two rankings agree, and it is not a trivial point: low-dose doxepin comes out well in both, by wholly different routes. Chiu et al. reach it from pooled efficacy and tolerability; IMPL-026 reaches it from a receptor argument about H1 selectivity below 10 mg. Convergence from independent premises is the kind of thing that should raise our confidence in the doxepin row, and it does.

But the more instructive result is the disagreement, and it is internal to this paper rather than something I have had to import. Eszopiclone ranks jointly first for total sleep time. Eszopiclone is a Z-drug, and IMPL-026 puts the Z-drugs in its WORSENS tier on the grounds that they reduce Phase 1 slow-wave depth. Both statements can be true simultaneously -- an agent can reliably add half an hour of sleep while degrading the quality of what it adds -- and that is exactly the point. Sleep quantity and pipeline integrity are separable orderings, and here they visibly come apart. Anyone tempted to validate IMPL-026 by checking it against clinical hypnotic guidelines should look hard at this case first: the word "optimal" is doing different work in the two documents.

The other thing to record is an absence. IMPL-026 places the dual orexin receptor antagonists -- suvorexant, lemborexant -- at the top of its ordering, with "DPM potential: YES" and first-line status for MECH-177 trials, on the argument that removing wake-promoting orexin drive does not compress the phase machinery. They do not appear among this network's ranked optima. That is not a refutation; it most likely reflects the trials available to the network at the time. It does mean the table's single most consequential comparative assertion -- orexin antagonists above the GABAergics on architecture preservation -- remains untested by the best comparative synthesis in the field. The existing entries in this directory (Herring 2020, Snyder 2016) support the orexin-antagonist row on its own terms; what is missing corpus-wide is the head-to-head that IMPL-026's own notes assert exists.

Direction `mixed`, confidence 0.66. High source quality, deliberately low mapping fidelity, and the most useful thing it contributes is a demonstration that IMPL-026's ordering is not reducible to the clinical efficacy ordering it might otherwise be mistaken for.
