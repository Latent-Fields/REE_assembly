# Trazodone, slow-wave sleep and cognitive decline: the right question, asked with too small a sample

IMPL-026 makes one genuinely strong claim, and it is about trazodone. Most of the table is risk-avoidance -- do not use anticholinergics, deprescribe benzodiazepines -- but trazodone at 25-100 mg is assigned "Risk: IMPROVES Phase 1/2 fidelity. DPM potential: YES". A disease-progression-modifying assignment is a much heavier commitment than a risk-direction assignment, and it deserves to be tested against its actual source.

La and colleagues compared 25 trazodone users with 25 propensity-matched non-users over roughly four years in a memory-clinic population. Non-users declined 2.6-fold faster on MMSE: 0.70 points per year (95% CI 0.50 to 0.90) against 0.27 (95% CI 0.07 to 0.48), p = 0.023. In post-hoc analysis the benefit was especially associated with subjective improvement of sleep complaints (p = 0.0006). The authors conclude there is an association between trazodone use and delayed cognitive decline, and explicitly say prospective work is needed both to establish causation and to clarify whether the effect runs through slow-wave sleep enhancement.

That last clause is the one I want to hold onto, because it is where a careless citation would go wrong. The paper is titled for slow-wave sleep enhancers and it is routinely read as evidence that enhancing slow-wave sleep protects cognition. It performed no polysomnography. The mediator it actually found was *subjective* sleep improvement. Subjective sleep improvement is compatible with Phase 1/2 enhancement, and it is equally compatible with reduced nocturnal awakening, better mood, or simply feeling better about a night one slept no differently through. If REE cites this entry as confirming the Phase 1/2 mechanism, REE is asserting the mechanism and then reading it back out of a study that assumed it. I would rather the record say plainly: this supports the *outcome* half of the trazodone row and leaves the *mechanism* half unevidenced.

The other limitations are stated frankly by the authors and I will not soften them. Twenty-five per arm is small enough that an effect of this size is a live candidate for regression to the mean. The design is retrospective and non-randomised, so people prescribed trazodone may differ systematically from those who were not -- and in a memory clinic the reasons for prescribing a sedating antidepressant are unlikely to be independent of trajectory. Propensity matching narrows that gap; it does not close it.

Prospective confirmation is now actually underway: the REST trial (NCT05282550) is a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover study of trazodone in 100 older adults with amnestic MCI and sleep complaints, described by Eyob et al. (2024, J Alzheimers Dis 101(s1):S205-S215, doi 10.3233/JAD-230635). I considered adding that paper as a separate entry and decided against it: it is a design-and-rationale publication reporting no primary outcome, so it would be an entry containing no evidence. It is worth recording here as a pointer, because when REST reports it will be the single most decisive test of IMPL-026's trazodone row, in either direction.

Direction `supports`, confidence 0.55 -- deliberately at the low edge of the moderate band. The question is exactly right; the sample and the missing polysomnography are what keep this from being the strong entry the table's DPM assignment would need.
