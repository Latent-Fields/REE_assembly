# Buhle & Wager (2010) -- Performance-dependent inhibition of pain by an executive working memory task

**Claim tested:** INV-092 | **Direction:** supports | **Confidence:** 0.78

## What the paper did

Twenty-four healthy adults performed a demanding 3-back executive working-memory task, or a visually matched no-load control, while receiving noxious heat. Both variables were individually calibrated per participant -- task difficulty and stimulus intensity -- which is the methodological care that makes this paper usable as evidence rather than as illustration. Three heat levels (mean 41.4, 44.5 and 47.4 C) crossed with two load conditions over 36 trials.

Two effects, in opposite directions, in the same subjects. Participants reported less pain under working-memory load than under the matched control (F(1,768) = 48.3, p < .001), with a heat x task-demand interaction (F(1,768) = 12.64, p < .005). Conversely, increasing heat degraded task performance (F(1,378) = 9.24, p < .01). Path analysis showed pain completely mediated the heat-to-performance effect (ab = -.03, Z = -3.91, p < .001), and -- the finding I find most informative -- trial-by-trial pain fluctuations predicted performance decrements even within a fixed heat level (b = -.02, Z = -3.52, p < .001). The authors read this as evidence that pain processing and executive working memory draw on an overlapping, capacity-limited resource.

## Why this matters for INV-092

INV-092's falsifier asks for a paired measurement across a suppression-strength sweep: goal completion under distractor load *and* interrupt latency or rate for an injected harm signal, never collapsed to one number. This paper is that design, run in a biological system, before anyone was asking the question on REE's behalf. The manipulated variable is goal engagement; the two dependent measures are task performance and harm-signal magnitude; both are recorded on the same trials at graded levels of both inputs.

And it comes out the way the invariant fears. Engaging the goal attenuates the harm report, gradedly. A sweep that scored only completion would read the load condition as an unambiguous win.

Three things transfer to REE. First, the panel shape: this is a concrete instantiation of what INV-092 means by measuring jointly, and it can be used as a template for the substrate falsifier when a suppression knob exists. Second, the non-degeneracy guard is satisfied here in the only way that counts -- the no-load control produces robust pain report and robust heat-graded interference, so the harm channel is demonstrably live at zero suppression. INV-092 explicitly requires this and it is the step most easily skipped. Third, and least comfortable: if suppression and harm reception genuinely share a capacity pool, the trade may be structural rather than a tuning artefact. In that case preserved interruptibility is not free -- it *costs* completion -- and the acceptance shape has to price that in rather than hoping a sufficiently clever suppressor gets both. INV-092 currently reads as though the two can be jointly optimised. This paper suggests the honest version is that they can be jointly *bounded*.

## Limitations and caveats

The measured quantity is subjective pain report and task accuracy -- not an interrupt event. The paper never establishes whether the harm signal would have succeeded in breaking off the ongoing task, which is the variable INV-092 actually constrains. Attenuated report is a proxy for reduced interruptibility, and a loose one. The stimuli are brief phasic heat in a healthy laboratory sample, which excludes the weak, ambiguous cue INV-092 is most concerned about -- the claim's wording is "sufficiently important *weak* evidence", and 47.4 C is not weak evidence. The authors themselves flag that brief rapid-onset stimuli may behave unlike sustained pain. Finally, the shared-resource account is inferred from mediation, not manipulated; the authors concede directionality is not established. Importing that account into REE as a prediction about a suppression knob goes past what was tested.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality 0.80: well-controlled within-subject design in the field's leading journal with per-subject calibration of both manipulated variables, tempered by N = 24 and correlational mediation. Mapping fidelity 0.80 -- the highest in this directory for the falsifier's *shape* (paired axes, graded manipulation, working non-degeneracy control), pulled down only by the report-versus-interrupt gap in the dependent variable. Transfer risk 0.45: the direction of the trade transfers more safely than the capacity-competition mechanism, which REE has no reason to assume it shares. Aggregate 0.78.
