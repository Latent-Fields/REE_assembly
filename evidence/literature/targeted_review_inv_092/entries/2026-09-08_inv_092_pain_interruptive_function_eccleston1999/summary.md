# Eccleston & Crombez (1999) -- Pain demands attention

**Claim tested:** INV-092 (distractor suppression must remain selectively permeable to harm and other-agent signals)
**Direction:** supports | **Confidence:** 0.72

## What the paper did

This is the field's canonical synthesis of why pain captures attention, published in *Psychological Bulletin*. Eccleston and Crombez review the human experimental and clinical literature on pain-attention interaction and build a cognitive-affective model around a single organising idea: pain's function is to *interrupt*. It is not merely an unpleasant percept that happens to be salient; it is a signal selected for action out of a complex affective and motivational field, whose purpose is to break whatever the organism is currently doing and urge escape. The model's substantive move is that this interruptive function is not fixed. Its strength is set by the relationship between the pain's own characteristics -- above all its threat value -- and the characteristics of the competing environmental demand, including that demand's difficulty and emotional arousal. The paper closes by redefining chronic pain as chronic interruption, which is the same model run at its other extreme.

## Why this matters for INV-092

INV-092 asserts that a suppression mechanism must stay permeable to harm, and that a suppression setting which improves goal completion by silencing weak harm cues is a *failure*. That claim presupposes something this paper is the standard citation for: that harm occupies a distinguished interrupt channel in the first place, whose job is precisely to override an established commitment. If harm were just another high-salience input, INV-092 would be a preference about weighting rather than a safety invariant. Eccleston and Crombez are the argument that it is the latter.

But the more useful contribution to REE is the second half of the model, and it cuts against comfort rather than for it. The interrupt is *contingent* -- threat value is weighed against competing demand -- which means the biological harm channel already has the knob INV-092 is worried about. Turn up the competing goal's demand and the interrupt weakens, by design, in the healthy system. So a REE suppression mechanism cannot inherit permeability by asserting that harm signals are privileged. Privilege here is a term in a ratio, not an exemption. Permeability becomes a quantity that has to be measured at every suppression setting, which is exactly what INV-092's falsifier specifies and exactly what a single-axis attention score cannot deliver.

There is a third thing here worth stating because it constrains the acceptance shape from the other side. The paper's clinical endpoint -- chronic pain as chronic interruption -- is the degenerate failure at the low-threshold end: a harm channel that interrupts too readily monopolises capacity and destroys goal completion entirely. INV-092's non-degeneracy guard already insists the harm channel be demonstrably live at zero suppression. This paper adds that "more interruptible is always better" is false, and that the acceptance criterion needs an upper bound on interruption as well as a floor, or the falsifier will be passable by a system that simply cannot hold a goal.

## Limitations and caveats

This is a theoretical model over human nociception, not a computational mechanism, and it defines no suppression-strength parameter that REE could port. Pain is also only one member of INV-092's harm category; the claim's hardest case -- another agent's state becoming newly relevant -- is not addressed here at all, and is taken up by the Pessoa (2002) entry in this directory. Reading the threat-value-versus-demand interaction as a design specification for a REE knob is an extrapolation the authors do not make and would probably not endorse. The paper is also now old enough that the suppression-specific attention literature it is being read alongside postdates it.

## Confidence reasoning

Source quality is high (0.85): *Psychological Bulletin*, by the authors who set the field's framing of this question, and heavily cited since. Mapping fidelity is high on architecture and low on mechanism, netting 0.75 -- the privileged-but-contingent interrupt transfers cleanly; the absence of any parameterisation does not. Transfer risk is moderate (0.40): human nociception to an artificial agent's harm channel is a real leap, but INV-092 is itself stated at this level of abstraction, so the two meet on the same ground rather than requiring one to be pushed down to the other. Aggregate 0.72, weighted toward mapping fidelity because INV-092 is an architectural invariant rather than an empirical prediction.
