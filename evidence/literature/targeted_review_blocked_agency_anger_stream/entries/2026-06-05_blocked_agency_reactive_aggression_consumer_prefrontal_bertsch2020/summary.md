# Reactive aggression: the consumer and its prefrontal gate (Bertsch et al. 2020)

**Source:** Bertsch, K., Florange, J. & Herpertz, S.C. (2020). Understanding Brain Mechanisms of Reactive Aggression. *Current Psychiatry Reports*, 22:81. DOI [10.1007/s11920-020-01208-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11920-020-01208-6).

## What the paper does

A review of the neurobiology of reactive aggression in healthy and clinical populations. It anchors two distinctions REE needs. First, reactive aggression is "commonly defined as a response to social threat, provocation, or frustration, and is strongly associated with anger" — it is affect-driven and high-arousal, and it is *categorically distinct* from proactive/instrumental aggression, which is planned, goal-directed, and low-arousal. Second, it locates the response in the acute-threat circuit — "the amygdala, hypothalamus, and periaqueductal gray (PAG)" generating "behavioral, hormonal, and autonomic 'survival' actions to imminent threat" — the same subcortical axis Panksepp assigns to RAGE.

## Findings relevant to the candidate stream

The paper's value here is the **consumer architecture**, not the antecedent. It establishes that the affective-anger response has (a) a high-arousal subcortical *output* and (b) a *prefrontal regulatory gate*: "the ventrolateral prefrontal cortex (vlPFC) is involved in emotional action control and response selection," while "the dlPFC has been associated with keeping control strategies and goals in mind," and reduced vmPFC volume is associated with greater physical aggression. So the adaptive picture is: a frustration/threat signal proposes an assertive/escalatory response, and prefrontal circuitry decides whether to release, inhibit, or redirect it. The reactive-vs-proactive dissociation is the other load-bearing point — frustration-driven assertion is a different process from planned goal pursuit, even though both can end in similar overt acts.

## How it maps to REE

This grounds the verdict's **consumer set**. A blocked-agency signal in REE should drive an *assert / escalate-effort* response — the adaptive analogue of reactive aggression — that is explicitly gated by a regulatory channel. REE already has the candidate gates: commitment-threshold adjustment (ARC-016) and maintenance-time decommit (MECH-342). The mapping is therefore: blocked-agency rises → assert/escalate proposed → if assertion keeps failing, the prefrontal-analogue gate converts it to *decommit* (release the blocked commitment) rather than unbounded escalation. The reactive-vs-proactive split is the architectural warning: the assert channel is *distinct* from ordinary z_goal-driven planned pursuit, which supports registering a separate affect row rather than nudging goal salience.

## Limitations and confidence

This is a human clinical review of overt aggression; REE is a single-agent substrate with no aggressive behaviour and no social target, and several of the paper's triggers (social threat, provocation) are V4-scope. Only the *frustration* trigger and the *assert-plus-prefrontal-gate* consumer architecture transfer to the V3 proxy. I score 0.74: good source quality, moderate mapping fidelity (clean for the consumer architecture, partial because the behaviour and some triggers are out of V3 scope), moderate-high transfer risk from human social aggression to a single-agent assert/decommit channel.
