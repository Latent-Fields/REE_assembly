# Psychological reactance: the autonomy-threat pole and the assert/helplessness split (Steindl et al. 2015)

**Source:** Steindl, C., Jonas, E., Sittenthaler, S., Traut-Mattausch, E. & Greenberg, J. (2015). Understanding Psychological Reactance: New Developments and Findings. *Zeitschrift für Psychologie*, 223(4):205–214. DOI [10.1027/2151-2604/a000222](https://doi.org/10.1027/2151-2604/a000222).

## What the paper does

A review of psychological reactance theory (the Brehm lineage) and its recent experimental and physiological developments. Reactance is defined as "an unpleasant motivational arousal that emerges when people experience a threat to or loss of their free behaviors." Its intensity scales with the importance of the threatened freedom and the magnitude of the threat. The state is an "intermingled state of affect and cognition" — discomfort, hostility, and anger plus freedom-related cognitions — and it motivates *restoration* of the threatened freedom through four routes: direct behavioural restoration (do the restricted thing), restoration by implication (watch others do it), aggression toward the threat source, and subjective restoration via reappraisal (derogate the source, upgrade the restricted option, downgrade the imposed alternative).

## Findings relevant to the candidate stream

The single most useful sentence for the verdict is the contrast with helplessness: reactance has "energizing and behavior-directing properties, distinguishing reactance from learned helplessness by maintaining the individual's sense of capacity to alter their situation." This is the **controllability-axis dissociation** the whole verdict rests on. Two responses can follow from the same blocked outcome: if the agent retains a sense of capacity to change things, the response is *energised assertion / freedom-restoration* (reactance); if capacity-belief has collapsed, the response is *passive withdrawal* (learned helplessness). They are opposite poles on the controllability axis, not points on a single intensity scale. The paper also makes clear that reactance proper is a *social* phenomenon: it presupposes a threatened freedom and, usually, an agent or source imposing the restriction.

## How it maps to REE

Two things. First, **scope**: the reactance construct proper (anti-coercion, anti-domination, autonomy-threat, anti-exploitation) requires REE to model an *other agent* as the source of the restriction — that is V4-social, and should be deferred. Second, and importable to V3, **the assert/helplessness dissociation**. REE already encodes the controllability axis on the suffering side (Q-036 escapability gate on z_harm_a / SD-019b), and it already has a withdraw consumer (suffering → mode-switch, SD-032a). What it lacks is the *complementary pole*: the energised-assert response when an outcome is blocked but capacity-belief is retained. Reactance is the cleanest evidence in the set that this pole is real, separable, and behaviourally opposite to withdrawal. So the V3 proxy gets a principled gate: blocked-agency with capacity retained → assert/persist; as capacity-belief falls, the signal hands off to the suffering/withdraw pathway.

## Limitations and confidence

This is a secondary review of a human social-cognitive theory; REE-V3 is single-agent with no representation of a coercing other, so the construct proper does not transfer — only its abstract dissociation does, and even that is an analogy rather than a measured mechanism. I score 0.62: solid but secondary source, moderate mapping fidelity (tight for the assert-vs-helplessness split, V4-bound for the construct), high transfer risk. It earns its place by drawing the V3/V4 scope line and by naming the missing assert pole.
