# RAGE as a distinct primary-process affect system (Davis & Montag 2019)

**Source:** Davis, K.L. & Montag, C. (2019). Selected Principles of Pankseppian Affective Neuroscience. *Frontiers in Neuroscience*, 12:1025. DOI [10.3389/fnins.2018.01025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2018.01025).

## What the paper does

The paper is a consolidating review of Jaak Panksepp's affective-neuroscience framework: seven genetically-provided, subcortically-instantiated primary-process emotional systems — SEEKING, FEAR, RAGE, LUST, CARE, PANIC/GRIEF, PLAY — each with a dedicated, evolutionarily conserved neural circuit identified largely through electrical brain stimulation (ESB) and lesion work. The relevant system for this pull is RAGE/Anger. The authors describe its circuit as hierarchically organised: "rage responses evoked by ESB of the amygdala were abolished by lesions at the level of the hypothalamus or PAG," whereas "aggressive responses evoked at the PAG level were not dependent on either of the higher two levels" — i.e. medial amygdala → medial hypothalamus → dorsal periaqueductal gray, with each level controlling those below it.

## Findings relevant to the candidate stream

The load-bearing point for our verdict is *dissociation*. RAGE sits among the negative-valence systems (with FEAR and PANIC) but maintains a distinct neural architecture and activation pattern from them. Critically, the framework also separates RAGE (affective attack) from SEEKING-driven "quiet-bite" predatory behaviour — these are different systems with different subjective properties. So at the most basic mammalian level the brain does not represent anger as a sub-type of pain, a sub-type of fear, or a sub-type of appetitive pursuit; it is its own command system. This is exactly the biology-first evidence the proto-affective audit needs before deciding whether to register a boundary-violation/blocked-agency/anger row separate from the harm register.

## How it maps to REE

It grounds the *premise* of verdict (a): a distinct affect stream is biologically defensible. It does **not**, on its own, tell REE what the computational antecedent is — Panksepp's framework is about the affective *output* (the command system) rather than the upstream trigger computation. The antecedent ("intended/expected action or outcome was blocked") comes from the frustrative-non-reward and comparator anchors in this same review set. The honest reading is: anger is a distinct affective readout (this paper), and the *thing that feeds it* in the single-agent case is a frustrative-non-reward / agency-violation computation (the other papers). REE should therefore register the antecedent computation, not an emotion-named "anger module."

## Limitations and confidence

This is a framework-committed secondary review built on ESB and cross-species lesion data; it asserts the existence and distinctness of the systems rather than re-deriving them, and REE has no PAG/hypothalamus homolog, so nothing circuit-level transfers. What transfers is the *category boundary*: anger ≠ pain ≠ fear at the level of primary-process architecture. I score this 0.72 — strong for the distinctness premise that anchors the verdict, deliberately held below 0.8 because it is theory-laden and silent on the computational mechanism REE actually has to build.
