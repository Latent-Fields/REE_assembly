# Marder 2012 -- neuromodulation of neuronal circuits

**What it says.** Marder's review, built largely on the crustacean stomatogastric ganglion, argues that neuromodulators change both how individual neurons fire and how strongly they are effectively connected. The same wiring diagram can then produce very different circuits and outputs depending on the modulatory state. The connectome is a minimal scaffold, and the modulatory environment decides which functional circuit runs on it.

**Why it matters for ARC-111.** ARC-111 says REE has the gain form of context-conditioning (a context-dependent multiplier on a fixed transfer function) but not the weight form (the effective weights themselves depend on context). Marder is the standard reference that brains have the weight form, at least in the sense of effective synaptic strength. One modulatory context yields a different circuit, not a louder or quieter version of the same one.

**Where it strains.** The review also shows that biology doesn't keep gain and weight apart. The same modulator can change excitability (gain-like) and synaptic efficacy (weight-like) at once, and much of the circuit switching comes through excitability. The stomatogastric changes are also fast and reversible, not learned weight sets per context. So Marder supports the claim's direction, context reaching effective weights, but offers no support for treating gain and weight as cleanly separate mechanisms. That separability is what ARC-111's falsifier depends on.

**Confidence.** 0.58.
