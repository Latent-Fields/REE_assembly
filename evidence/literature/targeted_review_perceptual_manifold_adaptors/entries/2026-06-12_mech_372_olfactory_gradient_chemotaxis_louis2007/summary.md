# Louis et al. (2007) — Bilateral olfactory sensory input enhances chemotaxis behavior

*According to PubMed.* Louis, M., Huber, T., Benton, R., Sakmar, T. P., & Vosshall, L. B. (2007). *Nature Neuroscience*, 11(2), 187–199. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn2031)

**Claim tested:** MECH-372 (modality-heterogeneity / adaptor-depth gradient) — **L2 layer** of PA-7 (modality-specific adaptors: the olfactory-gradient / low-adaptor end).

## What the paper did

Where DiCarlo (this directory) anchors the *deep* end of the adaptor-depth gradient, this paper anchors the *shallow* end. Louis and colleagues asked how much neural machinery is actually required to navigate by smell. They reduced *Drosophila* larvae to a single functional olfactory sensory neuron — on either the left or the right side of the head — and watched them perform chemotaxis. The key methodological advance was building odorant environments with *stable, spectroscopically measured* concentration gradients, so that behaviour could be related to a known physical gradient rather than an inferred one. Two results matter here. First, a *single* functional olfactory neuron — unilateral input, no bilateral comparison available — was sufficient for larvae to climb the gradient and find the source: a near-raw monotonic concentration signal, sampled over time and movement, carries enough information to orient. Second, *bilateral* input improved navigational accuracy, and the improvement was consistent with a signal-to-noise benefit (two noisy estimates of concentration averaged into a better one) rather than a rich stereo-spatial geometry computation.

## Why it matters for the claim

MECH-372's load-bearing assertion is that senses are *not* interchangeable raw streams of equal adaptor depth — that smell sits near the raw end as a gradient primitive (stronger/weaker, nearer/farther, attractive/aversive, trail/no-trail) that can be followed, escaped, strengthened, weakened, and associated with outcome *with a minimal adaptor*, whereas sight needs a deep manifold constructor. This paper is direct behavioural warrant for the smell half. Orientation behaviour — the canonical use of a gradient — falls out of a near-raw chemical signal with essentially no deep transformation: one neuron, one scalar concentration estimate, and movement through the gradient suffices. That is exactly the "low-adaptor-depth" primitive MECH-372 describes, and it is the biological justification for the roadmap's PA-2 pillar (admit a smell-like gradient sense as the *cheapest, earliest-honest* V4 adaptor) and for MECH-372's developmental-ordering corollary that gradient-like senses are compatible with the earliest world-model construction ("smell may have been the first sense").

## How it translates to REE — and the honest limits

The transfer is principle-level, and I have priced it accordingly. What the paper licenses is "a gradient can drive orientation with minimal transformation of the raw signal." What it does *not* license is "all olfaction is low-adaptor." Two limits carry the weight. First, this is larval *Drosophila* with a *single* odorant and an externally imposed monotonic gradient — phylogenetically and ecologically distant from REE's intended agent and from mammalian olfaction. Odour *identity* and *mixture demixing* — recognising *what* a smell is, separating overlapping sources — are not tested here and may themselves demand a non-trivial adaptor; so the entry supports the gradient-*primitive* reading of MECH-372, not a blanket claim that the whole olfactory modality is shallow. Second, the bilateral benefit is an SNR effect, not evidence of a structured olfactory *manifold*; the paper therefore says nothing about the *upper bound* of olfactory adaptor depth. Both are recorded as failure signatures because they are exactly where a naive "smell = trivial" reading of MECH-372 would over-reach. This is the right caution for the cluster's canonical risk (biology-right / mechanism-wrong, per `feedback_biology_before_formal_definitions`): the gradient primitive is real, but "low-adaptor" should mean "orientation is cheap," not "the modality is shallow."
