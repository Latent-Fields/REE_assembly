# Bujack et al. (2022) — The non-Riemannian nature of perceptual color space

*According to PubMed.* Bujack, R., Teti, E., Miller, J., Caffrey, E., & Turton, T. L. (2022). *PNAS*, 119(18), e2119753119. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2119753119)

**Claims tested:** ARC-087 (sense-specific perceptual-manifold adaptor), MECH-372 (modality-heterogeneity / adaptor-depth gradient) — **L1 layer** of PA-7, and the closest published anchor to the *source trigger* for the whole ARC-087/MECH-372/Q-065 cluster (the Schrödinger colour-theory completion result noted in `docs/thoughts/2026-06-07_sight_specific_perceptual_manifolds.md`).

## What the paper did

The standard model of colour perception, traced from Riemann through Helmholtz and Schrödinger, holds that perceived colour lives in a three-dimensional *Riemannian* space — a smoothly curved metric space in which the distance between two colours is the length of the shortest path, and where, crucially, large differences are built additively from small ones. Bujack and colleagues tested that additivity assumption directly against human colour-difference judgements and found it violated. They show the principle of *diminishing returns*: the perceived difference between two well-separated colours is reliably *less* than the sum of the small perceptual steps along a path connecting them. This is not a measurement nuisance — it is geometrically fatal to the Riemannian model, because in any Riemannian geometry the metric is locally additive and a long distance *must* equal the integral of the short steps. The authors conclude perceptual colour space is non-Riemannian, that the colour-difference metrics used across imaging, visualization, and the paint/textile industries are valid only in the small-difference limit, and that the data hint at a second-order Weber–Fechner law.

## Why it matters for the claim

ARC-087 makes an unusually specific structural assertion: the perceptual manifold built by a sense adaptor is "curved, compressed, asymmetric, non-additive, unlike the raw measurement space." Colour is the cleanest possible test case because it *feels* like a simple physical-to-cognitive mapping — three cone types, three numbers. Bujack shows that even here the perceptual geometry is non-additive in a way the raw tristimulus space is not. That is ARC-087's thesis made concrete: the adaptor does not pass colour through; it constructs a metric whose global structure departs from the measurement space. For MECH-372, this is direct support for putting sight at the *deep-adaptor* end of the depth gradient — colour geometry is something that must be **constructed**, not a near-raw gradient that can be followed with a minimal transform. A flat feature extractor, inheriting the additive structure of the raw signal, would systematically misjudge large colour differences exactly as the legacy Riemannian metrics do.

## How it translates to REE — and the boundary

The translation is tight for colour and should be held there. The paper licenses "the colour adaptor's output manifold is non-Riemannian/non-additive"; it does *not* license a blanket "all perceptual manifolds are non-Riemannian" across touch, hearing, and proprioception — that remains an open conjecture under MECH-372, not a finding. Two further honest limits: (1) the paper *removes* a model (Riemannian) without supplying the replacement geometry, so it tells the adaptor designer that the target is exotic but not what it is; and (2) it is a geometric reanalysis of aggregate colour-difference data, silent on the adaptor-internal transform (how cone signals become this non-Riemannian metric) — it grounds the *output shape*, not the mechanism. It also usefully bounds Shepard (this directory): Shepard's locally additive Euclidean/city-block metrics are precisely the additive structure that fails at large colour differences, so the two papers together say "there is a lawful perceptual metric (Shepard), but its global geometry is more exotic than a simple additive embedding (Bujack)."
