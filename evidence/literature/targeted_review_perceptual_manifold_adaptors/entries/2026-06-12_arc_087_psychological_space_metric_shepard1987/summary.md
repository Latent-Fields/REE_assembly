# Shepard (1987) — Toward a universal law of generalization for psychological science

*According to PubMed.* Shepard, R. N. (1987). *Science*, 237(4820), 1317–1323. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.3629243)

**Claims tested:** ARC-087 (sense-specific perceptual-manifold adaptor), Q-065 (metric origin + cross-modal negotiability) — **L1 layer** of the PA-7 biology-grounding gap (perceptual-manifold / similarity geometry).

## What the paper did

Shepard asks whether there is a law behind stimulus generalization that holds across stimuli, species, and modalities. His move is to stop treating generalization as a function of physical stimulus difference and instead recover a *psychological* space: a metric embedding in which the measured probability that a response learned to one stimulus transfers to another is an invariant, monotonically decreasing function of the *psychological* distance between them. When you do this recovery honestly (via multidimensional scaling of confusion/similarity data), the generalization gradient collapses onto a near-exponential decay, and the admissible metric is one of two forms — Euclidean when the stimulus dimensions are integral (perceptually fused, e.g. hue and brightness), city-block when they are separable (independently attendable, e.g. size and colour). He then derives these regularities from prior assumptions about the probable size and shape of "consequential regions" (natural kinds) in the world, arguing the law is something evolution would internalize because it reflects the statistics of which things share consequences.

## Why it matters for the claim

This is the abstract spine under ARC-087's central premise. ARC-087 says a sense does not deliver raw data to the shared world model — it delivers a *shaped geometry of possible differences*, a perceptual metric. Shepard is the canonical demonstration that such a metric is real, recoverable, and lawful: the quantity that actually governs behaviour is distance in a structured psychological space, not distance in the raw measurement space. That is precisely the "physical signal → ... → perceptual metric / manifold" transformation ARC-087 names. For Q-065, the result speaks directly to the *learned-from-similarity* arm: the metric is constructible from the statistics of generalization/similarity, which is exactly the self-organisation route Q-065 part (1) offers as one fork. The exponential generalization gradient is the sort of non-trivial, evolutionarily shaped geometry the adaptor is supposed to output — not a flat, additive copy of the stimulus.

## How it translates to REE — and where the translation strains

The honest boundary is that Shepard characterises a *general* psychological-similarity space, not a *sense-specific adaptor*. It tells us what the adaptor's output should look like (a metric space with lawful generalization) but is silent on the two things MECH-372 and Q-065 actually fork on. First, adaptor *depth*: Shepard's framework applies equally to a near-raw gradient (smell) and a deeply constructed manifold (sight), so it cannot adjudicate MECH-372's depth gradient. Second, learned-vs-defined: Shepard explicitly argues the law is *evolutionarily internalized* — which reads as a structural prior — yet the space is also *recovered from* similarity data, which reads as learned. The same exponential law is compatible with both forks, so the paper grounds the existence of the metric without resolving its origin. ## Confidence reasoning

`supports`, confidence **0.76**. Source quality is about as high as the literature offers — a foundational *Science* result, replicated across decades and recently shown to hold even for naturalistic, high-dimensional stimuli. Transfer risk is low because the claim is explicitly universal across sentient organisms (so the animal-to-REE jump is licensed by the paper's own scope). The ceiling on confidence is mapping fidelity: this is a constraint on the *target geometry* of an adaptor, not a constructive account of the adaptor itself, and it leaves ARC-087's depth question and Q-065's origin fork open. It is the right foundational citation for the L1 layer — it earns the claim its "perception is a geometry of difference" premise — but it must be paired with a constructive mechanism paper (DiCarlo 2012, this directory) to ground the adaptor *transform* rather than just its output.
